\begin{abstract}
Gao proved that the random regular graphs \(\Gnd\) and \(G(n,d+1)\) can be coupled
so that \(\Gnd\subset G(n,d+1)\) with high probability whenever \(d\to\infty\) and
\(d=o(n^{1/3})\), and observed that the same argument should extend to
\(d=o(n^{1/2})\) via the sharper conditional edge probabilities of~\cite{Gao20},
leaving the details for a simpler exposition.
We supply those details, obtaining the range \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).

As a new consequence of the same error schedule, we prove long simultaneous
nesting chains. The length is the Chebyshev optimum for the patched relative
variance of perfect-matching counts. After the odd part of the Laplace expansion
is removed (Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}), that variance is of order
\(D^{-3}+D/n\), so the window has length
\(L=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}).
The term \(1/(6D^3)\) alone forces \(L=o(D)\). Chernoff-scale windows
\(L=\Theta\sqrt{D\log n}\) are then available for
\(\omega(\log n)=D=o\bigl(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7}\bigr)\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains}).
One further pass through the switching count, using the same edge estimate on the
pairs that were previously bounded, replaces the ratio error \(O(d^2/n)\) by
\(O(d/n)\). The same windows then run up to
\(o\bigl(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{3/5}\bigr)\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-two-fifths}).
The edge-linear part of that error cancels in moment ratios, and the centered
fourth moment is then a constant times the square of the variance, so the same
windows run up to \(o\bigl(n^{4/9}/(\log n)^{5/9}\bigr)\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}).
The same one-step bound sums from every \(D\to\infty\) up to
\(\lfloor n^{2/7}/\log n\rfloor\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:fourth-span}).
Carrying the expansion to an arbitrary fixed order \(q\) produces the moment
bound \(O_q(v(d)^q)\) and moves the Chernoff window to
\(o\bigl(n^{2q/(4q+1)}/(\log n)^{(2q+1)/(4q+1)}\bigr)\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment}, Theorem~\ref{thm:high-chains}).
Every exponent strictly below \(1/2\) is attained
(Corollary~\ref{cor:near-barrier}), and the summed chain reaches every
exponent strictly below \(1/3\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:high-span}).
For \(D=o(n^{1/6})\) the triangle projection gives a separate chain up to
degree \(\lfloor n^{1/6}/\log n\rfloor\). The one-step total variation is
\(O(d^{-6/5})\), which sums (Corollary~\ref{cor:summable}).

Past \(\sqrt{n}\), the absolute matching probability is known only up to
\(1+O(d^2/n)\). Concentration of the matching count cancels the universal part of
that error, and exact \(+1\) extends to \(o(n^{2/3})\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds}).
Tracking the error budget in the Kim--Vu sandwich proof of~\cite{BIM25} with
absolute switching constants, we obtain the vanishing rates
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) in general,
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) via linear connecting sets, and the
process-optimal \(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\), by a
last-layer Bernstein upgrade of two-vertex path upper bounds to Chernoff scale.
The Chernoff floor \(\varepsilon\asymp\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) requires a different
architecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}).
We prove that the process conjecture of~\cite{BIM25} implies Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q},
that for \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) the Chernoff problem reduces to a final-window
coupling through surplus degrees
\(\sqrt{d\log n}\le\delta\le d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), and that a hybrid
thinning of the \(1/3\)-sandwich residual already yields a Chernoff-scale
supergraph with the correct edge marginals (Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid}), and that
peeling \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\) perfect matchings from the residual produces an
exact \((d+m)\)-regular one-sided container inside the rate-\(1/3\) ER upper graph
(Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}, Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}), and that
host-restricted identification of that container with \(G(n,d+m)\) is open
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}). A sandwich coupling at degree \(d+m\)
does not place a uniform copy of \(G(n,d+m)\) inside the upper graph already
built at degree \(d\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Let \(\Gnd\) denote the uniform random \(d\)-regular graph on \(n\) labelled vertices
(with \(dn\) even). Comparing \(\Gnd\) across degrees is subtle because there is no
canonical monotone coupling.
Gao, Isaev and McKay conjectured that for all admissible \(d_1\le d_2\)
(excluding two parity pathologies), there is a coupling with
\(\PP(G(n,d_1)\subset G(n,d_2))=1-o(1)\).
The critical case is \(d_2=d_1+1\).

Gao~\cite{Gao21} proved exact \(+1\) nesting for \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/3})\),
by coupling through perfect matchings and controlling the number \(Y\) of perfect
matchings via an \(O(d^3/n)\) subgraph-probability error.
The same paper states explicitly that Theorem~8 should extend to all
\(d\to\infty\) with \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) using the sharper conditional edge probabilities
of~\cite{Gao20}, but leaves the details for a simpler exposition
(see~\cite[Remark~16(b), §5]{Gao21}).
We write those details out, and one further iteration of the same switching count extends exact \(+1\) from \(o(n^{1/2})\) to \(o(n^{2/3})\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds}),
and \(k\) iterations of the same substitution reach every degree
\(o(n^{1-\varepsilon})\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:below-linear}).
Letting the number of iterations grow like \(\log n/\log\log n\) reaches the
Laplace window \(d=o(n/(\log n)^2)\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:laplace-degree}).
A shorter overlap window, of width \(\bar k^{2/3}\) rather than \(\sqrt{\bar k}\,\log n\),
removes that restriction and gives exact \(+1\) for every \(d=o(n)\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sublinear}).
On \(n^{1/2}\le D=o(n/(\log n)^2)\) the same moment bound nests
\(o((n/D)^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) consecutive degrees
(Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-past-sqrt}).
Degree \(\Theta(n)\) with both \(d\) and \(n-d\) of order \(n\) lies outside the edge
estimate (Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-barrier}). The complementary range
\(n-d=o(n)\) follows by complementation (Theorem~\ref{thm:dense}).
As a new consequence of the same patched error schedule, we obtain long
simultaneous nesting chains (Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}). The window length is
\(o(v(D)^{-1/3})\), where \(v(D)\) is the patched relative variance of the
perfect-matching count. This reaches degrees up to \(o(\sqrt{n})\),
with Chernoff-scale increments up to \(o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\),
and, after one further switching pass, up to \(o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{3/5})\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-two-fifths}).
Inside \(D=o(n^{1/6})\) the one-step total variation is \(O(d^{-6/5})\), so the
chain runs from any \(D\to\infty\) up to \(\lfloor n^{1/6}/\log n\rfloor\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:summable}).
A fourth-moment bound on the matching count, proved by cancelling the
edge-linear factor in the moment ratios, moves that window past the Chebyshev
ceiling, to \(o(n^{4/9}/(\log n)^{5/9})\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment}, Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}).
The same cancellation at every fixed order \(q\) gives the moment bound
\(O_q(v(d)^q)\). Markov's inequality then reaches
\(o\bigl(n^{2q/(4q+1)}/(\log n)^{(2q+1)/(4q+1)}\bigr)\), and therefore every
degree \(o(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment},
Theorem~\ref{thm:high-chains}, Corollary~\ref{cor:near-barrier}).
The factor \(1+O(d^2/n)\) in the subgraph probabilities does not resolve a moment of
that size past \(o(n^{1/8})\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-precision}).

Independently, the Kim--Vu sandwich conjecture---coupling \(\Gnd\) between two
binomial graphs of density \((1\pm\varepsilon)d/n\)---was proved in full by
Behague, Il'kovi\v{c} and Montgomery~\cite{BIM25} for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
That yields constant-factor degree gaps, but not \(+1\).
We formulate the Chernoff-optimal quantitative refinement
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}) as the natural enabling theorem past \(\sqrt{n}\),
and extract from~\cite{BIM25} the vanishing rates
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) in general
(Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}), \(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) for all
\(d\ge C\log n\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-fifth}), and the process-optimal
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}), via Chernoff-scale two-sided path
counts (Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}).
For \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) the same rate \(1/3\) was already available from
~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25} (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}).
As structural progress toward Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}, we prove that the process
conjecture of~\cite{BIM25} implies the Chernoff sandwich
(Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q}), and that the Chernoff problem reduces to a
final surplus-degree window of width \(d^{2/3}\to d^{1/2}\) for every
\(d\ge C\log n\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}).
Peeling perfect matchings from the rate-\(1/3\) residual then yields an exact
\((d+m)\)-regular container at Chernoff scale \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}).
Free \(m\)-extensions of a \(d\)-regular graph are flat up to a \(1+o(1)\) factor
for every \(d=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{1/5})\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:free-flat}).
Identifying the host-restricted container with \(G(n,d+m)\) remains open
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}); Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} records why
conditioning on the degree-\(d\) upper graph does not inherit a total-variation
decomposition.

\subsection{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Main]
\label{thm:main}
Assume the conditional edge-probability estimate of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d=d(n)\) satisfy \(d\to\infty\) and
\(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\[
\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(+1\) up to \(n^{2/3}\)]
\label{thm:two-thirds}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\to\infty\) with \(d=o(n^{2/3})\).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\[
\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The hypothesis on~\cite{Gao20} is used as a black box, exactly as anticipated
in~\cite{Gao21}.
All other steps are either elementary or inherited from~\cite{Gao21} with a
tracked error substitution \(d^3/n\mapsto d^2/n\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Long simultaneous chains}

\begin{theorem}[Long \(+1\) chains]
\label{thm:chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D=D(n)\), \(L=L(n)\) be positive integers with \(Dn\) even.
Write
\[
v(D)
:=
D^{-3}+\frac{D}{n}.
\]
If \(D\to\infty\), \(D=o(n^{1/2})\), \(L\to\infty\), and \(L=o\bigl(v(D)^{-1/3}\bigr)\),
then there exists a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{D+L}\) such that each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Longest second-moment window]
\label{cor:mega}
At \(D=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor\) (with parity arranged) one has
\(v(D)=\Theta(n^{-3/4})\), so Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} supplies a simultaneous
exact nesting of length \(L=o(n^{1/4})\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Chernoff-length chains up to \(n^{2/7}\)]
\label{cor:chernoff-chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy
\[
\frac{D}{\log n}\to\infty,
\qquad
D=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{2/7}}{(\log n)^{3/7}}\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) a small absolute constant
(and parity arranged so that \(mn\) is even).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v(D)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}. Then
\[
v(D)^{1/3}
\le
2\max\Biggl\{
D^{-1},\;
\Bigl(\frac{D^2}{n}\Bigr)^{1/3}
\Biggr\},
\]
up to adjusting the absolute constant inside the \(O\)-notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, which is absorbed into the little-o below.
Hence
\[
m\,v(D)^{1/3}
=
O\Biggl(
\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{D}}
+
\frac{D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/3}}
\Biggr).
\]
The hypothesis \(D/\log n\to\infty\) kills the first summand.
Under \(D=o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\) one has
\(D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}/n^{1/3}=o(1)\), so the second summand vanishes as well.
The log power is sharp: substituting \(D=n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7}\) produces
\[
\frac{D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/3}}
=
1,
\]
because \((2/7)\cdot(7/6)=1/3\) and \((3/7)\cdot(7/6)=1/2\).
A big-O bound at that threshold leaves a constant Chebyshev cost and the union bound does not tend to zero.
Thus \(m=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\). Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} with \(L:=m\) yields the chain
\(G_D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+m}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ratio error after one further switching pass]
\label{lem:ratio-error}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), and let
\(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\). Then
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d}{n}\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} writes each subgraph probability as its
principal term times \(1+\theta\), and obtains \(|\theta|=O(d^2/n)\) because each of
\(O(n)\) factors from~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} contributes a relative
\(O(d^2/n^2)\). The second-moment ratio only needs a sharper \(\theta\).

In the proof of that theorem, the displayed error \(O(\Delta^5 n/M^3)\) comes from
two contributions. The first invokes an edge probability with relative error
\(O(\Delta^2/M)=O(d/n)\). Theorem~6, already proved, supplies the same
probabilities with relative error \(O(d^2/n^2)\). The main term being corrected
has size \(O(d^2)\), so the absolute error drops to \(O(d^4/n^2)\). Divided by the
stub count \(M=dn\), this is a relative \(O(d^3/n^3)\) per edge. Over \(O(n)\)
edges the product is \(1+O(d^3/n^2)\). For \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) one has
\(d^3/n^2=O(d/n)\), since \((n^{1/2})^3/n^2=n^{-1/2}\).

The second contribution is \(\EE X_{uv}\), previously bounded by \(O(d^3/n)\) and
then discarded. For a host of maximum degree at most \(2\), expose the neighbours
of the two endpoints. There are \(d^2\) candidate pairs, up to \(O(d)\) pairs that
meet the host neighbourhood. Theorem~6 gives each closing probability as
\((d/n)(1+O(d/n))\). Summing produces
\[
\EE X_{uv}=\frac{d^3}{n}+O\Bigl(\frac{d^2}{n}\Bigr).
\]
The summand \(d^3/n\) depends only on \(d\) and \(n\). Divided by \(M\) it
contributes \(d^2/n^2\) to every conditional probability. The host-dependent piece
\(O(d^2/n)/M=O(d/n^2)\) per edge, and \(O(n)\) edges give a factor \(1+O(d/n)\).

A perfect matching has \(n/2\) edges. The union of two perfect matchings with
\(k\) edges in common has \(n-k\) edges. In the ratio of these probabilities the
universal factors contribute
\[
\exp\bigl((n-k-n)d^2/n^2+O(d^4/n^3)\bigr)
=
\exp\bigl(-kd^2/n^2+O(d^4/n^3)\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} localises the second-moment sum to
\(k=O(\sqrt{n/d}\,\log n)\). On that range
\(kd^2/n^2=O((d/n)^{3/2}\log n)\), which is \(O(d/n)\) for \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
The remainder \(d^4/n^3\) is likewise \(O(d/n)\), because
\((d^4/n^3)/(d/n)=d^3/n^2\) and \((n^{1/2})^3/n^2=n^{-1/2}\).

Every ratio of a union probability to the product of the two matching
probabilities is therefore the ratio of the principal terms times \(1+O(d/n)\).
The Laplace argument in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} returns
\(1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4}+d/n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Chernoff-length chains up to \(n^{2/5}\)]
\label{cor:chernoff-two-fifths}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy
\[
\frac{D}{\log n}\to\infty,
\qquad
D=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{2/5}}{(\log n)^{3/5}}\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) a small absolute constant
(and parity arranged so that \(mn\) is even).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v_\star(D)=D^{-3}+D/n\). Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error} gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(v_\star(D))\) throughout \(D=o(n^{1/2})\).
The same Chebyshev schedule as in Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}, with \(v\) replaced by
\(v_\star\), has one-step failure \(O(v_\star(D)^{1/3})\). Hence
\[
m\,v_\star(D)^{1/3}
=
O\Biggl(
\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{D}}
+
\frac{D^{5/6}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/3}}
\Biggr).
\]
The hypothesis \(D/\log n\to\infty\) kills the first summand.
Under \(D=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{3/5})\) the second summand is \(o(1)\):
substituting the threshold \(D=n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{3/5}\) produces \(1\), because
\((2/5)\cdot(5/6)=1/3\) and \((3/5)\cdot(5/6)=1/2\), and
\((n^{2/5})^{5/6}/n^{1/3}=1\).
A big-O bound at that threshold leaves a constant cost, so the little-o is required.
The union bound over the \(m\) steps tends to zero.
The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-eta} and of the coupling in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} uses the relative variance only through Chebyshev's
inequality, so it applies with \(v_\star\) in place of \(v\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Triangle moments]
\label{lem:tri-moments}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~2]{Gao20}. Let \(X\) be the number of triangles in \(\Gnd\),
and assume \(d\to\infty\) together with \(d=o(n^{1/6})\). Write \(\mu=(d-1)^3/6\).
Then \(\EE X=(1+O(1/n))\mu\), and for \(k\in\{2,3,4\}\),
\[
\EE[X^k]
=
(\EE X)^k+\binom{k}{2}(\EE X)^{k-1}+O\bigl((\EE X)^{k-2}\bigr).
\]
In particular \(\Var X=\EE X+o(1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\cite[Theorem~2]{Gao20} gives, for a host \(H\) with \(O(1)\) edges and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\),
\[
\PP(uv\in\Gnd\mid H\subseteq\Gnd)
=
\frac{(d-d_u^H)(d-d_v^H)}{dn}
\Bigl(1-\frac{\varphi_H(uv)}{dn}\Bigr)
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr).
\]
On a triangle the stub product of the three factors is \((d-1)^3/n^3\), and each
factor \(1-\varphi_H(uv)/(dn)\) is \(1+O(1/n)\). There are \(\binom{n}{3}=(1+O(1/n))n^3/6\)
potential triangles, so \(\EE X=(1+O(1/n))\mu\).

For the factorial moment, split \(k\)-tuples of distinct triangles into those whose
union has at most as many edges as vertices, and the rest. A component with as many
edges as vertices is a cycle, so the first class consists of \(k\) vertex-disjoint
triangles. Their number is \((1+O(1/n))(n^3/6)^k\), and the same edge estimate
gives probability \((1+O(1/n))((d-1)/n)^{3k}\). The contribution is
\((\EE X)^k+O((\EE X)^k/n)\). Since \(d=o(n^{1/6})\) one has
\((\EE X)^k/n=O((\EE X)^{k-2})\) for \(k\le 4\): the identity
\((n^{1/6})^{12}/n\big/(n^{1/6})^6=1\) shows that the boundary case \(k=4\) meets
\((\EE X)^2\), and the little-o hypothesis makes the ratio \(o(1)\).

A tuple with more edges than vertices uses \(h\le 3k\) edges on at most \(h-1\)
vertices, hence contributes \(O(d^h/n)\). The largest term is \(O(d^{3k}/n)\),
which is \(o((\EE X)^{k-2})\) under the same hypothesis. Expanding the factorial
moments into ordinary moments produces the displayed formula. The \(k=2\) case is
\(\EE[X^2]=(\EE X)^2+\EE X+o(1)\), so \(\Var X=\EE X+o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Matching count tracks triangles]
\label{lem:proj}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} and~\cite[Theorem~10]{Gao21}, and let \(d\to\infty\)
with \(d=o(n^{1/6})\). Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\) and
let \(X\) be the number of triangles. Set \(a=\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)/\Var X\) and
\(Y^\star=aX+\EE Y-a\EE X\). Then
\[
\Var(Y-Y^\star)=O\bigl((\EE Y)^2/d^4\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\EE\bigl[(Y^\star-\EE Y)^4\bigr]=O\bigl((\EE Y)^4/d^6\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\cite[Theorem~10]{Gao21} gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=(-d^{-3}+O(d^{-4}+d/n))\EE X\,\EE Y\) for \(d=o(n^{1/3})\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4}+d^2/n)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tri-moments} gives \(\Var X=\EE X+o(1)\) and \(\EE X=(1+O(1/n))\mu\)
with \(\mu=(d-1)^3/6\). Orthogonality yields \(\Var(Y-Y^\star)=\Var Y-\Var Y^\star\)
and \(\Var Y^\star=\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)^2/\Var X\). Substituting the expansions,
\[
\frac{\Var Y^\star}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d}{n}\Bigr),
\]
because \((1/d^6)\mu=1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4})\) and the covariance error \(O(d/n)\) is
absorbed. The difference of the two variance formulae is \(O(d^{-4}+d^2/n)\).
The identity \((n^{1/6})^6/n=1\) shows \(d^2/n=o(d^{-4})\) throughout
\(d=o(n^{1/6})\), so the difference is \(O(d^{-4})\).

For the fourth moment, Lemma~\ref{lem:tri-moments} gives
\(\EE[X^2]=\mu_X^2+\mu_X+O(1)\),
\(\EE[X^3]=\mu_X^3+3\mu_X^2+O(\mu_X)\), and
\(\EE[X^4]=\mu_X^4+6\mu_X^3+O(\mu_X^2)\), where \(\mu_X=\EE X\).
The central moment \(\EE[(X-\mu_X)^4]\) has coefficients \(1-4+6-3=0\) on
\(\mu_X^4\) and \(6-12+6=0\) on \(\mu_X^3\), hence is \(O(\mu_X^2)\).
Since \(a=\Theta((\EE Y)/d^3)\),
\(\EE[(Y^\star-\EE Y)^4]=a^4 O(\mu_X^2)=O((\EE Y)^4/d^6)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Summable one-step total variation]
\label{prop:summable-step}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} and~\cite[Theorem~10]{Gao21}.
If \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/6})\), then
\[
\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})=O(d^{-6/5}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:proj} in \(G(n,d+1)\), which stays inside \(o(n^{1/6})\).
Set \(\alpha=\tfrac12(d+1)^{-6/5}\). Chebyshev's inequality and the variance bound
give
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-Y^\star|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
=O(d^{-4}/\alpha^2)=O(d^{-8/5}),
\]
since \(-4+2\cdot(6/5)=-8/5\). Markov's inequality and the fourth-moment bound give
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y^\star-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
=O(d^{-6}/\alpha^4)=O(d^{-6/5}),
\]
since \(-6+4\cdot(6/5)=-6/5\). Thus \(Y\) is \(\alpha\)-concentrated with failure
\(O(d^{-6/5})\). Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} yields the total-variation bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Summable chains up to \(n^{1/6}\)]
\label{cor:summable}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} and~\cite[Theorem~10]{Gao21}.
Let \(n\) be even, let \(D\to\infty\), and let
\(M=\lfloor n^{1/6}/\log n\rfloor\). If \(D\le M\), then there is a coupling
\((G_d)_{d=D}^{M}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_M\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:summable-step} and Theorem~\ref{thm:approx} give, at each
\(d\in[D,M-1]\), a coupling of \(G(n,d)\) with \(G(n,d+1)\) of failure probability
\(O(d^{-6/5})\). Compose these couplings. For \(D\ge 2\),
\[
\sum_{d=D}^{M-1} O(d^{-6/5})
\le
C\int_{D-1}^{\infty} x^{-6/5}\,dx
=
5C(D-1)^{-1/5},
\]
since \(\int_D^\infty x^{-6/5}\,dx=5D^{-1/5}\). The hypothesis \(D\to\infty\) sends
the bound to \(0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fourth moment of the matching count]
\label{lem:fourth-moment}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), and let
\(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\). Write
\(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\). Then
\[
\frac{\EE\bigl[(Y-\EE Y)^4\bigr]}{(\EE Y)^4}
\le
K\,v(d)^2
\]
for an absolute constant \(K\) and all large \(n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(N\) for the number of perfect matchings and \(p=\PP(M\subseteq\Gnd)\),
which does not depend on the perfect matching \(M\). Then \(\EE Y=Np\).
For matchings \(M_1,\ldots,M_k\) let \(\sigma\) be the number of edge-identifications
in their union, so \(|E(\cup_i M_i)|=kn/2-\sigma\), and write \(s_{ij}=|M_i\cap M_j|\).
Thus \(\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}=\sum_e\binom{m_e}{2}\), where \(m_e\) is the number of the
\(k\) matchings that contain the edge \(e\), while
\(\sigma=\sum_e(m_e-1)_+\). The two agree on every edge with \(m_e\le 2\), and
\[
\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}-\sigma
=
\sum_{m_e\ge 3}\bigl(\binom{m_e}{2}-(m_e-1)\bigr)
\]
is carried only by edges that lie in at least three of the matchings.

As in Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error}, the universal part of \(\EE X_{uv}\) contributes
a relative factor \(d^2/n^2\) at every edge, with a coefficient \(c\) that depends
only on \(d\) and \(n\). The ratio of this factor on the union to its value on the
\(k\) separate matchings is \(\exp(-c\sigma d^2/n^2)\). The identity
\(\exp(\lambda kn/2)/\exp(\lambda n/2)^k=1\) is the case \(\sigma=0\).
An edge-linear factor therefore cancels in \(\EE[Y^k]/(\EE Y)^k\), and the
coefficient \(-3+8-6=-1\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:moment-precision} does not
arise for this factor.

The normalized moment is the average of these ratios. Split the average into
quadruples in which some pair has \(s_{ij}>2n/d\), and the rest.
On the first class, \(\PP(M_1\cup M_2\cup M_3\cup M_4\subseteq\Gnd)\le\PP(M_i\cup M_j\subseteq\Gnd)\).
Summing out the other two matchings reproduces the tail of the second-moment sum.
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} bounds that tail by \(O(e^{-cn/d})\) times the main term,
which is \(o(v(d)^2)\) throughout \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).

On the complementary class every pairwise overlap is at most \(2n/d\), so
\(\sigma=O(n/d)\) and \(c\sigma d^2/n^2=O(d/n)\). Expand
\[
\exp(-c\sigma d^2/n^2)
=
1-c\sigma d^2/n^2+O\bigl((d/n)^2\bigr).
\]
The constant \(1\) reproduces the product of the means. The linear term is a sum
of pairwise overlaps. Averaged under Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}, one pair
contributes the second-moment excess \(v_\star=\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(v(d))\).
A \(k\)-tuple has \(\binom{k}{2}\) pairs: \(\binom{3}{2}=3\) and \(\binom{4}{2}=6\).
An edge with \(m_e\ge 3\) meets three of the matchings. Inside the light class its
multiplicity is \(O(1)\), since an overlap of size \(\omega(1)\) that is still
\(O(n/d)\) is charged to the pair that contains it and is already present in
\(v_\star\). The triple defect, multiplied by \(d^2/n^2\), is
\(O(d^2/n^2)=O((d/n)^2)\).

The quadratic piece of the exponential, averaged on the Laplace window, has size
\((d^2/n^2)^2\cdot O(n/d)=O(d^3/n^3)\).
Wolfram gives \((d^4/n^4)\cdot(n/d)=d^3/n^3\) and
\((d^3/n^3)/(d/n)^2=d/n\).
For \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) this is \(o((d/n)^2)\). On \(d=o(n^{1/4})\), where
\(v(d)\asymp d^{-3}\), the coarser piece \(d^2/n^2\) satisfies
\((n^{1/4})^2/n^2=(n^{1/4})^{-6}=n^{-3/2}\), so \(d^2/n^2=O(d^{-6})=O(v(d)^2)\).
In both regimes the remainder is \(O(v(d)^2)\).

Therefore, with \(v_\star=O(v(d))\),
\[
\frac{\EE[Y^k]}{(\EE Y)^k}
=
1+\binom{k}{2}v_\star+O(v(d)^2)
\qquad(k\in\{2,3,4\}).
\]
The centered combination of the main terms vanishes at order \(v_\star\),
\[
(1+6v_\star)-4(1+3v_\star)+6(1+v_\star)-3=0,
\]
and the order-\(v_\star^2\) identity
\((1+6v+3v^2)-4(1+3v)+6(1+v)-3=3v^2\)
shows that the Gaussian piece, together with the remainder above, is
\(O(v(d)^2)\). Hence
\[
\frac{\EE[(Y-\EE Y)^4]}{(\EE Y)^4}=O(v(d)^2).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fourth-moment chains]
\label{thm:fourth-chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(D/\log n\to\infty\) and
\[
D=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{4/9}}{(\log n)^{5/9}}\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) small and \(mn\) even.
Then there is a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\) and \(\alpha:=v(d)^{2/5}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error} supplies the variance bound of this order.
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment} and Markov's inequality give
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
\le
\frac{K\,v(d)^2}{\alpha^4}.
\]
The choice \(\alpha=v(d)^{2/5}\) makes \(\alpha^4=v(d)^{8/5}\) and
\(v(d)^2/v(d)^{8/5}=v(d)^{2/5}\), hence the probability is \(O(v(d)^{2/5})\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} therefore bounds the one-step coupling failure by
\(O(\alpha+v(d)^{2/5})=O(v(d)^{2/5})\), once \(n\) is large enough that \(\alpha<1/2\).
The Chernoff length satisfies \(m=o(D)\) throughout the stated range, so
\(d\in[D,D+m]\) obeys \(v(d)\le 4v(D)\).
Thus each step fails with probability \(O(v(D)^{2/5})\).
The product of the length with this cost is
\[
m\,v(D)^{2/5}
=O\Biggl(
\frac{(\log n)^{1/2}}{D^{7/10}}
+
\frac{D^{9/10}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{2/5}}
\Biggr).
\]
The first summand tends to \(0\) because \(D/\log n\to\infty\): the exponent
\(7/10\) is larger than \(1/2\).
For the second, the threshold substitution \(D=n^{4/9}/(\log n)^{5/9}\) produces
\[
\frac{D^{9/10}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{2/5}}
=1,
\]
because \((4/9)\cdot(9/10)=2/5\) and \((5/9)\cdot(9/10)=1/2\), and
\((n^{4/9})^{9/10}/n^{2/5}=1\).
Since \(4/9-2/5=2/45>0\), this range lies past
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-two-fifths}.
A little-o hypothesis makes the product \(o(1)\). A union bound over the \(m\) steps
yields the chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fourth-moment chains from every large degree]
\label{cor:fourth-span}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even, let \(D\to\infty\), and let
\(M=\lfloor n^{2/7}/\log n\rfloor\). If \(D\le M\), then there is a coupling
\((G_d)_{d=D}^{M}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_M\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The one-step coupling in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains} fails
with probability \(O(v(d)^{2/5})\) at degree \(d\), where \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\).
For \(d\le n^{1/4}\) one has \(v(d)\le 2d^{-3}\), so the failure is
\(O(d^{-6/5})\). The sum of these terms from \(D\) up to \(n^{1/4}\) is
\(O(D^{-1/5})\) by the integral bound in Corollary~\ref{cor:summable}, and
\(D\to\infty\) sends it to \(0\).

For \(n^{1/4}\le d\le M\) one has \(v(d)\le 2d/n\), so the failure is
\(O((d/n)^{2/5})\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{n^{1/4}\le d\le M}O\bigl((d/n)^{2/5}\bigr)
\le
C n^{-2/5}\int_{1}^{M}x^{2/5}\,dx
\le
C'n^{-2/5}M^{7/5}.
\]
The identity \((2/7)\cdot(7/5)=2/5\) gives \((n^{2/7})^{7/5}/n^{2/5}=1\).
Substituting \(M=\lfloor n^{2/7}/\log n\rfloor\) leaves a factor
\((\log n)^{-7/5}\to 0\). The two ranges together cost \(o(1)\), and composing
the couplings gives the chain. Since \(2/7-1/6=5/42>0\), the upper end lies
past Corollary~\ref{cor:summable}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moments of every fixed order]
\label{lem:high-moment}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix an integer \(q\ge 2\). Let \(d\to\infty\) and
\(d=o(n^{1/2})\), and let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\).
Write \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\). Then
\[
\frac{\EE\bigl[(Y-\EE Y)^{2q}\bigr]}{(\EE Y)^{2q}}
\le
K_q\,v(d)^q
\]
for a constant \(K_q\) that depends only on \(q\), and for all large \(n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu_r=\EE[Y^r]/(\EE Y)^r\) for \(r\le 2q\), and write \(\sigma\) for the
edge-identification count of an \(r\)-tuple, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment}.
The host formed by at most \(2q\) matchings has maximum degree at most \(2q\).
Every constant below may therefore depend on \(q\), through~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20},
and on nothing else.

Split the average that defines \(\mu_r\) into tuples in which some pair shares
more than \(2n/d\) edges, and the rest. On the first class, discarding the other
matchings reproduces the tail of the second-moment sum. Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}
bounds that tail by \(O(e^{-cn/d})\). For \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) one has
\(v(d)\ge n^{-1}\), and \(e^{-cn/d}\) is smaller than every fixed power of
\(v(d)\).

On the complementary class, \(\sigma=O_q(n/d)\), so the edge-linear exponent
\(c\sigma d^2/n^2\) is \(O_q(d/n)\). Expand the exponential through order \(q-1\).
The remainder is \(O_q((d/n)^q)\). Since \(v(d)\ge d/n\), this is \(O_q(v(d)^q)\).

The Taylor polynomial is a sum of products of at most \(q-1\) factors of
\(\sigma\). Write \(\sigma=\sum_{a<b}s_{ab}-\Delta\), where \(\Delta\) is carried
only by edges that lie in at least three of the matchings. A product that uses
only the pairwise overlaps \(s_{ab}\) mentions at most \(2(q-1)\) matchings.
Averaging it over an \(r\)-tuple produces a polynomial in \(r\) of degree at most
\(2q-2\): each pattern of overlaps contributes a constant, and the number of
ways to place that pattern among \(r\) indices is a falling factorial.
The central moment of order \(2q\) is the finite difference of order \(2q\) of
\(\mu_r\). That difference vanishes on every polynomial of degree at most
\(2q-2\). The case of order \(6\) and degree \(4\) is the identity
\(\sum_{k=0}^{6}\binom{6}{k}(-1)^{6-k}k^4=0\).

A product whose degree in \(r\) is at least \(2q\) must contain at least two
factors of \(\Delta\). Indeed a product of \(j\le q-1\) factors has pairwise
degree at most \(2j\le 2q-2\), and each factor of \(\Delta\) raises the degree
by at most \(3\), so one such factor reaches degree at most \(2q-1\).
Under the uniform measure on perfect matchings, a fixed pair lies in a matching
with probability \(1/(n-1)\), and
\[
\binom{n}{2}\frac1{(n-1)^3}
=
\frac{n}{2(n-1)^2}
=
O\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
Thus each triple intersection has expectation \(O(1/n)\), and a product of two
or more factors of \(\Delta\) has expectation \(O_q(1/n^2)\). Multiplied by at
most \((d^2/n^2)^{q-1}\), the contribution is \(O_q\bigl((d^2/n^2)^{q-1}/n^2\bigr)\).
Divided by \((d/n)^q\) this ratio equals \(d^{q-2}/n^q\). Substituting the
endpoint \(d=n^{1/2}\) produces \(n^{-q/2-1}\), which tends to \(0\), and the
same comparison holds for \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). The light-class factor
\(1+O_q(d/n)\) does not change the order. Every term that the finite difference
does not kill is therefore \(O_q(v(d)^q)\), and
\[
\frac{\EE[(Y-\EE Y)^{2q}]}{(\EE Y)^{2q}}=O_q\bigl(v(d)^q\bigr).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chains from moments of fixed order]
\label{thm:high-chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix an integer \(q\ge 2\). Let \(n\) be even and
let \(D\) satisfy \(D/\log n\to\infty\) and
\[
D=o\Biggl(
\frac{n^{2q/(4q+1)}}{(\log n)^{(2q+1)/(4q+1)}}
\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) small and \(mn\) even.
Then there is a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\). The implied constant may depend on \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\), \(\gamma=q/(2q+1)\), and \(\alpha:=v(d)^\gamma\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment} and Markov's inequality give
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
\le
\frac{K_q\,v(d)^q}{\alpha^{2q}}.
\]
The choice of \(\alpha\) makes the right-hand side \(O_q(v(d)^\gamma)\), because
\(q-2q\gamma=\gamma\). Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} converts this into a one-step coupling
failure \(O_q(v(d)^\gamma)\). As in Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}, one has
\(m=o(D)\) and \(v(d)\le 4v(D)\) for \(d\in[D,D+m]\). The product of the length
with the failure is
\[
m\,v(D)^\gamma
=
O_q\Biggl(
(\log n)^{1/2}\,D^{1/2-3\gamma}
+
\frac{D^{1/2+\gamma}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^\gamma}
\Biggr).
\]
The exponent \(1/2-3\gamma=(1-4q)/(2(2q+1))\) is negative for every \(q\ge 2\),
so the first summand tends to \(0\) under \(D/\log n\to\infty\).
For the second, the threshold
\(D=n^{2q/(4q+1)}/(\log n)^{(2q+1)/(4q+1)}\) produces \(1\): the degree identity
\[
\frac{2q}{4q+1}\cdot\frac{4q+1}{2(2q+1)}
=
\frac{q}{2q+1}
\]
matches the power of \(n\) with \(\gamma\), and the logarithmic identity
\[
\frac{2q+1}{4q+1}\cdot\frac{4q+1}{2(2q+1)}
=
\frac12
\]
cancels \((\log n)^{1/2}\). A little-o hypothesis makes the product \(o(1)\).
For \(q=2\) the degree exponent is \(4/9\), so the range contains
Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Chernoff nesting below the enumeration barrier]
\label{cor:near-barrier}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(n\) be even and let
\(D\) satisfy \(D/\log n\to\infty\) and \(D=o(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})\). Set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) small and \(mn\) even.
Then there is a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The gap identity \(1/2-2q/(4q+1)=1/(2(4q+1))\) lets us choose a fixed integer
\(q\ge 2\) with \(1/(2(4q+1))<\varepsilon\). For that \(q\),
\[
n^{1/2-\varepsilon}
=
o\Biggl(
\frac{n^{2q/(4q+1)}}{(\log n)^{(2q+1)/(4q+1)}}
\Biggr),
\]
because the remaining power of \(n\) is \(1/(2(4q+1))-\varepsilon<0\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:high-chains} supplies the coupling. The same choice of \(q\)
shows that every exponent strictly below \(1/2\) is attained, while
Proposition~\ref{prop:barrier} still stops exact \(+1\) at \(o(n^{1/2})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Long chains below \(n^{1/3}\)]
\label{cor:high-span}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(n\) be even, let
\(D\to\infty\), and let \(M=\lfloor n^{1/3-\varepsilon}\rfloor\). If \(D\le M\),
then there is a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{M}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_M\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the one-step failure \(O_q(v(d)^\gamma)\) from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:high-chains}, with \(\gamma=q/(2q+1)\). The gap identity
\(1/3-q/(3q+1)=1/(3(3q+1))\) lets us fix \(q\) so that
\(q/(3q+1)>1/3-\varepsilon\). For \(d\le n^{1/4}\) one has
\(v(d)\le 2d^{-3}\), so the failure is \(O_q(d^{-3q/(2q+1)})\). The exponent
\(-3q/(2q+1)\) is less than \(-1\), and the integral from \(D\) onward is
\(O_q(D^{(1-q)/(2q+1)})\), which tends to \(0\) as \(D\to\infty\).
For \(n^{1/4}\le d\le M\) the failure is \(O_q((d/n)^\gamma)\), and
\[
\sum_{d\le M}O_q\bigl((d/n)^\gamma\bigr)
\le
C_q n^{-\gamma}M^{\gamma+1}.
\]
Here \(\gamma+1=(3q+1)/(2q+1)\), and
\[
\frac{q}{3q+1}\cdot\frac{3q+1}{2q+1}
=
\frac{q}{2q+1},
\]
so \(n^{-\gamma}(n^{q/(3q+1)})^{\gamma+1}=1\). The stricter upper end
\(n^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) makes the sum \(o(1)\). For \(q=2\) the endpoint exponent
is \(2/7\), so the range contains Corollary~\ref{cor:fourth-span}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Moment precision]
\label{prop:moment-precision}
The factor \(1+O(d^2/n)\) in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15} determines each
ratio \(\EE[Y^k]/(\EE Y)^k\), for fixed \(k\), only up to an additive uncertainty
\(O(d^2/n)\).
That uncertainty is \(o(d^{-6})\) only for \(d=o(n^{1/8})\).
For \(\omega(n^{1/8})=d=o(n^{1/2})\) it is larger than both \(d^{-6}\) and
\((d^2/n)^2\).
In particular it is larger than \(v(d)^2\) throughout the range where
Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains} would improve on Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15} estimates \(\PP(H\subseteq\Gnd)\) for a graph \(H\)
with \(m\) edges by a product of \(m\) conditional probabilities.
Each factor carries a relative error \(O(d^2/n^2)\) by~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
The union of \(k\) perfect matchings has \(m=O(n)\) edges, and
\[
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr)^{O(n)}=1+O(d^2/n)
\]
whenever \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
Thus every summand in \(\EE[Y^k]\) is known up to a multiplicative factor \(1+O(d^2/n)\),
and so is \(\EE[Y^k]/(\EE Y)^k\).
Whenever that ratio is \(1+o(1)\), the multiplicative uncertainty is an additive
uncertainty \(O(d^2/n)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} puts the second-moment ratio in this class.
A fourth-moment bound of size \(O(v(d)^2)\) would put the centered moment in the
same class.

The centered moment expands as
\[
\frac{\EE[(Y-\EE Y)^4]}{(\EE Y)^4}
=
\frac{\EE[Y^4]}{(\EE Y)^4}
-4\frac{\EE[Y^3]}{(\EE Y)^3}
+6\frac{\EE[Y^2]}{(\EE Y)^2}
-3.
\]
The constant terms cancel, and the same cancellation does not apply to the
\(O(d^2/n)\) errors: their coefficients are not asserted to be equal.
The combination is therefore known only up to an additive \(O(d^2/n)\).
A common multiplicative factor does not remove the obstruction.
If every subgraph probability is the principal term times the same \(1+\varepsilon\),
then \(\EE[Y^k]/(\EE Y)^k\) acquires a factor \((1+\varepsilon)^{1-k}\).
Differentiating the centered combination in \(\varepsilon\) at \(\varepsilon=0\) produces
the coefficient \(-3+8-6=-1\).
An error of size \(\varepsilon\) therefore survives as an additive \(-\varepsilon\) in the
centered fourth moment.

A bound \(\EE[(Y-\EE Y)^4]\le K(\Var Y)^2\) is a statement at scale \(v(d)^2\), since
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(v(d))\).
Now \(v(d)^2\le 2d^{-6}+2(d^2/n)^2\).
The identity \((n^{1/8})^8/n=1\) shows that \(d^2/n=o(d^{-6})\) if and only if
\(d=o(n^{1/8})\).
For \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) one has \(n/d^2\to\infty\), hence
\(d^2/n=\omega((d^2/n)^2)\).
At \(d=n^{2/7}\) the two comparisons are
\((n^{2/7})^8/n=n^{9/7}\) and \(n/(n^{2/7})^2=n^{3/7}\).
Thus for \(\omega(n^{1/8})=d=o(n^{1/2})\) the uncertainty is \(\omega(v(d)^2)\).

The unsigned bound of this proposition does not by itself supply these moments.
The edge-linear factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment}, carried to every fixed
order in Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment}, does, and Corollary~\ref{cor:near-barrier}
is the resulting Chernoff window.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Secondary and conditional results}

\begin{theorem}[TV-controlled nesting]
\label{thm:approx}
Let \(3\le d\le n/2-1\) and write \(\mu_d\) for the law of \(\Gnd\).
Let \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\) be the law of \(G\cup M\) where \(G\sim\mu_d\) and \(M\) is
uniform among perfect matchings of \(K_n\setminus G\)
(every such complement has degree \(\ge n/2\), hence a perfect matching by Dirac's
theorem).
If \(\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le\delta\), then there is a coupling with
\(H\sim\mu_{d+1}\) and \(\PP(G\subset H)\ge 1-\delta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Square-root window]
\label{cor:sqrt}
Under the patched variance estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, for all sufficiently
small absolute constants \(c>0\) and all large \(n\), if \(d\le c\sqrt{n}\) then
\(\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le 1/2\), hence a coupling with
\(\PP(G\subset H)\ge 1/2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Sandwich degree defect]
\label{thm:defect}
For each fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) there exists \(C=C(\varepsilon)\) such that if
\(d\ge C\log n\), then there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\[
\PP\bigl(\Delta(G\setminus H)\le 3\varepsilon d\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative sandwich at rate \(1/9\)]
\label{thm:quant}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_*\) such that the following holds.
Whenever \(d\ge C_*\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_*\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/9},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) with
\[
G_*\sim G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),\quad
G\sim\Gnd,\quad
G^*\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),
\]
and \(\PP(G_*\subset G\subset G^*)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Medium increments and defects]
\label{cor:quant-nest}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}, writing
\(k_0:=3C_*\,d^{8/9}(\log n)^{1/9}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item if \(k\ge k_0\) then there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and
\(H\sim G(n,d+k)\) with \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\);
\item there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\(\PP\bigl(\Delta(G\setminus H)\le k_0\bigr)=1-o(1)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/3\) for polynomial degree]
\label{thm:quant-third}
For each fixed \(\sigma\in(0,1)\) there exists \(C_{**}=C_{**}(\sigma)\) such that the following
holds.
Whenever \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_{**}\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
Moreover, the exponent \(1/3\) is optimal for the deletion-process~/~B2
architecture of~\cite[§3]{BIM25}: no smaller exponent is possible without
changing that coupling scheme.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/3\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)]
\label{thm:quant-third-global}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_3\) such that whenever
\(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_3\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Global increments at rate \(1/3\)]
\label{cor:global-inc}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}, writing
\(k_3:=3C_3\,d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), the conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest} hold with \(k_0\) replaced by \(k_3\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial increments]
\label{cor:poly-inc}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}, writing
\(k_1:=3C_{**}\,d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), the conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest} hold with \(k_0\) replaced by \(k_1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Chernoff floor]
\label{prop:chernoff}
Let \(d\ge 2\log n\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\).
If \(\varepsilon\le c\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) for a sufficiently small absolute constant \(c>0\),
then \(\PP\bigl(\Delta(G(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n))>d\bigr)\not\to 0\).
In particular, no coupling can realise
\(G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\subseteq\Gnd\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[Quantitative sandwich]
\label{conj:Q}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that whenever \(d\ge C_0\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C_0\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\), there is a coupling
\((G_*,G,G^*)\) with
\[
G_*\sim G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),\quad
G\sim\Gnd,\quad
G^*\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),
\]
and \(\PP(G_*\subset G\subset G^*)=1-o(1)\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional increments]
\label{thm:Q1}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
If \(d\ge C_0\log n\) and \(k\ge 3C_0\sqrt{d\log n}\), then there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+k)\) with \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Process conjecture implies Chernoff sandwich]
\label{thm:process-to-Q}
Assume~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds: for every \(d\ge C\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large absolute, there is a coupling
\((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Final-window reduction]
\label{thm:window}
Suppose that whenever \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\[
C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{d}}
\le\varepsilon
\le C_3\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
the following holds: if \((G,G^*_0)\) is a coupling furnished by
Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate \(\varepsilon_0:=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\)
(so \(G\subset G^*_0\) a.a.s.\ with \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\)), then there
exists a coupling of a graph \(H\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) with
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
Assume the symmetric lower-window statement for \(G_*\).
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds for all \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
\end{theorem}

\section{Patched subgraph probabilities}
\label{sec:patch}

Let \(\rho(n,d,\alpha)\) and \(\varphi(d,\alpha)\) denote the main term and phase from
Gao~\cite[Corollary~15]{Gao21}.

\begin{lemma}[Patched Corollary~15]
\label{lem:cor15}
Let \(0\le k\le n/2\) and let \(H\) be a graph on \([n]\) consisting of \(k\) isolated edges
together with a collection of vertex-disjoint cycles covering the remaining
\(n-2k\) vertices.
Set \(\alpha=2k/n\).
If \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), then
\[
\PP\bigl(H\subseteq\Gnd\bigr)=(1+O(d^2/n))\,\rho(n,d,\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Order \(E(H)=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)\) with \(m=|E(H)|\le n\).
Then
\[
\PP(H\subseteq\Gnd)
=\prod_{i=1}^{m}
\PP\bigl(e_i\in\Gnd\bigm|\{e_1,\ldots,e_{i-1}\}\subseteq\Gnd\bigr).
\]
Apply~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} at each step.
For \(d\)-regular degree sequences one has \(M=dn\) and \(\Delta=d\), so the relative
error per step is \(O(\Delta^5 n/M^3)=O(d^2/n^2)\).
Over \(m\le n\) steps,
\[
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr)^{O(n)}=1+O(d^2/n),
\]
using \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).

Write \(P_{\mathrm{seq}}\) for the product of the principal terms in Theorem~6, and
\(P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\) for the error-free McKay principal ratio appearing
in~\cite[proof of Corollary~15]{Gao21}.
Both approximate \(\PP(H\subseteq\Gnd)\), to within \(1+O(d^2/n)\) and \(1+O(d^3/n)\)
respectively~\cite[Remark~8]{Gao20}.
Hence \(P_{\mathrm{seq}}=(1+O(d^2/n))P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\).
Defining \(\rho:=P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\) yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Laplace window for intersection size]
\label{lem:laplace}
Let \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). Let \((a_k)\) be a positive sequence whose
consecutive ratios satisfy, for \(1\le k\le n/3\),
\[
\frac{a_k}{a_{k-1}}
=
\frac{(d-2)n+2k}{2(d-1)^2 k}\,(1+O(1/n)),
\]
and \(a_k/a_{k-1}\le 1/2\) for \(k>n/3\). Set \(\bar k=n/(2d)\), \(m=\lfloor\bar k\rfloor\), and
\[
\beta
:=
1-\frac1{(d-1)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_k a_k
=
a_m\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\bar k}{\beta}}\,\bigl(1+O(d/n)\bigr).
\]
The prefactor admits the expansion
\(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\).
It depends only on \(d\), and it is part of the main-term expansion in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21},
not a truncation error.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(r_0(k)=((d-2)n+2k)/(2(d-1)^2 k)\) and \(a=(d-1)^2\). The identity \(r_0(\bar k)=1\) rearranges to
\[
r_0(\bar k+t)
=
\frac{\bar k+t/a}{\bar k+t}.
\]
Set \(\sigma=\sqrt{\bar k}\) and \(\lambda=\log n\), and write \(\delta=k-m\).
The window \(|\delta|\le\lambda\sigma\) lies inside \(k\le n/3\) for large \(n\), because
\(\lambda\sigma=o(\bar k)\).

On this window, \(u=t/\bar k\) satisfies \(|u|\le 2\lambda/\sigma\to 0\). For \(|u|<1\) and \(|u/a|<1\),
\[
\log\Bigl(1+\frac{u}{a}\Bigr)-\log(1+u)
=
\sum_{j\ge 1}\frac{(-1)^j}{j}\Bigl(u^j-\bigl(\frac{u}{a}\bigr)^j\Bigr).
\]
The \(j=1\) summand is \(-u(1-1/a)=-u\beta\). Summing it from the mode produces the even function
\[
-\frac{\beta\,\delta^2}{2\bar k}+O\Bigl(\frac{|\delta|}{\bar k}\Bigr).
\]
The comparison weights are therefore the even function
\(g(\delta)=\exp(-\beta\delta^2/(2\bar k))\).
Poisson summation, after the change of scale \(\sigma_\beta=\sigma/\sqrt{\beta}\), gives
\[
\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}g(\delta)
=
\sigma_\beta\sqrt{2\pi}\,(1+O(e^{-c\bar k})),
\]
since \(\beta\to 1\). This is the main term \(a_m\sqrt{2\pi\bar k/\beta}\).
The expansion \(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\) is the Taylor expansion of that
prefactor at infinity. It is independent of the window. In the ratio of moments it is evaluated
by the algebraic expansion of~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}, whose output is already
\(1+1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4})\). It is not charged again below.

The \(j=2\) summand is \((u^2/2)(1-1/a^2)\), an even function of \(t\), with \(1-1/a^2=1+O(d^{-2})\). Its partial sums are
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{\delta}\frac{t^2}{2\bar k^2}
=
\frac{\delta(\delta+1)(2\delta+1)}{12\bar k^2}
=
\frac{\delta^3}{6\bar k^2}+\frac{\delta^2}{4\bar k^2}+\frac{\delta}{12\bar k^2}.
\]
The cubic and linear terms are odd in \(\delta\). At the window edge \(|\delta|=\lambda\sigma\) the cubic term has size
\[
\frac{\lambda^3}{3\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{n/d}}
=
\Theta\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\,\log^3 n\Bigr).
\]
Bounding that odd function by its maximum, rather than summing it, is the truncation error in
Gao's Lemma~18. Against the even weight \(g\), on a symmetric window, the odd part sums to zero.
The even piece \(\delta^2/(4\bar k^2)\) is \(O((\log n)^2\,d/n)\) on the window. Summing it against \(g\) costs a relative
error \(O(d/n)\), since \(\sum g(\delta)\,\delta^2=O(\sigma^3)\) and \(\bar k^2=\sigma^4\).
The same cancellation removes the odd part of the discrepancy \(O(|\delta|/\bar k)\)
and the odd part of the accumulated \(O(1/n)\) ratio error.

The remainder is even. Its leading piece is the \(j=3\) sum, of size \(O(\delta^4/\bar k^3)\).
The Gaussian moment \(\sum g(\delta)\,\delta^4\) is \(O(\sigma^5)\). Dividing by \(\bar k^3=\sigma^6\)
and by the main-term size \(\sigma\) produces a relative error \(O(1/\sigma^2)=O(d/n)\),
since \(1/\sigma^2=2d/n\). Terms with \(j\ge 4\) are of size \(O(\lambda^4/\sigma^2)\) uniformly on the
window, and
\[
\frac{\lambda^4}{\sigma^2}
=
\frac{2d\,(\log n)^4}{n}\to 0
\]
throughout \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). They contribute the same relative order \(O(d/n)\) after summation.
Thus the window sum equals \(a_m\sqrt{2\pi\bar k/\beta}\,(1+O(d/n))\).

Outside the window and up to distance \(\bar k/2\) from \(m\), the quadratic exponent still yields
\(\log(a_{m+\delta}/a_m)\le-\delta^2/(4\bar k)\). Those tails are
\(O(a_m\sigma\exp(-c\log^2 n))\). Beyond \(2\bar k\), one has \(r_0(k)\le 2/3\), so the
remaining series is geometric and of size \(O(a_m e^{-c\bar k})\). Both are absorbed in
the factor \(1+O(d/n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Patched moments]
\label{lem:moments}
Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\), and assume \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
Then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:EY}
\EE Y
&=(1+O(d^2/n))\frac{n!}{(n/2)!\,2^{n/2}}\,\rho(n,d,1),\\
\label{eq:VarY}
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
&=\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d^2}{n}\Bigr).
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For~\eqref{eq:EY}, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15} with \(\alpha=1\)
(so \(\varphi(d,1)=1/4\)) and sum over the \(n!/\bigl((n/2)!2^{n/2}\bigr)\) perfect
matchings.

For~\eqref{eq:VarY}, expand \(\EE[Y^2]=\sum_{H_1,H_2}\PP(H_1\cup H_2\subseteq\Gnd)\).
If \(|H_1\cap H_2|=k\), then \(H_1\cup H_2\) has the shape required by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}, so
\[
\PP(H_1\cup H_2\subseteq\Gnd)=(1+O(d^2/n))\,\rho(n,d,2k/n).
\]
Count pairs via~\cite[Lemma~17]{Gao21}. The resulting summand in \(k\), up to the factor
\(1+O(d^2/n)\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}, satisfies the ratio hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} (the same ratio computed in~\cite[§3.4.2]{Gao21}).
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} replaces the truncation error \(O(\sqrt{d/n}\,\log^3 n)\) in Gao's
Lemma~18 by \(O(d/n)\): that error is the window maximum of the odd \(j=2\) sum, and the sum of
an odd function against the even Gaussian weight vanishes.
The prefactor \(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\) is not a new error.
It is a function of \(d\) alone and is already expanded in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}.
For \(d\ge 1\) one has \(d/n\le d^2/n\), so the improved truncation is absorbed
in the enumeration error \(O(d^2/n)\).
The ratio \(\EE[Y^2]/(\EE Y)^2\) is then the same algebraic value as
in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}, with every \(d^3/n\) replaced by \(d^2/n\), namely
\(1+1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4}+d^2/n)\).
\end{proof}

\section{The coupling}
\label{sec:coupling}

\begin{lemma}[Error schedule]
\label{lem:eta}
Define
\[
\eta(\alpha)=2\alpha+\frac1{d^3\alpha^2}+C\frac{d^2}{n\alpha^2}
\]
for a sufficiently large absolute constant \(C\), and set
\[
\alpha=\max\bigl\{d^{-1},\,(d^2/n)^{1/8}\bigr\}.
\]
If \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), then \(\alpha=o(1)\) and \(\eta(\alpha)=o(1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two arguments of the maximum meet when \(d\cdot(d^2/n)^{1/8}=1\), i.e.\ \(d=n^{1/10}\),
since \(d\cdot(d^2/n)^{1/8}=d^{5/4}/n^{1/8}\).

If \(d\le n^{1/10}\), then \(\alpha=d^{-1}\). Hence \(2\alpha=o(1)\) and
\(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}=o(1)\). The remaining term is
\(d^2/(n\alpha^2)=d^4/n\le n^{-3/5}\) at the right endpoint, and is smaller for smaller \(d\).

If \(n^{1/10}\le d=o(n^{1/2})\), then \(\alpha=(d^2/n)^{1/8}\). This tends to \(0\), and
\[
\frac{d^2}{n\alpha^2}=(d^2/n)^{3/4}=o(1),
\]
while \(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}=o(1)\) because \(\alpha\ge d^{-1}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} is what removes the former summand \(\sqrt{d/n}\,\log^3 n\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, so no extra logarithmic loss remains in the schedule.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(G(n,d+1)\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} and Chebyshev's inequality,
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
\le\frac{\Var Y}{\alpha^2(\EE Y)^2}
=O\bigl(\eta(\alpha)\bigr)=o(1).
\]
The same bound holds for the number of perfect matchings in complements of
\(\Gnd\) (equivalently, matching counts in \(G(n,n-1-d)\), to which
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} applies in our range after complement duality).

Apply the bipartite coupling of~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21}, replacing Gao's error
function \(\eta\) (which contains the term \(d^3/(n\alpha^2)\)) by the function of
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta}.
Every estimate in~\cite[§4.2]{Gao21} that invokes~\cite[Theorem~10 or
Corollary~15]{Gao21} remains valid under Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}.
The coupling therefore satisfies
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})\ge 1-5\eta(\alpha)=1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds}]
\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} assumes only \(\Delta^2=o(M)\). For a \(d\)-regular host this is \(d=o(n)\), which contains \(d=o(n^{2/3})\).
The iteration in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error} therefore applies verbatim.
Its first contribution is a relative \(O(d^3/n^3)\) per edge and a factor \(1+O(d^3/n^2)\) over \(O(n)\) edges.
The host-dependent piece remains \(1+O(d/n)\).
For \(d=\omega(n^{1/2})\) one has \(d/n=o(d^3/n^2)\), since \((d/n)/(d^3/n^2)=n/d^2\).
The overlap window of Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} contributes \(O((d/n)^{3/2}\log n)\).
Divided by \(d^3/n^2\) this equals \(O\bigl(n\sqrt{d/n}\,\log n/d^2\bigr)\).
The square of \(n\sqrt{d/n}/d^2\) is \(n/d^3\), the same as the square of \(n^{1/2}/d^{3/2}\), so the ratio is \(O(n^{1/2}\log n/d^{3/2})\) and tends to \(0\) for \(d=\omega(n^{1/3})\).
Thus, for \(n^{1/2}\le d=o(n^{2/3})\),
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d^3}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
On \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) the claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, so it remains to treat \(n^{1/2}\le d=o(n^{2/3})\).
The identity \((n^{2/3})^3/n^2=1\) shows that the new error tends to \(0\) precisely on this range.
Set
\[
\alpha=\max\bigl\{d^{-1},\,(d^3/n^2)^{1/8}\bigr\}.
\]
The two arguments meet when \(d^{-8}=d^3/n^2\), i.e.\ \(d=n^{2/11}\).
If \(d\le n^{2/11}\), then \(\alpha=d^{-1}\) and
\(d^3/(n^2\alpha^2)=d^5/n^2\le n^{-12/11}\) at the right endpoint.
If \(n^{2/11}\le d=o(n^{2/3})\), then \(\alpha=(d^3/n^2)^{1/8}\to 0\) and
\[
\frac{d^3/n^2}{\alpha^2}
=
\Bigl(\frac{d^3}{n^2}\Bigr)^{3/4}\to 0,
\]
while \(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}\to 0\).
Chebyshev's inequality at this \(\alpha\) is therefore \(o(1)\).
The coupling argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the variance only through this probability, so it applies with the present error in place of \(d^2/n\).
Since \(2/3-1/2=1/6>0\), the range lies past Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(+1\) below linear degree]
\label{thm:below-linear}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix an integer \(k\ge 0\). Let \(n\) be even and let
\(d\to\infty\) with
\[
d=o\bigl(n^{(k+1)/(k+2)}\bigr).
\]
Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
In particular, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) the same conclusion holds for all
\(d=o(n^{1-\varepsilon})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The error displayed in~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} is produced by a main term of size
\(O(d^2)\) multiplied by the relative error of one conditional edge probability and
then divided by \(M=dn\), as tracked in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error}.
Feeding in a per-edge relative error \(\varepsilon\) therefore returns a new per-edge
relative error \(O((d/n)\varepsilon)\).
Theorem~6 itself supplies the base value \(\varepsilon_0=O(d^2/n^2)\).
After \(k\) substitutions,
\[
\varepsilon_k=O\Bigl(\frac{d^{k+2}}{n^{k+2}}\Bigr).
\]
A perfect matching has \(O(n)\) edges, so the product of these factors is
\(1+O(d^{k+2}/n^{k+1})\). The threshold substitution
\(d=n^{(k+1)/(k+2)}\) produces \(1\), because the exponent
\(((k+1)/(k+2))\cdot(k+2)-(k+1)\) equals \(0\),
and the little-o hypothesis makes the product \(1+o(1)\).
The cases \(k=0\) and \(k=1\) are the errors \(d^2/n\) and \(d^3/n^2\) already used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:barrier} and Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds}.
The host-dependent piece remains \(O(d/n)\) and tends to \(0\) throughout
\(d=o(n)\). The overlap window contributes \(O((d/n)^{3/2}\log n)\), which is
\(o(d^{k+2}/n^{k+1})\) at the upper end of the range, since that end is
\(\omega(n^{1/3})\).
Hence
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d}{n}+\frac{d^{k+2}}{n^{k+1}}\Bigr).
\]
The same Chebyshev schedule as in Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds}, with \(d^3/n^2\)
replaced by \(d^{k+2}/n^{k+1}\), is \(o(1)\). The coupling argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the variance only through this probability.

For the last claim, the gap identity
\(1-(k+1)/(k+2)=1/(k+2)\) lets us choose a fixed \(k\) with \(1/(k+2)<\varepsilon\).
Then \(n^{1-\varepsilon}=o(n^{(k+1)/(k+2)})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(+1\) up to the Laplace window]
\label{thm:laplace-degree}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\to\infty\) with
\[
d=o\Biggl(\frac{n}{(\log n)^2}\Biggr).
\]
Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each substitution in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:below-linear} multiplies the
per-edge relative error by \(O(d/n)\), and the implied factor is absolute: it is
the ratio of the \(O(d^2)\) main term to the stub count \(dn\), taken from the
same two contributions at every pass. Thus there is an absolute constant \(C\)
such that after
\[
k=\Bigl\lceil\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\Bigr\rceil
\]
passes one has
\[
\varepsilon_k\le B\Bigl(C\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^k\frac{d^2}{n^2}
\]
for an absolute \(B\). The product over \(O(n)\) edges is then at most
\[
Bn\Bigl(C\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^k\frac{d^2}{n^2}.
\]
The hypothesis \(d=o(n/(\log n)^2)\) gives \(d/n=o(1/(\log n)^2)\). For large \(n\)
one has \(Cd/n\le 1/(\log n)^2\), and the product is at most
\[
Bn\big/(\log n)^{2k}.
\]
The exponent identity
\((\log n/\log\log n)\cdot\log\log n=\log n\) gives
\((\log n)^{2k}\ge n^{2}(\log n)^{-O(1)}\), so the product is \(o(1)\).

The same upper bound on \(d\) is the Laplace condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}:
\(\log n=o(\sqrt{n/d})\) rearranges to \(d=o(n/(\log n)^2)\). On this range the
host-dependent piece \(O(d/n)\) tends to \(0\), and
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)+o(1)
\]
tends to \(0\). Chebyshev's inequality at height
\(\alpha=\max\{d^{-1},(d/n)^{1/8}\}\) is \(o(1)\), and the coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Overlap sum past the logarithmic window]
\label{lem:overlap-wide}
Let \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n)\). Let \((a_k)\) satisfy the ratio hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}. Set \(\bar k=n/(2d)\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt{\bar k}\). Then
\(\bar k\to\infty\) and
\[
\sum_k a_k
=
a_m\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\,(1+o(1)),
\]
where \(m=\lfloor\bar k\rfloor\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis \(d=o(n)\) is \(\bar k\to\infty\). Set \(\lambda=\bar k^{1/6}\). The window
\(|\delta|\le\lambda\sigma\) has width \(\lambda\sigma=\bar k^{2/3}=o(\bar k)\), so it lies
inside \(k\le n/3\) for large \(n\), and the scale \(u=\delta/\bar k\) tends to \(0\).
The expansion in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} therefore applies on this window.
The even piece of size \(\delta^2/\bar k^2\) is at most \(\lambda^2/\bar k=\bar k^{-2/3}\).
The terms of order \(j\ge 4\) are of size \(\lambda^4/\sigma^2\). The identity
\((\bar k^{1/6})^4/\bar k=\bar k^{-1/3}\) shows that this tends to \(0\).
The cubic piece sums to a relative \(O(1/\sigma^2)=O(d/n)\), as in that proof, and
\(d/n\to 0\).
Outside the window and up to distance \(\bar k/2\) from \(m\), the same quadratic bound
gives \(\log(a_{m+\delta}/a_m)\le-\delta^2/(4\bar k)\). At the window edge
\(\delta^2/\bar k=\lambda^2=\bar k^{1/3}\), so those tails are
\(O(e^{-c\bar k^{1/3}})=o(1)\). Beyond \(2\bar k\), \(r_0(k)\le 2/3\) and the series is
geometric of size \(O(e^{-c\bar k})\).
Odd summands cancel against the even Gaussian weight, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}.
The resulting relative error tends to \(0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(+1\) for every sublinear degree]
\label{thm:sublinear}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\to\infty\) with \(d=o(n)\).
Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(L=n/d\), so \(L\to\infty\). Let \(C\) and \(B\) be the absolute constants of
Theorem~\ref{thm:laplace-degree}, and set
\[
k=\Bigl\lceil\frac{2\log n}{\log(L/C)}\Bigr\rceil
\]
once \(L>2C\). The same recurrence gives a per-edge relative error at most
\(B(Cd/n)^k(d^2/n^2)\). The product over \(O(n)\) edges is at most
\[
Bn\Bigl(\frac{C}{L}\Bigr)^k\frac1{L^2}.
\]
The choice of \(k\) makes \((L/C)^k\ge n^2\), so the product is \(O(1/(nL^2))=o(1)\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap-wide} replaces Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} in the second-moment
sum. The Gaussian integral is therefore known up to a factor \(1+o(1)\), and the
algebra of~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21} returns
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6d^3}+O(d^{-4})+O\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)+o(1).
\]
Every term tends to \(0\). Chebyshev's inequality at height
\(\alpha=\max\{d^{-1},(d/n)^{1/8}\}\) is \(o(1)\).
The two arguments of the maximum meet when \((d/n)^{1/8}=d^{-1}\), i.e.\ \(d^9=n\).
For \(d\ge n^{1/9}\) one has \((d/n)/\alpha^2=(d/n)^{3/4}\to 0\).
For \(d\le n^{1/9}\) one has \((d/n)/\alpha^2=d^3/n\le n^{-2/3}\), because
\((n^{1/9})^3/n=n^{-2/3}\), and \(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}\to 0\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} and the coupling in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} use only
this concentration, together with subgraph ratios of relative error \(o(1)\), both
of which hold here.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chains past \(\sqrt{n}\)]
\label{thm:chain-past-sqrt}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(n\) be even and let
\(D\) satisfy \(n^{1/2}\le D=o\bigl(n/(\log n)^2\bigr)\). Let
\(L=o\bigl((n/D)^{1/2-\varepsilon}\bigr)\).
Then there is a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{D+L}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment} uses \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) only to compare the
Laplace tail \(e^{-cn/d}\) with a power of \(v(d)\). For
\(d=o(n/(\log n)^2)\) one has \(n/d=\omega((\log n)^2)\), so the tail is
\(e^{-\omega((\log n)^2)}\), while \(v(d)\ge d/n\). The tail is smaller than every
fixed power of \(v(d)\). The remaining estimates in that proof use only
\(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n)\). Thus for each fixed \(q\ge 2\),
\[
\frac{\EE[(Y-\EE Y)^{2q}]}{(\EE Y)^{2q}}=O_q\bigl(v(d)^q\bigr)
\]
on this range, with \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d/n\). Theorem~\ref{thm:laplace-degree} supplies
the variance bound.

Choose \(q\) so that \(\gamma=q/(2q+1)\) satisfies \(\gamma>1/2-\varepsilon\). The identity
\(1/2-q/(2q+1)=1/(2(2q+1))\) makes such a finite \(q\) exist. Markov's inequality
at height \(v(d)^\gamma\), as in Theorem~\ref{thm:high-chains}, fails with
probability \(O_q(v(d)^\gamma)\). For \(D\ge n^{1/2}\) one has \(v(D)\le 2D/n\).
Also \(L=o(D)\), because \((n/n^{1/2})^{1/2}=n^{1/4}=o(n^{1/2})\). Hence
\(v(d)\le 4v(D)\) on the interval, and
\[
L\cdot O_q\bigl((D/n)^\gamma\bigr)=o(1)
\]
by the choice of \(L\). Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} converts each step into a coupling.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linear degree lies outside the edge estimate]
\label{prop:linear-barrier}
\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} assumes \(\Delta^2=o(M)\). For a \(d\)-regular graph this
is \(d=o(n)\), since \(\Delta^2/M=d/n\). The substitutions that prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:sublinear} therefore do not start at a degree \(d\) for which
both \(d\) and \(n-d\) are of order \(n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the degree sequence \((d,\ldots,d)\) one has \(\Delta=d\) and \(M=dn\), so
\(\Delta^2/M=d/n\). This tends to \(0\) if and only if \(d=o(n)\). At \(d=\Theta(n)\)
the hypothesis of Theorem~6 fails. Theorem~\ref{thm:dense} recovers the
complementary range by applying the sparse theorem to \(n-1-d\), so the
unresolved degrees are those with both \(d\) and \(n-d\) of order \(n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact \(+1\) in the dense range]
\label{thm:dense}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\) satisfy
\(n-d\to\infty\) and \(n-d=o(n)\). Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\)
and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) with \(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(s=n-2-d\). The hypotheses on \(d\) are exactly \(s\to\infty\) and \(s=o(n)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:sublinear} supplies a coupling \(G_s\subset G_{s+1}\) of uniform
random regular graphs. The complement of a uniform \(s\)-regular graph is a
uniform \((n-1-s)\)-regular graph, and complementation reverses inclusions.
The complement of \(G_{s+1}\) is uniform of degree \(n-1-(s+1)=n-2-s=d\).
The complement of \(G_s\) is uniform of degree \(n-1-s=d+1\).
Thus the complemented coupling is a coupling of \(G(n,d)\) inside \(G(n,d+1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Middle band coupling]
\label{thm:middle}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d=d(n)\) satisfy \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\), with \(dn\) even.
Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

The coupling asked for is deletion of one uniform perfect matching from a uniform
\((d+1)\)-regular simple graph. The output is uniform of degree \(d\) precisely when
the number of perfect matchings is relatively concentrated on the simple model.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error} gives that concentration only while \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
The configuration multigraph is a different probability space.
Each simple \(d\)-regular graph arises from exactly \((d!)^n\) pairings, so the
uniform simple graph is the pairing conditioned on simplicity.
At half degree that conditioning event is not of probability \(1-o(1)\).

\begin{proposition}[Half-degree configurations have a loop]
\label{prop:config-loops}
Let \(n\ge 4\) be even and let \(d=n/2\). In the configuration model of degree \(d\),
the number \(L\) of loops satisfies \(\EE L\to\infty\) and \(\PP(L\ge 1)\to 1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A loop is a pair of stubs at the same vertex that the random pairing joins.
There are \(n\binom{d}{2}\) such pairs of stubs, and each is joined with probability
\(1/(dn-1)\), so
\[
\EE L
=
\frac{n\,d(d-1)}{2(dn-1)}
=
\frac{d(d-1)}{2\bigl(d-1/n\bigr)}.
\]
Substituting \(d=n/2\) gives
\[
\EE L
=
\frac{n^2(n-2)}{4(n^2-2)}.
\]
The quotient of this quantity by \(n\) tends to \(1/4\), so \(\EE L\to\infty\).

For the second moment, count ordered pairs of distinct potential loops.
Two potential loops occur together only when their four stubs are disjoint, in which
case the probability is \(1/((dn-1)(dn-3))\).
The number of unordered stub-disjoint pairs of potential loops is
\[
\binom{n}{2}\binom{d}{2}^2
+
n\cdot\binom{d}{2}\binom{d-2}{2}/2.
\]
Hence \(\EE[L(L-1)]\) equals twice that count, divided by \((dn-1)(dn-3)\).
At \(d=n/2\) the ratio \(\EE[L(L-1)]/(\EE L)^2\) tends to \(1\).
Since \(\EE L\to\infty\), one also has \(\EE[L^2]/(\EE L)^2\to 1\).
Cauchy--Schwarz in the form
\((\EE L)^2=\bigl(\EE[L\,1_{L\ge 1}]\bigr)^2\le\EE[L^2]\,\PP(L\ge 1)\)
therefore yields \(\PP(L\ge 1)\to 1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Middle band from matching concentration]
\label{prop:middle-reduction}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\) satisfy \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\), with \(dn\) even.
Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(G(n,d+1)\).
If \(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=o(1)\), then Theorem~\ref{thm:middle} holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(v=\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2\), and set \(\alpha=v^{1/3}\).
The hypothesis \(v=o(1)\) forces \(\alpha=o(1)\).
Chebyshev's inequality bounds the failure probability by \(v/\alpha^2\).
The identity \(v/(v^{1/3})^2=v^{1/3}\) makes that failure \(o(1)\) as well.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} converts the pair \((\alpha,o(1))\) into total variation \(o(1)\) between
\(\mu_{d+1}\) and the law of a uniform \(d\)-regular graph plus a uniform perfect matching.
That is the coupling in Theorem~\ref{thm:middle}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Half-degree main term is Poisson, and the error is not small]
\label{prop:half-main-term}
At degree \(d=n/2\) and for each integer \(k\ge 1\),
\[
\frac{(d-2)n+2k}{2(d-1)^2 k}
=
\frac1k\left(1+\frac{4(k-1)}{(n-2)^2}\right).
\]
The comparison series \(\sum_{k\ge 0}e^{-1}/k!\) equals \(1\).
The exponent remainder permitted by the same enumeration is \(n^2/4\) at this degree,
so that enumeration does not identify the main term with the probability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(d=n/2\). The identity
\[
\frac{((n/2)-2)n+2k}{2((n/2)-1)^2 k}
=
\frac{n^2-4n+4k}{(n-2)^2 k}
\]
holds, and the right-hand side equals
\(\frac1k\bigl(1+4(k-1)/(n-2)^2\bigr)\)
because \((n-2)^2=n^2-4n+4\).
The series \(\sum_{k\ge 0}e^{-1}/k!\) is the Taylor series of \(e\cdot e^{-1}\).
In McKay's enumeration the remainder in the exponent is of order \(\hat\Delta^2/m\).
For residual degree \(d\) one has \(m=dn/2\) and \(\hat\Delta\) of order \(d^2\), so the
quotient has order \(2d^3/n\). At \(d=n/2\) this equals \(n^2/4\).
The hypothesis \(\hat\Delta=o(m)\) fails, and an additive \(n^2/4\) in the exponent
is not \(o(1)\). The factorial ratio above is therefore not yet the ratio of probabilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dense degree corrections do not separate bounded overlaps]
\label{prop:gamma-flat}
Let \(d=n/2\) and let \(k\) be an integer with \(0\le k<n/2\).
The residual degrees \(d-1\) on \(2k\) vertices and \(d-2\) on the remaining vertices
differ from their mean by
\[
\delta_+=1-\frac{2k}{n},\qquad \delta_-=-\frac{2k}{n}.
\]
These deviations satisfy \(\sum\delta_j=0\) and \(\sum\delta_j^2=2k-4k^2/n\).
For each fixed \(k\) the power sum that enters the dense degree-sequence exponent is \(o(1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean residual degree is \(d-2+2k/n\). Subtracting it from \(d-1\) and from \(d-2\)
gives \(\delta_+\) and \(\delta_-\). The sum of the deviations is
\[
2k\Bigl(1-\frac{2k}{n}\Bigr)+(n-2k)\Bigl(-\frac{2k}{n}\Bigr)=0.
\]
The sum of squares is
\[
2k\Bigl(1-\frac{2k}{n}\Bigr)^2+(n-2k)\Bigl(\frac{2k}{n}\Bigr)^2=2k-\frac{4k^2}{n}.
\]
Dividing by \((n-1)^2\) produces a quantity of order \(k/n^2\). For each fixed \(k\) this tends to \(0\),
so an exponent built as a constant multiple of \(n\) times that power sum also tends to \(0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linear factor of a forbidden host]
\label{prop:linear-host}
Let \(H\) have \(k\) disjoint edges and a \(2\)-factor on the remaining \(n-2k\) vertices.
Write \(a_v=\deg_H(v)-2(n-k)/n\) and let \(\theta\) be Gaussian on \(\sum\theta_v=0\) with density proportional to
\(\exp\bigl(-(n-2)\|\theta\|^2/8\bigr)\).
Then \(\sum a_v=0\) and
\[
\Var\Bigl(\sum_v a_v\theta_v\Bigr)
=
\frac{4}{n-2}\Bigl(2k-\frac{4k^2}{n}\Bigr).
\]
For each fixed \(k\) this variance is \((8k/n)(1+o(1))\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The same algebra as in Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-flat} gives \(\sum a_v=0\) and
\(\sum a_v^2=2k-4k^2/n\): the values of \(a_v\) are \(-(1-2k/n)\) on the \(2k\) vertices of degree \(1\)
and \(2k/n\) on the vertices of degree \(2\).
On the hyperplane \(\sum\theta_v=0\), the stated density has covariance
\(\frac{4}{n-2}\) times projection onto that hyperplane.
For a vector \(a\) with \(\sum a_v=0\),
\[
\Var(a\cdot\theta)=\frac{4}{n-2}\sum a_v^2
=
\frac{4}{n-2}\Bigl(2k-\frac{4k^2}{n}\Bigr).
\]
The identity
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{8k}\cdot\frac{4}{n-2}\Bigl(2k-\frac{4k^2}{n}\Bigr)=1
\]
gives the asymptotic \((8k/n)(1+o(1))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic factor of a forbidden host]
\label{prop:quad-host}
Let \(H\) be a graph of maximum degree \(2\) with \(e\) edges, and let \(M_0=(n-2)I+{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1}^{\mathsf T}\).
The Gaussian integral that deletes the edges of \(H\) from the complete quadratic form differs from the empty-host integral by
\[
\frac{e}{n-1}-e\log 2+O\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr)
\]
in the logarithm, with the \(O(1/n)\) uniform over all such hosts.
If \(H\) is the union of two perfect matchings with \(k\) edges in common, and \(M\) is one perfect matching, the ratio of these integrals satisfies
\[
\log\frac{I(H)\,I(\emptyset)}{I(M)^2}=k\log 2+O\Bigl(\frac{k+1}{n}\Bigr).
\]
Averaging \(2^{k}\) over a uniform second perfect matching tends to \(e^{1/2}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(B=D_H+A_H\) and \(C=M_0^{-1}B\). Sherman--Morrison gives
\[
M_0^{-1}=\frac1{n-2}I-\frac{{\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1}^{\mathsf T}}{(n-2)(2n-2)},
\]
and a direct contraction produces \(\operatorname{tr}(C)=2e/(n-1)\). The identity
\(2e/(n-2)\cdot(1-1/(n-1))=2e/(n-1)\) records that contraction.
The operator norm of \(B\) is at most \(4\), so \(\|C\|\le 4/(n-2)\).
For \(j\ge 2\), \(|\operatorname{tr}(C^j)|\le n\|C\|^j=O(n^{1-j})\).
Hence
\[
\log\det(M_0-B)=\log\det M_0-\frac{2e}{n-1}+O\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
The prefactor that omits \(e\) edges at \(r=1\) contributes \(-e\log 2\).
Half the determinant change contributes \(e/(n-1)\). This is the first display.

A perfect matching has \(n/2\) edges, and the union with intersection \(k\) has \(n-k\) edges.
The difference of the edge counts is \(-k\), so the log ratio is
\(k\log 2-k/(n-1)+O(1/n)\).

Let \(K\) be the number of edges shared by two uniform perfect matchings.
The factorial moment of order \(r\) is
\[
\frac{(n/2)(n/2-1)\cdots(n/2-r+1)}{(n-1)(n-3)\cdots(n-2r+1)}.
\]
Each factor \((n/2-j)/(n-1-2j)\) tends to \(1/2\), and the case \(r=1\) is the checked limit \((n/2)/(n-1)\to 1/2\).
Thus the moments tend to those of a Poisson random variable of mean \(1/2\).
The probability generating function at \(2\) tends to \(\exp((2-1)/2)=\exp(1/2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Degree binomials do not cancel \(2^k\)]
\label{prop:degree-binomial}
At degree \(d=n/2\), the residual degree sequence with intersection \(k\) satisfies
\[
\log\frac{\prod_v\binom{n-1}{g_v}}{\binom{n-1}{d-1}^n}
=
(n-2k)\log\frac{n-2}{n+2}.
\]
For each fixed \(k\) the right-hand side tends to \(-4\). The factor \(2^k\) from Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host} remains.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here \(g_v=d-1\) on \(2k\) vertices and \(g_v=d-2\) on the rest.
The identity \(\binom{n-1}{d}/\binom{n-1}{d-1}=(n-d)/d\) gives ratio \(1\) at \(d=n/2\).
The next step is
\[
\frac{\binom{n-1}{d-2}}{\binom{n-1}{d-1}}=\frac{d-1}{n-d+1}=\frac{n/2-1}{n/2+1}=\frac{n-2}{n+2},
\]
so only the \(n-2k\) vertices of degree \(d-2\) contribute, and the logarithm is the displayed product.
The limit \((n-2k)\log((n-2)/(n+2))\to -4\) is independent of the fixed integer \(k\).
The expansion \(-4+(8k)/n+O(1/n^2)\) shows that distinct fixed overlaps differ by \(O(k/n)\), which does not cancel a factor \(2^k\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Principal ratio averages to \(1\)]
\label{prop:principal-average}
At degree \(d=n/2\), write \(\rho_2(k)\) for the principal part of \(\PP(H_1\cup H_2\subseteq G(n,d))\) in~\cite[display~(14)]{Gao21} and \(\rho_1\) for the principal part of \(\PP(H\subseteq G(n,d))\) in~\cite[display~(12)]{Gao21}.
For each fixed intersection \(k\),
\[
\log\frac{\rho_2(k)}{\rho_1^2}=k\log 2-\frac12+o(1).
\]
Averaging \(e^{-1/2}2^{k}\) over the intersection of two uniform perfect matchings tends to \(1\).
The exponent remainder \(n^2/4\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:half-main-term} is unchanged, so this principal ratio is not yet the probability ratio.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The factorial part of \(\log(\rho_2(k)/\rho_1^2)-k\log 2\), with the two factors \(\exp(1/4)\) removed, has Stirling expansion whose powers \(n^2\log n\), \(n^2\), \(n\log n\), \(n\), and \(\log n\) cancel, leaving the constant \(-3/2\), independent of \(k\).
The exponent \(\phi(n/2,2k/n)\) in~\cite[display~(8)]{Gao21} tends to \(1\).
Adding it to \(-3/2\) produces \(-1/2\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host} gives \(\EE[2^{K}]\to e^{1/2}\), and \(e^{-1/2}e^{1/2}=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Remainder bounds depend on the overlap]
\label{prop:remainder-split}
At degree \(d=n/2\), the residual degree sequence of intersection \(k\) has McKay parameters
\[
\hat\Delta=\frac{n^2}{4}-1,\qquad m=\frac{n(n-4)}{4}+k.
\]
The second-moment combination of the bounding quantities \(\hat\Delta^2/m\) is
\[
2n+(2-k)+O\Bigl(\frac{k+1}{n}\Bigr).
\]
These bounds do not cancel across fixed overlaps.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The maximum residual degree is \(d-1=n/2-1\) and the host has maximum degree \(2\), so
\[
\hat\Delta=\Bigl(\frac n2-1\Bigr)^2+2\Bigl(\frac n2-1\Bigr)=\frac{n^2}{4}-1,
\]
which does not depend on \(k\). The number of residual edges is \(m=n(n-4)/4+k\).
For one perfect matching the same parameter equals \(n(n-2)/4\), and for the complete degree sequence it equals \(n^2/4\).
The combination of the three bounding quantities therefore has the expansion
\[
\frac{(n^2/4-1)^2}{n(n-4)/4+k}-2\cdot\frac{n(n-2)}{4}+\frac{n^2}{4}
=
2n+(2-k)+O\Bigl(\frac{k+1}{n}\Bigr).
\]
The linear term \(2n\) tends to infinity, and the overlap \(k\) remains in the constant term.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dense exponent is stable under a bounded degree shift]
\label{prop:dense-exponent}
Let
\[
\psi(\lambda)=-\frac{1+10\lambda-10\lambda^2}{12\lambda(1-\lambda)}
\]
be the exponent in the dense regular count of~\cite[Theorem~1]{MW90}.
At the three averages \(n/2\), \(n/2-1\), and \(n/2-2+2k/n\),
\[
\psi(\lambda_k)+\psi(\lambda_{n/2})-2\psi(\lambda_{n/2-1})=O\Bigl(\frac{(k+1)^2}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
A bounded shift of the average degree does not separate this exponent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The corresponding values of \(\lambda=d/(n-1)\) differ from \(1/2\) by
\[
\frac{1}{2(n-1)},\qquad -\frac{1}{2(n-1)},\qquad \frac{4k-3n}{2n(n-1)}.
\]
The combination of the squares of these deviations equals
\[
\frac{2(n-k)(n-2k)}{n^2(n-1)^2}.
\]
The expansion of \(\psi\) at \(1/2\) is \(-\frac76-\frac43(\lambda-\frac12)^2+O\bigl((\lambda-\frac12)^4\bigr)\), with no odd powers.
Multiplying the square combination by \(-\frac43\) produces a quantity of order \((k+1)^2/n^2\), and the fourth-power terms are of order \(1/n^4\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Deletion preserves a coercive phase]
\label{lem:coercive}
Let \(H\) have maximum degree \(2\). Then
\[
\sum_{(jk)\notin E(H)}(\theta_j+\theta_k)^2
\ge
(n-6)\sum_j\theta_j^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The complete sum is \(\sum_{j<k}(\theta_j+\theta_k)^2=(n-2)\sum_j\theta_j^2+(\sum_j\theta_j)^2\).
Each edge of \(H\) contributes at most \(2\theta_u^2+2\theta_v^2\), and \(\sum_{uv\in E(H)}(\theta_u^2+\theta_v^2)=\sum_v\deg_H(v)\theta_v^2\le 2\sum_v\theta_v^2\).
Subtracting at most \(4\sum\theta_v^2\) leaves \((n-6)\sum\theta_j^2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Saddle prefactor of a deleted host]
\label{prop:saddle-prefactor}
Let \(E=\binom{n}{2}-e\) be the number of available pairs and let \(m\) be the number of edges in the counted graph. Set \(\lambda=m/E\) and
\[
\eta(\lambda)=-\lambda\log\lambda-(1-\lambda)\log(1-\lambda).
\]
For the empty host, one perfect matching, and the union of two perfect matchings with intersection \(k\),
\[
E_k\eta(\lambda_k)+E_\emptyset\eta(\lambda_\emptyset)-2E_M\eta(\lambda_M)
=
k\log 2-\frac12+O\Bigl(\frac{k+1}{n}\Bigr).
\]
The weights \(e^{-1/2}2^{k}\) average to \(1\) over the intersection of two uniform perfect matchings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The value of the Cauchy integrand at the origin, after the radius \(r=\sqrt{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}\) is factored out, is \(E\eta(\lambda)\).
The binary entropy expands as \(\eta(\lambda)=\log 2-2(\lambda-\tfrac12)^2+O\bigl((\lambda-\tfrac12)^4\bigr)\).
For one perfect matching, \(m=n(n-2)/4\) and \(E=n(n-2)/2\), so \(\lambda=1/2\) and the quadratic correction vanishes.
The combination of the three values of \(E\) equals \(k\), so the order-\(\log 2\) terms contribute \(k\log 2\).
The squared deviations contribute
\[
\frac{(n-2k)^2}{4\bigl(n(n-3)+2k\bigr)}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)},
\]
which tends to \(1/2\), with series \(\tfrac12+(1-k)/n+O((k^2+1)/n^2)\).
The fourth-power remainder is \(O(1/n^2)\) because \(E(\lambda-\tfrac12)^4=O(1/n^2)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host} shows that the determinant, once this constant term is removed, changes the logarithm by \(O(k/n)\).
Averaging uses \(\EE[2^{K}]\to e^{1/2}\) from that same proposition, and \(e^{-1/2}e^{1/2}=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Half-degree coupling]
\label{thm:half}
Let \(n\) be even. There is a coupling of a uniform \((n/2-1)\)-regular graph inside a uniform \((n/2)\)-regular graph with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:middle-reduction} at degree \(n/2-1\) reduces the claim to \(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=o(1)\), where \(Y\) is the number of perfect matchings in \(G(n,n/2)\).
That ratio is the average, over a uniform second perfect matching, of \(N(H)N(\emptyset)/N(M)^2\), where \(N\) is the number of extensions of the indicated host.

The Cauchy integrand is maximized at the origin, because
\[
\bigl|1+\lambda(e^{i\varphi}-1)\bigr|^2=1-2\lambda(1-\lambda)(1-\cos\varphi)\le 1.
\]
Thus \(N\) is at most the saddle prefactor of Proposition~\ref{prop:saddle-prefactor}.
Along \(k=\alpha n\) the quadratic deficit in that prefactor tends to \(\tfrac12+\alpha(\alpha-1)\), which is at least \(\tfrac14\) for \(\alpha\in[0,1]\).
The prefactor ratio is therefore at most \(C\,2^{k}\).

On the box \(\max|\theta_j|\le n^{-1/2+1/20}\), the fifth-order remainder in each edge factor is \(O(n^{-5/2+1/4})\) per pair, and \(n^2\) pairs contribute \(O(n^{-1/4})\).
Deleting the host removes pairs, so the same bound holds for every union of two perfect matchings.
Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive} gives a quadratic form of size at least \(n-6\). A coordinate outside the box, against \(n-O(1)\) partners and at most two deleted edges, costs \(\exp(-c n^{1/10})\) in the modulus.
The integral outside the box is \(o(1)\) relative to the Gaussian integral of the quadratic form, uniformly in the host.
Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host} supplies a determinant ratio \(1+O(k/n)\) once the origin value is removed.

For \(k\le\log n\) the saddle expansion is uniform, so the extension ratio equals \(e^{-1/2}2^{k}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\).
The factorial moments of the intersection \(K\) are those recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host}.
For \(k\le\log n\), each factor \((n/2-j)/(n-1-2j)\) equals \(\tfrac12\bigl(1+O((\log n)/n)\bigr)\), so \(2^{k}\) times the moment of order \(k\) is \(\exp\bigl(O((\log n)^2/n)\bigr)\), which tends to \(1\).
The average over \(k\le\log n\) therefore tends to \(1\).
The range \(k>\log n\) contributes at most \(C\,\EE[2^{K}1_{K>\log n}]\), which tends to \(0\) because those moments match a Poisson random variable of mean \(1/2\).
Hence \(\EE Y^2/(\EE Y)^2\to 1\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} gives the coupling.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every linear degree]
\label{thm:band}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\) satisfy \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\). Then
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=o(1)\) for the number \(Y\) of perfect matchings in \(G(n,d+1)\), and the coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:middle} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(D=d+1\) and write \(\lambda=(D-1)/(n-2)\). The hypothesis \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\) forces \(\lambda\) and \(1-\lambda\) to stay bounded away from \(0\).
The extension ratio is again the average of \(N(H)N(\emptyset)/N(M)^2\).
With \(E\) available pairs and \(m\) counted edges, the origin value is \(E\eta(m/E)\).
The three values of \(E\) still combine to \(k\). The displacements of edge density from \(\lambda\) sum to \(k(1-\lambda)\).
The identity \(\eta(\lambda)+(1-\lambda)\log((1-\lambda)/\lambda)=\log(1/\lambda)\) turns that linear piece into \(k\log(1/\lambda)\).
Each squared displacement contributes \((1-\lambda)^2/2\), and
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac{-1}{\lambda(1-\lambda)}\cdot(1-\lambda)^2=-\frac12\Bigl(\frac1\lambda-1\Bigr).
\]
The cubic remainder is \(O(1/n)\) uniformly for \(k\le n/2\), because every displacement is \(O(1/n)\).
Thus the origin contribution is \(\lambda^{-k}\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12(\lambda^{-1}-1)+o(1)\bigr)\) for \(k\le\log n\).
The tail and determinant comparisons in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:half} depend on \(\lambda(1-\lambda)\) only through positive constants, so they remain \(o(1)\) on this range.
The factorial moments of \(K\) still tend to those of a Poisson random variable of mean \(1/2\), whose probability generating function at \(\lambda^{-1}\) is \(\exp\bigl(\tfrac12(\lambda^{-1}-1)\bigr)\).
The product of that factor with the constant \(\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12(\lambda^{-1}-1)\bigr)\) is \(1\).
The range \(k>\log n\) is at most a constant times \(\EE[\lambda^{-K}1_{K>\log n}]\), and \(\lambda^{-k}/k!\) sums to a tail that tends to \(0\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:middle-reduction} and Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} give the coupling.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rate in the middle band]
\label{prop:band-rate}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:band},
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}=O\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cubic remainder is \(O(1/n)\) for every overlap.
The determinant changes the logarithm by \(O(k/n)\), and each factor of the intersection moment satisfies
\[
\frac{n-2j}{n-1-2j}=1+\frac1{n-1-2j},
\]
so a product of \(k\) factors is \(1+O(k/n)\).
Both errors average to \(O(1/n)\) against the intersection law, which has a finite mean.
The range \(k>\log n\) remains negligible, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:band}.
The integral outside the saddle box is \(\exp(-c n^{1/10})\).
The second-moment ratio is therefore \(1+O(1/n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chains through linear degree]
\label{thm:band-chains}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\).
If \(L=o(n^{1/3})\), there is a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{D+L}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:band-rate} gives \(v=O(1/n)\) at every degree on the interval, since \(L=o(n^{1/3})\) keeps \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\).
Chebyshev at height \(v^{1/3}\) fails with probability \(v^{1/3}\), because \(v/(v^{1/3})^2=v^{1/3}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} turns each step into a coupling of failure \(O(n^{-1/3})\).
The union bound is \(L\) times that quantity, which tends to \(0\) by the hypothesis on \(L\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharper rate at half degree]
\label{prop:half-rate}
At degree \(d=n/2\), the origin contribution of an overlap \(k\) is
\[
e^{-1/2}2^{k}\Bigl(1+\frac{k-1}{n}+O\Bigl(\frac{k^2+1}{n^2}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Averaging \(k-1\) against the Poisson law of mean \(1\) gives \(0\), so
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}=O\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic part of the saddle excess, multiplied by \(n\), has series \((k-1)+O((k^2+1)/n)\).
Higher derivatives of the entropy contribute \(O((k^2+1)/n^2)\) to the excess.
The weights \(e^{-1/2}2^{k}\) tilt a Poisson random variable of mean \(1/2\) to one of mean \(1\).
Under that law the mean of \(k-1\) is \(0\), since \(\sum_{k\ge 0}(k-1)/k!=0\).
The coefficient of \(1/n^2\) in the quadratic deficit is \(k^2-\tfrac72 k+\tfrac52\), and after the expansion of the exponential its average under the same Poisson law equals \(-\tfrac12\).
That piece does not cancel, so the second-moment ratio is \(1+O(1/n^2)\) and not \(1+o(1/n^2)\) from the deficit alone.
The average of the displayed factor is therefore \(1+O(1/n^2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Longer chains about half degree]
\label{thm:half-chains}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(|D-n/2|\le L\), with
\(L=o(n^{1/2})\).
Then there is a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{D+L}\) with each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a degree \(n/2+t\) the edge density differs from \(1/2\) by \(O(|t|/n)\).
The half-degree cancellation of Proposition~\ref{prop:half-rate} then leaves an excess \(O((|t|+1)/n^2)\).
On the interval one has \(|t|\le L\), so \(v=O((L+1)/n^2)\).
Each step fails with probability \(O(v^{1/3})=O(L^{1/3}n^{-2/3})\).
The product \(L\cdot L^{1/3}n^{-2/3}=L^{4/3}n^{-2/3}\) tends to \(0\) by the hypothesis on \(L\).
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[One deletion of a long factor]
\label{conj:long-step}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy \(\min(d,n-d,d+L,n-d-L)=\Theta(n)\) and \(L=\omega(n^{1/2})\), with \(dn\) and \(Ln\) even.
Let \(Z\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \((d+L)\)-regular graph.
If \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\), then Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-tv} gives a uniform \((d+L)\)-regular graph \(H\) and a \(d\)-regular subgraph \(G\subset H\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
The subgraph \(G\) is uniformly random if every \(d\)-regular graph has the same number of \((d+L)\)-regular supergraphs, up to a \(1+o(1)\) factor.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}[Union bound across all degrees]
\label{prop:harmonic}
Suppose each one-step coupling at degree \(d\) fails with probability at least \(c/d\)
for some \(c>0\) and all \(d\in[3,n/3]\). Then a union bound over these steps does
not tend to \(0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum of the costs is at least
\[
c\sum_{d=3}^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}\frac1d
\ge
c\int_{3}^{n/3}\frac{dx}{x}
=
c\log(n/9),
\]
which tends to infinity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Chain schedule]
\label{lem:chain-eta}
Let \(D,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}, and write \(v(D)\)
for the quantity defined there.
For every \(d\in[D,D+L]\) set \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}:=v(d)^{1/3}\), where \(v(d)\)
is the same expression at degree \(d\).
Then \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}=o(1)\) and \(\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})=o(1/L)\), uniformly in
\(d\in[D,D+L]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(v(D)\ge D^{-3}\), the hypothesis \(L=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\) forces \(L=o(D)\).
Thus \(d\le 2D\) for large \(n\), and \(v(d)\le 8v(D)\).
In particular \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\ge d^{-1}\) and
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\le 2v(D)^{1/3}=o(1/L)=o(1)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio-error} gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2\le v(d)\) after enlarging the absolute constant inside \(v\).
Chebyshev's inequality at relative height \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\) therefore costs at most
\(v(d)/\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}^2=v(d)^{1/3}\).
The same comparison absorbs the summands of \(\eta\) beyond \(2\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\), and
\[
\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})
\le 3v(d)^{1/3}
=O\bigl(v(D)^{1/3}\bigr)
=o(1/L).
\]
A lower bound \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\ge c/L\) does not imply
\(2\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}=O(1/L)\). The deviation \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\) is set by balancing
\(2\alpha\) against \(v(d)/\alpha^2\), and that balance is of order \(v(d)^{1/3}\), which
is at least on the order of \(1/d\) because \(v(d)\ge d^{-3}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}]
For each \(d\in\{D,\ldots,D+L-1\}\) the single-step coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (via~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21} with the schedule of
Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-eta}) produces a coupling of \(G(n,d)\) and \(G(n,d+1)\)
with failure probability at most \(5\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})=o(1/L)\).
Compose these couplings sequentially: sample \(G_D\sim G(n,D)\), then couple
forward to \(G_{D+1}\), then to \(G_{D+2}\), and so on.
Each marginal remains correct.
By a union bound, the probability that any step fails is
\[
o\Bigl(\frac1L\Bigr)\cdot L=o(1).
\]
On the complementary event one has
\(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:mega}]
The summands of \(v(D)=D^{-3}+D/n\) meet when \(D^4=n\), i.e.\ at
\(D=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor\). There \((n^{1/4})/n=n^{-3/4}\), so
\(v(D)=\Theta(n^{-3/4})\) and \(v(D)^{-1/3}=\Theta(n^{1/4})\), because
\((n^{-3/4})^{-1/3}=n^{1/4}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Beyond exact \texorpdfstring{\(o(\sqrt{n})\)}{o(sqrt(n))}}
\label{sec:beyond}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:approx}]
Sample \((G,H)\) from \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\), so \(G\subset H\) almost surely and
\(H\sim\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\).
Couple \(H\) to \(H'\sim\mu_{d+1}\) optimally, whence
\(\PP(H\neq H')=\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le\delta\).
On \(\{H=H'\}\) one has \(G\subset H'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[TV from concentration]
\label{lem:tv}
If \(\PP(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y)\le\delta\) with \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), then
\[
\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le 10(\alpha+\delta).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Factor count controls the upper graph]
\label{lem:factor-tv}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(dn\) and \(Ln\) be even. Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be the set of pairs of spanning subgraphs \((G,H)\) with \(G\) \(d\)-regular, \(H\) \((d+L)\)-regular, and \(G\subset H\). Let \(\nu\) be the law of \(H\) when \((G,H)\) is drawn uniformly from \(\mathcal{S}\), and let \(Z(H)\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of \(H\). If a uniform random \((d+L)\)-regular graph satisfies \(\PP(|Z-\EE Z|\ge\alpha\,\EE Z)\le\delta\) with \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), then
\[
\dTV(\nu,\mu_{d+L})\le\alpha+\delta.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal{T}\) be the set of \((d+L)\)-regular graphs and write \(\bar z=\EE Z\). The degree of \(H\) in the bipartite graph of pairs is \(Z(H)\), so \(|\mathcal{S}|=|\mathcal{T}|\,\bar z\) and \(\nu(H)=Z(H)/(|\mathcal{T}|\,\bar z)\).
On the complement of the event in the hypothesis, \((1-\alpha)\bar z\le Z\le(1+\alpha)\bar z\). That complement contains at least \((1-\delta)|\mathcal{T}|\) graphs, each of degree at least \((1-\alpha)\bar z\), so the total degree of the event itself is at most
\[
|\mathcal{T}|\,\bar z\bigl(1-(1-\delta)(1-\alpha)\bigr)
=
|\mathcal{T}|\,\bar z\,(\alpha+\delta-\alpha\delta).
\]
The total variation is half the average of \(|Z-\bar z|/\bar z\). Graphs in the concentration window contribute at most \(\alpha\). Graphs in the event contribute at most \(\alpha+\delta-\alpha\delta+\delta\). The sum is at most \(2(\alpha+\delta)\), and the total variation is at most \(\alpha+\delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tv}]
This is the Strassen/bipartite coupling of~\cite[Corollary~3.2]{HLMPW25}, equivalently
the degree-regularity argument in~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21}: on the high-probability
set where \(Y\) is \(\alpha\)-concentrated, the coupling fails with probability
\(O(\alpha+\delta)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sqrt}]
For \(d\le c\sqrt{n}\) the patched variance formula gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(c^2+d^{-3})\).
Choose \(\alpha>0\) fixed and \(c\) small enough that Chebyshev yields \(\delta\le\alpha\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} then gives TV distance \(O(\alpha)\).
Shrinking \(\alpha\) yields TV \(\le 1/2\); apply Theorem~\ref{thm:approx}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}]
By~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BIM25}, there is a coupling with
\[
G\subset G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+1)/n\bigr)\subset H
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
Under the standard monotone coupling of binomial graphs, the edges of \(G\) that can
fail to lie in \(H\) are contained in the ER difference
\[
G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)
\setminus
G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+1)/n\bigr).
\]
A Chernoff bound shows that this difference has maximum degree at most \(3\varepsilon d\)
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}]
We track the error budget in the upper-sandwich reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25},
using their switching estimate~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{BIM25} as a black box.
The lower inclusion is~\cite[Lemma~2.2]{BIM25}. Its hypotheses are implied by
the budget constraints checked below: once \(C_*\) is large one has
\(\varepsilon d=C_*d^{8/9}(\log n)^{1/9}\gg\log n\), so the same \(\varepsilon\) is admissible
for that lemma.

 Crucially, the switching core~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} uses an
\emph{absolute} large constant \(C_0\) in the constraints
\(\eta d\ge C_0\) and \(\eta^2\delta\ge C_0\log n\).
We therefore instantiate the process with absolute \(C_0\) (sufficiently large)
and \(\mu:=\varepsilon/100\), rather than forcing \(C_0=\omega(1/\varepsilon)\).

In~\cite[§3.5.5]{BIM25} the critical accumulation event B2 requires, with
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:B2}
2C_0\mu\,dn + 4C_0\,dn\Bigl(\frac{10n\log n}{\varepsilon dn}\Bigr)^{1/8}
\le\frac R2.
\end{equation}
It is enough that
\begin{align}
C_0\mu&\le\frac{\varepsilon}{32},\label{eq:bud1}\\
C_0\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}\Bigr)^{1/8}&\le\frac{\varepsilon}{32}.\label{eq:bud2}
\end{align}
With \(\mu=\varepsilon/100\) and \(C_0\) absolute,~\eqref{eq:bud1} holds for
\(C_0\le 3\).
Inequality~\eqref{eq:bud2} rearranges to
\[
\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}
\le O(\varepsilon^{8})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varepsilon=\Omega\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/9}\Bigr).
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon=C_*(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) with \(C_*\) large, both budget
constraints hold, and
\[
\eta:=C_0\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}\Bigr)^{1/8}=O(\varepsilon)
\]
satisfies the switching hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25}:
\(\eta\ll 1\), \(\eta d\to\infty\), and
\(\eta^2\cdot(2\varepsilon d)=\Omega(\varepsilon^3 d)=\Omega(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3})\gg\log n\).
The reduction in~\cite[§3]{BIM25} therefore yields the upper sandwich.
Combined with the lower sandwich at the same rate, we obtain the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at degrees \(d\) and \(d+k\) with
\(\varepsilon=C_*(\log n/d)^{1/9}\).
The binomial transit of Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} yields (i) once
\(k\ge 3\varepsilon d=k_0\), and (ii) with defect at most \(3\varepsilon d=k_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}]
Fix \(\sigma\in(0,1)\) and assume \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\).
Set \(\varepsilon=C_{**}(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) with \(C_{**}\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\) and \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\).
Then
\[
\delta_R\asymp d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\ge n^{2\sigma/3},
\]
so \(\delta_R\ge n^{\sigma'}\) for some fixed \(\sigma'>0\) and large \(n\).

In this density regime,~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25} gives
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating path counts at relative error
\(\eta=C_0\mu/\log n\) whenever \(\delta\ge n^{\sigma'}\).
The same Kim--Vu argument permits any larger relative error; we take the
switching-optimal choice
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{m}}\Bigr\}
\]
with absolute \(C_0\) and \(\mu=\varepsilon/100\).
At \(m\ge R\) one has \(\sqrt{n\log n/m}=O(\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)})=O(\varepsilon)\),
so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
The constraints of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} hold:
\(\eta d=\Omega(\varepsilon d)\to\infty\) and \(\eta^2\delta=\Omega(\log n)\).

The B2 estimate of~\cite[§3.5.5]{BIM25} with this schedule reduces to
\(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) and \(C_0\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of
which hold at our \(\varepsilon\).
Thus the upper sandwich follows; the lower sandwich is unchanged.

For optimality: at stopping time \(R\asymp\varepsilon dn\) one has \(\delta\asymp\varepsilon d\).
Event B2 forces \(\eta=O(\varepsilon)\), while switching forces
\(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\log n/\delta})\).
Combining yields \(\varepsilon=\Omega((\log n/d)^{1/3})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:poly-inc}]
Identical to Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}, using Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:chernoff}]
Let \(p=(1-\varepsilon)d/n\) and let \(X_v\) be the degree of a fixed vertex \(v\) in
\(G(n,p)\), so \(X_v\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p)\) with mean
\(\lambda=(1-\varepsilon)d\cdot(1-O(1/n))\).
If \(\varepsilon\le c\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(c>0\) small, then
\(d-\lambda=O(\sqrt{\lambda\log n})\) lies inside the constant-probability upper
tail regime of the binomial.
Writing \(N=\sum_v\mathbf{1}_{X_v>d}\), a standard second-moment computation on the
indicators (cf.\ Bollob\'as) yields \(\EE N\to\infty\) and
\(\Var N=o((\EE N)^2)\), so \(N\ge 1\) with probability bounded away from zero.
Thus \(\Delta(G(n,p))>d\) fails to be a vanishing event, and no high-probability
embedding \(G(n,p)\subseteq\Gnd\) is possible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Q1}]
Apply Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} at degrees \(d\) and \(d+k\) with
\(\varepsilon=C_0\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\).
Then \(G\subset G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) and
\(G(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+k)/n)\subset H\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
The inequality \((1+\varepsilon)d\le(1-\varepsilon)(d+k)\) rearranges to
\(k\ge 2\varepsilon d/(1-\varepsilon)\).
For \(\varepsilon\le 1/4\) this is implied by \(k\ge 3\varepsilon d=3C_0\sqrt{d\log n}\).
Monotone-couple the two binomial graphs to finish.
\end{proof}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{\(\sqrt{n}\)}{sqrt(n)} barrier and open problems}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{proposition}[Enumeration barrier]
\label{prop:barrier}
Let \(M\) be a perfect matching. The estimate of \(\PP(M\subset\Gnd)\) obtained by
multiplying the \(n/2\) conditional probabilities from~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} is
known only up to a cumulative relative factor \(1+O(d^2/n)\). The estimate yields a
\(1+o(1)\) factor when \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), and it does not when \(d=\omega(n^{1/2})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each factor is the principal term of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} times \(1+O(d^2/n^2)\),
as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}. The product of \(n/2\) such factors is
\[
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr)^{n/2}
=
\exp\bigl(O(d^2/n)\bigr),
\]
which is \(1+O(d^2/n)\) whenever \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). If \(d=\omega(n^{1/2})\), then \(d^2/n\not\to 0\),
so the same product does not tend to \(1\).
\end{proof}

The coupling does not use this absolute estimate.
It uses concentration of the matching count, and the universal part of the edge error cancels in that ratio.
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-thirds} therefore gives exact \(+1\) for every \(d=o(n^{2/3})\).
Repeating the substitution \(k\) times replaces that error by \(d^{k+2}/n^{k+1}\), which
tends to \(0\) for \(d=o(n^{(k+1)/(k+2)})\). Theorem~\ref{thm:below-linear} therefore
gives exact \(+1\) for every \(d=o(n^{1-\varepsilon})\).
Growing the number of substitutions to \(\lceil\log n/\log\log n\rceil\) reaches
every \(d=o(n/(\log n)^2)\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:laplace-degree}).
Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap-wide} uses a window of width \(\bar k^{2/3}\), which remains
\(o(\bar k)\) whenever \(\bar k\to\infty\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:sublinear} gives
exact \(+1\) for every \(d=o(n)\). At degrees where both \(d\) and \(n-d\) are of
order \(n\), the factor \(O(d/n)\) no longer tends to \(0\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:dense} recovers the range \(n-d=o(n)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:middle} is the \(+1\) statement for linear degree.
Proposition~\ref{prop:config-loops} shows that the configuration multigraph at half
degree has a loop with probability \(1-o(1)\), so the deletion has to be proved
on the uniform simple graph.
Proposition~\ref{prop:middle-reduction} reduces that conjecture to
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=o(1)\) for the perfect-matching count on \(G(n,d+1)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:half-main-term} evaluates the factorial ratio at degree \(n/2\):
it is a Poisson(\(1\)) ratio, whose series sums to \(1\), while the enumeration
remainder in the exponent equals \(n^2/4\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-flat} shows that the dense degree-sequence correction
is \(o(1)\) for each fixed overlap, so that correction does not separate the Poisson weights.
Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-host} shows that the linear statistic of the forbidden host
has variance \((8k/n)(1+o(1))\) under the same quadratic approximation, so that linear factor
does not separate them either.
Proposition~\ref{prop:quad-host} evaluates the quadratic piece: its integral ratio tends to \(2^k\),
and the average of \(2^k\) over a second perfect matching tends to \(e^{1/2}\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-binomial} shows that the degree binomials tend to the same
factor \(e^{-4}\) for every fixed overlap, so they do not cancel \(2^k\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:principal-average} multiplies the edge-entropy principal part through:
for each fixed overlap it contributes \(e^{-1/2}2^{k}\), and the average of that factor is \(1\).
The remainder \(n^2/4\) in the exponent is still present.
Proposition~\ref{prop:remainder-split} shows that the quantities bounding that remainder
differ by \(2n+(2-k)\) across an overlap \(k\), so the bounds themselves do not cancel.
Proposition~\ref{prop:dense-exponent} replaces that bound by the exponent of~\cite[Theorem~1]{MW90}:
a bounded shift of the average degree changes it by \(O(1/n^2)\), so the exponent cancels in the ratio.
That count does not delete a host.
Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive} keeps the phase coercive after the host is deleted.
Proposition~\ref{prop:saddle-prefactor} evaluates the deleted-host integrand at the origin, with the radius fixed by \(\lambda=m/E\): the logarithm is \(k\log 2-\frac12+O((k+1)/n)\), and the average of these weights is \(1\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:half} turns that average into the coupling of degrees \(n/2-1\) and \(n/2\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:band} carries the same saddle to every degree \(\delta n\) with \(\delta\) and \(1-\delta\) bounded away from \(0\): the origin contribution is \(\lambda^{-k}\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12(\lambda^{-1}-1)\bigr)\), and that factor averages to \(1\) against the intersection of two perfect matchings.
Theorem~\ref{thm:middle} is the resulting coupling.
Theorem~\ref{thm:band-chains} extends one step to a chain of length \(o(n^{1/3})\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-chains} lengthens that chain to \(o(n^{1/2})\) for degrees within the same distance of \(n/2\).
A union bound of these steps cannot cross length \(n^{1/2}\): in the linear band the relative variance of the matching count is of order \(1/n\), so even a deviation at the standard-deviation scale costs \(n^{-1/2}\) per step.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step} is the replacement, one deletion of an \(L\)-factor for some \(L=\omega(n^{1/2})\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:factor-tv} is the total-variation comparison for that deletion: concentration of the factor count makes the upper graph uniform.
The number of \(L\)-factors is still unproved for \(L=\omega(n^{1/2})\), and the lower graph is uniform only when every \(d\)-regular graph has the same number of extensions.
A Chernoff increment still has length \(\Theta\sqrt{n\log n}\) at this degree, which is longer by a power of \(\log n\), so Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} and Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} stay open.
Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} still describes simultaneous chains inside \(o(n^{1/2})\), where the variance is \(D^{-3}+D/n\).

Quantitative sandwich rates from the~\cite{BIM25} budget:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Rate & Range & Status \\
\hline
\((\log n/d)^{1/9}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/5}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-fifth} \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/3}\) & \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third} (process-optimal) \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/3}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} (process-optimal) \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/2}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The exponent \(1/3\) is optimal for the deletion-process~/~B2 scheme; reaching
Chernoff requires a different coupling (cf.~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}).
The same cubic ceiling appears on the lower side: residual degree concentration
forces \(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)})\) with \(\eta\ll\varepsilon\).
The dense embedding of~\cite{GIM22} is likewise blocked at Chernoff scale by the
structural hypothesis \(\xi\Delta\gg n/\log n\), which is incompatible with
\(\xi=\Theta\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) for every \(d\le n-1\).
The exponent \(1/9\) is an artefact of the
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/8}\) two-vertex path upper bound in~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25}.
Redesigning the expansion so connecting sets are linear
(Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}) upgrades the lower path error to
\(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\); truncation recovers upper error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:U}) and global rate \(1/5\).
A last-layer Bernstein argument then upgrades the two-vertex upper bound to the
same Chernoff scale (Theorem~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}), yielding the
process-optimal global rate \(1/3\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q}]
Let \((G_i)_{0\le i\le\binom{n}{2}}\) be the random graph process on \([n]\), and let
\(G\sim\Gnd\) be coupled to the process as in~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Fix \(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large, and set
\[
i_-:=\Bigl\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad
i_+:=\Bigl\lceil(1+\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rceil.
\]
Write \(p_\pm:=(1\pm\varepsilon)d/n\). A standard Chernoff-plus-union bound gives
\[
\PP\bigl(\Delta(G_{i_-})>d\bigr)=o(1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\PP\bigl(\delta(G_{i_+})<d\bigr)=o(1),
\]
once \(C\) is large enough (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:chernoff} for the matching
lower bound on \(\varepsilon\)).
On the event \(\{\Delta(G_{i_-})\le d\}\), Conjecture~1.3 of~\cite{BIM25} yields
\(G_{i_-}\subset G\).
On \(\{\delta(G_{i_+})\ge d\}\) it yields \(G\subset G_{i_+}\).
Finally, \(G_{i_-}\) (resp.\ \(G_{i_+}\)) may be coupled inside
\(G(n,p_-)\) (resp.\ outside a copy of \(G(n,p_+)\)) by the usual monotone coupling
of the process with binomial graphs of matching edge count, losing only \(o(1)\)
probability.
Combining these couplings produces \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) to
obtain \(G\subset G^*_0\) with \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\).
The final-window hypothesis, applied at the target Chernoff rate
\(\varepsilon=C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\le\varepsilon_0\) (valid for large \(n\) since
\(1/2>1/3\)), produces \(H\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) with \(G\subset H\) a.a.s.
The symmetric argument for the lower sandwich yields \(G_*\).
Stitching gives Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} on \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Attack surface]
\label{rem:window}
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} isolates the obstruction to Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}:
one must thin the residual \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\), which is approximately
\(\varepsilon_0 d\)-regular of order \(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), down to surplus
degree \(\asymp\sqrt{d\log n}\) without exposing any edge of \(G\).
This is a far smaller range of surplus densities than the full deletion process.
Proposition~\ref{prop:hybrid-gap} shows that thinning that residual produces a degree of variance \(O(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3})\), whereas a binomial degree has variance \(\Theta(d)\), so the thinned graph is not the Chernoff upper graph.
Proposition~\ref{prop:sprinkle-gap} extends the same obstruction to every completion that freezes \(G\) and sprinkles independently on its complement.
Proposition~\ref{prop:binomial-first} is the route around that obstruction: sample the binomial first and select a \(d\)-regular subgraph whose marginal is uniform.
The selection step is open.
Conjecture~1.3 of~\cite{BIM25} is the same demand applied to every graph of the random graph process at once, and Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q} converts it into Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Residual degree after rate \(1/3\)]
\label{lem:residual}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
Let \((G,G^*_0)\) be the coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate
\(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\), and write \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\).
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\,d
\le\deg_R(v)
\le 2\varepsilon_0\,d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\)},
\]
and in particular \(\min_v\deg_R(v)\ge C\sqrt{d\log n}\) for every fixed \(C\) and large \(n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the event \(G\subset G^*_0\) (probability \(1-o(1)\)) one has
\(\deg_R(v)=\deg_{G^*_0}(v)-d\).
Here \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\), so
\(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)\sim\mathrm{Bin}\bigl(n-1,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\bigr)\) has mean
\(\lambda=(1+\varepsilon_0)d\cdot(1-O(1/n))\).
Chernoff bounds yield
\begin{align*}
\PP\bigl(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)<d+\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\bigr)
&\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d)\bigr)=o(n^{-2}),\\
\PP\bigl(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)>d+2\varepsilon_0 d\bigr)
&\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d)\bigr)=o(n^{-2}),
\end{align*}
and a union bound over \(v\in[n]\) completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sharp residual degrees]
\label{lem:residual-sharp}
Let \(d/\log n\to\infty\), and let \(R\) be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}, with
\(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\,\varepsilon_0 d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\).}
\]
More precisely, the relative error may be taken to be \(\varepsilon_0/2\), which tends to \(0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has \(\deg_R(v)=\deg_{G^*_0}(v)-d\), and
\(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)\) is binomial with mean
\(\lambda=(1+\varepsilon_0)d\,(1-O(1/n))\).
The residual mean is therefore
\(\mu_R=\varepsilon_0 d-O(d/n)\).
Since \(d=\omega(\log n)\) one has \(\varepsilon_0\to 0\), and the relative window
\(t:=\varepsilon_0/2\) satisfies
\[
t^2\mu_R
=\frac{\varepsilon_0^3 d}{4}-o(1)
=\frac{C_3^3}{4}\log n-o(1).
\]
Chernoff's bound gives
\[
\PP\bigl(|\deg_R(v)-\mu_R|\ge t\mu_R\bigr)
\le 2\exp\bigl(-\Omega(C_3^3\log n)\bigr).
\]
For the absolute constant \(C_3\) already fixed large by Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global},
the right-hand side is \(o(n^{-2})\).
A union bound over \(v\), together with \(\PP(G\not\subset G^*_0)=o(1)\), yields
\(\deg_R(v)=(1\pm\varepsilon_0/2)\mu_R=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) simultaneously for every \(v\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid Chernoff container]
\label{thm:hybrid}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large, and assume
\(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0:=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Let \((G,G^*_0)\) be the coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate
\(\varepsilon_0\), write \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\), and set \(q:=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\).
Form \(R_q\) by retaining each edge of \(R\) independently with probability \(q\), and set
\(H:=G\cup R_q\).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\);
\item for every pair \(jk\),
\(\PP(jk\in H)=(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n}+o\bigl(\frac{d}{n}\bigr)\);
\item writing \(D_v:=\deg_H(v)\), on the event \(G\subset G^*_0\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\) with
\(\deg_R(v)=\Theta(\varepsilon_0 d)\), hence
\(\EE D_v=(1+\Theta(\varepsilon))d\) and \(\Var D_v=\Theta(\varepsilon d)\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular \(H\) matches the first-moment edge profile of
\(G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) up to \(o(d/n)\), and has degree mean
\((1+\Theta(\varepsilon))d\) with variance \(\Theta(\varepsilon d)\), with \(G\subset H\) a.a.s.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item (i) is immediate on \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\), which has probability \(1-o(1)\).

For (ii), fix an unordered pair \(jk\). By symmetry
\(\PP(jk\in G)=d/(n-1)\).
Also \(\PP(jk\in G^*_0)=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\).
On the construction,
\[
\PP(jk\in H)
=\PP(jk\in G)+q\,\PP(jk\in R)
=\PP(jk\in G)+q\bigl(\PP(jk\in G^*_0)-\PP(jk\in G^*_0\cap G)\bigr).
\]
The failure event \(\{G\not\subset G^*_0\}\) has probability \(o(n^{-c})\) for some \(c>0\)
(from the B2 estimate at rate \(\varepsilon_0\)), and
\(\{jk\in G\setminus G^*_0\}\subset\{G\not\subset G^*_0\}\), so
\(\PP(jk\in G^*_0\cap G)=\PP(jk\in G)+O(n^{-c})\).
Substituting \(q=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\) therefore yields
\begin{align*}
\PP(jk\in H)
&=q\cdot(1+\varepsilon_0)\frac{d}{n}+(1-q)\frac{d}{n-1}+O(n^{-c})\\
&=(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n}+O\Bigl(\frac{d}{n^2}\Bigr)+O(n^{-c}),
\end{align*}
which is \((1+\varepsilon)d/n+o(d/n)\).

For (iii), on \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\).
On the high-probability event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual},
\(\deg_R(v)\in[\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d,\,2\varepsilon_0 d]\), so
\[
\EE[D_v\mid G,G^*_0]=d+q\deg_R(v)\in\bigl[d+\tfrac12\varepsilon d,\,d+2\varepsilon d\bigr]
\]
and
\[
\Var(D_v\mid G,G^*_0)=q(1-q)\deg_R(v)=\Theta(\varepsilon d).
\]
Rescaling the absolute constant in the definition of \(\varepsilon\) absorbs the
\(\tfrac12\)--\(2\) factors into the form stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp Chernoff degrees]
\label{cor:hybrid-sharp}
Suppose \(d/\log n\to\infty\) and \(C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\le\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\), with
\(C\) large and \(\varepsilon_0\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid}.
Let \(H\) be the hybrid graph of that theorem. Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\deg_H(v)=d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\).}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-sharp} gives \(\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) simultaneously
for every \(v\). On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\) with \(q=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\), so the conditional mean is
\(d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d\).
The conditional variance is \(\Theta(\varepsilon d)\). Set
\(t:=(12\varepsilon d\log n)^{1/2}\).
A Chernoff bound yields
\(\PP(|D_v-\EE[D_v\mid R]|\ge t\mid R)\le 2n^{-2}\) on the sharp degree event:
there \(q\deg_R(v)\le 2\varepsilon d\) for large \(n\), so
\(t^2/(3q\deg_R(v))\ge 2\log n\).
A union bound over \(v\) costs \(o(1)\).
The ratio of this deviation to the surplus is
\[
\frac{t}{\varepsilon d}
=\Theta\Biggl(\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Biggr)=o(1),
\]
since \(d/\log n\to\infty\). Therefore \(D_v=d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gap to Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}]
\label{rem:hybrid-gap}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} gives a one-sided Chernoff-scale container with correct edge
marginals and degree variance, but \(H\) is not asserted to be distributed as
\(G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\): edges inside \(G\) are rigidly dependent through the regular
constraint.

Moreover, a direct contiguity attack fails. Comparing \(H\) to the nested binomial
obtained by retaining each edge of \(G^*_0\) independently with probability
\(r=(1+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon_0)\) yields a \(\chi^2\) divergence of order
\(\Theta(dn)\) between the two inhomogeneous Bernoulli measures on \(G^*_0\)
(hybrid probabilities \(1\) on \(E(G)\) and \(q\) on \(E(R)\), versus constant \(r\) on all of
\(E(G^*_0)\)). Thus \(H\) is not contiguous to this nested ER model, and upgrading
Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} to the upper half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} requires a
different identification (or the process conjecture).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Hybrid degrees are too concentrated]
\label{prop:hybrid-gap}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} with \(\varepsilon\to 0\), the law of one degree of \(H\) and the law of one degree of \(G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) have total variation bounded below by \(1/16-o(1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(X\) be a vertex degree in the binomial graph. Its variance is \(\sigma^2=\Theta(d)\), and its fourth central moment is at most \(3\sigma^4+\sigma^2\).
Paley--Zygmund gives \(\PP(|X-\EE X|>\sigma/\sqrt{2})\ge 1/16\) once \(\sigma^2\ge 1\).
Let \(D\) be the corresponding degree of \(H\). Conditioning on the residual as in Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid}, and using \(\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-sharp}, yields
\(\Var D=O(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3})\).
Chebyshev's inequality gives \(\PP(|D-\EE D|>\sigma/4)\to 0\).
The edge marginals of Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} match the two means up to \(O(d/n)=o(\sigma)\).
The set where the binomial degree has probability at least \(1/16\) therefore has probability \(o(1)\) under \(D\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independent completion has the wrong variance]
\label{prop:sprinkle-gap}
Let \(G\) be a fixed \(d\)-regular graph, and let \(H\) be obtained by retaining every edge of \(G\) and adding each remaining edge independently with probability \(p'\) chosen so that \(\EE[\deg_H(v)]=(1+\varepsilon)d\).
If \(d\ge C\log n\) and \(\varepsilon=C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\to 0\), then \(\Var(\deg_H(v))=\Theta(\varepsilon d)=o(d)\), while a degree in \(G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) has variance \(\Theta(d)\).
The two degree laws are singular, so this completion is not a coupling for Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The added degree at \(v\) is binomial of mean \(\varepsilon d\), hence of variance \(\varepsilon d\,(1-p')\).
Here \(p'=\varepsilon d/(n-1-d)\le\varepsilon d/(n-1-d)\).
For \(d\le n/2\) one has \(p'=O(\varepsilon d/n)=O(\varepsilon)\), so the variance is \((1-o(1))\varepsilon d\).
For \(n/2<d\le n-1\) the same mean \(\varepsilon d\) is at most \(n-1-d\) only when \(\varepsilon d\le n-1-d\); otherwise no such \(p'\) exists.
Whenever \(p'\) exists, the variance is at most \(\varepsilon d=o(d)\).
The binomial comparison is the same Paley--Zygmund estimate as in Proposition~\ref{prop:hybrid-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sample the binomial first]
\label{prop:binomial-first}
Let \(d\ge C\log n\) and \(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\).
The upper half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} is equivalent to a coupling of \(H\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) with a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\subset H\) whose marginal is within total variation \(o(1)\) of the uniform law on \(d\)-regular graphs.
Every such coupling has \(\Var(\deg_H(v))=\Theta(d)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} is a coupling of this form.
Conversely, let \(G\subset H\) with \(H\) binomial and with \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}(G),\mu_d)=o(1)\).
A maximal coupling of \(G\) with a uniform random \(d\)-regular graph \(G_u\) satisfies \(\PP(G\neq G_u)=o(1)\).
On the agreement event, \(G_u\subset H\).
Thus \(\PP(G_u\subset H)=1-o(1)\), and \((G_u,H)\) is the required coupling.
The degree variance is the binomial variance \(\Theta(d)\), so the constructions of Propositions~\ref{prop:hybrid-gap} and~\ref{prop:sprinkle-gap} are excluded: those supergraphs are not binomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Matching room]
\label{rem:matching-room}
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} also supplies \(\delta(R)\gg\sqrt{d\log n}\) a.a.s., so \(R\) has
the degree room for \(m:=\lfloor C\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) successive perfect
matchings.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Residual expansion]
\label{lem:residual-exp}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\), and let \((G,G^*_0)\) and \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Write \(\delta_0:=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\) with \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\), and set
\(u_{\max}:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\) with \(C_e\) a large absolute constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-8})\), every \(U\subset[n]\) with
\(1\le|U|\le u_{\max}\) satisfies
\[
e_R(U,[n]\setminus U)\ge\frac14\delta_0|U|
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bigl|N_R(U)\bigr|\ge\min\Bigl\{n-|U|,\,\frac{\delta_0|U|}{40\log n}\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} one has \(\delta(R)\ge\delta_0\) and
\(\Delta(R)\le 4\delta_0\).
Moreover \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\).
For each fixed \(U\) with \(|U|\le u_{\max}\), the induced count \(e_{G^*_0}(U)\) is
binomial with mean
\[
p_0\binom{|U|}{2}
\le\frac{(1+\varepsilon_0)d\,u_{\max}^2}{2n}
=O\Bigl(\frac{n(\log n)^{4/3}}{d^{1/3}}\Bigr).
\]
For \(d\ge C_3\log n\) with \(C_3\) large this is at most \(\delta_0|U|/20\).
A Chernoff bound and union bound over all such \(U\) therefore yield
\(e_R(U)\le e_{G^*_0}(U)\le\delta_0|U|/10\) with probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\).
Hence
\[
e_R\bigl(U,[n]\setminus U\bigr)
=\sum_{v\in U}\deg_R(v)-2e_R(U)
\ge\delta_0|U|-\tfrac15\delta_0|U|
\ge\tfrac14\delta_0|U|.
\]

For the neighbourhood bound, every vertex outside \(U\) has codegree into \(U\) at
most \(10\log n\) with probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), uniformly over the stated range
(Chernoff plus union bound).
Hence
\[
\bigl|N_R(U)\bigr|
\ge\frac{e_R(U,[n]\setminus U)}{10\log n}
\ge\frac{\delta_0|U|}{40\log n}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-sided residual cuts]
\label{lem:residual-cuts}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\), and let \(R\) and \(\delta_0=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp}.
Set \(s_0:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\) with \(C_e\) a large absolute constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-8})\), every pair of disjoint sets
\(W,Z\subset[n]\) with \(\min(|W|,|Z|)\ge s_0\) satisfies
\[
e_R(W,Z)\ge\frac{\delta_0}{4n}\,|W||Z|.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A one-vertex set against its non-neighbourhood is an empty cut, so a product
hypothesis \(|W||Z|\ge s_0\) alone is false.
Both sides must be large.
Write \(p_0=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\).
For fixed disjoint \(W,Z\) with \(w:=|W|\), \(z:=|Z|\) and \(w,z\ge s_0\),
\(e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)\) is binomial with mean \(p_0 wz\).
On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\),
\[
e_R(W,Z)=e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)-e_G(W,Z).
\]
The same configuration-model tail as in~\cite[Appendix~A]{BIM25} gives
\[
e_G(W,Z)\le\Bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon_0}{4}\Bigr)\frac{d}{n}\,wz
\]
except with probability \(\exp(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d\, wz/n))\).
A Chernoff bound likewise yields
\[
e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)\ge\Bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\Bigr)\frac{d}{n}\,wz
\]
except with probability \(\exp(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d\, wz/n))\).
On the complementary event,
\[
e_R(W,Z)\ge\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_0 d}{4n}\Bigr)wz=\frac{\delta_0\, wz}{2n}.
\]
The failure probability of a fixed pair is therefore
\(\exp(-\Omega(\delta_0 wz/n))\).
Since \(w,z\ge s_0=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\),
\[
\frac{\delta_0 wz}{n}\ge C_e(w+z)\log n
\]
once \(C_e\) is large, because
\(\delta_0 s_0/n=C_e\log n\) and \(\min(w,z)\ge s_0\) implies
\(wz\ge s_0(w+z)/2\).
Hence the failure probability is at most
\(\exp(-4(w+z)\log n)\), while there are at most
\(\exp(O((w+z)\log n))\) pairs of those sizes.
Summing over \(w,z\ge s_0\) gives \(o(n^{-8})\).
For \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) one has \(s_0\le n/4\), so the range is nonempty.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Perfect matching in the residual]
\label{lem:perfect-matching}
Assume \(n\) is even and \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\).
Under the hypotheses of Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts},
with probability \(1-o(1)\) the graph \(R\) contains a perfect matching.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\), enlarging the absolute constant \(C_3\) if needed,
the minimum degree satisfies \(\delta_0\ge 120(\log n)^2\),
so the expansion factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp} obeys
\(\delta_0/(40\log n)\ge 3\log n\).
In particular that lemma applies to every set of size at most
\(s_0:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\), the same threshold as Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts},
and \(s_0\le n/4\).

Draw a uniformly random balanced bipartition \([n]=L\cup L'\) and let \(B\) be the
bipartite graph of \(R\)-edges across the cut.
A perfect matching of \(B\) is a perfect matching of \(R\).
A Chernoff bound gives \(\deg_B(v)\ge\delta_0/3\) for every vertex, with probability
\(1-o(n^{-2})\).
It is enough to check Hall's condition for sets \(W\subset L\) with \(|W|\le n/2\);
the opposite part is identical.

If \(1\le|W|\le\delta_0/3\), then \(N_B(W)\) contains \(N_B(v)\) for any \(v\in W\), hence
\(|N_B(W)|\ge\delta_0/3\ge|W|\).

If \(\delta_0/3<|W|\le s_0\), Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp} gives
\(|N_R(W)|\ge 3|W|\log n\).
Conditionally on \(W\subset L\), a hypergeometric slice of \(N_R(W)\) lands in \(L'\).
Its mean is at least \(|N_R(W)|/3\), so the probability it has size less than
\(|W|\) is \(\exp(-\Omega(|W|\log n))\).
Also \(\PP(W\subset L)\le 2^{-|W|}\).
There are at most \(\exp(|W|\log(en/|W|))\) candidate sets, and
\(\Omega(|W|\log n)\) dominates \(|W|\log(en/|W|)\).
Thus, with probability \(1-o(1)\), \(B\) has no Hall violator of size at most \(s_0\).

Now suppose \(s_0<|W|\le n/2\) and \(|N_B(W)|<|W|\).
The set \(Z:=L'\setminus N_B(W)\) then satisfies \(e_R(W,Z)=0\) and
\(|Z|>n/2-|W|\).
If \(|W|\le n/2-s_0\), both \(|W|\) and \(|Z|\) exceed \(s_0\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} forces \(e_R(W,Z)>0\), a contradiction.
If instead \(|W|>n/2-s_0\), then \(|L\setminus W|<s_0\) and
\(|N_B(Z)|\le|L\setminus W|\), while \(|Z|>|L\setminus W|\).
Every \(z\in Z\) has its \(\delta_0/3\) neighbours in \(L\setminus W\), so
\(|Z|>\delta_0/3\).
If \(|Z|\ge s_0\), then both sides of the empty cut \((W,Z)\) have size at least
\(s_0\) (using \(s_0\le n/4\), so \(|W|>n/2-s_0\ge s_0\)), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} yields \(e_R(W,Z)>0\).
If \(|Z|<s_0\), then \(Z\) itself is a Hall violator of size at most \(s_0\), which
the previous paragraph forbids.
Therefore no Hall violator exists, and \(B\) has a perfect matching.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matching peel in the rate-\(1/3\) residual]
\label{thm:matching-peel}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and let
\((G,G^*_0)\) and \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\) be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) a sufficiently small absolute
constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\) the residual \(R\) contains an \(m\)-regular spanning
subgraph, and writing \(\Omega_m(R)\) for the set of all such subgraphs one may sample
\(H\in\Omega_m(R)\) so that \(G_m:=G\cup H\) is exactly \((d+m)\)-regular and
\(G\subset G_m\subset G^*_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Start with \(R_0:=R\) and \(G^{(0)}:=G\).
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} one has
\(\delta(R_0)\ge\delta_0=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} apply to \(R_0\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching} yields a perfect matching \(M_1\subset E(R_0)\).
Set \(G^{(1)}:=G\cup M_1\) and \(R_1:=G^*_0\setminus G^{(1)}\).
Iterate: after \(i<m\) steps, \(G^{(i)}\) is exactly \((d+i)\)-regular,
\(R_i=G^*_0\setminus G^{(i)}\), and \(\delta(R_i)\ge\delta_0-i\ge\delta_0/2\) provided
\(c\) is small enough that \(m\le\delta_0/2\).
The proofs of Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} apply
verbatim to \(R_i\) upon replacing \(G\) by \(G^{(i)}\) and \(\delta_0\) by
\(\delta_0/2\): the \(G^{(i)}\)-cuts concentrate about
\((d+i)|W||Z|/n\le(d+m)|W||Z|/n\), while \(G^*_0\)-cuts concentrate about
\((1+\varepsilon_0)d|W||Z|/n\), so the residual cut mean remains
at least \((\varepsilon_0 d-m)|W||Z|/n\ge\delta_0|W||Z|/(2n)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching} therefore supplies a perfect matching
\(M_{i+1}\) of \(R_i\).
After \(m\) steps the union \(H_\star:=\bigcup_{i=1}^m M_i\) is an \(m\)-regular spanning
subgraph of \(R\), so \(\Omega_m(R)\neq\emptyset\).
Sampling \(H\) uniformly from \(\Omega_m(R)\) and setting \(G_m:=G\cup H\) yields
the claim.
For \(\omega(\log n)=d<(\log n)^{5/2}\) the Chernoff-scale nesting
\(G(n,d)\subset G(n,d+m)\) is already given by Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains},
which does not use this peel. At \(d=\Theta(\log n)\) the same corollary does not
apply: the perfect-matching variance \(1/(6d^3)\) makes each \(+1\) step fail with
Chebyshev probability of order \(1/d\), and a block of length \(\Theta(d)\) does not
survive the union bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact Chernoff-scale regular container]
\label{cor:chernoff-container}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}, there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with an exactly \((d+m)\)-regular graph \(G_m\) on \([n]\) such that
\(G\subset G_m\subset G^*_0\) and \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gap to exact Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}]
\label{rem:peel-gap}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container} produces an exact \((d+m)\)-regular one-sided
container at Chernoff scale \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\), nested between \(G\) and the
rate-\(1/3\) ER upper graph \(G^*_0\).
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} asks for total variation between this container
and \(G(n,d+m)\). Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} explains why the free decomposition
of~\cite{CFG25} does not transfer onto \(G^*_0\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction: container identification implies Chernoff increments]
\label{thm:id-implies-Q}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
Write \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) for the law of the graph \(G_m\) produced by
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}, and \(\mu_{d+m}\) for the law of
\(G(n,d+m)\).
If \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\), then there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with \(H\sim G(n,d+m)\) such that \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container} supplies a coupling with
\(G\subset G_m\) a.a.s.\ and \(G_m\sim\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\).
A maximal TV coupling of \(G_m\) with \(H\sim\mu_{d+m}\) satisfies
\(\PP(G_m\neq H)=\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
On the joint success event \(\{G\subset G_m\}\cap\{G_m=H\}\) one has \(G\subset H\), and
this event has probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Perfect-matching count in the residual]
\label{lem:pm-count}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching}, writing \(\Phi(R)\) for the
number of perfect matchings of \(R\), one has
\[
\Bigl(\frac{\delta_0}{10e}\Bigr)^{n/2}
\le\Phi(R)
\le\bigl(4\delta_0\bigr)^{n/2}
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from \(\Delta(R)\le 4\delta_0\): there are at most
\((4\delta_0)^{n/2}\) ways to choose partners sequentially for an ordered
partition into pairs.
For the lower bound, form the bipartite graph \(B\) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching}; on that lemma's success event one has
\(\delta(B)\ge\delta_0/3\).
Peeling \(\delta':=\lfloor\delta_0/6\rfloor\) perfect matchings from \(B\) by the same
Hall argument (minimum degree remains at least \(\delta_0/6\) throughout) yields a
\(\delta'\)-regular spanning subgraph \(B'\subset B\).
The biadjacency matrix \(A\) of \(B'\) satisfies that \(A/\delta'\) is doubly
stochastic, so the van der Waerden permanent bound gives
\[
\Phi(B')
=\mathrm{per}(A)
\ge(\delta')^{n/2}\cdot\frac{(n/2)!}{(n/2)^{n/2}}
\ge\Bigl(\frac{\delta'}{e}\Bigr)^{n/2}.
\]
Every perfect matching of \(B'\) is a perfect matching of \(R\), and
\(\delta'\ge\delta_0/10\) for large \(n\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Switching toward identification]
\label{rem:switching}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pm-count} shows that each peel step samples from an
exponentially large pool of perfect matchings.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Host has room for \(G(n,d+m)\)]
\label{lem:host-room}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\), \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c\) small, and let
\(G^*_0\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\bigr)\) with \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Then \(\varepsilon':=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/(d+m)-1\) satisfies
\(\varepsilon'\ge C_3'(\log n/(d+m))^{1/3}\) for an absolute \(C_3'>0\) and large \(n\).
Thus \(G^*_0\) has the marginal density of an upper sandwich graph at degree \(d+m\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} records that this does not place a uniform copy of
\(G(n,d+m)\) inside the already sampled graph \(G^*_0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compute
\[
\varepsilon'
=\frac{(1+\varepsilon_0)d}{d+m}-1
=\frac{\varepsilon_0 d-m}{d+m}
=\frac{\varepsilon_0-m/d}{1+m/d}.
\]
Since \(m/d=O\bigl(\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\bigr)=o(\varepsilon_0)\), one has
\(\varepsilon'\ge\varepsilon_0/2\) for small \(c\), and
\(\varepsilon_0/2\ge C_3'(\log n/(d+m))^{1/3}\) upon adjusting constants.
The density identity \((1+\varepsilon')(d+m)/n=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\) is the only use of
\(\varepsilon'\). Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at degree \(d+m\), applied to a
fresh binomial graph of this density, produces some coupling in which a copy of
\(G(n,d+m)\) sits inside that fresh graph. It does not alter the joint law of the
pair \((G,G^*_0)\) already built at degree \(d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-switches do not identify the container]
\label{prop:switch-barrier}
Condition on the degree event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-sharp}, and write
\(\delta:=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) and \(m\le c\sqrt{d\log n}\).
Let \(\Lambda(H)\) be the number of ordered \(4\)-tuples of distinct vertices
\((a,b,c,d)\) with \(ab,cd\in E(H)\) and \(ac,bd\in E(R)\setminus E(H)\).
In the configuration model with \(m\) stubs at each vertex, before conditioning on
the pairs lying in \(R\),
\[
\EE[\Lambda]=(1+o(1))\,m^2\delta^2.
\]
The centre \(nm^2\delta^2\) is larger by a factor \(n\).
A stub exposure changes \(\Lambda\) by at most \(O(m\delta)\). Azuma's inequality at
relative height \(\eta\to 0\) therefore has exponent
\(O(\eta^2 m\delta^2/n)\). Substituting the Chernoff scale gives
\[
\frac{m\delta^2}{n}
=
\Theta\Biggl(\frac{d^{11/6}(\log n)^{7/6}}{n}\Biggr),
\]
which tends to \(0\) for \(d=o(n^{6/11})\).
At \(d=n^{1/2}\) the count is \(n^{-1/12}(\log n)^{7/6}\).
At \(d=n^{2/7}\) it is \(n^{-10/21}(\log n)^{7/6}\).
The simplicity weight \(\exp(-O(mn\log(n/m)))\) is exponentially smaller:
\(\delta\le n\) forces \(\delta^2=o(n^2\log n)\).
Thus this \(2\)-switch count neither concentrates on the uniform simple
\(m\)-factor nor supplies \(\omega(1)\) switches per edge anywhere in the open
interval between the chain ceiling and \(\sqrt{n}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the pairing model, two fixed disjoint vertex pairs are matched with probability
\((1+O(m/n))(m/n)^2\), and a third fixed pair fails to be matched with probability
\(1-O(m/n)\).
On the sharp degree event the number of ordered crossing pairs \((a,c),(b,d)\) in \(R\)
with four distinct vertices is \((1+o(1))(n\delta)^2\): there are \((1+o(1))n\delta\)
ordered edges, and the \(O(\delta)\) edges incident to a given vertex change the count
by a relative \(O(1/n)\).
Multiplying these contributions yields
\(\EE[\Lambda]=(1+o(1))(m/n)^2(n\delta)^2=(1+o(1))m^2\delta^2\).

The ratio of \(nm^2\delta^2\) to this mean is \(n\).
For the Lipschitz bound, fix an edge \(ab\) of the pairing. A switch through \(ab\)
requires the opposite edge \(cd\) and the crossings \(ac,bd\in E(R)\), so \(c\in N_R(a)\)
and \(d\in N_R(b)\). Each of the \(O(\delta)\) choices of \(c\) has at most \(m\) partners
in the pairing, hence \(O(m\delta)\) switches touch \(ab\).
Over \(nm\) stub exposures, Azuma's inequality at deviation \(\eta\cdot\Theta(m^2\delta^2)\)
has exponent
\[
\frac{(\eta m^2\delta^2)^2}{nm\cdot O(m^2\delta^2)}
=O\Biggl(\eta^2\frac{m\delta^2}{n}\Biggr).
\]
The identities
\(m\delta^2/n=\Theta(d^{11/6}(\log n)^{7/6}/n)\),
\((n^{1/2})^{11/6}/n=n^{-1/12}\), and
\((n^{2/7})^{11/6}/n=n^{-10/21}\),
up to the factor \((\log n)^{7/6}\) from \(m\) and \(\delta\).
A single simple \(m\)-factor has pairing probability
\(\exp(-O(mn\log(n/m)))\), as in the count \((m!)^n/(mn-1)!!\).
Dividing an Azuma tail by this weight produces a useful bound only if
\(m\delta^2/n\) dominates \(mn\log(n/m)\), i.e.\ only if \(\delta^2\) dominates
\(n^2\log(n/m)\). No degree \(\delta\le n\) satisfies that comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the switching programme stops]
\label{rem:quasi-regular}
Proposition~\ref{prop:switch-barrier} is the obstruction to reading a uniform edge
law off \(2\)-switches of the residual \(m\)-factor.
The count is of order \(m^2\delta^2\), not \(nm^2\delta^2\), and the resulting
Azuma exponent is too small to pass from the pairing model to the uniform simple
factor.
On \(n^{2/7}\le d\le n^{1/2}\) a typical edge lies in \(o(1)\) such switches, so the
comparison that would identify \(\mathrm{Unif}(\Omega_m(R))\) with \(\mu_{d+m}\) does not
start.
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} blocks the shortcut through~\cite[(1.1)]{CFG25}.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} stays open.
The route past the second-moment ceiling is Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}:
the edge-linear factor cancels in the moment ratios, and the centered fourth
moment is \(O(v(d)^2)\). Carrying the same expansion to every fixed order
\(q\) gives Lemma~\ref{lem:high-moment}, and Chernoff nesting then extends to
every degree \(o(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) by Corollary~\ref{cor:near-barrier}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Contiguity preserved by a.a.s.\ conditioning]
\label{lem:cond-contiguity}
Let \(\mu_n,\nu_n\) be contiguous probability measures on a common space, and let
\(A_n\) be events with \(\mu_n(A_n)\to 1\). Then \(\nu_n(A_n)\to 1\), and the
conditionals \(\mu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\) and \(\nu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\) are contiguous.
Moreover if \(\dTV(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to 0\), then
\(\dTV\bigl(\mu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n),\nu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\bigr)\to 0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contiguity forces \(\nu_n(A_n)\to 1\). For an event \(B_n\),
\(\mu_n(B_n\mid A_n)=\mu_n(B_n\cap A_n)/\mu_n(A_n)\), so
\(\mu_n(B_n\mid A_n)\to 0\) iff \(\mu_n(B_n\cap A_n)\to 0\) iff \(\mu_n(B_n)\to 0\)
(using \(\mu_n(A_n)\to 1\)), and likewise for \(\nu_n\); contiguity of the
unconditional laws therefore passes to the conditionals.
If instead \(\dTV(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to 0\), a maximal coupling gives
\(\PP(X\neq Y)\to 0\) for \(X\sim\mu_n\), \(Y\sim\nu_n\), and restricting to
\(\{X\in A_n\}\cap\{Y\in A_n\}\) (probability \(1-o(1)\)) yields a coupling of the
conditionals with failure probability \(o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sandwich marginals do not fill a fixed host]
\label{prop:host-gap}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and \(m=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:host-room}, and let \(G^*_0\) be the upper graph of the degree-\(d\)
sandwich in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Let \(H\) be drawn from \(\mu_{d+m}\) independently of \(G^*_0\).
Then
\[
\PP(H\subset G^*_0)=\exp\bigl(-\Theta(nd\log(n/d))\bigr).
\]
In particular this probability does not tend to \(1\), so the event
\(\{K\subset G^*_0\}\) cannot be fed to Lemma~\ref{lem:cond-contiguity} in order to
condition the decomposition of~\cite[(1.1)]{CFG25} on the already sampled host.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper graph \(G^*_0\) is distributed as \(G(n,p)\) with \(p=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\),
and \(H\) has \(n(d+m)/2\) edges.
On \(d\ge C_3\log n\) one has \(\varepsilon_0\le C_3^{2/3}\), hence
\(p\le(1+C_3^{2/3})d/n\).
For \(d=o(n)\) this is at most \(1/2\) when \(n\) is large, so
\(\log(1/p)=\log(n/d)+O(1)\).
Independence gives
\[
\PP(H\subset G^*_0)
=p^{n(d+m)/2}
=\exp\bigl(-\Theta(nd\log(n/d))\bigr).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at degree \(d+m\) produces a different coupling,
in which some \((d+m)\)-regular graph sits inside a fresh binomial graph of density
\(p\). That joint law is not the product of \(\mu_{d+m}\) with the degree-\(d\) upper
graph, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cond-contiguity} requires the containment event to have
probability \(1-o(1)\) under the measure being conditioned.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the chain range still gives]
\label{rem:chain-vs-split}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains} supplies the nesting
\(G(n,D)\subset G(n,D+m)\) for Chernoff \(m\) throughout
\(\omega(\log n)=D=o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\), by iterating exact \(+1\) couplings.
The free total-variation decomposition of~\cite{CFG25} stops at \(o(n^{1/7})\), which
sits inside that range, and Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} blocks the attempt to
push it onto the rate-\(1/3\) host.
Past the chain ceiling, Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel} still produces an exact
\((d+m)\)-regular container. Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} is the missing
identification.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Host-restricted splitting]
\label{conj:config-split}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
Write \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) for the law of the graph \(G_m\) produced by
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}, and \(\mu_{d+m}\) for the law of
\(G(n,d+m)\).
Then \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}[Splitting without host]
\label{prop:split-free}
Let \(d\to\infty\), \(m\to\infty\), and \(d+m=o(n^{1/3})\).
Then the law of \(G\cup H\), where \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,m)\) are coupled to be
edge-disjoint via independent configuration models on disjoint stub sets, is
contiguous to \(\mu_{d+m}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
See~\cite{Wormald99,JLR00}: the configuration model of degree \(d+m\) is contiguous
to the union of independent configuration models of degrees \(d\) and \(m\) on
disjoint stub sets (equivalently, a random partition of the
\((d+m)\)-regular configuration into a \(d\)-factor and an \(m\)-factor), after
conditioning on simplicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Free \(m\)-extension flatness past the chain ceiling]
\label{lem:free-flat}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(d=d(n)\to\infty\), and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) fixed and \(mn\) even.
If
\[
d=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{2/5}}{(\log n)^{1/5}}\Biggr),
\]
then any two \(d\)-regular graphs \(G,G'\) on \([n]\) satisfy
\[
N_m(G)=(1+o(1))\,N_m(G'),
\]
where \(N_m(G)\) is the number of \(m\)-regular graphs on \([n]\) edge-disjoint from \(G\).
In particular the conclusion holds for a sequence \(d=\omega(n^{2/7})\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply McKay's enumeration of sparse graphs in the complement of a sparse
host~\cite[Theorem~4.6]{McK85} (recorded as Theorem~2.1 in~\cite{HLMPW25}).
Take the degree sequence \(g_i=m\) and the forbidden graph \(X=G\), which is
\(d\)-regular.
The hypotheses of that theorem hold for any fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,2/3)\) and large
\(n\): writing \(\widehat\Delta\) for McKay's degree parameter,
\[
\widehat\Delta
=2+m\Bigl(\tfrac32 m+d+1\Bigr)
=O\bigl(m(d+m)\bigr)
=o(mn),
\]
since \(d=o(n^{2/5})\) forces \(d+m=o(n)\).
The main terms \(\lambda=(m-1)/2\) and
\(\mu=md/2\) depend on \(G\) only through its degree and its number of edges, hence
are the same for every \(d\)-regular graph.
The relative error in the exponent is
\[
O\Biggl(\frac{\widehat\Delta^{\,2}}{mn}\Biggr)
=O\Biggl(\frac{m(d+m)^2}{n}\Biggr)
=O\Biggl(\frac{d^{5/2}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n}\Biggr)
=o(1)
\]
under the stated bound on \(d\), and the implied constant is uniform in the host.
Therefore
\[
N_m(G)
=
\frac{(mn)!}{(mn/2)!\,2^{mn/2}(m!)^n}
\exp\bigl(-\lambda-\lambda^2-\mu+o(1)\bigr),
\]
which is independent of the choice of \(G\) up to the factor \(1+o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Toward Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}]
\label{rem:config-split}
Proposition~\ref{prop:switch-barrier} shows that the \(2\)-switch count of a residual
\(m\)-factor is of order \(m^2(\varepsilon_0 d)^2\). That supplies \(o(1)\) switches per
edge throughout \(d=o(n^{6/11})\), which contains the open interval past
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains} up through \(o(\sqrt{n})\). The same proposition
shows that the Azuma tail does not survive conditioning on simplicity.
Lemma~\ref{lem:free-flat} makes the number of \(m\)-factors of the complement of \(G\)
independent of which \(d\)-regular graph was chosen, for every
\(d=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{1/5})\).
That complement has degree \(n-1-d\). The container in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} counts \(m\)-factors of the residual \(R\), whose
degree is of order \(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\). Flat free counts do not identify
\(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) with \(\mu_{d+m}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Chernoff increments]
\label{thm:conditional-chernoff-inc}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}.
Then under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel} there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with \(H\sim G(n,d+m)\) such that \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\), with
\(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} is the hypothesis
\(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:id-implies-Q}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Crude neighbourhood expansion]
\label{lem:crude-exp}
Let \(1/n\ll 1/C\ll\sigma\ll 1\), let \(d\ge C\log n\), and let
\(C\log n\le\delta=2m/n\le n^{\sigma}\).
Form \(K\sim G(n,d)\) and \(F=K+E\) as in~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25}, and set
\(\lambda:=C\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-20})\), every \(U\subset[n]\) with
\[
\frac{\lambda n}{100\delta}\le|U|\le\frac{n}{2\delta}
\]
satisfies
\[
\bigl|N_{F\setminus K}(U)\bigr|
=(1\pm\lambda)\,\delta|U|.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on \(K\). For each \(w\notin U\) the number of \(K\)-free pairs
\(\{w,u\}\) with \(u\in U\) is \(|U|-|N_K(w)\cap U|\).
On the event that every codegree of \(K\) is \(O(\log n)\) (probability
\(1-o(n^{-30})\) for \(d\le n^{\sigma}\)), one has
\(|N_K(w)\cap U|\le|U|/2\) whenever \(|U|\ge\lambda n/(100\delta)\) and \(C\) is large,
hence
\[
p_w:=\PP\bigl(w\in N_{F\setminus K}(U)\bigm|K\bigr)
=1-(1-p)^{|U|-|N_K(w)\cap U|}
\]
satisfies \(\tfrac12\min(1,p|U|)\le p_w\le\min(1,2p|U|)\).
For \(|U|\le n/(2\delta)\) one has \(p|U|\le 1\), so
\[
\EE\bigl(|N_{F\setminus K}(U)|\,\big|\,K\bigr)
=
\bigl(1\pm\tfrac12\lambda\bigr)\delta|U|
\]
for large \(C\).
The indicators \(\mathbf{1}_{w\sim U}\) are conditionally independent given \(K\), so
a Chernoff bound yields relative error
\[
O\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{\delta|U|}}\Bigr)
\le O\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{100\log n}{\lambda n}}\Bigr)\le\frac{\lambda}{2}
\]
with probability \(1-\exp(-\omega(\log n))\) for each fixed \(U\).
A union bound over all admissible cardinalities and choices of \(U\) costs
\(\sum_s\binom{n}{s}e^{-\omega(\log n)}=o(n^{-20})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex lower bounds at Chernoff scale]
\label{thm:path-lower-chernoff}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_L\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{BIM25} with \(\delta=2m/n\ge C_L\log n\) and
\(d\le n^{\sigma}\) for a fixed small \(\sigma>0\), set
\(\lambda:=C_L\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
With probability \(1-o(n^{-14})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\), all
\(Z\subset[n]\) with \(|Z|\le 40\log n\), and all
\(\ell\in[10\ell_0,10\log n]\) (with \(\ell_0\) as in~\cite[§6]{BIM25}), the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) with no internal
vertex in \(Z\) is at least
\[
\Bigl(1-C_L\lambda-\frac{|(Z\cup\{x\})\cap N_K(y)|}{d}\Bigr)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We adapt~\cite[Claim~6.4]{BIM25} by expanding past the I4 ceiling so that the
final connecting sets have linear size, then invoke~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25} at
a constant scale \(\mu_0\).

Fix \(\mu_0:=10^{-4}\) and \(S_A:=\lambda n/(100\delta)\).
Properties I1--I5 of~\cite[§6]{BIM25} hold at our \(\lambda\) by the same proofs
as there: the degree event G1 of~\cite[Lemma~5.3]{BIM25} requires only
\(\lambda\ge C\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\), and the small-set edge/codegree events I3--I5
are applied only to sets of size at most \(S_A\), which sits inside the I4 range.

Phase~A.
Run the inductive expansion of~\cite[Claim~6.4]{BIM25} until both sides have
cardinality at least \(S_A\), stopping at the first layer index \(i_A\) with
\(|X_{2i_A}|,|Y_{2i_A}|\ge S_A\).
The same relative-error bookkeeping as in that claim yields
\[
|X_{2i_A}|,|Y_{2i_A}|=(1\pm C\lambda)\,d^{i_A}\delta^{i_A}r
\]
with \(r\) the Claim~6.4 seed size (chosen so the early layers remain inside the
I4 range).

Phase~B.
While the current set \(X\) satisfies \(|X|<\mu_0 n/\delta\), replace \(X\) by
\(N_{F\setminus K}(X)\) (discarding the \(O(|X|+|Y|+|W|)\) used vertices) and then
by a large subset of the corresponding \(K\)-neighbourhood, exactly as in
Claim~6.4.
Lemma~\ref{lem:crude-exp} supplies the \(F\setminus K\) step to relative error
\(\lambda\); the \(K\)-step is \((1\pm\lambda)\,d\) by \(d\)-regularity and the codegree
bounds already recorded in I3--I5 for the current scale.
Each full step multiplies cardinality by \((1\pm C\lambda)\delta d\).

For the final step, the current set \(X\) may satisfy
\(\delta|X|>\mu_0 n\). In that case downsample: pass to a subset
\(X'\subset X\) with \(|X'|=\lfloor\mu_0 n/\delta\rfloor\), chosen so that every
vertex of \(X\) retains a \((1\pm\lambda)\)-proportion of the incoming layered paths
from the seeds (possible by degree regularity of the preceding layer).
Lemma~\ref{lem:crude-exp} then yields
\[
\bigl|N_{F\setminus K}(X')\bigr|=(1\pm\lambda)\mu_0 n,
\]
and likewise on the \(Y\)-side. One further \(K\)-step, followed if needed by a
\((1\pm\lambda)\)-proportion downsample, produces connecting sets
\(X_\star,Y_\star\) with \(|X_\star|,|Y_\star|=\mu_0 n\) and accumulated branching
factor \((1\pm C_L\lambda)\,d^{i_B}\delta^{i_B}r\).
(The downsample ratios are \(\Theta(1)\) only relative to the immediately
preceding layer; because that layer is itself \((1\pm C\lambda)\)-regular with
respect to path counts, no fixed-factor loss appears in the final
\((1\pm C_L\lambda)\) budget.)

Phase~C.
Apply~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25} with \(\mu=\mu_0\). The hypothesis
\(\lambda^2\mu_0\delta=\Omega(\log n)\) holds because \(\lambda^2\delta=C_L^2\log n\).
Hence
\[
e_{F\setminus K}(X_\star,Y_\star)=(1\pm 3\lambda)\frac{\delta}{n}|X_\star||Y_\star|
=(1\pm 3\lambda)\mu_0^2\,\delta n.
\]
Each such edge closes a unique alternating path of the prescribed length from the
seed sets, exactly as in Claim~6.4. Multiplying by the Phase~A/B branching
factors and summing over the sliding windows of~\cite[§6.5]{BIM25} produces the
stated I7 lower bound at error \(C_L\lambda\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex path upper bound at scale \(1/4\)]
\label{thm:U}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_U\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25} with \(\delta=2m/n\ge C_U\log n\), with
probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\) and all
\(\ell\in[\tfrac12\log n,\,10\log n]\), the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) is at most
\[
\Bigl(1+C_U\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\delta}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\lambda:=C_L\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) be the error of
Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}, and run the truncation argument
of~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25} (Claims~7.2--7.3) with this \(\lambda\) in place of the
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\) lower-bound error from~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{BIM25}.
That argument forces \(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\lambda})\) for the two-vertex upper
error; substituting the Chernoff-scale \(\lambda\) yields
\[
\eta=\Omega\Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac{\log n}{\delta}\bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr),
\]
as required. (The same conclusion is available from~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25}
whenever \(\delta\ge n^{\sigma}\).)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/5\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)]
\label{thm:quant-fifth}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_5\) such that whenever \(d\ge C_5\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_5\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/5},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(\varepsilon=C_5(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) with \(C_5\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\), so \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\) satisfies
\(\delta_R\ge C_U\log n\) for large \(C_5\).
Run the deletion-process reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25} with absolute \(C_0\),
\(\mu=\varepsilon/100\), and
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\Bigl(\frac{n\log n}{m}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr\}.
\]
At \(m\ge R\) one has \((n\log n/m)^{1/4}=O(\varepsilon)\), so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:U} supplies the two-vertex upper bound at error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), while Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} supplies
the matching lower bound at the stronger error \(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\)
(hence also at \((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\)).
The hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} therefore hold, and the B2 estimate
reduces to \(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) together with
\(C_0(\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/4}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of which hold at our
\(\varepsilon\).
The lower sandwich is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex path upper bound at Chernoff scale]
\label{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_P\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}, set
\(\lambda:=C_P\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
With probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\) and all
\[
\ell\in\bigl[\max\{10\ell_0,\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil+1\},\,10\log n\bigr],
\]
the number of \((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) is at
most
\[
(1+C_P\lambda)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu:=d^\ell\delta^\ell/n\) and \(\mu':=d^{\ell-1}\delta^{\ell-1}/n\), so
\(\mu=(d\delta)\mu'\).
Fix distinct \(x,y\) and a length \(\ell\) in the stated range, and condition on
\(K\).
An \((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-path of length \(2\ell\) has the form
\[
x=v_0\overset{F\setminus K}{\longrightarrow}v_1\overset{K}{\longrightarrow}
\cdots\overset{F\setminus K}{\longrightarrow}v_{2\ell-1}\overset{K}{\longrightarrow}y,
\]
so \(z:=v_{2\ell-1}\in N_K(y)\).
Let \(Z:=N_K(y)\cup\{y\}\) and, for each \(w\notin Z\), let \(M(w)\) be the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,w\)-paths of length \(2\ell-2\) with no internal
vertex in \(Z\).
Set \(S:=\sum_{w\notin Z}M(w)\).

Since \(\ell-1\ge\tfrac12\log n\), Theorem~\ref{thm:U} supplies a pointwise bound
\(M_{\mathrm{tot}}(w)\le(1+\eta)\mu'\) on the \emph{unrestricted} path counts of
length \(2\ell-2\), with \(\eta:=C_U(\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), on an event of
probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\).
A fortiori \(M(w)\le(1+\eta)\mu'\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-vertex-chernoff} likewise yields
\(S\le(1+30\lambda_1)(d\delta)^{\ell-1}\) with
\(\lambda_1:=C_1\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
Work henceforth on the intersection of these events.

The multiplicities \(M(w)\) depend only on edges with both ends outside \(Z\),
hence are independent of the cut edges
\(\{wz:w\notin Z,\,z\in N_K(y)\}\).
Writing \(X_e\) for the Bernoulli indicators of those cut edges,
\[
Y_{\mathrm{clean}}
:=\sum_{z\in N_K(y)}\sum_{\substack{w\notin Z\\ wz\notin E(K)}}M(w)\,X_{wz}
\]
is, conditionally on \(M\) and \(K\), a weighted sum of independent Bernoullis.
Every \(x,y\)-path of length \(2\ell\) that avoids \(Z\) until its penultimate vertex
is counted by \(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\).
Paths that visit \(Z\) earlier are handled by a first-visit decomposition.
Write \(J_0:=\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil\).
For a first visit to some \(z'\in Z\) at length \(2j\):
\begin{itemize}
\item if \(j\ge J_0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:U} gives
\(P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+\eta)\mu_j\), hence
\[
\sum_{z'\in Z}P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+\eta)|Z|\mu_j;
\]
\item if \(j<J_0\), the same bound
\(\sum_{z'\in Z}P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+C\lambda)|Z|\mu_j\)
follows by applying the last-layer Bernoulli argument of the present proof
to the shorter length \(2j\) (here the ``target set'' is the whole of \(Z\),
of size \(d+1\), and the one-vertex mass at length \(2j-2\) is controlled by
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-vertex-chernoff}; no appeal to Theorem~\ref{thm:U} is
required because the jump size is \(O((d\delta)^{j-1})\) while the mean is
\(|Z|\mu_j=\Theta(d(d\delta)^j/n)\), and Bernstein still yields relative error
\(O(\lambda)\) uniformly over the \(O(\log n)\) short lengths).
\end{itemize}
In all cases the continuation from \(z'\) to \(y\) of length \(2(\ell-j)\) is at most
\((1+\eta)\mu_{\ell-j}\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:U} (since \(\ell-j\ge J_0\) for
\(\ell\ge J_0+1\) and \(j\le\ell-J_0\)).
Therefore the dirty contribution is at most
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}(1+O(\eta))|Z|\mu_j\mu_{\ell-j}
\le(d+1)\ell(1+O(\eta))\frac{\mu}{n}
=O\Bigl(\frac{d\log n}{n}\Bigr)\mu
=o(\lambda)\mu.
\]
Thus the full path count \(Y\) satisfies \(Y\le Y_{\mathrm{clean}}+o(\lambda)\mu\).

Conditionally,
\[
\EE[Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K]
\le p\,d\,S
\le\frac{\delta d}{n-d-1}\,S
\le(1+C\lambda)\mu,
\]
and
\begin{align*}
\Var(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K)
&\le p\,d\sum_w M(w)^2
\le p\,d\,(\max M)\,S
\le(1+O(\eta))\frac{\mu^2}{d\delta}.
\end{align*}
Each summand changes \(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\) by at most
\((1+\eta)\mu'=(1+\eta)\mu/(d\delta)\).
Bernstein's inequality therefore yields, for \(t:=C_P\lambda\mu/2\),
\[
\PP\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}>\EE[Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K]+t\bigm|M,K\bigr)
\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(d\log n)\bigr)=o(n^{-20}),
\]
since \(t^2/(\Var+Mt)=\Omega(\lambda^2 d\delta)=\Omega(d\log n)\) and
\(Mt/\Var=O(\lambda)\ll 1\).
Hence \(Y\le(1+C_P\lambda)\mu\) with room to spare.
A union bound over \(x,y,\ell\) completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why \(L^{2}\) stops at \(1/4\) but Bernstein reaches \(1/2\)]
\label{rem:second-moment}
A global second-moment computation on \(Y\) yields only
\(\Var(Y)=O(\lambda\mu^2)\), hence relative standard deviation
\(\Theta(\sqrt{\lambda})=(\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), matching the truncation
scale of Theorem~\ref{thm:U}.
The last-layer decomposition of Theorem~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff} replaces
that global \(L^{2}\) proxy by a \emph{conditional} Bernoulli variance of order
\(\mu^2/(d\delta)\), for which Bernstein's inequality is exp-sharp at the
Chernoff scale \(\lambda=\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
The only external input is a coarse \(\ell_\infty\) bound on the incoming
multiplicities, supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:U} at the weaker error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}]
Set \(\varepsilon=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) with \(C_3\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\), so \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\) satisfies
\(\delta_R\ge C_P\log n\).
Run the deletion-process reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25} with absolute \(C_0\),
\(\mu=\varepsilon/100\), and
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\Bigl(\frac{n\log n}{m}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr\}.
\]
At \(m\ge R\) one has \((n\log n/m)^{1/2}=O(\varepsilon)\), so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
supply two-sided path counts at error \(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) for every length
in the nonempty range
\(\bigl[\max\{10\ell_0,\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil+1\},10\log n\bigr]\).
Fixing any such length, the hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} hold, and
the B2 estimate reduces to \(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) together with
\(C_0(\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/2}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of which hold at our
\(\varepsilon\).
The lower inclusion is again~\cite[Lemma~2.2]{BIM25}, at the same \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:global-inc}]
Identical to Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}, using Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}
in place of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}, and \(k_3=3C_3 d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\) in place of \(k_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Hypothesis~P resolved]
\label{rem:hyp-P}
Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
together give two-sided two-vertex path counts at
\(\lambda=\Theta\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) throughout the length window needed by
~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25}, which is Hypothesis~P.
The deletion-process~/~B2 architecture nevertheless cannot beat rate \(1/3\)
(cf.\ Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}); Chernoff-scale sandwiching remains
Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[B2 excess at the Chernoff rate]
\label{prop:b2-excess}
In the sum of Section~3.5.5 of~\cite{BIM25}, replace the path error
\((n\log n/j)^{1/8}\) by \((n\log n/j)^{1/2}\), as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}.
At \(\varepsilon=(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) the resulting expectation is of order \(R\).
At \(\varepsilon=(\log n/d)^{1/2}\) it exceeds \(R\) by the factor \((d/\log n)^{1/4}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The expectation is of order \(dn\,(\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/2}\), while
\(R=\Theta(\varepsilon dn)\), so the ratio is \(\Theta\bigl((\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/2}/\varepsilon\bigr)\).
Substituting \(\varepsilon=(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) gives the value \(1\).
Substituting \(\varepsilon=(\log n/d)^{1/2}\) gives \((d/\log n)^{1/4}\).
Both identities were checked symbolically.
\end{proof}
