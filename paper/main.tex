\begin{abstract}
Gao proved that the random regular graphs \(\Gnd\) and \(G(n,d+1)\) can be coupled
so that \(\Gnd\subset G(n,d+1)\) with high probability whenever \(d\to\infty\) and
\(d=o(n^{1/3})\), and observed that the same argument should extend to
\(d=o(n^{1/2})\) via the sharper conditional edge probabilities of~\cite{Gao20},
leaving the details for a simpler exposition.
We supply those details, obtaining the range \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).

As a new consequence of the same error schedule, we prove long simultaneous
nesting chains. The length is the Chebyshev optimum for the patched relative
variance of perfect-matching counts. After the odd part of the Laplace expansion
is removed (Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace}), that variance is of order
\(D^{-3}+D^2/n\), so the window has length
\(L=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}).
The term \(1/(6D^3)\) alone forces \(L=o(D)\). Chernoff-scale windows
\(L=\Theta\sqrt{D\log n}\) are then available for
\(\omega(\log n)=D=o\bigl(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7}\bigr)\)
(Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains}).

Past \(\sqrt{n}\), exact \(+1\) is blocked by an enumeration barrier.
Tracking the error budget in the Kim--Vu sandwich proof of~\cite{BIM25} with
absolute switching constants, we obtain the vanishing rates
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) in general,
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) via linear connecting sets, and the
process-optimal \(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\), by a
last-layer Bernstein upgrade of two-vertex path upper bounds to Chernoff scale.
The Chernoff floor \(\varepsilon\asymp\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) requires a different
architecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}).
We prove that the process conjecture of~\cite{BIM25} implies Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q},
that for \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) the Chernoff problem reduces to a final-window
coupling through surplus degrees
\(\sqrt{d\log n}\le\delta\le d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), and that a hybrid
thinning of the \(1/3\)-sandwich residual already yields a Chernoff-scale
supergraph with the correct edge marginals (Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid}), and that
peeling \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\) perfect matchings from the residual produces an
exact \((d+m)\)-regular one-sided container inside the rate-\(1/3\) ER upper graph
(Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}, Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}), and that
host-restricted identification of that container with \(G(n,d+m)\) is open
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}). A sandwich coupling at degree \(d+m\)
does not place a uniform copy of \(G(n,d+m)\) inside the upper graph already
built at degree \(d\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Let \(\Gnd\) denote the uniform random \(d\)-regular graph on \(n\) labelled vertices
(with \(dn\) even). Comparing \(\Gnd\) across degrees is subtle because there is no
canonical monotone coupling.
Gao, Isaev and McKay conjectured that for all admissible \(d_1\le d_2\)
(excluding two parity pathologies), there is a coupling with
\(\PP(G(n,d_1)\subset G(n,d_2))=1-o(1)\).
The critical case is \(d_2=d_1+1\).

Gao~\cite{Gao21} proved exact \(+1\) nesting for \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/3})\),
by coupling through perfect matchings and controlling the number \(Y\) of perfect
matchings via an \(O(d^3/n)\) subgraph-probability error.
The same paper states explicitly that Theorem~8 should extend to all
\(d\to\infty\) with \(d=o(n^{1/2})\) using the sharper conditional edge probabilities
of~\cite{Gao20}, but leaves the details for a simpler exposition
(see~\cite[Remark~16(b), §5]{Gao21}).
We write those details out.
As a new consequence of the same patched error schedule, we obtain long
simultaneous nesting chains (Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}). The window length is
\(o(v(D)^{-1/3})\), where \(v(D)\) is the patched relative variance of the
perfect-matching count. This reaches degrees up to \(o(\sqrt{n})\),
with Chernoff-scale increments up to \(o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\).

Independently, the Kim--Vu sandwich conjecture---coupling \(\Gnd\) between two
binomial graphs of density \((1\pm\varepsilon)d/n\)---was proved in full by
Behague, Il'kovi\v{c} and Montgomery~\cite{BIM25} for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
That yields constant-factor degree gaps, but not \(+1\).
We formulate the Chernoff-optimal quantitative refinement
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}) as the natural enabling theorem past \(\sqrt{n}\),
and extract from~\cite{BIM25} the vanishing rates
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) in general
(Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}), \(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) for all
\(d\ge C\log n\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-fifth}), and the process-optimal
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}), via Chernoff-scale two-sided path
counts (Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}).
For \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) the same rate \(1/3\) was already available from
~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25} (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}).
As structural progress toward Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}, we prove that the process
conjecture of~\cite{BIM25} implies the Chernoff sandwich
(Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q}), and that the Chernoff problem reduces to a
final surplus-degree window of width \(d^{2/3}\to d^{1/2}\) for every
\(d\ge C\log n\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}).
Peeling perfect matchings from the rate-\(1/3\) residual then yields an exact
\((d+m)\)-regular container at Chernoff scale \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}).
Free \(m\)-extensions of a \(d\)-regular graph are flat up to a \(1+o(1)\) factor
for every \(d=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{1/5})\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:free-flat}).
Identifying the host-restricted container with \(G(n,d+m)\) remains open
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}); Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} records why
conditioning on the degree-\(d\) upper graph does not inherit a total-variation
decomposition.

\subsection{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Main]
\label{thm:main}
Assume the conditional edge-probability estimate of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d=d(n)\) satisfy \(d\to\infty\) and
\(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) such that
\[
\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The hypothesis on~\cite{Gao20} is used as a black box, exactly as anticipated
in~\cite{Gao21}.
All other steps are either elementary or inherited from~\cite{Gao21} with a
tracked error substitution \(d^3/n\mapsto d^2/n\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Long simultaneous chains}

\begin{theorem}[Long \(+1\) chains]
\label{thm:chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D=D(n)\), \(L=L(n)\) be positive integers with \(Dn\) even.
Write
\[
v(D)
:=
D^{-3}+\frac{D^2}{n}.
\]
If \(D\to\infty\), \(D=o(n^{1/2})\), \(L\to\infty\), and \(L=o\bigl(v(D)^{-1/3}\bigr)\),
then there exists a coupling \((G_d)_{d=D}^{D+L}\) such that each \(G_d\sim G(n,d)\) and
\[
\PP\bigl(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Longest second-moment window]
\label{cor:mega}
At \(D=\lfloor n^{1/5}\rfloor\) (with parity arranged) one has
\(v(D)=\Theta(n^{-3/5})\), so Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} supplies a simultaneous
exact nesting of length \(L=o(n^{1/5})\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Chernoff-length chains up to \(n^{2/7}\)]
\label{cor:chernoff-chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy
\[
\frac{D}{\log n}\to\infty,
\qquad
D=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{2/7}}{(\log n)^{3/7}}\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) a small absolute constant
(and parity arranged so that \(mn\) is even).
Then there exists a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v(D)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}. Then
\[
v(D)^{1/3}
\le
2\max\Biggl\{
D^{-1},\;
\Bigl(\frac{D^2}{n}\Bigr)^{1/3}
\Biggr\},
\]
up to adjusting the absolute constant inside the \(O\)-notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, which is absorbed into the little-o below.
Hence
\[
m\,v(D)^{1/3}
=
O\Biggl(
\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{D}}
+
\frac{D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/3}}
\Biggr).
\]
The hypothesis \(D/\log n\to\infty\) kills the first summand.
Under \(D=o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\) one has
\(D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}/n^{1/3}=o(1)\), so the second summand vanishes as well.
The log power is sharp: substituting \(D=n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7}\) produces
\[
\frac{D^{7/6}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/3}}
=
1,
\]
because \((2/7)\cdot(7/6)=1/3\) and \((3/7)\cdot(7/6)=1/2\).
A big-O bound at that threshold leaves a constant Chebyshev cost and the union bound does not tend to zero.
Thus \(m=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\). Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} with \(L:=m\) yields the chain
\(G_D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+m}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fourth-moment chains]
\label{thm:fourth-chains}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}.
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(D/\log n\to\infty\) and
\[
D=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{4/13}}{(\log n)^{5/13}}\Biggr).
\]
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{D\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) small and \(mn\) even.
Assume that the perfect-matching count \(Y\) in \(G(n,d)\) satisfies
\[
\EE\bigl[(Y-\EE Y)^4\bigr]
\le
K\,(\Var Y)^2
\]
for an absolute constant \(K\), at every degree \(d\in[D,D+m]\).
Then there is a coupling of \(G\sim G(n,D)\) with \(H\sim G(n,D+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v(d)=d^{-3}+d^2/n\) and \(\alpha:=v(d)^{2/5}\).
The fourth-moment hypothesis and Markov's inequality give
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
\le
K\frac{(\Var Y)^2}{\alpha^4(\EE Y)^4}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} supplies \(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(v(d))\) for \(d=o(n^{1/2})\),
and the stated range is inside that hypothesis, so the right-hand side is
\(O(v(d)^2/\alpha^4)\).
The choice \(\alpha=v(d)^{2/5}\) makes \(\alpha^4=v(d)^{8/5}\) and
\(v(d)^2/v(d)^{8/5}=v(d)^{2/5}\), hence the probability is \(O(v(d)^{2/5})\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} therefore bounds the one-step coupling failure by
\(O(\alpha+v(d)^{2/5})=O(v(d)^{2/5})\), once \(n\) is large enough that \(\alpha<1/2\).
The Chernoff length satisfies \(m=o(D)\) throughout the stated range, so
\(d\in[D,D+m]\) obeys \(v(d)\le 4v(D)\).
Thus each step fails with probability \(O(v(D)^{2/5})\).
The product of the length with this cost is
\[
m\,v(D)^{2/5}
=O\Biggl(
\frac{(\log n)^{1/2}}{D^{7/10}}
+
\frac{D^{13/10}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{2/5}}
\Biggr).
\]
The first summand tends to \(0\) because \(D/\log n\to\infty\).
For the second, the threshold substitution \(D=n^{4/13}/(\log n)^{5/13}\) produces
\[
\frac{D^{13/10}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{2/5}}
=1,
\]
because \((4/13)\cdot(13/10)=2/5\) and \((5/13)\cdot(13/10)=1/2\).
A little-o hypothesis makes the product \(o(1)\). A union bound over the \(m\) steps
yields the chain.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Secondary and conditional results}

\begin{theorem}[TV-controlled nesting]
\label{thm:approx}
Let \(3\le d\le n/2-1\) and write \(\mu_d\) for the law of \(\Gnd\).
Let \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\) be the law of \(G\cup M\) where \(G\sim\mu_d\) and \(M\) is
uniform among perfect matchings of \(K_n\setminus G\)
(every such complement has degree \(\ge n/2\), hence a perfect matching by Dirac's
theorem).
If \(\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le\delta\), then there is a coupling with
\(H\sim\mu_{d+1}\) and \(\PP(G\subset H)\ge 1-\delta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Square-root window]
\label{cor:sqrt}
Under the patched variance estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, for all sufficiently
small absolute constants \(c>0\) and all large \(n\), if \(d\le c\sqrt{n}\) then
\(\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le 1/2\), hence a coupling with
\(\PP(G\subset H)\ge 1/2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Sandwich degree defect]
\label{thm:defect}
For each fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) there exists \(C=C(\varepsilon)\) such that if
\(d\ge C\log n\), then there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\[
\PP\bigl(\Delta(G\setminus H)\le 3\varepsilon d\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative sandwich at rate \(1/9\)]
\label{thm:quant}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_*\) such that the following holds.
Whenever \(d\ge C_*\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_*\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/9},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) with
\[
G_*\sim G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),\quad
G\sim\Gnd,\quad
G^*\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),
\]
and \(\PP(G_*\subset G\subset G^*)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Medium increments and defects]
\label{cor:quant-nest}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}, writing
\(k_0:=3C_*\,d^{8/9}(\log n)^{1/9}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item if \(k\ge k_0\) then there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and
\(H\sim G(n,d+k)\) with \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\);
\item there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\(\PP\bigl(\Delta(G\setminus H)\le k_0\bigr)=1-o(1)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/3\) for polynomial degree]
\label{thm:quant-third}
For each fixed \(\sigma\in(0,1)\) there exists \(C_{**}=C_{**}(\sigma)\) such that the following
holds.
Whenever \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_{**}\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
Moreover, the exponent \(1/3\) is optimal for the deletion-process~/~B2
architecture of~\cite[§3]{BIM25}: no smaller exponent is possible without
changing that coupling scheme.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/3\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)]
\label{thm:quant-third-global}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_3\) such that whenever
\(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_3\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Global increments at rate \(1/3\)]
\label{cor:global-inc}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}, writing
\(k_3:=3C_3\,d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), the conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest} hold with \(k_0\) replaced by \(k_3\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial increments]
\label{cor:poly-inc}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}, writing
\(k_1:=3C_{**}\,d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), the conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest} hold with \(k_0\) replaced by \(k_1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Chernoff floor]
\label{prop:chernoff}
Let \(d\ge 2\log n\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\).
If \(\varepsilon\le c\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) for a sufficiently small absolute constant \(c>0\),
then \(\PP\bigl(\Delta(G(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n))>d\bigr)\not\to 0\).
In particular, no coupling can realise
\(G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\subseteq\Gnd\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[Quantitative sandwich]
\label{conj:Q}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that whenever \(d\ge C_0\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C_0\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\), there is a coupling
\((G_*,G,G^*)\) with
\[
G_*\sim G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),\quad
G\sim\Gnd,\quad
G^*\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr),
\]
and \(\PP(G_*\subset G\subset G^*)=1-o(1)\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional increments]
\label{thm:Q1}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
If \(d\ge C_0\log n\) and \(k\ge 3C_0\sqrt{d\log n}\), then there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,d+k)\) with \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Process conjecture implies Chernoff sandwich]
\label{thm:process-to-Q}
Assume~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds: for every \(d\ge C\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large absolute, there is a coupling
\((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Final-window reduction]
\label{thm:window}
Suppose that whenever \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\[
C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{d}}
\le\varepsilon
\le C_3\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
the following holds: if \((G,G^*_0)\) is a coupling furnished by
Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate \(\varepsilon_0:=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\)
(so \(G\subset G^*_0\) a.a.s.\ with \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\)), then there
exists a coupling of a graph \(H\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) with
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
Assume the symmetric lower-window statement for \(G_*\).
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds for all \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
\end{theorem}

\section{Patched subgraph probabilities}
\label{sec:patch}

Let \(\rho(n,d,\alpha)\) and \(\varphi(d,\alpha)\) denote the main term and phase from
Gao~\cite[Corollary~15]{Gao21}.

\begin{lemma}[Patched Corollary~15]
\label{lem:cor15}
Let \(0\le k\le n/2\) and let \(H\) be a graph on \([n]\) consisting of \(k\) isolated edges
together with a collection of vertex-disjoint cycles covering the remaining
\(n-2k\) vertices.
Set \(\alpha=2k/n\).
If \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), then
\[
\PP\bigl(H\subseteq\Gnd\bigr)=(1+O(d^2/n))\,\rho(n,d,\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Order \(E(H)=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)\) with \(m=|E(H)|\le n\).
Then
\[
\PP(H\subseteq\Gnd)
=\prod_{i=1}^{m}
\PP\bigl(e_i\in\Gnd\bigm|\{e_1,\ldots,e_{i-1}\}\subseteq\Gnd\bigr).
\]
Apply~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} at each step.
For \(d\)-regular degree sequences one has \(M=dn\) and \(\Delta=d\), so the relative
error per step is \(O(\Delta^5 n/M^3)=O(d^2/n^2)\).
Over \(m\le n\) steps,
\[
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr)^{O(n)}=1+O(d^2/n),
\]
using \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).

Write \(P_{\mathrm{seq}}\) for the product of the principal terms in Theorem~6, and
\(P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\) for the error-free McKay principal ratio appearing
in~\cite[proof of Corollary~15]{Gao21}.
Both approximate \(\PP(H\subseteq\Gnd)\), to within \(1+O(d^2/n)\) and \(1+O(d^3/n)\)
respectively~\cite[Remark~8]{Gao20}.
Hence \(P_{\mathrm{seq}}=(1+O(d^2/n))P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\).
Defining \(\rho:=P_{\mathrm{McKay}}\) yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Laplace window for intersection size]
\label{lem:laplace}
Let \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). Let \((a_k)\) be a positive sequence whose
consecutive ratios satisfy, for \(1\le k\le n/3\),
\[
\frac{a_k}{a_{k-1}}
=
\frac{(d-2)n+2k}{2(d-1)^2 k}\,(1+O(1/n)),
\]
and \(a_k/a_{k-1}\le 1/2\) for \(k>n/3\). Set \(\bar k=n/(2d)\), \(m=\lfloor\bar k\rfloor\), and
\[
\beta
:=
1-\frac1{(d-1)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_k a_k
=
a_m\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\bar k}{\beta}}\,\bigl(1+O(d/n)\bigr).
\]
The prefactor admits the expansion
\(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\).
It depends only on \(d\), and it is part of the main-term expansion in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21},
not a truncation error.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(r_0(k)=((d-2)n+2k)/(2(d-1)^2 k)\) and \(a=(d-1)^2\). The identity \(r_0(\bar k)=1\) rearranges to
\[
r_0(\bar k+t)
=
\frac{\bar k+t/a}{\bar k+t}.
\]
Set \(\sigma=\sqrt{\bar k}\) and \(\lambda=\log n\), and write \(\delta=k-m\).
The window \(|\delta|\le\lambda\sigma\) lies inside \(k\le n/3\) for large \(n\), because
\(\lambda\sigma=o(\bar k)\).

On this window, \(u=t/\bar k\) satisfies \(|u|\le 2\lambda/\sigma\to 0\). For \(|u|<1\) and \(|u/a|<1\),
\[
\log\Bigl(1+\frac{u}{a}\Bigr)-\log(1+u)
=
\sum_{j\ge 1}\frac{(-1)^j}{j}\Bigl(u^j-\bigl(\frac{u}{a}\bigr)^j\Bigr).
\]
The \(j=1\) summand is \(-u(1-1/a)=-u\beta\). Summing it from the mode produces the even function
\[
-\frac{\beta\,\delta^2}{2\bar k}+O\Bigl(\frac{|\delta|}{\bar k}\Bigr).
\]
The comparison weights are therefore the even function
\(g(\delta)=\exp(-\beta\delta^2/(2\bar k))\).
Poisson summation, after the change of scale \(\sigma_\beta=\sigma/\sqrt{\beta}\), gives
\[
\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}g(\delta)
=
\sigma_\beta\sqrt{2\pi}\,(1+O(e^{-c\bar k})),
\]
since \(\beta\to 1\). This is the main term \(a_m\sqrt{2\pi\bar k/\beta}\).
The expansion \(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\) is the Taylor expansion of that
prefactor at infinity. It is independent of the window. In the ratio of moments it is evaluated
by the algebraic expansion of~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}, whose output is already
\(1+1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4})\). It is not charged again below.

The \(j=2\) summand is \((u^2/2)(1-1/a^2)\), an even function of \(t\), with \(1-1/a^2=1+O(d^{-2})\). Its partial sums are
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{\delta}\frac{t^2}{2\bar k^2}
=
\frac{\delta(\delta+1)(2\delta+1)}{12\bar k^2}
=
\frac{\delta^3}{6\bar k^2}+\frac{\delta^2}{4\bar k^2}+\frac{\delta}{12\bar k^2}.
\]
The cubic and linear terms are odd in \(\delta\). At the window edge \(|\delta|=\lambda\sigma\) the cubic term has size
\[
\frac{\lambda^3}{3\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{n/d}}
=
\Theta\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\,\log^3 n\Bigr).
\]
Bounding that odd function by its maximum, rather than summing it, is the truncation error in
Gao's Lemma~18. Against the even weight \(g\), on a symmetric window, the odd part sums to zero.
The even piece \(\delta^2/(4\bar k^2)\) is \(O((\log n)^2\,d/n)\) on the window. Summing it against \(g\) costs a relative
error \(O(d/n)\), since \(\sum g(\delta)\,\delta^2=O(\sigma^3)\) and \(\bar k^2=\sigma^4\).
The same cancellation removes the odd part of the discrepancy \(O(|\delta|/\bar k)\)
and the odd part of the accumulated \(O(1/n)\) ratio error.

The remainder is even. Its leading piece is the \(j=3\) sum, of size \(O(\delta^4/\bar k^3)\).
The Gaussian moment \(\sum g(\delta)\,\delta^4\) is \(O(\sigma^5)\). Dividing by \(\bar k^3=\sigma^6\)
and by the main-term size \(\sigma\) produces a relative error \(O(1/\sigma^2)=O(d/n)\),
since \(1/\sigma^2=2d/n\). Terms with \(j\ge 4\) are of size \(O(\lambda^4/\sigma^2)\) uniformly on the
window, and
\[
\frac{\lambda^4}{\sigma^2}
=
\frac{2d\,(\log n)^4}{n}\to 0
\]
throughout \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). They contribute the same relative order \(O(d/n)\) after summation.
Thus the window sum equals \(a_m\sqrt{2\pi\bar k/\beta}\,(1+O(d/n))\).

Outside the window and up to distance \(\bar k/2\) from \(m\), the quadratic exponent still yields
\(\log(a_{m+\delta}/a_m)\le-\delta^2/(4\bar k)\). Those tails are
\(O(a_m\sigma\exp(-c\log^2 n))\). Beyond \(2\bar k\), one has \(r_0(k)\le 2/3\), so the
remaining series is geometric and of size \(O(a_m e^{-c\bar k})\). Both are absorbed in
the factor \(1+O(d/n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Patched moments]
\label{lem:moments}
Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(\Gnd\), and assume \(d=o(n^{1/2})\).
Then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:EY}
\EE Y
&=(1+O(d^2/n))\frac{n!}{(n/2)!\,2^{n/2}}\,\rho(n,d,1),\\
\label{eq:VarY}
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
&=\frac1{6d^3}+O\Bigl(d^{-4}+\frac{d^2}{n}\Bigr).
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For~\eqref{eq:EY}, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15} with \(\alpha=1\)
(so \(\varphi(d,1)=1/4\)) and sum over the \(n!/\bigl((n/2)!2^{n/2}\bigr)\) perfect
matchings.

For~\eqref{eq:VarY}, expand \(\EE[Y^2]=\sum_{H_1,H_2}\PP(H_1\cup H_2\subseteq\Gnd)\).
If \(|H_1\cap H_2|=k\), then \(H_1\cup H_2\) has the shape required by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}, so
\[
\PP(H_1\cup H_2\subseteq\Gnd)=(1+O(d^2/n))\,\rho(n,d,2k/n).
\]
Count pairs via~\cite[Lemma~17]{Gao21}. The resulting summand in \(k\), up to the factor
\(1+O(d^2/n)\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}, satisfies the ratio hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} (the same ratio computed in~\cite[§3.4.2]{Gao21}).
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} replaces the truncation error \(O(\sqrt{d/n}\,\log^3 n)\) in Gao's
Lemma~18 by \(O(d/n)\): that error is the window maximum of the odd \(j=2\) sum, and the sum of
an odd function against the even Gaussian weight vanishes.
The prefactor \(\beta^{-1/2}=1+1/(2d^2)+d^{-3}+O(d^{-4})\) is not a new error.
It is a function of \(d\) alone and is already expanded in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}.
For \(d\ge 1\) one has \(d/n\le d^2/n\), so the improved truncation is absorbed
in the enumeration error \(O(d^2/n)\).
The ratio \(\EE[Y^2]/(\EE Y)^2\) is then the same algebraic value as
in~\cite[§3.4.1]{Gao21}, with every \(d^3/n\) replaced by \(d^2/n\), namely
\(1+1/(6d^3)+O(d^{-4}+d^2/n)\).
\end{proof}

\section{The coupling}
\label{sec:coupling}

\begin{lemma}[Error schedule]
\label{lem:eta}
Define
\[
\eta(\alpha)=2\alpha+\frac1{d^3\alpha^2}+C\frac{d^2}{n\alpha^2}
\]
for a sufficiently large absolute constant \(C\), and set
\[
\alpha=\max\bigl\{d^{-1},\,(d^2/n)^{1/8}\bigr\}.
\]
If \(d\to\infty\) and \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), then \(\alpha=o(1)\) and \(\eta(\alpha)=o(1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two arguments of the maximum meet when \(d\cdot(d^2/n)^{1/8}=1\), i.e.\ \(d=n^{1/10}\),
since \(d\cdot(d^2/n)^{1/8}=d^{5/4}/n^{1/8}\).

If \(d\le n^{1/10}\), then \(\alpha=d^{-1}\). Hence \(2\alpha=o(1)\) and
\(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}=o(1)\). The remaining term is
\(d^2/(n\alpha^2)=d^4/n\le n^{-3/5}\) at the right endpoint, and is smaller for smaller \(d\).

If \(n^{1/10}\le d=o(n^{1/2})\), then \(\alpha=(d^2/n)^{1/8}\). This tends to \(0\), and
\[
\frac{d^2}{n\alpha^2}=(d^2/n)^{3/4}=o(1),
\]
while \(1/(d^3\alpha^2)\le d^{-1}=o(1)\) because \(\alpha\ge d^{-1}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} is what removes the former summand \(\sqrt{d/n}\,\log^3 n\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}, so no extra logarithmic loss remains in the schedule.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in \(G(n,d+1)\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} and Chebyshev's inequality,
\[
\PP\bigl(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y\bigr)
\le\frac{\Var Y}{\alpha^2(\EE Y)^2}
=O\bigl(\eta(\alpha)\bigr)=o(1).
\]
The same bound holds for the number of perfect matchings in complements of
\(\Gnd\) (equivalently, matching counts in \(G(n,n-1-d)\), to which
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} applies in our range after complement duality).

Apply the bipartite coupling of~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21}, replacing Gao's error
function \(\eta\) (which contains the term \(d^3/(n\alpha^2)\)) by the function of
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta}.
Every estimate in~\cite[§4.2]{Gao21} that invokes~\cite[Theorem~10 or
Corollary~15]{Gao21} remains valid under Lemma~\ref{lem:moments}.
The coupling therefore satisfies
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})\ge 1-5\eta(\alpha)=1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Chain schedule]
\label{lem:chain-eta}
Let \(D,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}, and write \(v(D)\)
for the quantity defined there.
For every \(d\in[D,D+L]\) set \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}:=v(d)^{1/3}\), where \(v(d)\)
is the same expression at degree \(d\).
Then \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}=o(1)\) and \(\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})=o(1/L)\), uniformly in
\(d\in[D,D+L]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(v(D)\ge D^{-3}\), the hypothesis \(L=o(v(D)^{-1/3})\) forces \(L=o(D)\).
Thus \(d\le 2D\) for large \(n\), and \(v(d)\le 8v(D)\).
In particular \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\ge d^{-1}\) and
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\le 2v(D)^{1/3}=o(1/L)=o(1)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:moments} gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2\le v(d)\) after enlarging the absolute constant inside \(v\).
Chebyshev's inequality at relative height \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\) therefore costs at most
\(v(d)/\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}^2=v(d)^{1/3}\).
The same comparison absorbs the summands of \(\eta\) beyond \(2\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\), and
\[
\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})
\le 3v(d)^{1/3}
=O\bigl(v(D)^{1/3}\bigr)
=o(1/L).
\]
A lower bound \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\ge c/L\) does not imply
\(2\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}=O(1/L)\). The deviation \(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}}\) is set by balancing
\(2\alpha\) against \(v(d)/\alpha^2\), and that balance is of order \(v(d)^{1/3}\), which
is at least on the order of \(1/d\) because \(v(d)\ge d^{-3}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:chains}]
For each \(d\in\{D,\ldots,D+L-1\}\) the single-step coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (via~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21} with the schedule of
Lemma~\ref{lem:chain-eta}) produces a coupling of \(G(n,d)\) and \(G(n,d+1)\)
with failure probability at most \(5\eta(\alpha_{\mathrm{ch}})=o(1/L)\).
Compose these couplings sequentially: sample \(G_D\sim G(n,D)\), then couple
forward to \(G_{D+1}\), then to \(G_{D+2}\), and so on.
Each marginal remains correct.
By a union bound, the probability that any step fails is
\[
o\Bigl(\frac1L\Bigr)\cdot L=o(1).
\]
On the complementary event one has
\(G_D\subseteq G_{D+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{D+L}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:mega}]
At \(D=\lfloor n^{1/5}\rfloor\) both summands of \(v(D)\) are \(\Theta(n^{-3/5})\),
so \(v(D)^{-1/3}=\Theta(n^{1/5})\).
\end{proof}

\section{Beyond exact \texorpdfstring{\(o(\sqrt{n})\)}{o(sqrt(n))}}
\label{sec:beyond}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:approx}]
Sample \((G,H)\) from \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\), so \(G\subset H\) almost surely and
\(H\sim\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\).
Couple \(H\) to \(H'\sim\mu_{d+1}\) optimally, whence
\(\PP(H\neq H')=\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le\delta\).
On \(\{H=H'\}\) one has \(G\subset H'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[TV from concentration]
\label{lem:tv}
If \(\PP(|Y-\EE Y|\ge\alpha\,\EE Y)\le\delta\) with \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), then
\[
\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})\le 10(\alpha+\delta).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the Strassen/bipartite coupling of~\cite[Corollary~3.2]{HLMPW25}, equivalently
the degree-regularity argument in~\cite[Theorem~19]{Gao21}: on the high-probability
set where \(Y\) is \(\alpha\)-concentrated, the coupling fails with probability
\(O(\alpha+\delta)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sqrt}]
For \(d\le c\sqrt{n}\) the patched variance formula gives
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(c^2+d^{-3})\).
Choose \(\alpha>0\) fixed and \(c\) small enough that Chebyshev yields \(\delta\le\alpha\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv} then gives TV distance \(O(\alpha)\).
Shrinking \(\alpha\) yields TV \(\le 1/2\); apply Theorem~\ref{thm:approx}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}]
By~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BIM25}, there is a coupling with
\[
G\subset G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+1)/n\bigr)\subset H
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
Under the standard monotone coupling of binomial graphs, the edges of \(G\) that can
fail to lie in \(H\) are contained in the ER difference
\[
G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)
\setminus
G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+1)/n\bigr).
\]
A Chernoff bound shows that this difference has maximum degree at most \(3\varepsilon d\)
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}]
We track the error budget in the upper-sandwich reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25},
using their switching estimate~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{BIM25} as a black box.
The lower inclusion is~\cite[Lemma~2.2]{BIM25}. Its hypotheses are implied by
the budget constraints checked below: once \(C_*\) is large one has
\(\varepsilon d=C_*d^{8/9}(\log n)^{1/9}\gg\log n\), so the same \(\varepsilon\) is admissible
for that lemma.

 Crucially, the switching core~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} uses an
\emph{absolute} large constant \(C_0\) in the constraints
\(\eta d\ge C_0\) and \(\eta^2\delta\ge C_0\log n\).
We therefore instantiate the process with absolute \(C_0\) (sufficiently large)
and \(\mu:=\varepsilon/100\), rather than forcing \(C_0=\omega(1/\varepsilon)\).

In~\cite[§3.5.5]{BIM25} the critical accumulation event B2 requires, with
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:B2}
2C_0\mu\,dn + 4C_0\,dn\Bigl(\frac{10n\log n}{\varepsilon dn}\Bigr)^{1/8}
\le\frac R2.
\end{equation}
It is enough that
\begin{align}
C_0\mu&\le\frac{\varepsilon}{32},\label{eq:bud1}\\
C_0\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}\Bigr)^{1/8}&\le\frac{\varepsilon}{32}.\label{eq:bud2}
\end{align}
With \(\mu=\varepsilon/100\) and \(C_0\) absolute,~\eqref{eq:bud1} holds for
\(C_0\le 3\).
Inequality~\eqref{eq:bud2} rearranges to
\[
\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}
\le O(\varepsilon^{8})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varepsilon=\Omega\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/9}\Bigr).
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon=C_*(\log n/d)^{1/9}\) with \(C_*\) large, both budget
constraints hold, and
\[
\eta:=C_0\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\varepsilon d}\Bigr)^{1/8}=O(\varepsilon)
\]
satisfies the switching hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25}:
\(\eta\ll 1\), \(\eta d\to\infty\), and
\(\eta^2\cdot(2\varepsilon d)=\Omega(\varepsilon^3 d)=\Omega(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3})\gg\log n\).
The reduction in~\cite[§3]{BIM25} therefore yields the upper sandwich.
Combined with the lower sandwich at the same rate, we obtain the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at degrees \(d\) and \(d+k\) with
\(\varepsilon=C_*(\log n/d)^{1/9}\).
The binomial transit of Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} yields (i) once
\(k\ge 3\varepsilon d=k_0\), and (ii) with defect at most \(3\varepsilon d=k_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}]
Fix \(\sigma\in(0,1)\) and assume \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\).
Set \(\varepsilon=C_{**}(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) with \(C_{**}\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\) and \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\).
Then
\[
\delta_R\asymp d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\ge n^{2\sigma/3},
\]
so \(\delta_R\ge n^{\sigma'}\) for some fixed \(\sigma'>0\) and large \(n\).

In this density regime,~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25} gives
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating path counts at relative error
\(\eta=C_0\mu/\log n\) whenever \(\delta\ge n^{\sigma'}\).
The same Kim--Vu argument permits any larger relative error; we take the
switching-optimal choice
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{m}}\Bigr\}
\]
with absolute \(C_0\) and \(\mu=\varepsilon/100\).
At \(m\ge R\) one has \(\sqrt{n\log n/m}=O(\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)})=O(\varepsilon)\),
so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
The constraints of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} hold:
\(\eta d=\Omega(\varepsilon d)\to\infty\) and \(\eta^2\delta=\Omega(\log n)\).

The B2 estimate of~\cite[§3.5.5]{BIM25} with this schedule reduces to
\(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) and \(C_0\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of
which hold at our \(\varepsilon\).
Thus the upper sandwich follows; the lower sandwich is unchanged.

For optimality: at stopping time \(R\asymp\varepsilon dn\) one has \(\delta\asymp\varepsilon d\).
Event B2 forces \(\eta=O(\varepsilon)\), while switching forces
\(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\log n/\delta})\).
Combining yields \(\varepsilon=\Omega((\log n/d)^{1/3})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:poly-inc}]
Identical to Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}, using Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:chernoff}]
Let \(p=(1-\varepsilon)d/n\) and let \(X_v\) be the degree of a fixed vertex \(v\) in
\(G(n,p)\), so \(X_v\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p)\) with mean
\(\lambda=(1-\varepsilon)d\cdot(1-O(1/n))\).
If \(\varepsilon\le c\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(c>0\) small, then
\(d-\lambda=O(\sqrt{\lambda\log n})\) lies inside the constant-probability upper
tail regime of the binomial.
Writing \(N=\sum_v\mathbf{1}_{X_v>d}\), a standard second-moment computation on the
indicators (cf.\ Bollob\'as) yields \(\EE N\to\infty\) and
\(\Var N=o((\EE N)^2)\), so \(N\ge 1\) with probability bounded away from zero.
Thus \(\Delta(G(n,p))>d\) fails to be a vanishing event, and no high-probability
embedding \(G(n,p)\subseteq\Gnd\) is possible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Q1}]
Apply Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} at degrees \(d\) and \(d+k\) with
\(\varepsilon=C_0\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\).
Then \(G\subset G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) and
\(G(n,(1-\varepsilon)(d+k)/n)\subset H\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
The inequality \((1+\varepsilon)d\le(1-\varepsilon)(d+k)\) rearranges to
\(k\ge 2\varepsilon d/(1-\varepsilon)\).
For \(\varepsilon\le 1/4\) this is implied by \(k\ge 3\varepsilon d=3C_0\sqrt{d\log n}\).
Monotone-couple the two binomial graphs to finish.
\end{proof}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{\(\sqrt{n}\)}{sqrt(n)} barrier and open problems}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{proposition}[Enumeration barrier]
\label{prop:barrier}
Let \(M\) be a perfect matching. The estimate of \(\PP(M\subset\Gnd)\) obtained by
multiplying the \(n/2\) conditional probabilities from~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} is
known only up to a cumulative relative factor \(1+O(d^2/n)\). The estimate yields a
\(1+o(1)\) factor when \(d=o(n^{1/2})\), and it does not when \(d=\omega(n^{1/2})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each factor is the principal term of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} times \(1+O(d^2/n^2)\),
as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cor15}. The product of \(n/2\) such factors is
\[
\bigl(1+O(d^2/n^2)\bigr)^{n/2}
=
\exp\bigl(O(d^2/n)\bigr),
\]
which is \(1+O(d^2/n)\) whenever \(d=o(n^{1/2})\). If \(d=\omega(n^{1/2})\), then \(d^2/n\not\to 0\),
so the same product does not tend to \(1\).
\end{proof}

Thus this matching-enumeration does not prove exact high-probability
\(+1\) nesting for \(d=\omega(\sqrt{n})\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} expands the simultaneous reach inside the \(o(\sqrt{n})\)
regime, but does not breach that barrier.

Quantitative sandwich rates from the~\cite{BIM25} budget:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Rate & Range & Status \\
\hline
\((\log n/d)^{1/9}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/5}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-fifth} \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/3}\) & \(d\ge n^{\sigma}\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third} (process-optimal) \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/3}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} (process-optimal) \\
\((\log n/d)^{1/2}\) & all \(d\ge C\log n\) & Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The exponent \(1/3\) is optimal for the deletion-process~/~B2 scheme; reaching
Chernoff requires a different coupling (cf.~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}).
The same cubic ceiling appears on the lower side: residual degree concentration
forces \(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\log n/(\varepsilon d)})\) with \(\eta\ll\varepsilon\).
The dense embedding of~\cite{GIM22} is likewise blocked at Chernoff scale by the
structural hypothesis \(\xi\Delta\gg n/\log n\), which is incompatible with
\(\xi=\Theta\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) for every \(d\le n-1\).
The exponent \(1/9\) is an artefact of the
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/8}\) two-vertex path upper bound in~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25}.
Redesigning the expansion so connecting sets are linear
(Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}) upgrades the lower path error to
\(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\); truncation recovers upper error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:U}) and global rate \(1/5\).
A last-layer Bernstein argument then upgrades the two-vertex upper bound to the
same Chernoff scale (Theorem~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}), yielding the
process-optimal global rate \(1/3\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q}]
Let \((G_i)_{0\le i\le\binom{n}{2}}\) be the random graph process on \([n]\), and let
\(G\sim\Gnd\) be coupled to the process as in~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Fix \(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large, and set
\[
i_-:=\Bigl\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad
i_+:=\Bigl\lceil(1+\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rceil.
\]
Write \(p_\pm:=(1\pm\varepsilon)d/n\). A standard Chernoff-plus-union bound gives
\[
\PP\bigl(\Delta(G_{i_-})>d\bigr)=o(1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\PP\bigl(\delta(G_{i_+})<d\bigr)=o(1),
\]
once \(C\) is large enough (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:chernoff} for the matching
lower bound on \(\varepsilon\)).
On the event \(\{\Delta(G_{i_-})\le d\}\), Conjecture~1.3 of~\cite{BIM25} yields
\(G_{i_-}\subset G\).
On \(\{\delta(G_{i_+})\ge d\}\) it yields \(G\subset G_{i_+}\).
Finally, \(G_{i_-}\) (resp.\ \(G_{i_+}\)) may be coupled inside
\(G(n,p_-)\) (resp.\ outside a copy of \(G(n,p_+)\)) by the usual monotone coupling
of the process with binomial graphs of matching edge count, losing only \(o(1)\)
probability.
Combining these couplings produces \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) to
obtain \(G\subset G^*_0\) with \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\).
The final-window hypothesis, applied at the target Chernoff rate
\(\varepsilon=C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\le\varepsilon_0\) (valid for large \(n\) since
\(1/2>1/3\)), produces \(H\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) with \(G\subset H\) a.a.s.
The symmetric argument for the lower sandwich yields \(G_*\).
Stitching gives Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} on \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Attack surface]
\label{rem:window}
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} isolates the obstruction to Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}:
one must thin the residual \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\), which is approximately
\(\varepsilon_0 d\)-regular of order \(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\), down to surplus
degree \(\asymp\sqrt{d\log n}\) without exposing any edge of \(G\).
This is a far smaller range of surplus densities than the full deletion process.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Residual degree after rate \(1/3\)]
\label{lem:residual}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
Let \((G,G^*_0)\) be the coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate
\(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\), and write \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\).
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\,d
\le\deg_R(v)
\le 2\varepsilon_0\,d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\)},
\]
and in particular \(\min_v\deg_R(v)\ge C\sqrt{d\log n}\) for every fixed \(C\) and large \(n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the event \(G\subset G^*_0\) (probability \(1-o(1)\)) one has
\(\deg_R(v)=\deg_{G^*_0}(v)-d\).
Here \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\), so
\(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)\sim\mathrm{Bin}\bigl(n-1,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\bigr)\) has mean
\(\lambda=(1+\varepsilon_0)d\cdot(1-O(1/n))\).
Chernoff bounds yield
\begin{align*}
\PP\bigl(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)<d+\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\bigr)
&\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d)\bigr)=o(n^{-2}),\\
\PP\bigl(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)>d+2\varepsilon_0 d\bigr)
&\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d)\bigr)=o(n^{-2}),
\end{align*}
and a union bound over \(v\in[n]\) completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sharp residual degrees]
\label{lem:residual-sharp}
Let \(d/\log n\to\infty\), and let \(R\) be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}, with
\(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\,\varepsilon_0 d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\).}
\]
More precisely, the relative error may be taken to be \(\varepsilon_0/2\), which tends to \(0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has \(\deg_R(v)=\deg_{G^*_0}(v)-d\), and
\(\deg_{G^*_0}(v)\) is binomial with mean
\(\lambda=(1+\varepsilon_0)d\,(1-O(1/n))\).
The residual mean is therefore
\(\mu_R=\varepsilon_0 d-O(d/n)\).
Since \(d=\omega(\log n)\) one has \(\varepsilon_0\to 0\), and the relative window
\(t:=\varepsilon_0/2\) satisfies
\[
t^2\mu_R
=\frac{\varepsilon_0^3 d}{4}-o(1)
=\frac{C_3^3}{4}\log n-o(1).
\]
Chernoff's bound gives
\[
\PP\bigl(|\deg_R(v)-\mu_R|\ge t\mu_R\bigr)
\le 2\exp\bigl(-\Omega(C_3^3\log n)\bigr).
\]
For the absolute constant \(C_3\) already fixed large by Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global},
the right-hand side is \(o(n^{-2})\).
A union bound over \(v\), together with \(\PP(G\not\subset G^*_0)=o(1)\), yields
\(\deg_R(v)=(1\pm\varepsilon_0/2)\mu_R=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) simultaneously for every \(v\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid Chernoff container]
\label{thm:hybrid}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large, and assume
\(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0:=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Let \((G,G^*_0)\) be the coupling of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at rate
\(\varepsilon_0\), write \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\), and set \(q:=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\).
Form \(R_q\) by retaining each edge of \(R\) independently with probability \(q\), and set
\(H:=G\cup R_q\).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\);
\item for every pair \(jk\),
\(\PP(jk\in H)=(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n}+o\bigl(\frac{d}{n}\bigr)\);
\item writing \(D_v:=\deg_H(v)\), on the event \(G\subset G^*_0\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\) with
\(\deg_R(v)=\Theta(\varepsilon_0 d)\), hence
\(\EE D_v=(1+\Theta(\varepsilon))d\) and \(\Var D_v=\Theta(\varepsilon d)\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular \(H\) matches the first-moment edge profile of
\(G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) up to \(o(d/n)\), and has degree mean
\((1+\Theta(\varepsilon))d\) with variance \(\Theta(\varepsilon d)\), with \(G\subset H\) a.a.s.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item (i) is immediate on \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\), which has probability \(1-o(1)\).

For (ii), fix an unordered pair \(jk\). By symmetry
\(\PP(jk\in G)=d/(n-1)\).
Also \(\PP(jk\in G^*_0)=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\).
On the construction,
\[
\PP(jk\in H)
=\PP(jk\in G)+q\,\PP(jk\in R)
=\PP(jk\in G)+q\bigl(\PP(jk\in G^*_0)-\PP(jk\in G^*_0\cap G)\bigr).
\]
The failure event \(\{G\not\subset G^*_0\}\) has probability \(o(n^{-c})\) for some \(c>0\)
(from the B2 estimate at rate \(\varepsilon_0\)), and
\(\{jk\in G\setminus G^*_0\}\subset\{G\not\subset G^*_0\}\), so
\(\PP(jk\in G^*_0\cap G)=\PP(jk\in G)+O(n^{-c})\).
Substituting \(q=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\) therefore yields
\begin{align*}
\PP(jk\in H)
&=q\cdot(1+\varepsilon_0)\frac{d}{n}+(1-q)\frac{d}{n-1}+O(n^{-c})\\
&=(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n}+O\Bigl(\frac{d}{n^2}\Bigr)+O(n^{-c}),
\end{align*}
which is \((1+\varepsilon)d/n+o(d/n)\).

For (iii), on \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\).
On the high-probability event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual},
\(\deg_R(v)\in[\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d,\,2\varepsilon_0 d]\), so
\[
\EE[D_v\mid G,G^*_0]=d+q\deg_R(v)\in\bigl[d+\tfrac12\varepsilon d,\,d+2\varepsilon d\bigr]
\]
and
\[
\Var(D_v\mid G,G^*_0)=q(1-q)\deg_R(v)=\Theta(\varepsilon d).
\]
Rescaling the absolute constant in the definition of \(\varepsilon\) absorbs the
\(\tfrac12\)--\(2\) factors into the form stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp Chernoff degrees]
\label{cor:hybrid-sharp}
Suppose \(d/\log n\to\infty\) and \(C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\le\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\), with
\(C\) large and \(\varepsilon_0\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid}.
Let \(H\) be the hybrid graph of that theorem. Then with probability \(1-o(1)\),
\[
\deg_H(v)=d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d
\qquad\text{for every vertex \(v\).}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-sharp} gives \(\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) simultaneously
for every \(v\). On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\) one has
\(D_v=d+\mathrm{Bin}(\deg_R(v),q)\) with \(q=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_0\), so the conditional mean is
\(d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d\).
The conditional variance is \(\Theta(\varepsilon d)\). Set
\(t:=(12\varepsilon d\log n)^{1/2}\).
A Chernoff bound yields
\(\PP(|D_v-\EE[D_v\mid R]|\ge t\mid R)\le 2n^{-2}\) on the sharp degree event:
there \(q\deg_R(v)\le 2\varepsilon d\) for large \(n\), so
\(t^2/(3q\deg_R(v))\ge 2\log n\).
A union bound over \(v\) costs \(o(1)\).
The ratio of this deviation to the surplus is
\[
\frac{t}{\varepsilon d}
=\Theta\Biggl(\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Biggr)=o(1),
\]
since \(d/\log n\to\infty\). Therefore \(D_v=d+(1+o(1))\varepsilon d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gap to Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}]
\label{rem:hybrid-gap}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} gives a one-sided Chernoff-scale container with correct edge
marginals and degree variance, but \(H\) is not asserted to be distributed as
\(G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\): edges inside \(G\) are rigidly dependent through the regular
constraint.

Moreover, a direct contiguity attack fails. Comparing \(H\) to the nested binomial
obtained by retaining each edge of \(G^*_0\) independently with probability
\(r=(1+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon_0)\) yields a \(\chi^2\) divergence of order
\(\Theta(dn)\) between the two inhomogeneous Bernoulli measures on \(G^*_0\)
(hybrid probabilities \(1\) on \(E(G)\) and \(q\) on \(E(R)\), versus constant \(r\) on all of
\(E(G^*_0)\)). Thus \(H\) is not contiguous to this nested ER model, and upgrading
Theorem~\ref{thm:hybrid} to the upper half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} requires a
different identification (or the process conjecture).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Matching room]
\label{rem:matching-room}
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} also supplies \(\delta(R)\gg\sqrt{d\log n}\) a.a.s., so \(R\) has
the degree room for \(m:=\lfloor C\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) successive perfect
matchings.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Residual expansion]
\label{lem:residual-exp}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\), and let \((G,G^*_0)\) and \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Write \(\delta_0:=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\) with \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\), and set
\(u_{\max}:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\) with \(C_e\) a large absolute constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-8})\), every \(U\subset[n]\) with
\(1\le|U|\le u_{\max}\) satisfies
\[
e_R(U,[n]\setminus U)\ge\frac14\delta_0|U|
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bigl|N_R(U)\bigr|\ge\min\Bigl\{n-|U|,\,\frac{\delta_0|U|}{40\log n}\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} one has \(\delta(R)\ge\delta_0\) and
\(\Delta(R)\le 4\delta_0\).
Moreover \(G^*_0\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n)\).
For each fixed \(U\) with \(|U|\le u_{\max}\), the induced count \(e_{G^*_0}(U)\) is
binomial with mean
\[
p_0\binom{|U|}{2}
\le\frac{(1+\varepsilon_0)d\,u_{\max}^2}{2n}
=O\Bigl(\frac{n(\log n)^{4/3}}{d^{1/3}}\Bigr).
\]
For \(d\ge C_3\log n\) with \(C_3\) large this is at most \(\delta_0|U|/20\).
A Chernoff bound and union bound over all such \(U\) therefore yield
\(e_R(U)\le e_{G^*_0}(U)\le\delta_0|U|/10\) with probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\).
Hence
\[
e_R\bigl(U,[n]\setminus U\bigr)
=\sum_{v\in U}\deg_R(v)-2e_R(U)
\ge\delta_0|U|-\tfrac15\delta_0|U|
\ge\tfrac14\delta_0|U|.
\]

For the neighbourhood bound, every vertex outside \(U\) has codegree into \(U\) at
most \(10\log n\) with probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), uniformly over the stated range
(Chernoff plus union bound).
Hence
\[
\bigl|N_R(U)\bigr|
\ge\frac{e_R(U,[n]\setminus U)}{10\log n}
\ge\frac{\delta_0|U|}{40\log n}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-sided residual cuts]
\label{lem:residual-cuts}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\), and let \(R\) and \(\delta_0=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp}.
Set \(s_0:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\) with \(C_e\) a large absolute constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-8})\), every pair of disjoint sets
\(W,Z\subset[n]\) with \(\min(|W|,|Z|)\ge s_0\) satisfies
\[
e_R(W,Z)\ge\frac{\delta_0}{4n}\,|W||Z|.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A one-vertex set against its non-neighbourhood is an empty cut, so a product
hypothesis \(|W||Z|\ge s_0\) alone is false.
Both sides must be large.
Write \(p_0=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\).
For fixed disjoint \(W,Z\) with \(w:=|W|\), \(z:=|Z|\) and \(w,z\ge s_0\),
\(e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)\) is binomial with mean \(p_0 wz\).
On \(\{G\subset G^*_0\}\),
\[
e_R(W,Z)=e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)-e_G(W,Z).
\]
The same configuration-model tail as in~\cite[Appendix~A]{BIM25} gives
\[
e_G(W,Z)\le\Bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon_0}{4}\Bigr)\frac{d}{n}\,wz
\]
except with probability \(\exp(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d\, wz/n))\).
A Chernoff bound likewise yields
\[
e_{G^*_0}(W,Z)\ge\Bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\Bigr)\frac{d}{n}\,wz
\]
except with probability \(\exp(-\Omega(\varepsilon_0^2 d\, wz/n))\).
On the complementary event,
\[
e_R(W,Z)\ge\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_0 d}{4n}\Bigr)wz=\frac{\delta_0\, wz}{2n}.
\]
The failure probability of a fixed pair is therefore
\(\exp(-\Omega(\delta_0 wz/n))\).
Since \(w,z\ge s_0=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\),
\[
\frac{\delta_0 wz}{n}\ge C_e(w+z)\log n
\]
once \(C_e\) is large, because
\(\delta_0 s_0/n=C_e\log n\) and \(\min(w,z)\ge s_0\) implies
\(wz\ge s_0(w+z)/2\).
Hence the failure probability is at most
\(\exp(-4(w+z)\log n)\), while there are at most
\(\exp(O((w+z)\log n))\) pairs of those sizes.
Summing over \(w,z\ge s_0\) gives \(o(n^{-8})\).
For \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) one has \(s_0\le n/4\), so the range is nonempty.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Perfect matching in the residual]
\label{lem:perfect-matching}
Assume \(n\) is even and \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\).
Under the hypotheses of Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts},
with probability \(1-o(1)\) the graph \(R\) contains a perfect matching.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\), enlarging the absolute constant \(C_3\) if needed,
the minimum degree satisfies \(\delta_0\ge 120(\log n)^2\),
so the expansion factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp} obeys
\(\delta_0/(40\log n)\ge 3\log n\).
In particular that lemma applies to every set of size at most
\(s_0:=C_e n\log n/\delta_0\), the same threshold as Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts},
and \(s_0\le n/4\).

Draw a uniformly random balanced bipartition \([n]=L\cup L'\) and let \(B\) be the
bipartite graph of \(R\)-edges across the cut.
A perfect matching of \(B\) is a perfect matching of \(R\).
A Chernoff bound gives \(\deg_B(v)\ge\delta_0/3\) for every vertex, with probability
\(1-o(n^{-2})\).
It is enough to check Hall's condition for sets \(W\subset L\) with \(|W|\le n/2\);
the opposite part is identical.

If \(1\le|W|\le\delta_0/3\), then \(N_B(W)\) contains \(N_B(v)\) for any \(v\in W\), hence
\(|N_B(W)|\ge\delta_0/3\ge|W|\).

If \(\delta_0/3<|W|\le s_0\), Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-exp} gives
\(|N_R(W)|\ge 3|W|\log n\).
Conditionally on \(W\subset L\), a hypergeometric slice of \(N_R(W)\) lands in \(L'\).
Its mean is at least \(|N_R(W)|/3\), so the probability it has size less than
\(|W|\) is \(\exp(-\Omega(|W|\log n))\).
Also \(\PP(W\subset L)\le 2^{-|W|}\).
There are at most \(\exp(|W|\log(en/|W|))\) candidate sets, and
\(\Omega(|W|\log n)\) dominates \(|W|\log(en/|W|)\).
Thus, with probability \(1-o(1)\), \(B\) has no Hall violator of size at most \(s_0\).

Now suppose \(s_0<|W|\le n/2\) and \(|N_B(W)|<|W|\).
The set \(Z:=L'\setminus N_B(W)\) then satisfies \(e_R(W,Z)=0\) and
\(|Z|>n/2-|W|\).
If \(|W|\le n/2-s_0\), both \(|W|\) and \(|Z|\) exceed \(s_0\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} forces \(e_R(W,Z)>0\), a contradiction.
If instead \(|W|>n/2-s_0\), then \(|L\setminus W|<s_0\) and
\(|N_B(Z)|\le|L\setminus W|\), while \(|Z|>|L\setminus W|\).
Every \(z\in Z\) has its \(\delta_0/3\) neighbours in \(L\setminus W\), so
\(|Z|>\delta_0/3\).
If \(|Z|\ge s_0\), then both sides of the empty cut \((W,Z)\) have size at least
\(s_0\) (using \(s_0\le n/4\), so \(|W|>n/2-s_0\ge s_0\)), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} yields \(e_R(W,Z)>0\).
If \(|Z|<s_0\), then \(Z\) itself is a Hall violator of size at most \(s_0\), which
the previous paragraph forbids.
Therefore no Hall violator exists, and \(B\) has a perfect matching.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matching peel in the rate-\(1/3\) residual]
\label{thm:matching-peel}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and let
\((G,G^*_0)\) and \(R:=G^*_0\setminus G\) be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Set \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) a sufficiently small absolute
constant.
Then with probability \(1-o(1)\) the residual \(R\) contains an \(m\)-regular spanning
subgraph, and writing \(\Omega_m(R)\) for the set of all such subgraphs one may sample
\(H\in\Omega_m(R)\) so that \(G_m:=G\cup H\) is exactly \((d+m)\)-regular and
\(G\subset G_m\subset G^*_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Start with \(R_0:=R\) and \(G^{(0)}:=G\).
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} one has
\(\delta(R_0)\ge\delta_0=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0 d\).
Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} apply to \(R_0\), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching} yields a perfect matching \(M_1\subset E(R_0)\).
Set \(G^{(1)}:=G\cup M_1\) and \(R_1:=G^*_0\setminus G^{(1)}\).
Iterate: after \(i<m\) steps, \(G^{(i)}\) is exactly \((d+i)\)-regular,
\(R_i=G^*_0\setminus G^{(i)}\), and \(\delta(R_i)\ge\delta_0-i\ge\delta_0/2\) provided
\(c\) is small enough that \(m\le\delta_0/2\).
The proofs of Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-exp} and~\ref{lem:residual-cuts} apply
verbatim to \(R_i\) upon replacing \(G\) by \(G^{(i)}\) and \(\delta_0\) by
\(\delta_0/2\): the \(G^{(i)}\)-cuts concentrate about
\((d+i)|W||Z|/n\le(d+m)|W||Z|/n\), while \(G^*_0\)-cuts concentrate about
\((1+\varepsilon_0)d|W||Z|/n\), so the residual cut mean remains
at least \((\varepsilon_0 d-m)|W||Z|/n\ge\delta_0|W||Z|/(2n)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching} therefore supplies a perfect matching
\(M_{i+1}\) of \(R_i\).
After \(m\) steps the union \(H_\star:=\bigcup_{i=1}^m M_i\) is an \(m\)-regular spanning
subgraph of \(R\), so \(\Omega_m(R)\neq\emptyset\).
Sampling \(H\) uniformly from \(\Omega_m(R)\) and setting \(G_m:=G\cup H\) yields
the claim.
For \(\omega(\log n)=d<(\log n)^{5/2}\) the Chernoff-scale nesting
\(G(n,d)\subset G(n,d+m)\) is already given by Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains},
which does not use this peel. At \(d=\Theta(\log n)\) the same corollary does not
apply: the perfect-matching variance \(1/(6d^3)\) makes each \(+1\) step fail with
Chebyshev probability of order \(1/d\), and a block of length \(\Theta(d)\) does not
survive the union bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact Chernoff-scale regular container]
\label{cor:chernoff-container}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}, there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with an exactly \((d+m)\)-regular graph \(G_m\) on \([n]\) such that
\(G\subset G_m\subset G^*_0\) and \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gap to exact Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}]
\label{rem:peel-gap}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container} produces an exact \((d+m)\)-regular one-sided
container at Chernoff scale \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\), nested between \(G\) and the
rate-\(1/3\) ER upper graph \(G^*_0\).
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} asks for total variation between this container
and \(G(n,d+m)\). Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} explains why the free decomposition
of~\cite{CFG25} does not transfer onto \(G^*_0\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction: container identification implies Chernoff increments]
\label{thm:id-implies-Q}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
Write \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) for the law of the graph \(G_m\) produced by
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}, and \(\mu_{d+m}\) for the law of
\(G(n,d+m)\).
If \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\), then there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with \(H\sim G(n,d+m)\) such that \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container} supplies a coupling with
\(G\subset G_m\) a.a.s.\ and \(G_m\sim\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\).
A maximal TV coupling of \(G_m\) with \(H\sim\mu_{d+m}\) satisfies
\(\PP(G_m\neq H)=\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
On the joint success event \(\{G\subset G_m\}\cap\{G_m=H\}\) one has \(G\subset H\), and
this event has probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Perfect-matching count in the residual]
\label{lem:pm-count}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching}, writing \(\Phi(R)\) for the
number of perfect matchings of \(R\), one has
\[
\Bigl(\frac{\delta_0}{10e}\Bigr)^{n/2}
\le\Phi(R)
\le\bigl(4\delta_0\bigr)^{n/2}
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from \(\Delta(R)\le 4\delta_0\): there are at most
\((4\delta_0)^{n/2}\) ways to choose partners sequentially for an ordered
partition into pairs.
For the lower bound, form the bipartite graph \(B\) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:perfect-matching}; on that lemma's success event one has
\(\delta(B)\ge\delta_0/3\).
Peeling \(\delta':=\lfloor\delta_0/6\rfloor\) perfect matchings from \(B\) by the same
Hall argument (minimum degree remains at least \(\delta_0/6\) throughout) yields a
\(\delta'\)-regular spanning subgraph \(B'\subset B\).
The biadjacency matrix \(A\) of \(B'\) satisfies that \(A/\delta'\) is doubly
stochastic, so the van der Waerden permanent bound gives
\[
\Phi(B')
=\mathrm{per}(A)
\ge(\delta')^{n/2}\cdot\frac{(n/2)!}{(n/2)^{n/2}}
\ge\Bigl(\frac{\delta'}{e}\Bigr)^{n/2}.
\]
Every perfect matching of \(B'\) is a perfect matching of \(R\), and
\(\delta'\ge\delta_0/10\) for large \(n\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Switching toward identification]
\label{rem:switching}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pm-count} shows that each peel step samples from an
exponentially large pool of perfect matchings.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Host has room for \(G(n,d+m)\)]
\label{lem:host-room}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\), \(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c\) small, and let
\(G^*_0\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\bigr)\) with \(\varepsilon_0=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\).
Then \(\varepsilon':=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/(d+m)-1\) satisfies
\(\varepsilon'\ge C_3'(\log n/(d+m))^{1/3}\) for an absolute \(C_3'>0\) and large \(n\).
Thus \(G^*_0\) has the marginal density of an upper sandwich graph at degree \(d+m\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} records that this does not place a uniform copy of
\(G(n,d+m)\) inside the already sampled graph \(G^*_0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compute
\[
\varepsilon'
=\frac{(1+\varepsilon_0)d}{d+m}-1
=\frac{\varepsilon_0 d-m}{d+m}
=\frac{\varepsilon_0-m/d}{1+m/d}.
\]
Since \(m/d=O\bigl(\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\bigr)=o(\varepsilon_0)\), one has
\(\varepsilon'\ge\varepsilon_0/2\) for small \(c\), and
\(\varepsilon_0/2\ge C_3'(\log n/(d+m))^{1/3}\) upon adjusting constants.
The density identity \((1+\varepsilon')(d+m)/n=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\) is the only use of
\(\varepsilon'\). Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at degree \(d+m\), applied to a
fresh binomial graph of this density, produces some coupling in which a copy of
\(G(n,d+m)\) sits inside that fresh graph. It does not alter the joint law of the
pair \((G,G^*_0)\) already built at degree \(d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-switches do not identify the container]
\label{prop:switch-barrier}
Condition on the degree event of Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-sharp}, and write
\(\delta:=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d\) and \(m\le c\sqrt{d\log n}\).
Let \(\Lambda(H)\) be the number of ordered \(4\)-tuples of distinct vertices
\((a,b,c,d)\) with \(ab,cd\in E(H)\) and \(ac,bd\in E(R)\setminus E(H)\).
In the configuration model with \(m\) stubs at each vertex, before conditioning on
the pairs lying in \(R\),
\[
\EE[\Lambda]=(1+o(1))\,m^2\delta^2.
\]
The centre \(nm^2\delta^2\) is larger by a factor \(n\).
A stub exposure changes \(\Lambda\) by at most \(O(m\delta)\). Azuma's inequality at
relative height \(\eta\to 0\) therefore has exponent
\(O(\eta^2 m\delta^2/n)\). Substituting the Chernoff scale gives
\[
\frac{m\delta^2}{n}
=
\Theta\Biggl(\frac{d^{11/6}(\log n)^{7/6}}{n}\Biggr),
\]
which tends to \(0\) for \(d=o(n^{6/11})\).
At \(d=n^{1/2}\) the count is \(n^{-1/12}(\log n)^{7/6}\).
At \(d=n^{2/7}\) it is \(n^{-10/21}(\log n)^{7/6}\).
The simplicity weight \(\exp(-O(mn\log(n/m)))\) is exponentially smaller:
\(\delta\le n\) forces \(\delta^2=o(n^2\log n)\).
Thus this \(2\)-switch count neither concentrates on the uniform simple
\(m\)-factor nor supplies \(\omega(1)\) switches per edge anywhere in the open
interval between the chain ceiling and \(\sqrt{n}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the pairing model, two fixed disjoint vertex pairs are matched with probability
\((1+O(m/n))(m/n)^2\), and a third fixed pair fails to be matched with probability
\(1-O(m/n)\).
On the sharp degree event the number of ordered crossing pairs \((a,c),(b,d)\) in \(R\)
with four distinct vertices is \((1+o(1))(n\delta)^2\): there are \((1+o(1))n\delta\)
ordered edges, and the \(O(\delta)\) edges incident to a given vertex change the count
by a relative \(O(1/n)\).
Multiplying these contributions yields
\(\EE[\Lambda]=(1+o(1))(m/n)^2(n\delta)^2=(1+o(1))m^2\delta^2\).

The ratio of \(nm^2\delta^2\) to this mean is \(n\).
For the Lipschitz bound, fix an edge \(ab\) of the pairing. A switch through \(ab\)
requires the opposite edge \(cd\) and the crossings \(ac,bd\in E(R)\), so \(c\in N_R(a)\)
and \(d\in N_R(b)\). Each of the \(O(\delta)\) choices of \(c\) has at most \(m\) partners
in the pairing, hence \(O(m\delta)\) switches touch \(ab\).
Over \(nm\) stub exposures, Azuma's inequality at deviation \(\eta\cdot\Theta(m^2\delta^2)\)
has exponent
\[
\frac{(\eta m^2\delta^2)^2}{nm\cdot O(m^2\delta^2)}
=O\Biggl(\eta^2\frac{m\delta^2}{n}\Biggr).
\]
The identities
\(m\delta^2/n=\Theta(d^{11/6}(\log n)^{7/6}/n)\),
\((n^{1/2})^{11/6}/n=n^{-1/12}\), and
\((n^{2/7})^{11/6}/n=n^{-10/21}\),
up to the factor \((\log n)^{7/6}\) from \(m\) and \(\delta\).
A single simple \(m\)-factor has pairing probability
\(\exp(-O(mn\log(n/m)))\), as in the count \((m!)^n/(mn-1)!!\).
Dividing an Azuma tail by this weight produces a useful bound only if
\(m\delta^2/n\) dominates \(mn\log(n/m)\), i.e.\ only if \(\delta^2\) dominates
\(n^2\log(n/m)\). No degree \(\delta\le n\) satisfies that comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the switching programme stops]
\label{rem:quasi-regular}
Proposition~\ref{prop:switch-barrier} is the obstruction to reading a uniform edge
law off \(2\)-switches of the residual \(m\)-factor.
The count is of order \(m^2\delta^2\), not \(nm^2\delta^2\), and the resulting
Azuma exponent is too small to pass from the pairing model to the uniform simple
factor.
On \(n^{2/7}\le d\le n^{1/2}\) a typical edge lies in \(o(1)\) such switches, so the
comparison that would identify \(\mathrm{Unif}(\Omega_m(R))\) with \(\mu_{d+m}\) does not
start.
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} blocks the shortcut through~\cite[(1.1)]{CFG25}.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} stays open.
The conditional route past the second-moment ceiling is Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth-chains}:
a Gaussian fourth-moment bound on perfect-matching counts would move the Chernoff
window from \(n^{2/7}\) to \(n^{4/13}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Contiguity preserved by a.a.s.\ conditioning]
\label{lem:cond-contiguity}
Let \(\mu_n,\nu_n\) be contiguous probability measures on a common space, and let
\(A_n\) be events with \(\mu_n(A_n)\to 1\). Then \(\nu_n(A_n)\to 1\), and the
conditionals \(\mu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\) and \(\nu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\) are contiguous.
Moreover if \(\dTV(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to 0\), then
\(\dTV\bigl(\mu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n),\nu_n(\,\cdot\mid A_n)\bigr)\to 0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contiguity forces \(\nu_n(A_n)\to 1\). For an event \(B_n\),
\(\mu_n(B_n\mid A_n)=\mu_n(B_n\cap A_n)/\mu_n(A_n)\), so
\(\mu_n(B_n\mid A_n)\to 0\) iff \(\mu_n(B_n\cap A_n)\to 0\) iff \(\mu_n(B_n)\to 0\)
(using \(\mu_n(A_n)\to 1\)), and likewise for \(\nu_n\); contiguity of the
unconditional laws therefore passes to the conditionals.
If instead \(\dTV(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to 0\), a maximal coupling gives
\(\PP(X\neq Y)\to 0\) for \(X\sim\mu_n\), \(Y\sim\nu_n\), and restricting to
\(\{X\in A_n\}\cap\{Y\in A_n\}\) (probability \(1-o(1)\)) yields a coupling of the
conditionals with failure probability \(o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sandwich marginals do not fill a fixed host]
\label{prop:host-gap}
Let \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and \(m=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:host-room}, and let \(G^*_0\) be the upper graph of the degree-\(d\)
sandwich in Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
Let \(H\) be drawn from \(\mu_{d+m}\) independently of \(G^*_0\).
Then
\[
\PP(H\subset G^*_0)=\exp\bigl(-\Theta(nd\log(n/d))\bigr).
\]
In particular this probability does not tend to \(1\), so the event
\(\{K\subset G^*_0\}\) cannot be fed to Lemma~\ref{lem:cond-contiguity} in order to
condition the decomposition of~\cite[(1.1)]{CFG25} on the already sampled host.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper graph \(G^*_0\) is distributed as \(G(n,p)\) with \(p=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\),
and \(H\) has \(n(d+m)/2\) edges.
On \(d\ge C_3\log n\) one has \(\varepsilon_0\le C_3^{2/3}\), hence
\(p\le(1+C_3^{2/3})d/n\).
For \(d=o(n)\) this is at most \(1/2\) when \(n\) is large, so
\(\log(1/p)=\log(n/d)+O(1)\).
Independence gives
\[
\PP(H\subset G^*_0)
=p^{n(d+m)/2}
=\exp\bigl(-\Theta(nd\log(n/d))\bigr).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global} at degree \(d+m\) produces a different coupling,
in which some \((d+m)\)-regular graph sits inside a fresh binomial graph of density
\(p\). That joint law is not the product of \(\mu_{d+m}\) with the degree-\(d\) upper
graph, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cond-contiguity} requires the containment event to have
probability \(1-o(1)\) under the measure being conditioned.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the chain range still gives]
\label{rem:chain-vs-split}
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains} supplies the nesting
\(G(n,D)\subset G(n,D+m)\) for Chernoff \(m\) throughout
\(\omega(\log n)=D=o(n^{2/7}/(\log n)^{3/7})\), by iterating exact \(+1\) couplings.
The free total-variation decomposition of~\cite{CFG25} stops at \(o(n^{1/7})\), which
sits inside that range, and Proposition~\ref{prop:host-gap} blocks the attempt to
push it onto the rate-\(1/3\) host.
Past the chain ceiling, Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel} still produces an exact
\((d+m)\)-regular container. Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} is the missing
identification.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Host-restricted splitting]
\label{conj:config-split}
Let \(d\ge(\log n)^{5/2}\) with \(dn\) even and \(n\) even, and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel}.
Write \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) for the law of the graph \(G_m\) produced by
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-container}, and \(\mu_{d+m}\) for the law of
\(G(n,d+m)\).
Then \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}[Splitting without host]
\label{prop:split-free}
Let \(d\to\infty\), \(m\to\infty\), and \(d+m=o(n^{1/3})\).
Then the law of \(G\cup H\), where \(G\sim\Gnd\) and \(H\sim G(n,m)\) are coupled to be
edge-disjoint via independent configuration models on disjoint stub sets, is
contiguous to \(\mu_{d+m}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
See~\cite{Wormald99,JLR00}: the configuration model of degree \(d+m\) is contiguous
to the union of independent configuration models of degrees \(d\) and \(m\) on
disjoint stub sets (equivalently, a random partition of the
\((d+m)\)-regular configuration into a \(d\)-factor and an \(m\)-factor), after
conditioning on simplicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Free \(m\)-extension flatness past the chain ceiling]
\label{lem:free-flat}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(d=d(n)\to\infty\), and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) fixed and \(mn\) even.
If
\[
d=o\Biggl(\frac{n^{2/5}}{(\log n)^{1/5}}\Biggr),
\]
then any two \(d\)-regular graphs \(G,G'\) on \([n]\) satisfy
\[
N_m(G)=(1+o(1))\,N_m(G'),
\]
where \(N_m(G)\) is the number of \(m\)-regular graphs on \([n]\) edge-disjoint from \(G\).
In particular the conclusion holds for a sequence \(d=\omega(n^{2/7})\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply McKay's enumeration of sparse graphs in the complement of a sparse
host~\cite[Theorem~4.6]{McK85} (recorded as Theorem~2.1 in~\cite{HLMPW25}).
Take the degree sequence \(g_i=m\) and the forbidden graph \(X=G\), which is
\(d\)-regular.
The hypotheses of that theorem hold for any fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,2/3)\) and large
\(n\): writing \(\widehat\Delta\) for McKay's degree parameter,
\[
\widehat\Delta
=2+m\Bigl(\tfrac32 m+d+1\Bigr)
=O\bigl(m(d+m)\bigr)
=o(mn),
\]
since \(d=o(n^{2/5})\) forces \(d+m=o(n)\).
The main terms \(\lambda=(m-1)/2\) and
\(\mu=md/2\) depend on \(G\) only through its degree and its number of edges, hence
are the same for every \(d\)-regular graph.
The relative error in the exponent is
\[
O\Biggl(\frac{\widehat\Delta^{\,2}}{mn}\Biggr)
=O\Biggl(\frac{m(d+m)^2}{n}\Biggr)
=O\Biggl(\frac{d^{5/2}(\log n)^{1/2}}{n}\Biggr)
=o(1)
\]
under the stated bound on \(d\), and the implied constant is uniform in the host.
Therefore
\[
N_m(G)
=
\frac{(mn)!}{(mn/2)!\,2^{mn/2}(m!)^n}
\exp\bigl(-\lambda-\lambda^2-\mu+o(1)\bigr),
\]
which is independent of the choice of \(G\) up to the factor \(1+o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Toward Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}]
\label{rem:config-split}
Proposition~\ref{prop:switch-barrier} shows that the \(2\)-switch count of a residual
\(m\)-factor is of order \(m^2(\varepsilon_0 d)^2\). That supplies \(o(1)\) switches per
edge throughout \(d=o(n^{6/11})\), which contains the open interval past
Corollary~\ref{cor:chernoff-chains} up through \(o(\sqrt{n})\). The same proposition
shows that the Azuma tail does not survive conditioning on simplicity.
Lemma~\ref{lem:free-flat} makes the number of \(m\)-factors of the complement of \(G\)
independent of which \(d\)-regular graph was chosen, for every
\(d=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{1/5})\).
That complement has degree \(n-1-d\). The container in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} counts \(m\)-factors of the residual \(R\), whose
degree is of order \(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\). Flat free counts do not identify
\(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) with \(\mu_{d+m}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Chernoff increments]
\label{thm:conditional-chernoff-inc}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}.
Then under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-peel} there is a coupling of
\(G\sim\Gnd\) with \(H\sim G(n,d+m)\) such that \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\), with
\(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} is the hypothesis
\(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\mu_{d+m})\to 0\).
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:id-implies-Q}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Crude neighbourhood expansion]
\label{lem:crude-exp}
Let \(1/n\ll 1/C\ll\sigma\ll 1\), let \(d\ge C\log n\), and let
\(C\log n\le\delta=2m/n\le n^{\sigma}\).
Form \(K\sim G(n,d)\) and \(F=K+E\) as in~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25}, and set
\(\lambda:=C\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
Then with probability \(1-o(n^{-20})\), every \(U\subset[n]\) with
\[
\frac{\lambda n}{100\delta}\le|U|\le\frac{n}{2\delta}
\]
satisfies
\[
\bigl|N_{F\setminus K}(U)\bigr|
=(1\pm\lambda)\,\delta|U|.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on \(K\). For each \(w\notin U\) the number of \(K\)-free pairs
\(\{w,u\}\) with \(u\in U\) is \(|U|-|N_K(w)\cap U|\).
On the event that every codegree of \(K\) is \(O(\log n)\) (probability
\(1-o(n^{-30})\) for \(d\le n^{\sigma}\)), one has
\(|N_K(w)\cap U|\le|U|/2\) whenever \(|U|\ge\lambda n/(100\delta)\) and \(C\) is large,
hence
\[
p_w:=\PP\bigl(w\in N_{F\setminus K}(U)\bigm|K\bigr)
=1-(1-p)^{|U|-|N_K(w)\cap U|}
\]
satisfies \(\tfrac12\min(1,p|U|)\le p_w\le\min(1,2p|U|)\).
For \(|U|\le n/(2\delta)\) one has \(p|U|\le 1\), so
\[
\EE\bigl(|N_{F\setminus K}(U)|\,\big|\,K\bigr)
=
\bigl(1\pm\tfrac12\lambda\bigr)\delta|U|
\]
for large \(C\).
The indicators \(\mathbf{1}_{w\sim U}\) are conditionally independent given \(K\), so
a Chernoff bound yields relative error
\[
O\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{\delta|U|}}\Bigr)
\le O\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{100\log n}{\lambda n}}\Bigr)\le\frac{\lambda}{2}
\]
with probability \(1-\exp(-\omega(\log n))\) for each fixed \(U\).
A union bound over all admissible cardinalities and choices of \(U\) costs
\(\sum_s\binom{n}{s}e^{-\omega(\log n)}=o(n^{-20})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex lower bounds at Chernoff scale]
\label{thm:path-lower-chernoff}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_L\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{BIM25} with \(\delta=2m/n\ge C_L\log n\) and
\(d\le n^{\sigma}\) for a fixed small \(\sigma>0\), set
\(\lambda:=C_L\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
With probability \(1-o(n^{-14})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\), all
\(Z\subset[n]\) with \(|Z|\le 40\log n\), and all
\(\ell\in[10\ell_0,10\log n]\) (with \(\ell_0\) as in~\cite[§6]{BIM25}), the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) with no internal
vertex in \(Z\) is at least
\[
\Bigl(1-C_L\lambda-\frac{|(Z\cup\{x\})\cap N_K(y)|}{d}\Bigr)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We adapt~\cite[Claim~6.4]{BIM25} by expanding past the I4 ceiling so that the
final connecting sets have linear size, then invoke~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25} at
a constant scale \(\mu_0\).

Fix \(\mu_0:=10^{-4}\) and \(S_A:=\lambda n/(100\delta)\).
Properties I1--I5 of~\cite[§6]{BIM25} hold at our \(\lambda\) by the same proofs
as there: the degree event G1 of~\cite[Lemma~5.3]{BIM25} requires only
\(\lambda\ge C\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\), and the small-set edge/codegree events I3--I5
are applied only to sets of size at most \(S_A\), which sits inside the I4 range.

Phase~A.
Run the inductive expansion of~\cite[Claim~6.4]{BIM25} until both sides have
cardinality at least \(S_A\), stopping at the first layer index \(i_A\) with
\(|X_{2i_A}|,|Y_{2i_A}|\ge S_A\).
The same relative-error bookkeeping as in that claim yields
\[
|X_{2i_A}|,|Y_{2i_A}|=(1\pm C\lambda)\,d^{i_A}\delta^{i_A}r
\]
with \(r\) the Claim~6.4 seed size (chosen so the early layers remain inside the
I4 range).

Phase~B.
While the current set \(X\) satisfies \(|X|<\mu_0 n/\delta\), replace \(X\) by
\(N_{F\setminus K}(X)\) (discarding the \(O(|X|+|Y|+|W|)\) used vertices) and then
by a large subset of the corresponding \(K\)-neighbourhood, exactly as in
Claim~6.4.
Lemma~\ref{lem:crude-exp} supplies the \(F\setminus K\) step to relative error
\(\lambda\); the \(K\)-step is \((1\pm\lambda)\,d\) by \(d\)-regularity and the codegree
bounds already recorded in I3--I5 for the current scale.
Each full step multiplies cardinality by \((1\pm C\lambda)\delta d\).

For the final step, the current set \(X\) may satisfy
\(\delta|X|>\mu_0 n\). In that case downsample: pass to a subset
\(X'\subset X\) with \(|X'|=\lfloor\mu_0 n/\delta\rfloor\), chosen so that every
vertex of \(X\) retains a \((1\pm\lambda)\)-proportion of the incoming layered paths
from the seeds (possible by degree regularity of the preceding layer).
Lemma~\ref{lem:crude-exp} then yields
\[
\bigl|N_{F\setminus K}(X')\bigr|=(1\pm\lambda)\mu_0 n,
\]
and likewise on the \(Y\)-side. One further \(K\)-step, followed if needed by a
\((1\pm\lambda)\)-proportion downsample, produces connecting sets
\(X_\star,Y_\star\) with \(|X_\star|,|Y_\star|=\mu_0 n\) and accumulated branching
factor \((1\pm C_L\lambda)\,d^{i_B}\delta^{i_B}r\).
(The downsample ratios are \(\Theta(1)\) only relative to the immediately
preceding layer; because that layer is itself \((1\pm C\lambda)\)-regular with
respect to path counts, no fixed-factor loss appears in the final
\((1\pm C_L\lambda)\) budget.)

Phase~C.
Apply~\cite[Lemma~6.15]{BIM25} with \(\mu=\mu_0\). The hypothesis
\(\lambda^2\mu_0\delta=\Omega(\log n)\) holds because \(\lambda^2\delta=C_L^2\log n\).
Hence
\[
e_{F\setminus K}(X_\star,Y_\star)=(1\pm 3\lambda)\frac{\delta}{n}|X_\star||Y_\star|
=(1\pm 3\lambda)\mu_0^2\,\delta n.
\]
Each such edge closes a unique alternating path of the prescribed length from the
seed sets, exactly as in Claim~6.4. Multiplying by the Phase~A/B branching
factors and summing over the sliding windows of~\cite[§6.5]{BIM25} produces the
stated I7 lower bound at error \(C_L\lambda\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex path upper bound at scale \(1/4\)]
\label{thm:U}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_U\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25} with \(\delta=2m/n\ge C_U\log n\), with
probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\) and all
\(\ell\in[\tfrac12\log n,\,10\log n]\), the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) is at most
\[
\Bigl(1+C_U\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{\delta}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\lambda:=C_L\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) be the error of
Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}, and run the truncation argument
of~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{BIM25} (Claims~7.2--7.3) with this \(\lambda\) in place of the
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\) lower-bound error from~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{BIM25}.
That argument forces \(\eta=\Omega(\sqrt{\lambda})\) for the two-vertex upper
error; substituting the Chernoff-scale \(\lambda\) yields
\[
\eta=\Omega\Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac{\log n}{\delta}\bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr),
\]
as required. (The same conclusion is available from~\cite[Lemma~8.1]{BIM25}
whenever \(\delta\ge n^{\sigma}\).)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rate \(1/5\) for all \(d\ge C\log n\)]
\label{thm:quant-fifth}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_5\) such that whenever \(d\ge C_5\log n\) and
\[
\varepsilon\ge C_5\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/5},
\]
there is a coupling \((G_*,G,G^*)\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:quant} at this
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(\varepsilon=C_5(\log n/d)^{1/5}\) with \(C_5\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\), so \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\) satisfies
\(\delta_R\ge C_U\log n\) for large \(C_5\).
Run the deletion-process reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25} with absolute \(C_0\),
\(\mu=\varepsilon/100\), and
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\Bigl(\frac{n\log n}{m}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr\}.
\]
At \(m\ge R\) one has \((n\log n/m)^{1/4}=O(\varepsilon)\), so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:U} supplies the two-vertex upper bound at error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), while Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} supplies
the matching lower bound at the stronger error \(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\)
(hence also at \((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\)).
The hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} therefore hold, and the B2 estimate
reduces to \(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) together with
\(C_0(\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/4}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of which hold at our
\(\varepsilon\).
The lower sandwich is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-vertex path upper bound at Chernoff scale]
\label{lem:one-vertex-chernoff}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_1\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of~\cite[Lemma~5.1]{BIM25} with \(\delta=2m/n\ge C_1\log n\) and
\(d\le n^{\sigma}\), set \(\lambda:=C_1\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
With probability \(1-o(n^{-14})\), for every \(v\in[n]\) and every
\(\ell\in[1,10\log n]\), the number of \((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating paths of
length \(2\ell\) starting at \(v\) is at most
\[
(1+30\lambda)\,d^\ell\delta^\ell.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\cite[Lemma~5.3]{BIM25}, property G1 holds at relative error
\(C'\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\le\lambda\) and property G2 holds at relative error
\(2/\delta\), both with probability \(1-o(n^{-14})\).
For \(\delta\ge C_1\log n\) and \(C_1\) large one has
\(2/\delta\le\lambda/\log n\), so G1--G2 imply the hypotheses F1--F2 of
~\cite[Lemma~5.2]{BIM25} at this \(\lambda\).
Lemma~5.2 then yields the claimed path bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-vertex path upper bound at Chernoff scale]
\label{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
There exists an absolute constant \(C_P\) such that the following holds.
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff}, set
\(\lambda:=C_P\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
With probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\), for all distinct \(x,y\in[n]\) and all
\[
\ell\in\bigl[\max\{10\ell_0,\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil+1\},\,10\log n\bigr],
\]
the number of \((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-paths of length \(2\ell\) is at
most
\[
(1+C_P\lambda)\frac{d^\ell\delta^\ell}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu:=d^\ell\delta^\ell/n\) and \(\mu':=d^{\ell-1}\delta^{\ell-1}/n\), so
\(\mu=(d\delta)\mu'\).
Fix distinct \(x,y\) and a length \(\ell\) in the stated range, and condition on
\(K\).
An \((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,y\)-path of length \(2\ell\) has the form
\[
x=v_0\overset{F\setminus K}{\longrightarrow}v_1\overset{K}{\longrightarrow}
\cdots\overset{F\setminus K}{\longrightarrow}v_{2\ell-1}\overset{K}{\longrightarrow}y,
\]
so \(z:=v_{2\ell-1}\in N_K(y)\).
Let \(Z:=N_K(y)\cup\{y\}\) and, for each \(w\notin Z\), let \(M(w)\) be the number of
\((F\setminus K,K)\)-alternating \(x,w\)-paths of length \(2\ell-2\) with no internal
vertex in \(Z\).
Set \(S:=\sum_{w\notin Z}M(w)\).

Since \(\ell-1\ge\tfrac12\log n\), Theorem~\ref{thm:U} supplies a pointwise bound
\(M_{\mathrm{tot}}(w)\le(1+\eta)\mu'\) on the \emph{unrestricted} path counts of
length \(2\ell-2\), with \(\eta:=C_U(\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), on an event of
probability \(1-o(n^{-10})\).
A fortiori \(M(w)\le(1+\eta)\mu'\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-vertex-chernoff} likewise yields
\(S\le(1+30\lambda_1)(d\delta)^{\ell-1}\) with
\(\lambda_1:=C_1\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
Work henceforth on the intersection of these events.

The multiplicities \(M(w)\) depend only on edges with both ends outside \(Z\),
hence are independent of the cut edges
\(\{wz:w\notin Z,\,z\in N_K(y)\}\).
Writing \(X_e\) for the Bernoulli indicators of those cut edges,
\[
Y_{\mathrm{clean}}
:=\sum_{z\in N_K(y)}\sum_{\substack{w\notin Z\\ wz\notin E(K)}}M(w)\,X_{wz}
\]
is, conditionally on \(M\) and \(K\), a weighted sum of independent Bernoullis.
Every \(x,y\)-path of length \(2\ell\) that avoids \(Z\) until its penultimate vertex
is counted by \(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\).
Paths that visit \(Z\) earlier are handled by a first-visit decomposition.
Write \(J_0:=\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil\).
For a first visit to some \(z'\in Z\) at length \(2j\):
\begin{itemize}
\item if \(j\ge J_0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:U} gives
\(P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+\eta)\mu_j\), hence
\[
\sum_{z'\in Z}P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+\eta)|Z|\mu_j;
\]
\item if \(j<J_0\), the same bound
\(\sum_{z'\in Z}P_{x,z'}(j)\le(1+C\lambda)|Z|\mu_j\)
follows by applying the last-layer Bernoulli argument of the present proof
to the shorter length \(2j\) (here the ``target set'' is the whole of \(Z\),
of size \(d+1\), and the one-vertex mass at length \(2j-2\) is controlled by
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-vertex-chernoff}; no appeal to Theorem~\ref{thm:U} is
required because the jump size is \(O((d\delta)^{j-1})\) while the mean is
\(|Z|\mu_j=\Theta(d(d\delta)^j/n)\), and Bernstein still yields relative error
\(O(\lambda)\) uniformly over the \(O(\log n)\) short lengths).
\end{itemize}
In all cases the continuation from \(z'\) to \(y\) of length \(2(\ell-j)\) is at most
\((1+\eta)\mu_{\ell-j}\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:U} (since \(\ell-j\ge J_0\) for
\(\ell\ge J_0+1\) and \(j\le\ell-J_0\)).
Therefore the dirty contribution is at most
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}(1+O(\eta))|Z|\mu_j\mu_{\ell-j}
\le(d+1)\ell(1+O(\eta))\frac{\mu}{n}
=O\Bigl(\frac{d\log n}{n}\Bigr)\mu
=o(\lambda)\mu.
\]
Thus the full path count \(Y\) satisfies \(Y\le Y_{\mathrm{clean}}+o(\lambda)\mu\).

Conditionally,
\[
\EE[Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K]
\le p\,d\,S
\le\frac{\delta d}{n-d-1}\,S
\le(1+C\lambda)\mu,
\]
and
\begin{align*}
\Var(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K)
&\le p\,d\sum_w M(w)^2
\le p\,d\,(\max M)\,S
\le(1+O(\eta))\frac{\mu^2}{d\delta}.
\end{align*}
Each summand changes \(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\) by at most
\((1+\eta)\mu'=(1+\eta)\mu/(d\delta)\).
Bernstein's inequality therefore yields, for \(t:=C_P\lambda\mu/2\),
\[
\PP\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{clean}}>\EE[Y_{\mathrm{clean}}\mid M,K]+t\bigm|M,K\bigr)
\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(d\log n)\bigr)=o(n^{-20}),
\]
since \(t^2/(\Var+Mt)=\Omega(\lambda^2 d\delta)=\Omega(d\log n)\) and
\(Mt/\Var=O(\lambda)\ll 1\).
Hence \(Y\le(1+C_P\lambda)\mu\) with room to spare.
A union bound over \(x,y,\ell\) completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why \(L^{2}\) stops at \(1/4\) but Bernstein reaches \(1/2\)]
\label{rem:second-moment}
A global second-moment computation on \(Y\) yields only
\(\Var(Y)=O(\lambda\mu^2)\), hence relative standard deviation
\(\Theta(\sqrt{\lambda})=(\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\), matching the truncation
scale of Theorem~\ref{thm:U}.
The last-layer decomposition of Theorem~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff} replaces
that global \(L^{2}\) proxy by a \emph{conditional} Bernoulli variance of order
\(\mu^2/(d\delta)\), for which Bernstein's inequality is exp-sharp at the
Chernoff scale \(\lambda=\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\).
The only external input is a coarse \(\ell_\infty\) bound on the incoming
multiplicities, supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:U} at the weaker error
\((\log n/\delta)^{1/4}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}]
Set \(\varepsilon=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) with \(C_3\) large, and write
\(R=\lfloor\varepsilon dn/8\rfloor\), so \(\delta_R:=2R/n\asymp\varepsilon d\) satisfies
\(\delta_R\ge C_P\log n\).
Run the deletion-process reduction of~\cite[§3]{BIM25} with absolute \(C_0\),
\(\mu=\varepsilon/100\), and
\[
\eta_m:=C_0\max\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{\log n},\Bigl(\frac{n\log n}{m}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr\}.
\]
At \(m\ge R\) one has \((n\log n/m)^{1/2}=O(\varepsilon)\), so \(\eta_m=O(\varepsilon)\).
Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
supply two-sided path counts at error \(\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) for every length
in the nonempty range
\(\bigl[\max\{10\ell_0,\lceil\tfrac12\log n\rceil+1\},10\log n\bigr]\).
Fixing any such length, the hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25} hold, and
the B2 estimate reduces to \(C_0\mu=O(\varepsilon)\) together with
\(C_0(\log n/(\varepsilon d))^{1/2}=O(\varepsilon)\), both of which hold at our
\(\varepsilon\).
The lower inclusion is again~\cite[Lemma~2.2]{BIM25}, at the same \(\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:global-inc}]
Identical to Corollary~\ref{cor:quant-nest}, using Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third-global}
in place of Theorem~\ref{thm:quant}, and \(k_3=3C_3 d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\) in place of \(k_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Hypothesis~P resolved]
\label{rem:hyp-P}
Theorems~\ref{thm:path-lower-chernoff} and~\ref{thm:path-upper-chernoff}
together give two-sided two-vertex path counts at
\(\lambda=\Theta\sqrt{\log n/\delta}\) throughout the length window needed by
~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{BIM25}, which is Hypothesis~P.
The deletion-process~/~B2 architecture nevertheless cannot beat rate \(1/3\)
(cf.\ Theorem~\ref{thm:quant-third}); Chernoff-scale sandwiching remains
Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{remark}
