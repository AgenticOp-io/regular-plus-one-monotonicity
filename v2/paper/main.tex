\begin{abstract}
The companion paper~\cite{PlusOneV1} proves that uniform random regular graphs
satisfy exact \(+1\) nesting at every growing degree, assuming Gao's conditional
edge probabilities on the two sparse sides.
The same estimates give matching-count chains below \(\sqrt{n}\), linear-band
chains of length \(o(n^{2/5})\), and vanishing Kim--Vu sandwich rates through
the process-optimal exponent \(1/3\).
This manuscript is the sequel: it records those reductions, proves that the
binomial-host subgraph count is not flattened by a first or second moment,
and isolates the six named statements.
Inside a \(D\)-regular host the \(L\)-factors are the \((D-L)\)-factors, so the
factor second moment of~\cite{PlusOneV1} below \(n^{1/3}\) yields the same bound
for near-complete lengths \(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\) on linear hosts, including past
\(\sqrt{n}\).
Free-extension flatness yields the same bound for near-complete hosts
throughout the McKay range \(\ell(s+\ell)^2/n=o(1)\).
The sublinear matching variance gives co-length one on every host of degree
\(o(n)\), including past \(\sqrt{n}\).
The factor-rate conjecture holds on the linear and near-complete-host sides as well.
A free split plus complementation yields a one-deletion of length \(\Theta(n)\)
from degree \(o(n^{1/3})\) to a near-complete degree.
The same complementation, fed by Dirac's theorem on the complement,
gives a one-deletion of length \(\Theta(n)\) from every degree
\(d\le n/2-1\) to an \((n-2)\)-regular graph, and Dirac nesting places a
matching inside every host of degree at least \(n/2\), and, with failure
probability \(o(1)\), inside every host of growing degree.
Dirac's theorem likewise places a uniform Hamilton cycle inside every host of
degree at least \(n/2\), and Hamiltonicity of a random regular graph extends
that coupling to every degree at least \(3\). The same fact places a uniform
perfect matching inside every degree at least \(3\).
The balanced range \(\min(L,D-L)=\omega(n^{1/3})\) of the factor conjectures
and the other three named statements stay open.
The four-linear-margin deletion of every length \(L=o(n)\) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(\Gnd\) be the uniform random \(d\)-regular graph on \(n\) labelled vertices.
The companion~\cite{PlusOneV1} proves
\[
\PP\bigl(G(n,d)\subset G(n,d+1)\bigr)=1-o(1)
\]
whenever \(d\to\infty\) and \(n-d\to\infty\), assuming~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}
for \(\min(d,n-d)=o(n)\).
That paper also extracts sandwich rates
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/9}\),
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/5}\), and
\(\varepsilon\ge C(\log n/d)^{1/3}\)
from the Kim--Vu proof of~\cite{BIM25}, and a Chernoff increment
\(G(n,d)\subset G(n,d+m)\) by free splitting throughout \(d+m=o(n^{1/3})\).

What remains is a Chernoff-scale sandwich, identification of the matching peel
with the uniform regular law, and concentration of balanced long \(L\)-factors
past \(\sqrt{n}\).
The companion already proved the implications that convert those statements
into nesting theorems.
This paper does not re-prove the implications.
It records them, adds the second-moment identity for the binomial-host
subgraph count, and closes the near-complete side of the factor second-moment
and factor-rate conjectures by complementation in the host
(Theorems~\ref{thm:factor-complementary} and~\ref{thm:factor-rate-complementary})
and by free-extension flatness for near-complete hosts
(Theorems~\ref{thm:factor-dense-host} and~\ref{thm:factor-rate-dense-host}),
by the sublinear matching variance for co-length one
(Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-sublinear-colength-one}),
together with long deletions past \(\sqrt{n}\) from sparse degrees
(Theorem~\ref{thm:long-sparse-near-complete}) and from every degree
\(d\le n/2-1\) to an \((n-2)\)-regular host
(Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap}), and from a matching into every host of
degree at least \(n/2\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}) and into
every growing degree (Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-growing}) and every
degree at least \(3\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-at-least-three}), and a
Hamilton cycle into every Dirac host (Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac})
and, with failure probability \(o(1)\), into every degree at least \(3\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-wide}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} gives the factor second moment on every
linear host for \(L^2=o(n)\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion-below-sqrt} turns that moment into one deletion of
the same length between linear degrees.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step} is the four-linear-margin deletion for every
\(L=o(n)\). The matching count has relative variance \(O(1/n^2)\) at every
linear degree (Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate}), and one deletion of a
perfect matching then has total variation \(O(1/n)\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:matching-step-l1}). A union bound of \(L\) such steps tends to
\(0\). Peeling \(L\) successive uniform perfect matchings from a uniform graph
above half degree keeps containment sure and the lower law within \(O(L/n)\)
of uniform (Theorem~\ref{thm:successive-peel}), and bounded second moments of
shorter peel counts give \(L^1\) flatness of the ordered peel count
(Theorem~\ref{thm:peel-bounded-to-flat}).
The balanced range past \(\sqrt{n}\) and the other three named statements
stay open.

The attacks that stop at a signed \(O(L^3/n)\) or a disjoint-support
comparison are proved in~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
They are not retried.

\section{Companion results}

\begin{theorem}[v1.0, recorded]
\label{thm:plus-one-recorded}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d=d(n)\) satisfy \(d\to\infty\), \(n-d\to\infty\), and \(dn\) even.
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} whenever \(\min(d,n-d)=o(n)\).
Then there is a coupling of \(G_d\sim\Gnd\) and \(G_{d+1}\sim G(n,d+1)\) with
\(\PP(G_d\subset G_{d+1})=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~1 of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The sandwich rates \(1/9\), \(1/5\), and process-optimal \(1/3\), the hybrid
container, and the matching peel also live in that paper.

\begin{theorem}[One deletion of length \(o(n^{1/3})\), recorded]
\label{thm:third-step-recorded}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy \(\min(d,n-d,d+L,n-d-L)=\Theta(n)\) and
\(L=o(n^{1/3})\), with \(dn\) and \(Ln\) even.
Then there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and a \((d+L)\)-regular
graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is within total variation
\(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

This is the one-deletion theorem of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, from the \(L\)-factor
second moment below \(n^{1/3}\).

\begin{theorem}[Chernoff increments by free splitting, recorded]
\label{thm:free-chernoff-recorded}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(d\to\infty\) with \(dn\) even, and set
\(m:=\lfloor c\sqrt{d\log n}\rfloor\) with \(c>0\) fixed, \(mn\) even, and
\(d+m=o(n^{1/3})\).
Then there is a coupling of \(G\sim\Gnd\) with \(H\sim G(n,d+m)\) such that
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

This is the free-splitting increment of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The coupling is a free configuration split~\cite{Wormald99,JLR00}, not the
matching peel of the rate-\(1/3\) residual.

\section{Open problems}

\begin{conjecture}[Quantitative sandwich]
\label{conj:Q}
There is a constant \(C\) such that for every \(d\ge C\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\), a uniform \(d\)-regular graph can be coupled
between \(G\bigl(n,(1-\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\) and \(G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n\bigr)\)
with failure probability \(o(1)\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Host-restricted splitting]
\label{conj:config-split}
The law of the \((d+m)\)-regular graph obtained by peeling an \(m\)-factor from
the rate-\(1/3\) residual is within total variation \(o(1)\) of \(\mu_{d+m}\),
for \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Residual moment]
\label{conj:residual-moment}
The number of \(m\)-factors of the BIM residual \(R=G^*_0\setminus G\),
conditionally on \(G\), has relative variance \(o(1)\).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Factor second moment past \(\sqrt{n}\)]
\label{conj:factor-moment}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\), and let \(L\) satisfy \(Ln\) even,
\(L\le D\), \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\), and \(\min(L,D-L)=\omega(n^{1/3})\).
The number \(Z\) of \(L\)-factors of a uniform \(D\)-regular graph satisfies
\(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\).
The complementary range \(\min(L,D-L)=o(n^{1/3})\) on linear hosts is
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary}; near-complete hosts are
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-dense-host}; co-length one on sublinear hosts is
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-sublinear-colength-one}.
On every linear host, Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} gives the moment for
every \(L\to\infty\) with \(L^2=o(n)\), that is every length \(o(\sqrt{n})\).
It stops short of \(\sqrt{n}\). Degree \(n/2\) is the special case
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt}.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Factor moment at the repetition scale]
\label{conj:factor-rate}
Under the hypotheses of Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment},
\(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(L^3/n^3)\).
The complementary range \(\min(L,D-L)=o(n^{1/3})\) on linear hosts is
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-rate-complementary}; near-complete hosts are
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-rate-dense-host}.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[One deletion of a long factor]
\label{thm:long-step}
\label{conj:long-step}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\) and \(L\) satisfy \(L=o(n)\),
\(\min(d,n-d,d+L,n-d-L)=\Theta(n)\), and \(dn\), \(Ln\) even.
There is a coupling of a uniform \(d\)-regular graph and a uniform
\((d+L)\)-regular graph with
\[
\PP\bigl(G_d\subseteq G_{d+L}\bigr)=1-o(1).
\]
In particular the coupling exists throughout
\(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The length \(L^2=o(n)\) is already Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion-below-sqrt}.
For the longer range, Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} supplies relative
variance \(O(1/n^2)\) for the perfect-matching count at every degree whose
margins are a fixed positive fraction of \(n\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:matching-step-l1} turns that variance into total variation
\(O(1/n)\) for one matching deletion.
The hypothesis on the four margins keeps every intermediate degree in that
band once \(L=o(n)\).
Each consecutive pair therefore admits a coupling of the two uniform laws in
which the containment fails with probability \(O(1/n)\).
The containment coupling of the lemma has one marginal exactly uniform and the
other within total variation \(O(1/n)\) of uniform.
Couple the displaced graph to an exact uniform graph so that they disagree
with probability equal to that total variation, and keep the exact marginal.
Containment survives on the agreement event.
The resulting kernel sends an exact uniform graph to an exact uniform graph.
Sampling the bottom graph from its uniform law and applying the kernels upward
keeps every marginal uniform.
A graph with no perfect matching in the complement has probability
\(O(1/n^2)\) by Chebyshev's inequality and the same variance bound, so these
empty steps contribute \(O(L/n^2)\) across the chain.
The union bound on the \(L\) containments is \(O(L/n)\) and tends to \(0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Closed cases of the factor conjectures}
\label{sec:factor-complementary}

Inside a fixed \(D\)-regular graph the \(L\)-factors are in bijection with the
\((D-L)\)-factors: the complement of an \(L\)-factor in the host is a
\((D-L)\)-factor. The two counts are therefore identical as random variables
on \(G(n,D)\). Combining that identity with the \(o(n^{1/3})\) moment of
~\cite{PlusOneV1} enters the past-\(\sqrt{n}\) regime along the near-complete
side.

\begin{proposition}[Complementary factor counts agree]
\label{prop:factor-complement-bijection}
Let \(G\) be a \(D\)-regular graph on \(n\) vertices and let \(0\le L\le D\) with
\(Ln\) and \((D-L)n\) even. Writing \(Z_L(G)\) for the number of spanning
\(L\)-regular subgraphs of \(G\), one has \(Z_L(G)=Z_{D-L}(G)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(F\subset G\) is \(L\)-regular and spanning then \(G\setminus F\) is
\((D-L)\)-regular and spanning. The map \(F\mapsto G\setminus F\) is an
involution of the set of spanning regular subgraphs of \(G\) that exchanges
the \(L\)-regular and \((D-L)\)-regular classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Factor second moment for near-complete factors]
\label{thm:factor-complementary}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\), and let \(L\) satisfy \(Ln\) even,
\(L\le D\), and \(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\). Let \(Z\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of a
uniform random \(D\)-regular graph. Then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\).
In particular the bound holds throughout
\(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\) whenever \(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:factor-complement-bijection} gives \(Z=Z_{D-L}\) pathwise on
every \(D\)-regular host. The hypotheses \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\),
\((D-L)n\) even (arranged by the parity of \(Ln\) and \(Dn\)), \(D-L\le D\), and
\(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\) are exactly those of the factor second-moment theorem of
~\cite{PlusOneV1} at length \(D-L\). That theorem yields
\(\Var Z_{D-L}/(\EE Z_{D-L})^2=o(1)\), hence the claim for \(Z\).
If in addition \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\) and \(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\), then
\(L=D-o(n^{1/3})=\Theta(n)\), so \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\). The identity
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n/2-n^{1/3})/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Factor rate for near-complete factors]
\label{thm:factor-rate-complementary}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary} one has
\[
\frac{\Var Z}{(\EE Z)^2}=o\Bigl(\frac{L^3}{n^3}\Bigr).
\]
If in addition \(D-L=1\), then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=O(1/n)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary} supplies \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\).
The same hypotheses force \(L=\Theta(n)\), so \(L^3/n^3=\Theta(1)\). Thus
\(o(1)=o(L^3/n^3)\).
If \(D-L=1\), Proposition~\ref{prop:factor-complement-bijection} identifies \(Z\) with
the number of perfect matchings of a uniform \(D\)-regular graph.
Proposition~band-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, applied with host degree \(D\), supplies
relative variance \(O(1/n)\).
Writing \(D=n/2+t\) with \(|t|\le n/3\), Proposition~distance-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1}
improves this to \(O((t^2+1)/n^2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Factor second moment for co-length one on sublinear hosts]
\label{thm:factor-sublinear-colength-one}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\to\infty\) satisfy
\(Dn\) even and \(D=o(n)\). Set \(L:=D-1\), and assume \(Ln\) even. Let \(Z\) be the
number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \(D\)-regular graph. Then
\[
\frac{\Var Z}{(\EE Z)^2}
=
O\Bigl(\frac1{D^3}+\frac{D}{n}\Bigr)=o(1).
\]
In particular the bound holds with \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\) throughout
\(\omega(n^{1/2})=D=o(n)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:factor-complement-bijection} identifies \(Z\) with the number
\(Y\) of perfect matchings of a uniform \(D\)-regular graph. The sublinear
nesting theorem of~\cite{PlusOneV1} records
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6D^3}+O(D^{-4})+O\Bigl(\frac{D}{n}\Bigr)+o(1),
\]
which is \(O(1/D^3+D/n)\) and tends to \(0\) under \(D\to\infty\) and \(D=o(n)\).
Along \(\omega(n^{1/2})=D=o(n)\) one has \(L=D-1=\omega(n^{1/2})\) and \(L=o(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Factor second moment for near-complete hosts]
\label{thm:factor-dense-host}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(s,\ell\ge 1\) satisfy \(sn\) and \(\ell n\) even,
\(\ell\le s\), and
\[
\frac{\ell(s+\ell)^2}{n}=o(1).
\]
Set \(D:=n-1-s\) and \(L:=D-\ell\).
Let \(Z\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \(D\)-regular graph.
Then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\). In particular the hypotheses force
\(s+\ell=o(n^{1/2})\) and \(L=n-o(n^{1/2})\), so \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
The bound includes every fixed co-length \(\ell\ge 1\) throughout
\(s=o(n^{1/2}/\ell^{1/2})\), and in particular co-length one for
\(n-D=o(n^{1/2})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:factor-complement-bijection} gives \(Z=Z_\ell\) pathwise on every
\(D\)-regular host. Complementation is a bijection between \(D\)-regular graphs and
\(s\)-regular graphs; writing \(S\) for the complement of the host, an \(\ell\)-factor
of the host is precisely an \(\ell\)-regular graph edge-disjoint from \(S\). Thus
\(Z_\ell\) equals the free-extension count \(N_\ell(S)\).
Apply McKay's enumeration of sparse graphs in the complement of a sparse
host~\cite[Theorem~4.6]{McK85}, exactly as in the free-extension flatness lemma
of~\cite{PlusOneV1}: the degree sequence is \(g_i=\ell\) and the forbidden graph is
the \(s\)-regular graph \(S\), with McKay parameter
\(\widehat\Delta=O(\ell(s+\ell))\). The hypothesis forces \(s+\ell=o(n)\), and the
relative error in the exponent is
\[
O\Biggl(\frac{\widehat\Delta^{\,2}}{\ell n}\Biggr)
=
O\Biggl(\frac{\ell(s+\ell)^2}{n}\Biggr)
=
o(1),
\]
uniformly in the choice of \(s\)-regular host. The main terms depend on \(S\) only
through its degree and its number of edges. Hence
\(N_\ell(S)=(1+o(1))\,N_\ell(S')\) for every pair of \(s\)-regular graphs \(S,S'\), and
\(Z\) is \((1+o(1))\) times a deterministic constant as the host varies over
\(\mu_D\). Relative variance \(o(1)\) follows.
The hypothesis with \(\ell\ge 1\) forces \((s+\ell)^2/n=o(1)\), so
\(s+\ell=o(n^{1/2})\); the identity
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(s+\ell)/\sqrt{n}=0\) under that assumption was checked.
Thus \(L=(n-1-s)-\ell=n-o(n^{1/2})\) and \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Factor rate for near-complete hosts]
\label{thm:factor-rate-dense-host}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-dense-host} one has
\[
\frac{\Var Z}{(\EE Z)^2}=o\Bigl(\frac{L^3}{n^3}\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-dense-host} supplies \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=o(1)\) and
\(L=\Theta(n)\), so \(o(1)=o(L^3/n^3)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Long-step is empty on the complementary side]
\label{rem:long-complement-empty}
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-moment} needs the moment bound at degrees \(d+L\) and
\(n-1-d\) simultaneously, with \(\min(d,n-d,d+L,n-d-L)=\Theta(n)\) and
\(L=\omega(n^{1/2})\). At degree \(d+L\), the complementary length is \(d\). The
bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary} therefore needs \(d=o(n^{1/3})\),
which contradicts \(d=\Theta(n)\). The near-complete moment does not feed
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Long deletion from sparse to near-complete]
\label{thm:long-sparse-near-complete}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,\ell\to\infty\) satisfy \(dn\) and \(\ell n\) even and
\(d+\ell=o(n^{1/3})\). Set \(L:=n-1-d-\ell\). Then \(L=\Theta(n)\) with
\(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\), and there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and
a \((d+L)\)-regular graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is
within total variation \(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_\ell\) for the law of an independent pair \((G',F')\) with
\(G'\sim\mu_d\) and \(F'\sim\mu_\ell\), conditioned on being edge-disjoint.
The free-splitting proposition of~\cite{PlusOneV1} realises a coupling whose union
is contiguous to \(\mu_{d+\ell}\); the same configuration-model comparison
of~\cite{Wormald99,JLR00} identifies that law with \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_\ell\) up to
total variation \(o(1)\).
The free-extension flatness lemma of~\cite{PlusOneV1} at length \(d\) against an
\(\ell\)-regular host applies because
\[
\frac{d(d+\ell)^2}{n}=o(1)
\]
throughout \(d+\ell=o(n^{1/3})\): the identity \((n^{1/3})^3/n=1\) was checked, so
any little-o forces the display to \(0\).
Hence \(\PP(G'\cap F'=\emptyset\mid F')\) is \(1+o(1)\) times a constant independent
of the \(\ell\)-regular host \(F'\), and the \(F'\)-marginal of \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_\ell\) is
within total variation \(o(1)\) of \(\mu_\ell\).
Contiguity passes that marginal bound to the free-split factor \(F\).
Let \(H\) be the complement of \(F\). Complementation is a bijection of
\(\ell\)-regular graphs with \((n-1-\ell)\)-regular graphs, so the marginal of
\(H\) is within total variation \(o(1)\) of \(\mu_{n-1-\ell}\).
The degree identity \(d+L=n-1-\ell\) was checked.
On the event that \(G\) and \(F\) are edge-disjoint one has \(G\subset H\), and that
event holds with probability \(1\) in the free-split coupling.
The identity \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n-1-2n^{1/4})/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked, so
\(L=\Theta(n)\) throughout \(d+\ell=o(n^{1/3})\) forces \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
The \(G\)-marginal is within total variation \(o(1)\) of \(\mu_d\) by the same
flatness-plus-contiguity argument with the roles of \(d\) and \(\ell\) exchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}]
\label{rem:long-sparse-vs-conj}
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-sparse-near-complete} is a one-deletion of length
\(\omega(n^{1/2})\), but the short degrees \(d\) and \(n-d-L=\ell\) are
\(o(n^{1/3})\) rather than \(\Theta(n)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step} is the same conclusion with all four margins linear.
The complementary factor count does not reach that band:
Remark~\ref{rem:long-complement-empty} records the obstruction.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Long deletion leaving a matching gap]
\label{thm:long-matching-gap}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d\) satisfy \(dn\) even and \(1\le d\le n/2-1\).
Set \(L:=n-2-d\). Then \(L\ge n/2-1\), so \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\), and there is a
coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and an \((n-2)\)-regular graph \(H\) with
\(G\subset H\) almost surely and with both marginals exactly uniform.
The same conclusion holds with failure probability \(o(1)\) whenever
\(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\) and \(d\le n-3\), by matching concentration on the
complement in place of Dirac's theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First assume \(1\le d\le n/2-1\). Sample \(G\sim\mu_d\). Write \(C\) for the
complement of \(G\), so \(C\sim\mu_{n-1-d}\). The degree bound
\(n-1-d\ge n/2\) was checked, so every such \(C\) has minimum degree at least
\(n/2\). Dirac's theorem therefore supplies a Hamilton cycle in \(C\), and hence
(since \(n\) is even) a perfect matching: the number \(Y\) of perfect matchings of
\(C\) satisfies \(Y\ge 1\) pathwise.
Let \(M\) be a uniform random perfect matching of \(C\). The law of \(G\) is
invariant under the action of \(S_n\) on labelled graphs, and that action is
transitive on the set of perfect matchings. Hence \(M\) is exactly uniform on
perfect matchings.
Set \(H\) for the complement of \(M\). Complementation is a bijection between
perfect matchings and \((n-2)\)-regular graphs, so \(H\sim\mu_{n-2}\) exactly.
Since \(M\subset C\) one has \(G\cap M=\emptyset\), hence \(G\subset H\) almost surely.
The degree identities \(L=(n-2)-d\) and \(n-d-L=2\) were checked. The identity
\((n-2)-(n/2-1)=n/2-1\) was checked, so \(L\ge n/2-1\). The identity
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n/2-1)/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked, so
\(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).

Now assume instead \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\) and \(d\le n-3\), allowing
\(d>n/2-1\). Sample \(G\sim\mu_d\) and let \(C\) be its complement, so
\(C\sim\mu_{n-1-d}\) with \(\min(n-1-d,d)=\Theta(n)\). Let \(Y\) be the number of
perfect matchings of \(C\). Proposition~band-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1} supplies
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(1/n)\). Chebyshev's inequality at relative height \(1/4\)
yields \(\PP(Y=0)=O(1/n)=o(1)\); the identity \((1/n)/(1/4)^2=16/n\) was checked.
On \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) let \(M\) be a uniform random perfect matching of \(C\); on the
complementary event let \(M\) be an independent uniform perfect matching of
\(K_n\). The same \(S_n\)-transitivity argument shows that \(M\sim\mu_1\) exactly, and
\(H:=\mathrm{complement}(M)\) satisfies \(H\sim\mu_{n-2}\) exactly with
\(G\subset H\) on \(\{Y\ge 1\}\). The failure probability is \(o(1)\). As before
\(L=n-2-d=\Theta(n)\) forces \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three linear margins]
\label{rem:long-matching-gap-vs-conj}
Under \(\min(d,n-d)=\Theta(n)\) Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap} has
\(\min(d,n-d,d+L)=\Theta(n)\) and \(n-d-L=2\). The missing linear margin is the
obstruction to Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}; the argument uses transitivity of
\(S_n\) on perfect matchings and does not extend to a gap of length
\(\omega(1)\) with more than one isomorphism type. The Dirac range
\(d\le n/2-1\) already includes every sparse and every linear degree on the
smaller side, so the matching-gap deletion is not confined to the linear band.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Long nesting from a matching into every Dirac host]
\label{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(Dn\) even and \(n/2\le D\le n-1\). Set
\(L:=D-1\). Then \(L\ge n/2-1\), so \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\), and there is a coupling
of a perfect matching \(M\) and a \(D\)-regular graph \(H\) with \(M\subset H\) almost
surely and with both marginals exactly uniform.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sample \(H\sim\mu_D\). The hypothesis \(D\ge n/2\) and Dirac's theorem supply a
Hamilton cycle in \(H\), hence a perfect matching, so the number \(Y\) of perfect
matchings of \(H\) satisfies \(Y\ge 1\) pathwise.
Let \(M\) be a uniform random perfect matching of \(H\). The law of \(H\) is
\(S_n\)-invariant and \(S_n\) acts transitively on perfect matchings, so \(M\) is
exactly uniform. On the same event \(M\subset H\) almost surely.
The identity \(L=D-1\ge n/2-1\) was checked, and
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n/2-1)/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap}]
\label{rem:matching-into-dirac}
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap} realises a \(d\)-regular graph inside an
\((n-2)\)-regular graph for every \(d\le n/2-1\). Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}
is the one-sided matching nesting into every host of Dirac degree, including
degrees other than \(n-2\). The case \(D=n-2\) recovers a matching inside
\(\mu_{n-2}\), which is the upper marginal of Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap}
at \(d=1\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Long nesting of a matching into every growing degree]
\label{thm:long-matching-growing}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} whenever \(D=o(n)\). Let \(n\) be even and let
\(D\to\infty\) satisfy \(Dn\) even and \(D\le n-1\). Set \(L:=D-1\). There is a
coupling of a perfect matching \(M\) and a \(D\)-regular graph \(H\) with both
marginals exactly uniform and with \(\PP(M\subset H)=1-o(1)\). If \(D\ge n/2\) the
containment holds almost surely, by Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}.
Throughout \(\omega(n^{1/2})=D=o(n)\) one has \(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
Throughout \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\) one has \(L=\Theta(n)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The case \(D\ge n/2\) is Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}.
Assume therefore \(D<n/2\). Sample \(H\sim\mu_D\), and write \(Y\) for the number of
perfect matchings of \(H\).
If \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\), Proposition~band-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1} supplies
\(\Var Y/(\EE Y)^2=O(1/n)\). Chebyshev's inequality at relative height \(1/2\) yields
\(\PP(Y=0)=O(1/n)\); the identity \((1/n)/(1/2)^2=4/n\) was checked.
If instead \(D=o(n)\), the sublinear nesting theorem of~\cite{PlusOneV1} supplies
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}
=
\frac1{6D^3}+O(D^{-4})+O\Bigl(\frac{D}{n}\Bigr)+o(1),
\]
which tends to \(0\), so Chebyshev at relative height \(1/2\) yields \(\PP(Y=0)=o(1)\).
On \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) let \(M\) be a uniform random perfect matching of \(H\); on the
complementary event let \(M\) be an independent uniform perfect matching of \(K_n\).
The law of \(H\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, \(S_n\) acts transitively on perfect matchings,
and \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, so the conditional law of \(M\) on
\(\{Y\ge 1\}\) is exactly \(\mu_1\). The mixture is therefore exactly \(\mu_1\), while
\(H\) is exactly \(\mu_D\). On \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) one has \(M\subset H\), and the failure
probability is \(o(1)\).
Throughout \(\omega(n^{1/2})=D=o(n)\) one has \(L=D-1\), and the identity
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n^{2/3}-1)/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked, so
\(n^{1/2}=o(L)=o(n)\).
Throughout \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\) one has \(L=\Theta(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hamilton cycle inside every Dirac host]
\label{thm:long-hamilton-dirac}
Let \(n\ge 4\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(Dn\) even and \(n/2\le D\le n-1\).
There is a coupling of a Hamilton cycle \(C\) and a \(D\)-regular graph \(H\) with
\(C\subset H\) almost surely, with \(H\) exactly uniform, and with \(C\) exactly uniform
among Hamilton cycles on \([n]\). The degree gap \(D-2\) is at least \(n/2-2\), so
\(n^{1/2}=o(D-2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sample \(H\sim\mu_D\). Dirac's theorem supplies a Hamilton cycle in every graph of
minimum degree at least \(n/2\), so the number of Hamilton cycles of \(H\) is at
least \(1\). Let \(C\) be a uniform random Hamilton cycle of \(H\). The law of \(H\)
is \(S_n\)-invariant. Any two Hamilton cycles on the labelled vertex set \([n]\)
differ by a permutation of the vertices, so \(S_n\) acts transitively on their edge
sets. Hence \(C\) is exactly uniform among Hamilton cycles, and \(C\subset H\) almost
surely. The identity \((n/2)-2=(n-4)/2\) was checked, so \(D-2\ge n/2-2\). The
identity \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(n/2-2)/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hamilton cycle in every degree at least \(3\)]
\label{thm:long-hamilton-wide}
Let \(Dn\) be even and let \(3\le D\le n-1\). There is a coupling of a Hamilton
cycle \(C\) and a \(D\)-regular graph \(H\) with both marginals exact and with
\(\PP(C\subset H)=1-o(1)\). If \(D\ge n/2\) the containment holds almost surely,
by Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac}. If \(\sqrt{n}\log n<D\), then
\(n^{1/2}=o(D-2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(D\ge n/2\), Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac} supplies the coupling with
failure probability \(0\). Assume therefore \(D<n/2\), and sample \(H\sim\mu_D\).
The coupling is the mixture below once \(\PP(H\text{ is Hamiltonian})=1-o(1)\).

Along any subsequence of \(n\), pass to a further subsequence on which either \(D\)
is constant or \(D\to\infty\).
If \(D=r\ge 3\) is constant, \cite[Theorem~1]{RW94} gives the probability \(1-o(1)\).
If \(D\to\infty\), the proof of \cite[Theorem~1(b)]{CFR02} gives the same
probability whenever \(10^{7}\le D\le c_0 n\) for a sufficiently small absolute
constant \(c_0>0\).
The identity \(\lim_{n\to\infty}2\sqrt{n}\log n/n=0\) was checked, so
\(\sqrt{n}\log n=o(n)\) and therefore \(\sqrt{n}\log n<c_0 n\) for all large \(n\).
The identity \(\lim_{n\to\infty}10^{7}/(\sqrt{n}\log n)=0\) was checked, so
\(10^{7}<\sqrt{n}\log n\) for all large \(n\).
Thus, for large \(n\), either \(10^{7}\le D\le c_0 n\), in which case
\cite[Theorem~1(b)]{CFR02} applies, or else \(D>c_0 n\).
In the latter case \(D>\sqrt{n}\log n\), and \(D<n/2\) forces \(D\le n-4\) for every
\(n\ge 8\), so \cite[Theorem~2.4]{KSVW01} applies.
Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence along which
\(\PP(H\text{ is Hamiltonian})\to 1\), and the probability tends to \(1\).

On that event let \(C\) be a uniform random Hamilton cycle of \(H\); on the
complementary event let \(C\) be an independent uniform Hamilton cycle of \(K_n\).
The law of \(H\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, the event that \(H\) is Hamiltonian is
\(S_n\)-invariant, and \(S_n\) acts transitively on Hamilton-cycle edge sets, as in
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac}.
The mixture keeps both marginals exact, and the failure probability is \(o(1)\).
If \(\sqrt{n}\log n<D\), then \(D-2>\sqrt{n}\log n-2\), and the identity
\(\lim_{n\to\infty}(\sqrt{n}\log n-2)/n^{1/2}=\infty\) was checked, so
\(n^{1/2}=o(D-2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matching inside every degree at least \(3\)]
\label{thm:long-matching-at-least-three}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(Dn\) be even, and let \(3\le D\le n-1\). There is a
coupling of a perfect matching \(M\) and a \(D\)-regular graph \(H\) with both
marginals exactly uniform and with \(\PP(M\subset H)=1-o(1)\). If \(D\ge n/2\)
the containment holds almost surely, by Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(D\ge n/2\), Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac} supplies the coupling
with failure probability \(0\). Assume therefore \(D<n/2\).
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-wide} shows that a uniform
\(D\)-regular graph is Hamiltonian with probability \(1-o(1)\).
Since \(n\) is even, a Hamilton cycle contains a perfect matching, so on that
event the number \(Y\) of perfect matchings is at least \(1\).
On \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) let \(M\) be a uniform random perfect matching of \(H\); on the
complementary event let \(M\) be an independent uniform perfect matching of \(K_n\).
The law of \(H\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, \(S_n\) acts transitively on perfect matchings,
and \(\{Y\ge 1\}\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, so the mixture is exactly \(\mu_1\), while
\(H\) is exactly \(\mu_D\). The failure probability is \(o(1)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-growing} reaches the same conclusion when
\(D\to\infty\), by a variance bound. The present argument does not need
\(D\to\infty\): it uses Hamiltonicity, and therefore also covers degrees that
stay bounded or oscillate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Not a uniform \(2\)-factor]
\label{rem:hamilton-not-mu2}
The marginal of \(C\) in Theorems~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac}
and~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-wide} is uniform on Hamilton cycles.
A uniform \(2\)-regular graph is a disjoint union of cycles and is
disconnected with probability \(1-o(1)\), so this marginal is at total variation
\(1-o(1)\) from \(\mu_2\). Neither theorem feeds Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
\(S_n\)-transitivity is used only to keep the Hamilton-cycle marginal exact.
It does not identify selection item (iii) across isomorphism types.
\end{remark}

\section{Reductions already proved}

The next four statements are the process-to-Q, window, long-from-moment, and
selection reductions of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The proofs are repeated so the sequel is self-contained.

\begin{theorem}[Process conjecture implies Chernoff sandwich]
\label{thm:process-to-Q}
Assume~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds: for every \(d\ge C\log n\) and
\(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large absolute, there is a coupling
as in Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \((G_i)_{0\le i\le\binom{n}{2}}\) be the random graph process on \([n]\), and let
\(G\sim\Gnd\) be coupled to the process as in~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}.
Fix \(\varepsilon\ge C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\) with \(C\) large, and set
\[
i_-:=\Bigl\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad
i_+:=\Bigl\lceil(1+\varepsilon)\frac{dn}{2}\Bigr\rceil.
\]
A Chernoff-plus-union bound gives
\(\PP(\Delta(G_{i_-})>d)=o(1)\) and \(\PP(\delta(G_{i_+})<d)=o(1)\)
once \(C\) is large enough (the Chernoff-floor proposition of~\cite{PlusOneV1}).
On \(\{\Delta(G_{i_-})\le d\}\), Conjecture~1.3 of~\cite{BIM25} yields
\(G_{i_-}\subset G\).
On \(\{\delta(G_{i_+})\ge d\}\) it yields \(G\subset G_{i_+}\).
The process graphs \(G_{i_\pm}\) couple with the binomial graphs of matching
density, losing \(o(1)\) probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Final-window reduction]
\label{thm:window}
Suppose that whenever \(d\ge C_3\log n\) and
\[
C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{d}}
\le\varepsilon
\le C_3\Bigl(\frac{\log n}{d}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\]
the following holds: if \((G,G^*_0)\) is a coupling at rate
\(\varepsilon_0:=C_3(\log n/d)^{1/3}\) from the process-optimal sandwich of~\cite{PlusOneV1},
then there exists a coupling of a graph \(H\sim G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) with
\(\PP(G\subset H)=1-o(1)\).
Assume the symmetric lower-window statement.
Then Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} holds for all \(d\ge C_3\log n\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the rate-\(1/3\) sandwich of~\cite{PlusOneV1} to obtain \(G\subset G^*_0\).
The final-window hypothesis at the target Chernoff rate
\(\varepsilon=C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\le\varepsilon_0\) produces the upper graph.
The symmetric argument produces the lower graph.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Long step from the factor moment]
\label{thm:long-from-moment}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment} at every degree \(D\) with
\(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\).
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
Then there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and a \((d+L)\)-regular
graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is within total variation
\(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(v=o(1)\) be the relative variance of the \(L\)-factor count at degree
\(d+L\), and set \(\alpha=v^{1/3}\).
Chebyshev costs at most \(\alpha\).
The factor-to-TV lemma of~\cite{PlusOneV1} places the upper marginal within
total variation \(2\alpha=o(1)\) of \(\mu_{d+L}\).
The lower marginal is the uniform \(d\)-regular law reweighted by the number of
\((d+L)\)-regular supergraphs, which that paper identifies with the \(L\)-factor
count at degree \(n-1-d\).
The same Chebyshev bound applies there.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Long step from in-probability flatness]
\label{thm:long-from-flat}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
Write \(Z_D\) for the number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \(D\)-regular
graph, and suppose that both
\[
\frac{Z_{d+L}}{\EE Z_{d+L}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{Z_{n-1-d}}{\EE Z_{n-1-d}}
\]
tend to \(1\) in probability.
Then there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and a \((d+L)\)-regular
graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is within total variation
\(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(D=d+L\) and let \(\mathcal T_D\) be the set of \(D\)-regular graphs on \([n]\).
Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of pairs \((G,H)\) with \(G\) \(d\)-regular,
\(H\) \(D\)-regular, and \(G\subset H\).
The complement in \(H\) of a \(d\)-regular spanning subgraph is an \(L\)-factor of
\(H\), and every \(L\)-factor arises once this way, so the number of partners of
\(H\) in \(\mathcal S\) is \(Z_D(H)\).
Thus \(|\mathcal S|=|\mathcal T_D|\,\EE Z_D\), and the law \(\nu\) of the upper graph in a
uniform pair from \(\mathcal S\) is \(\nu(H)=Z_D(H)/(|\mathcal T_D|\,\EE Z_D)\).
The uniform law is \(\mu_D(H)=1/|\mathcal T_D|\), so
\[
\dTV(\nu,\mu_D)
=
\frac12\,\EE\Bigl|\frac{Z_D}{\EE Z_D}-1\Bigr|.
\]
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and write \(B\) for the event
\(|Z_D-\EE Z_D|\ge\alpha\,\EE Z_D\).
In-probability flatness gives \(\PP(B)\le\delta\) for every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\)
and all large \(n\).
On the complement, \(Z_D\ge(1-\alpha)\EE Z_D\), and that complement contains at
least \((1-\delta)|\mathcal T_D|\) graphs, so the sum of \(Z_D\) over \(B\) is at most
\[
|\mathcal T_D|\,\EE Z_D\bigl(1-(1-\delta)(1-\alpha)\bigr)
=
|\mathcal T_D|\,\EE Z_D\,(\alpha+\delta-\alpha\delta).
\]
Splitting \(\EE|Z_D/\EE Z_D-1|\) into \(B\) and its complement, the graphs off
\(B\) contribute at most \(\alpha\), and the graphs on \(B\) contribute at most
\(\alpha+\delta-\alpha\delta+\delta\).
The sum is at most \(2\alpha+2\delta\), so
\(\dTV(\nu,\mu_D)\le\alpha+\delta\).
Sending \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\alpha\to 0\) yields total variation \(o(1)\).

The lower graph in a uniform pair from \(\mathcal S\) is the uniform \(d\)-regular
law reweighted by the number \(W\) of \((d+L)\)-regular supergraphs.
The complement of a \(d\)-regular graph is \((n-1-d)\)-regular, and an
\(L\)-factor of the complement is the edge set of such a supergraph, so \(W\) has
the same distribution as \(Z_{n-1-d}\).
The same splitting, using in-probability flatness at degree \(n-1-d\), places
the lower marginal within total variation \(o(1)\) of \(\mu_d\).
On \(\mathcal S\) one has \(G\subset H\).
\end{proof}

Relative variance \(o(1)\) implies the hypothesis, by Chebyshev's inequality, and
that route is Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-moment}.
The converse fails: a single graph with an exponentially large factor count
can dominate \(\EE[Z^2]\) while carrying an arbitrarily small fraction of
\(\EE Z\), which is all the total-variation estimate sees.
Flatness of the factor count on \(L=\omega(\sqrt{n})\) is still open.
The deletion itself is Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}, by the matching chain
rather than by that factor count.
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion-below-sqrt} gives the deletion for
\(L^2=o(n)\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:third-step-recorded} already
occupies \(L=o(n^{1/3})\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-exact-upper} removes the upper factor count from the list:
the upper graph can be sampled exactly, and only one inverse-weighted extension
count remains.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-peel} replaces that count by the number of ordered
sequences of \(L\) successive perfect matchings.
Proposition~\ref{prop:peel-budget} sums the matching-scale increment: under a
recurrence that adds \(O(1/n)\) at each matching, the relative variance of the
full sequence is \(O(L/n)\) and the long step follows for every \(L=o(n)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:aligned-peel} is the Cauchy--Schwarz form of the same
step. Its solution is \(O(L^2/n)\), which stops at \(\sqrt{n}\).

\begin{theorem}[Long step with an exact upper graph]
\label{thm:long-exact-upper}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
Write \(Z(H)\) for the number of \(L\)-factors of a \((d+L)\)-regular graph \(H\), and
write
\[
a(G)
=
\sum_{\substack{H\supset G\\ Z(H)>0}}\frac1{Z(H)}
\]
for the sum over \((d+L)\)-regular graphs containing a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\).
Let \(\delta=\PP(Z=0)\) and let \(\bar a\) be the average of \(a\) over the
\(d\)-regular graphs.
Sample \(H\) from the uniform \((d+L)\)-regular law.
If \(Z(H)\ge 1\), delete a uniform \(L\)-factor of \(H\) and call the remainder
\(G\); if \(Z(H)=0\), sample \(G\) from the uniform \(d\)-regular law
independently of \(H\).
Then \(H\) is exactly uniform, \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-\delta\), and
\[
\dTV(G,\mu_d)
=
\frac{1-\delta}{2}\,
\EE\Bigl|\frac{a}{\bar a}-1\Bigr|.
\]
In particular, \(\delta=o(1)\) and \(a/\bar a\to 1\) in \(L^1\) produce both
marginals within total variation \(o(1)\) of uniform, with containment failing
on an event of probability \(o(1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph \(H\) is drawn from \(\mu_{d+L}\), so its marginal is exact.
Containment fails precisely on \(\{Z=0\}\).
For a fixed \(d\)-regular graph \(g\),
\[
\PP(G=g)
=
\sum_{\substack{H\supset g\\ Z(H)>0}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{d+L}|\,Z(H)}
+
\frac{\delta}{|\mathcal T_d|}
=
\frac{a(g)}{|\mathcal T_{d+L}|}
+
\frac{\delta}{|\mathcal T_d|},
\]
where \(\mathcal T_D\) is the set of \(D\)-regular graphs.
Summing \(a\) over \(d\)-regular graphs counts each host with \(Z>0\) once, so
\[
\sum_g a(g)
=
(1-\delta)\,|\mathcal T_{d+L}|
\]
and therefore \(\bar a=(1-\delta)\,|\mathcal T_{d+L}|/|\mathcal T_d|\).
The uniform probability is \(1/|\mathcal T_d|\), and
\((1-\delta)/|\mathcal T_d|=\bar a/|\mathcal T_{d+L}|\), so
\[
\PP(G=g)-\frac1{|\mathcal T_d|}
=
\frac{a(g)}{|\mathcal T_{d+L}|}
-
\frac{1-\delta}{|\mathcal T_d|}
=
\frac{a(g)-\bar a}{|\mathcal T_{d+L}|}.
\]
Summing the absolute values and dividing by \(2\) produces
\[
\dTV(G,\mu_d)
=
\frac{|\mathcal T_d|}{2\,|\mathcal T_{d+L}|}\,\EE|a-\bar a|
=
\frac{1-\delta}{2}\,
\EE\Bigl|\frac{a}{\bar a}-1\Bigr|,
\]
where the last step substitutes \(|\mathcal T_d|/|\mathcal T_{d+L}|=(1-\delta)/\bar a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The upper count need not be flat]
\label{rem:exact-upper-eight}
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-flat} asks for flatness of both factor counts, because
the uniform containment pair reweights each side by its own count.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-exact-upper} uses the upper count only through the
weights \(1/Z\). A host with a large count contributes less per factor, so a
spike in \(Z\) need not move \(a\).
On eight vertices, with \(d=2\) and \(L=2\), every \(4\)-regular graph has a
\(2\)-factor, so \(\delta=0\). There are \(19355\) such hosts and \(3507\)
graphs of degree \(2\). The count \(Z\) attains \(90\) on each of the \(35\)
copies of \(K_{4,4}\) and is at least \(42\) on every host, so its maximum-to-minimum
ratio is \(15/7\). The same exhaustive count gives maximum-to-minimum ratio
\(409095/397144\) for \(a\), and total variation \(1806808/371712775<1/200\) for
the lower graph. The identities \(4!\,3!/2+3\cdot 3\cdot 2=90\),
\(\binom{8}{4}/2=35\), \(90/42=15/7\), and
\((9091/1610)/(198572/36225)=409095/397144\) were checked, as was
\(200\cdot 1806808/371712775<1\).

The value \(90\) is the number of \(2\)-factors of \(K_{4,4}\).
On eight vertices a \(2\)-factor of a bipartite graph is an \(8\)-cycle or a
disjoint pair of \(4\)-cycles.
The number of Hamilton cycles in \(K_{4,4}\) is \(4!\,3!/2=72\): relative to a
fixed vertex of the first part, order the remaining three vertices of that part
in \(3!\) ways and the four vertices of the second part in \(4!\) ways, and divide
by \(2\) for the direction of the cycle.
A decomposition into two \(4\)-cycles uses a partition of each part into two
pairs. There are three such partitions of a \(4\)-set. Matching the two
partitions and placing a \(4\)-cycle on each matched pair can be done in
\(3\cdot 3\cdot 2=18\) ways.
The total is \(72+18=90\).
The labelled copies of \(K_{4,4}\) are the unordered bipartitions into two sets
of size \(4\), and there are \(\binom{8}{4}/2=35\) of them.

The same enumeration splits \(a\). For a \(2\)-regular graph \(g\), let \(W(g)\)
be the number of \(2\)-factors of its complement and let \(\mu(g)\) be the
average of \(1/Z(H)\) over the hosts \(H=g\cup F\). Then \(a(g)=W(g)\mu(g)\).
The values of \(W\) are the integers from \(248\) to \(255\). The
maximum-to-minimum ratio of \(\mu\) is \(6227311/6205375=1+21936/6205375\).
The mean of \(a\) is \(2765/501=19355/3507\). The covariance of \(W\) and \(\mu\)
on these \(3507\) graphs is positive, so a larger factor count of the
complement is not offset by a smaller conditional mean of \(1/Z\). The narrow
range of \(W\) is the \(2\)-factor count of a \(5\)-regular graph, the
complement of a \(2\)-factor on eight vertices. The ratio
\(409095/397144\) for \(a\) is the ratio for that near-complete count, not a
cancellation that removes a factor-count spike when both degrees are linear.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Long step from an ordered matching peel]
\label{thm:long-from-peel}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
Write \(P_D\) for the number of ordered sequences of \(L\) successive perfect
matchings in a uniform random \(D\)-regular graph: the first matching is a
perfect matching of the graph, and each later matching is a perfect matching
of the graph that remains.
Suppose that both
\[
\frac{P_{d+L}}{\EE P_{d+L}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{P_{n-1-d}}{\EE P_{n-1-d}}
\]
tend to \(1\) in probability.
Then there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and a
\((d+L)\)-regular graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is
within total variation \(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal T_D\) be the set of \(D\)-regular graphs, and write
\(\pi=\EE P_{d+L}\) and \(\kappa=\EE P_{n-1-d}\).
Sample \(H\) from \(\mu_{d+L}\).
If \(P_{d+L}(H)\ge 1\), draw a uniform ordered sequence of \(L\) successive
perfect matchings of \(H\) and let \(G\) be the graph that remains; if
\(P_{d+L}(H)=0\), sample \(G\) from \(\mu_d\) independently of \(H\).
The upper marginal is exactly \(\mu_{d+L}\).
The union of the sequence is an \(L\)-factor of \(H\), so on
\(\{P_{d+L}\ge 1\}\) one has \(G\subset H\) with \(G\) \(d\)-regular.
In-probability flatness forces \(\delta=\PP(P_{d+L}=0)\) to tend to \(0\), and
therefore \(\PP(G\subset H)=1-\delta=1-o(1)\).

For an \(L\)-regular graph \(F\), write \(D(F)\) for the number of ordered
decompositions of \(F\) into \(L\) perfect matchings.
For a \(d\)-regular graph \(g\), the complement \(C(g)\) is
\((n-1-d)\)-regular, and the ordered sequences whose removal leaves \(g\) are
exactly the decompositions of the \(L\)-factors of \(C(g)\).
Hence, writing \(H_F=g\cup F\),
\[
\PP(G=g)
=
\sum_{F\,:\,D(F)>0}
\frac{D(F)}{|\mathcal T_{d+L}|\,P_{d+L}(H_F)}
+
\frac{\delta}{|\mathcal T_d|}.
\]
The sum of \(D(F)\) over the \(L\)-factors of \(C(g)\) is \(P_{n-1-d}(C(g))\),
because every ordered sequence in the complement unions to one such factor.
Define
\[
b(g)
=
\sum_{F\,:\,D(F)>0}
\frac{D(F)}{P_{d+L}(H_F)}.
\]
For each host with \(P_{d+L}>0\), the decompositions of its \(L\)-factors
partition its ordered sequences, so \(\sum_g b(g)=(1-\delta)|\mathcal T_{d+L}|\).
The average of \(b\) is therefore
\(\bar b=(1-\delta)\,|\mathcal T_{d+L}|/|\mathcal T_d|\).
The same algebra as in Theorem~\ref{thm:long-exact-upper} gives
\[
\dTV(G,\mu_d)
=
\frac{1-\delta}{2}\,
\EE\Bigl|\frac{b}{\bar b}-1\Bigr|.
\]

It remains to show that the expectation tends to \(0\).
Write \(Q(g)=P_{n-1-d}(C(g))\) and \(c=\kappa/\pi\), and write
\(\gamma=|\mathcal T_{d+L}|/|\mathcal T_d|\), so \(\bar b=(1-\delta)\gamma\).
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/8]\).
In-probability flatness supplies \(\eta\le\alpha\) and \(\delta\le\alpha\), for
all large \(n\), such that both
\(\PP(|P_{d+L}-\pi|\ge\alpha\pi)\le\eta\) and
\(\PP(|Q-\kappa|\ge\alpha\kappa)\le\eta\).
The same degree sum as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-flat}
converts the second of these into \(\EE|Q/\kappa-1|\le 2\alpha+2\eta\): the
identity \(1-(1-\eta)(1-\alpha)=\alpha+\eta-\alpha\eta\) was checked, so the
graphs outside the concentration window hold at most the fraction
\(\alpha+\eta\) of the total mass of \(Q\), and
\[
\EE\Bigl|\frac{Q}{\kappa}-1\Bigr|
\le
\alpha+(\alpha+\eta)+\eta
=
2\alpha+2\eta.
\]
Thus \(\EE|Q/\pi-c|\le c(2\alpha+2\eta)\).

On the window \(|P_{d+L}-\pi|<\alpha\pi\) one has \(P_{d+L}>(1-\alpha)\pi\), so
\[
\Bigl|\frac1{P_{d+L}}-\frac1\pi\Bigr|
=
\frac{|P_{d+L}-\pi|}{P_{d+L}\,\pi}
<
\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)\pi}.
\]
The minimum of \(2\alpha-\alpha/(1-\alpha)\) on \(0\le\alpha\le 1/2\) is \(0\),
so \(\alpha/(1-\alpha)\le 2\alpha\) and the difference of reciprocals is at most
\(2\alpha/\pi\).
Split \(b=b_{\mathrm{good}}+b_{\mathrm{bad}}\) according to that window.
The good part differs from the corresponding part of \(Q/\pi\) by at most
\((2\alpha/\pi)Q\), and \(\EE[(2\alpha/\pi)Q]=2\alpha c\).
The bad part contributes at most \(\eta\gamma\) through the terms \(D(F)/P_{d+L}\),
because those terms sum to \(1\) on each bad host, and at most
\(\gamma(\alpha+\eta)\) through the comparison terms \(D(F)/\pi\), because the
bad hosts hold at most the fraction \(\alpha+\eta\) of the total mass of
\(P_{d+L}\).
Therefore
\[
\EE\Bigl|b-\frac{Q}{\pi}\Bigr|
\le
2\alpha\, c+\gamma(\alpha+2\eta),
\]
and
\[
\EE|b-c|
\le
c(4\alpha+2\eta)+\gamma(\alpha+2\eta).
\]
The same bound dominates \(|\bar b-c|\).
Since \(\bar b\le\gamma\),
\[
c(1-4\alpha-2\eta)
\le
\gamma(1+\alpha+2\eta).
\]
At \(\alpha=\eta=1/8\) the coefficient of \(c\) is \(1/4\) and
\((1+1/8+2/8)/(1/4)=11/2\), both checked.
For every smaller pair one has \(1+\alpha+2\eta\le 1+1/8+2/8\) and
\(1-4\alpha-2\eta\ge 1/4\), so \(c/\gamma\le 11/2\) throughout
\(\alpha,\eta\le 1/8\).
Also \(\bar b\ge(1-\eta)\gamma\). Combining the comparisons gives
\[
\EE\Bigl|\frac{b}{\bar b}-1\Bigr|
\le
\frac{576}{7}\,\alpha,
\]
where \(576/7\) is the checked value of
\(2\bigl((11/2)\cdot 6+3\bigr)/(7/8)\) and the factors \(6\) and \(3\) are the
bounds \(4\alpha+2\eta\le 6\alpha\) and \(\alpha+2\eta\le 3\alpha\).
The total variation is at most half of this quantity.
Sending \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\alpha\to 0\) makes it \(o(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Matching peels add through every \(o(n)\) length]
\label{prop:peel-budget}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy the hypotheses of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
For \(1\le j\le L\) and for every degree \(D\) with
\(d\le D\le d+L\), write \(P_j\) for the number of ordered sequences of \(j\)
successive perfect matchings in a uniform random \(D\)-regular graph.
Suppose there is a constant \(C\), independent of \(j\) and \(n\), such that
for every such degree and every \(j<L\),
\[
\frac{\Var P_{j+1}}{(\EE P_{j+1})^2}
\le
\frac{\Var P_j}{(\EE P_j)^2}
+
\frac{C}{n}.
\]
Then \(\Var P_L/(\EE P_L)^2=O(L/n)\).
In particular the hypothesis \(L=o(n)\) forces \(P_L/\EE P_L\to 1\) in
probability at the degrees \(d+L\) and \(n-1-d\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-peel} supplies the coupling in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~band-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1} gives
\(\Var P_1/(\EE P_1)^2=O(1/n)\) at every linear degree.
The recurrence therefore yields
\[
\frac{\Var P_L}{(\EE P_L)^2}
\le
O\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr)
+
\frac{C(L-1)}{n}
=
O\Bigl(\frac{L}{n}\Bigr).
\]
The identity \((L/m)(m^2/n)=(Lm)/n\) was checked, and the case \(m=1\) is the
comparison of the sum with \(L/n\).
Whenever \(L=o(n)\) the right-hand side tends to \(0\).
Chebyshev's inequality gives the in-probability flatness required by
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-peel}, at degree \(d+L\) and at degree \(n-1-d\).
\end{proof}

The recurrence is the remaining estimate.
It asks for one additional perfect matching in the peel count, on a uniform
linear host, with relative variance increased by \(O(1/n)\).
That is the scale already proved for a single matching.
The sum of \(L\) such increments is \(O(L/n)\), which tends to \(0\) throughout
the range of Conjecture~\ref{conj:long-step}.

\begin{lemma}[Size-biased deletion density]
\label{lem:deletion-density}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(D\) satisfy \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\).
Let \((H,M)\) be a uniform random pair consisting of a \(D\)-regular graph and a
perfect matching of that graph, and write \(G=H\setminus M\).
Then \(G\) is \((D-1)\)-regular, and its law has Radon--Nikodym derivative
\(W/\EE W\) with respect to the uniform \((D-1)\)-regular graph, where \(W(g)\) is
the number of perfect matchings in the complement of \(g\).
In particular
\[
\EE\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{W}{\EE W}-1\Bigr)^2\Bigr]
=
O\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\EE\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{W}{\EE W}-1\Bigr)^4\Bigr]
=
O\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A pair with remainder \(g\) is a perfect matching of the complement of \(g\), so
the number of such pairs is \(W(g)\).
The complement of a \((D-1)\)-regular graph has degree \(n-D\).
Thus \(W\) has the law of the perfect-matching count in the uniform
\((n-D)\)-regular graph.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} supplies the second-moment bound, and
Lemma~band-fourth of~\cite{PlusOneV1} supplies the fourth-moment bound.
Both apply because \(\min(D,n-D)=\Theta(n)\) keeps the degree \(n-D\) inside
the linear band.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One deletion shifts a statistic by \(O(1/n)\)]
\label{lem:size-bias-shift}
Let \(X\) be a real function of a uniform \((D-1)\)-regular graph, with mean
\(\mu\neq 0\), and write
\(\varepsilon=\EE[(X/\mu-1)^2]\).
Under the size-biased law of Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion-density},
\[
\bigl|\EE_{\mathrm{b}}[X]-\mu\bigr|
\le
|\mu|\sqrt{\varepsilon\cdot O(1/n^2)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The size-biased expectation is \(\EE[XW]/\EE W\).
Since \(\EE[W/\EE W-1]=0\),
\[
\EE_{\mathrm{b}}[X]-\mu
=
\EE\bigl[(X-\mu)(W/\EE W-1)\bigr].
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and the second-moment bound of
Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion-density} bound the right-hand side by
\(\sqrt{\Var(X)\cdot O(1/n^2)}=|\mu|\sqrt{\varepsilon\cdot O(1/n^2)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Aligned peel recurrence stops at the square root]
\label{prop:aligned-peel}
Let \(C>0\) and suppose \(\varepsilon_1\le C/n\) and
\[
\varepsilon_{j+1}
\le
\bigl(\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}+\sqrt{C/n}\bigr)^2
\]
for every \(j<L\).
Then \(\varepsilon_L\le C L^2/n\).
The bound \(C L^2/n\) tends to \(0\) if and only if \(L=o(\sqrt{n})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The case \(L=1\) is the hypothesis.
If \(\varepsilon_j\le C j^2/n\), then
\[
\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}+\sqrt{C/n}
\le
\sqrt{C}\,(j+1)/\sqrt{n},
\]
and squaring gives \(\varepsilon_{j+1}\le C(j+1)^2/n\).
The identity
\((j/\sqrt{n}+1/\sqrt{n})^2=(j+1)^2/n\) was checked.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion-density} is the source of the square root in that
recurrence.
Under the uniform pair, a statistic of the remainder with relative variance
\(\varepsilon\) has its mean shifted by the covariance against \(W/\EE W\).
Cauchy--Schwarz and the second-moment bound of the lemma bound that
covariance by \(\sqrt{\varepsilon\cdot O(1/n^2)}\).
The same alignment, fed with the older second-moment bound \(O(1/n)\), is
Proposition~\ref{prop:aligned-peel} and stops at \(\sqrt{n}\).
With the bound now in the lemma, the same formal recurrence solves through
every length \(o(n)\).

\begin{proposition}[Formal peel bound with the linear matching variance]
\label{prop:aligned-peel-square}
Let \(C>0\) and suppose \(\varepsilon_1\le C/n^2\) and
\[
\varepsilon_{j+1}
\le
\bigl(\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}+C/n\bigr)^2
\]
for every \(j<L\).
Then \(\varepsilon_L\le C L^2/n^2\).
Whenever \(L=o(n)\) the right-hand side tends to \(0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The case \(L=1\) is the hypothesis.
If \(\varepsilon_j\le C j^2/n^2\), then
\(\sqrt{\varepsilon_j}+C/n\le \sqrt{C}\,(j+1)/n\),
and squaring gives \(\varepsilon_{j+1}\le C(j+1)^2/n^2\).
The identity \((j/n+1/n)^2=(j+1)^2/n^2\) was checked.
\end{proof}

The recurrence is not proved. Lemma~\ref{lem:size-bias-shift} is the mean
shift of one statistic. The relative variance under the size-biased law is
\[
\EE_{\mathrm{b}}\bigl[(X/\mu-1)^2\bigr]
=
\varepsilon
+
\operatorname{Cov}\bigl((X/\mu-1)^2,\,W/\EE W\bigr).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the covariance by
\(\sqrt{\EE[(X/\mu-1)^4]\cdot O(1/n^2)}\).
The fourth-moment bound on \(W\) in Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion-density} is
\(O(1/n^2)\), the same order as the second moment, so it does not put a factor
\(1/n\) in front of \(\varepsilon\).
Without a fourth-moment bound on the peel count, the covariance does not
close the recurrence.
Proposition~\ref{prop:aligned-peel-square} says
what the ordered-peel variances would satisfy if each step obeyed that
Cauchy--Schwarz alignment.
It concerns \(P_j\), the number of ordered matching sequences.
An \(L\)-factor \(F\) contributes its number \(D(F)\) of ordered decompositions
into perfect matchings, so \(P_L=\sum_F D(F)\).
For \(L=2\), a \(2\)-factor decomposes if and only if every cycle is even, and
then \(D(F)=2^{c(F)}\) where \(c(F)\) is the number of cycles.
Flatness of \(P_L\) does not identify flatness of the factor count \(Z_L\).
The additive increment in Proposition~\ref{prop:peel-budget} remains a
separate unproved cancellation.

\begin{theorem}[Successive uniform peeling above half degree]
\label{thm:successive-peel}
Let \(n\) be even, let \(c\in(0,1/2]\), and let \(D\) and \(L\ge 1\) satisfy
\(D\le(1-c)n\) and \(D-L+1\ge n/2\).
Let \(H_0\) be a uniform \(D\)-regular graph, and for \(1\le j\le L\) let
\(H_j=H_{j-1}\setminus M_j\), where \(M_j\) is a uniform perfect matching of
\(H_{j-1}\) chosen independently of the past given \(H_{j-1}\).
Every step is defined, \(H_L\subset H_0\) surely, \(H_0\) has law \(\mu_D\), and
\[
\dTV\bigl(\mathcal L(H_L),\mu_{D-L}\bigr)\le C_c\,\frac{L}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(j\le L\) the graph \(H_{j-1}\) has degree \(D-j+1\ge D-L+1\ge n/2\), so
Dirac's theorem gives a Hamilton cycle, and \(n\) even gives a perfect
matching. Every step is therefore defined, and \(H_L\subset H_0\).

Write \(K_j\) for the kernel that sends a \((D-j+1)\)-regular graph to the
graph left by removing a uniform perfect matching.
Complementation turns \(K_j\) into the kernel that adds a uniform perfect
matching of the complement to a graph of degree \(d_j=n-D+j-2\).
The identity
\((n-D+j-2)-(n/2-1)+(D-j+1-n/2)=0\)
was checked, so \(d_j\le n/2-1\) exactly when \(D-j+1\ge n/2\).
Also \(d_j\ge n-D-1\ge cn-1\) and \(n-d_j\ge n/2+1\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:matching-step-l1}, applied with \(c/2\) for large \(n\), gives
\[
\dTV\bigl(\mu_{D-j+1}K_j,\mu_{D-j}\bigr)\le\frac{C_c}{n},
\]
because complementation is a bijection and preserves total variation.
A Markov kernel does not increase total variation, so for every law
\(\lambda\) of a \((D-j+1)\)-regular graph,
\[
\dTV(\lambda K_j,\mu_{D-j})
\le
\dTV(\lambda,\mu_{D-j+1})+\frac{C_c}{n}.
\]
Starting from \(\lambda=\mu_D\) and iterating \(L\) times gives the bound.
\end{proof}

The theorem holds for every \(L\le D-n/2+1\), with no restriction
\(L=o(n)\). The bound tends to \(0\) when \(L=o(n)\).
Unlike Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}, the containment is sure, the algorithm is
the natural one, and only the upper marginal is exact.
Below half degree the same argument, applied by complementation, adds
successive uniform perfect matchings of the complement to a uniform
\(d\)-regular graph with \(d+L-1\le n/2-1\) and \(d\ge cn\): the lower marginal is exact,
containment is sure, and the upper law is within \(C_cL/n\) of
\(\mu_{d+L}\).

\begin{theorem}[Bounded peel moments give peel flatness]
\label{thm:peel-bounded-to-flat}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:successive-peel}.
Write \(P_j^{(e)}\) for the number of ordered sequences of \(j\) successive
perfect matchings in a uniform \(e\)-regular graph.
Suppose there is \(B\ge 0\) such that, for every \(1\le j<L\),
\[
\frac{\EE\bigl[(P_{j}^{(e_j)})^2\bigr]}{\bigl(\EE P_{j}^{(e_j)}\bigr)^2}
\le
1+B,
\qquad
e_j=n-D+j-1.
\]
Then
\[
\EE\Bigl|\frac{P_L^{(D)}}{\EE P_L^{(D)}}-1\Bigr|
\le
C_c\,\sqrt{1+B}\,\frac{L}{n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A \(j\)-prefix is a sequence \((H_0,M_1,\ldots,M_j)\) with \(H_0\)
\(D\)-regular and each \(M_i\) a perfect matching of
\(H_{i-1}=H_0\setminus(M_1\cup\cdots\cup M_{i-1})\).
Let \(N_j\) be the number of \(j\)-prefixes, so \(N_0=|\mathcal T_D|\) and
\(N_L=|\mathcal T_D|\,\EE P_L^{(D)}\).
Let \(U_j\) be the uniform law on \(j\)-prefixes.

For a \((D-j+1)\)-regular graph \(g\), the \((j-1)\)-prefixes ending at \(g\)
are obtained by adding successive perfect matchings of the complement, so their
number is \(Q(g)=P_{j-1}(C(g))\), where \(C(g)\) is the complement, of degree
\(e_{j-1}=n-D+j-2\).
Under \(U_{j-1}\) the endpoint \(H_{j-1}\) therefore has density
\(Q/\EE Q\) with respect to \(\mu_{D-j+1}\), and \(C(H_{j-1})\) is uniform
when \(H_{j-1}\) is.
For \(j=1\) the density is \(1\).

Let \(S\) be the law of the prefix produced by the successive peel of
Theorem~\ref{thm:successive-peel}; it gives the \(L\)-prefix \(\tau\) the mass
\(|\mathcal T_D|^{-1}\prod_{i\le L}W(H_{i-1})^{-1}\), where \(W(g)\) is the
number of perfect matchings of \(g\).
For \(0\le j\le L\) define the probability law \(\rho_j\) on \(L\)-prefixes by
\[
\rho_j(\tau)
=
\frac1{N_j}\prod_{i>j}\frac1{W(H_{i-1})},
\]
so \(\rho_0=S\) and \(\rho_L=U_L\).
Write \(c_j=N_j/N_{j-1}\), the mean of \(W(H_{j-1})\) under \(U_{j-1}\).
Then
\[
\rho_j(\tau)-\rho_{j-1}(\tau)
=
\frac1{N_{j-1}}
\Bigl(\frac1{c_j}-\frac1{W(H_{j-1})}\Bigr)
\prod_{i>j}\frac1{W(H_{i-1})}.
\]
Sum over the continuations after step \(j\): the product sums to \(1\).
Sum over the \(W(H_{j-1})\) choices of \(M_j\): the identity
\(W(1/c-1/W)=W/c-1\) was checked, so
\[
\sum_\tau\bigl|\rho_j(\tau)-\rho_{j-1}(\tau)\bigr|
=
\EE_{U_{j-1}}\Bigl|\frac{W(H_{j-1})}{c_j}-1\Bigr|.
\]

Write \(m=\EE W\) under \(\mu_{D-j+1}\) and \(\omega=W/m-1\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} applies at degree \(D-j+1\in[n/2,(1-c)n]\)
and gives \(\EE[\omega^2]\le C_c/n^2\).
Write \(q=Q/\EE Q\), so \(\EE[q^2]\le 1+B\), by the hypothesis at
\(j-1\) when \(j\ge 2\) and trivially when \(j=1\).
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\EE_{U_{j-1}}|\omega|=\EE[q|\omega|]\le\sqrt{1+B}\,\sqrt{C_c}/n,
\qquad
\Bigl|\frac{c_j}{m}-1\Bigr|=\bigl|\EE[q\,\omega]\bigr|\le\sqrt{1+B}\,\sqrt{C_c}/n.
\]
The identity \(a/c-1=(m/c)(a/m-1)+(m/c-1)\) was checked.
Applied with \(a=W\) it gives
\[
\EE_{U_{j-1}}\Bigl|\frac{W}{c_j}-1\Bigr|
\le
\frac{m}{c_j}\,\EE_{U_{j-1}}|\omega|+\Bigl|\frac{m}{c_j}-1\Bigr|
\le
C'_c\,\frac{\sqrt{1+B}}{n},
\]
for large \(n\), once \(\sqrt{1+B}/n\) is small; otherwise the claimed bound
exceeds \(2\) and holds trivially.

Summing over \(j\),
\[
\sum_\tau\bigl|S(\tau)-U_L(\tau)\bigr|
\le
C'_c\sqrt{1+B}\,\frac{L}{n}.
\]
Projection onto \(H_0\) does not increase the sum.
The projection of \(S\) is \(\mu_D\), because every step is defined.
The projection of \(U_L\) gives \(H\) the mass \(P_L^{(D)}(H)/N_L\).
The sum of the absolute differences of the projections is
\(\EE|P_L^{(D)}/\EE P_L^{(D)}-1|\), which is the claim.
\end{proof}

For \(L=2\) the hypothesis is the matching variance at degree \(e_1=n-D\),
which Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} supplies.
Thus \(\EE|P_2/\EE P_2-1|=O_c(1/n)\) unconditionally above half degree.
For \(L\ge 3\) the hypothesis asks for bounded, not vanishing, relative
second moments of shorter peel counts on the complementary degrees.
The conclusion concerns the ordered peel count, so it does not give
Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment}.

\begin{remark}[Matching traces leave a density coefficient]
\label{rem:matching-trace-coefficient}
Let \(c\in(0,1/2]\) and let \(a=D/n\) satisfy \(c\le a\le 1-c\).
For each fixed overlap \(k\), Proposition~\ref{prop:origin-linear} at \(L=1\)
gives the origin logarithm
\[
k\log\frac1\lambda-\frac12\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)
+\frac{1-a}{a}\cdot\frac{k-1}{n}
+O\Bigl(\frac{k^2+1}{n^2}\Bigr),
\]
where \(\lambda=(D-1)/(n-2)\).
The same contraction as in Proposition~quad-host of~\cite{PlusOneV1} gives
\(\operatorname{tr}(C)=2e/(n-1)\) for the host matrix of an edge set with \(e\)
edges. The three hosts therefore contribute \(-k/(n-1)\) to the logarithm of
the determinant ratio.
For the union, \(e=n-k\) and the sum of squared degrees is \(4n-6k\); for one
matching, \(e=n/2\) and the sum of squares is \(n\).
Write \(M_0^{-1}=\alpha(I-\gamma\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T})\) with
\(\alpha=1/(n-2)\) and \(\gamma=1/(2(n-1))\), and write \(B\) for the host
matrix. Then \(B\mathbf{1}=2\deg\), \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)=\Sigma+2e\), and
\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^2)
=
\frac{\Sigma\bigl(1-4/(n-1)\bigr)+2e+4e^2/(n-1)^2}{(n-2)^2}.
\]
The second-trace
combination \((\operatorname{tr}(C_U^2)-2\operatorname{tr}(C_M^2))/4\) equals
\[
\frac{2k^2-4kn^2+16kn-16k+n^3-5n^2+5n}{2(n^4-6n^3+13n^2-12n+4)}.
\]
Multiplied by \(n\), this tends to \(1/2\), so the second trace is
\(1/(2n)+O((k+1)/n^2)\). The limit was checked.

The factorial moments of the intersection of two uniform perfect matchings do
not depend on \(D\). For \(z\) bounded,
\[
e^{-(z-1)/2}\EE[z^K]
=
1+\frac{z-1}{2(n-1)}+O\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr),
\]
because \(\EE[(K)_r]=2^{-r}(1+r/(n-1)+O(r^2/n^2))\) and
\(\EE[z^K]=\sum_r(z-1)^r\EE[(K)_r]/r!\).
At \(z=1/a\) the excess is \((1-a)/(2an)\).

Averaging \(\frac{1-a}{a}(k-1)-k+\frac12\) against the \(z^k\)-tilt of a
Poisson random variable of mean \(1/2\), whose mean is \(1/(2a)\), and adding
that excess, produces the coefficient \((a-1)(2a-1)/(2a^2)\). The identity
was checked. It vanishes at \(a=1/2\) and not at a general density in
\((0,1)\).

The Gaussian factor \(-\frac{n-1}{2}\log(\lambda(1-\lambda))\), evaluated at
the three host densities, has \(1/n\) coefficient
\((4a^2 k-2a^2-2ak+1)/(2a^2)\).
Added to the origin and the first trace, the coefficient of \(k\) cancels.
The averaged coefficient becomes \((1-a)/(2a^2)\), which equals \(1\) at
\(a=1/2\). Both identities were checked. That value is incompatible with the
\(O(1/n^2)\) rate in Proposition~half-rate of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, so this
Gaussian factor is not an additional term in the ratio.

Those two packages leave a nonzero multiple of \(1/n\). The width by itself
is the incompatible package. The adjustment of the vertex radii contributes
the further term \(-\gamma k/n\), and with that term the width cancels, which
is Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Shifted radii for a matching union]
\label{lem:mismatch-shift}
Let \(\min(D,n-D)\ge cn\) and let \(k\le\log n\). On the union of two perfect
matchings with intersection \(k\), the binary-entropy value at the
degree-corrected radii differs from the equal-radius value by
\(-\gamma k/n+O_c((k^2+1)/n^2)\), where \(\gamma=(1-a)/a\) and \(a=D/n\).
The Gaussian determinant changes by the same order.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w=\lambda(1-\lambda)\) and let \(\delta\) be the vector of degree
mismatches at the equal-radius density of the union. The \(2k\) vertices of
host degree \(1\) have mismatch \(1-a+O((k+1)/n)\), and the remaining vertices
have mismatch \(O((k+1)/n)\), so
\(\|\delta\|^2=2k(1-a)^2+O((k^2+1)/n)\). The sum of the mismatches is zero.
The Hessian of the edge factors on the complete graph is
\(w\bigl((n-2)I+J\bigr)\). On the hyperplane its inverse contributes
\(\|\delta\|^2/(2w(n-2))\). Replacing \(n-2\) by \(n\), and using the checked
limit
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\Bigl(
n\cdot\frac{\|\delta\|^2}{2nw}-\gamma k
\Bigr)
=0,
\]
shows that this quadratic form equals \(\gamma k/n+O_c((k^2+1)/n^2)\).
The entropy is the negative of the standard action, so the quadratic change
is minus that quantity.

The correcting potential is \(\varepsilon=H^{-1}\delta\). The inverse has
diagonal \(O(1/n)\) and off-diagonal \(O(1/n^2)\), and \(\sum|\delta_v|=O(k+1)\),
so \(\varepsilon\) is \(O_c(1/n)\) on the \(2k\)-set and \(O_c((k+1)/n^2)\)
elsewhere. The third derivative of \(\log(1+e^{\theta_u+\theta_v})\) is bounded
for densities in \([c/2,1-c/2]\). The cubic remainder is therefore at most a
constant times \(\sum|\varepsilon_u+\varepsilon_v|^3\). Edges inside the
\(2k\)-set contribute \(O(k^2/n^3)\), edges with one end in that set contribute
\(O(k/n^2)\), and the remaining edges contribute \(O(k^3/n^4)\). For
\(k\le\log n\) the remainder is \(O_c((k^2+1)/n^2)\).

For the determinant, let \(\Delta\) be the change in the Hessian produced by
replacing the common weight \(w\) with the corrected edge weights, and let
\(B\) be \(w\) times the Laplacian of the host. The host has maximum degree
\(2\), so \(\|B\|=O(1)\). The equal-radius inverse on the complete graph is
\(\alpha I-c_n J\) with \(\alpha=1/(w(n-2))\). The trace identity for that
inverse gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(H^{-1}\Delta)
=
\frac{2}{w(n-1)}\sum_e\bigl(w_e-w\bigr),
\]
and \(\sum_e(w_e-w)=-\sum_e(\lambda_e-\lambda)^2\) because the corrected edge
densities have the same sum as \(\lambda\). The squared density changes are
\(O(1/n^2)\) on the \(O(kn)\) edges that meet the \(2k\)-set and
\(O(k^2/n^4)\) elsewhere, so the trace is \(O(k/n^2)\).
The resolvent expansion of the host-deleted inverse differs from the complete
inverse by a series in \(BH^{-1}\). Each host edge \(\{x,y\}\) contributes a
multiple of \((\Delta_{xx}+\Delta_{yy}-2\Delta_{xy})\), the diagonal changes
are \(O(1)\) on the \(2k\)-set and \(O(k/n)\) elsewhere, and
\(\alpha^2=O(1/n^2)\), so the first correction is \(O(k/n^2)\). Every further
factor of \(BH^{-1}\) costs \(O(1/n)\). The squared Frobenius remainder in \(\log\det(I+H^{-1}\Delta)\) is at most
\(\|H^{-1}\|^2\|\Delta\|_F^2\). The diagonal part of \(\Delta\) is \(O(1)\) on
the \(2k\)-set, so \(\|\Delta\|_F=O(\sqrt{k})\), and
\(\|H^{-1}\|=O(1/n)\). The product is \(O(k/n^2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matching variance throughout the linear band]
\label{thm:matching-linear-rate}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(\min(D,n-D)\ge cn\) for a fixed \(c\in(0,1/2]\),
with \(Dn\) even. Let \(Y\) be the number of perfect matchings in the uniform
\(D\)-regular graph. Then
\[
\frac{\Var Y}{(\EE Y)^2}=O_c\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a=D/n\), \(\gamma=(1-a)/a\), and \(\lambda=(D-1)/(n-2)\).
The ratio \(\EE[Y^2]/(\EE Y)^2\) is the average, over the intersection \(K\) of
two uniform perfect matchings, of the extension ratio
\(R(K)=N(H)N(\emptyset)/N(M)^2\).
At \(L=1\) the edge counts of the three hosts are at most \(n\). The density
deviation from \(a\) is \(O(1/n)\) for \(k\le\log n\), with the overlap entering
only at order \(1/n^2\). The fourth derivative of \(\eta\) is bounded on
\([c/2,1-c/2]\), so the Lagrange remainder in
Proposition~\ref{prop:origin-linear} is \(O_c((k^4+1)/n^2)\) on this longer
range, not only for a bounded overlap. The coefficient of \(1/n\) at \(L=1\)
reduces to \(\gamma(k-1)\), and that reduction was checked. Thus
\[
\log R_{\mathrm{ent}}(k)
=
k\log\frac1\lambda-\frac12\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)
+\gamma\frac{k-1}{n}
+O_c\Bigl(\frac{k^4+1}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
The empty host and one matching are regular, so their radii need no
correction. Lemma~\ref{lem:mismatch-shift} subtracts \(\gamma k/n\) on the
union and leaves \(-\gamma/n\), up to \(O_c((k^2+1)/n^2)\). The same lemma
bounds the change in the Gaussian determinant.

The Gaussian scale \(-\frac{n-1}{2}\log(\lambda_e(1-\lambda_e))\), evaluated at
the three host densities and multiplied by \(n\), has constant term
\[
W(k)=\frac{4a^2 k-2a^2-2ak+1}{2a^2}.
\]
The series agrees with \(W(k)\), and the algebraic reduction of \(W\) was
checked. The angular traces of Remark~\ref{rem:matching-trace-coefficient}
contribute \((1/2-k)/n\).
The logarithm of the ratio is therefore the main term plus \(c(k)/n\), where
\[
c(k)=-\gamma+W(k)+\frac12-k,
\]
up to an error \(O_c((k^4+1)/n^2)\) on \(k\le\log n\).

The factorial moments give
\[
e^{-(\lambda^{-1}-1)/2}\EE[\lambda^{-K}]
=
1+\frac{\lambda^{-1}-1}{2(n-1)}+O\Bigl(\frac1{n^2}\Bigr),
\]
and \((\lambda^{-1}-1)/2=(1-a)/(2a)+O(1/n)\).
The same tilt has mean \(1/(2a)+O(1/n)\).
Substituting that mean into \(c\) and adding \((1-a)/(2a)\) produces \(0\).
The identity was checked.
The average of the ratio over \(k\le\log n\) is therefore \(1+O_c(1/n^2)\).
The tail \(k>\log n\) is negligible, as in the linear-band argument
of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, because it is bounded by a constant multiple of
\(\EE[\lambda^{-K}1_{K>\log n}]\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One matching deletion has total variation \(O(1/n)\)]
\label{lem:matching-step-l1}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(\min(d,n-d)\ge cn\) for a fixed \(c\in(0,1/2]\),
with \(dn\) even and \(d\le n/2-1\).
Let \(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1\) be the law of a uniform \(d\)-regular graph with a
uniform perfect matching of its complement added.
Then
\[
\dTV(\mu_d\oplus\mu_1,\mu_{d+1})
=
O_c\Bigl(\frac1n\Bigr).
\]
At degrees \(d\ge n/2\) the same bound holds after complementation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(Z\) be the number of perfect matchings in the complement of a uniform
\(d\)-regular graph, and let \(Y\) be the number in a uniform \((d+1)\)-regular
graph. Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} gives relative variance
\(O_c(1/n^2)\) for both, since the complement has degree \(n-1-d\).
Write \(\mathcal T_d\) and \(\mathcal T_{d+1}\) for the two sets of graphs, and
write \(B\) for the bipartite graph of containments that differ by a perfect
matching, so \(|\mathcal T_d|\,\EE Z=|\mathcal T_{d+1}|\,\EE Y\).
The containment law of an upper graph \(H\) is
\[
\PP(H)=\frac1{|\mathcal T_d|}\sum_{G\subset H}\frac1{Z(G)}.
\]
The comparison measure \(\nu(H)=\mu_{d+1}(H)\cdot Y(H)/\EE Y\) is
\(Y(H)/(|\mathcal T_d|\,\EE Z)\). Their difference at \(H\) is
\[
\frac1{|\mathcal T_d|}\sum_{G\subset H}\Bigl(\frac1{Z(G)}-\frac1{\EE Z}\Bigr).
\]
Summing the absolute values and using that each \(G\) lies in \(Z(G)\) upper
graphs produces
\[
\sum_H\bigl|\PP(H)-\nu(H)\bigr|
=
\EE\bigl|Z/\EE Z-1\bigr|
\le
\bigl(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2\bigr)^{1/2},
\]
because \(Z\,|1/Z-1/\EE Z|=|1-Z/\EE Z|\) whenever \(Z>0\), and a graph with
\(Z=0\) contributes to neither side.
Thus the total variation between \(\PP\) and \(\nu\) is \(O_c(1/n)\).
The total variation between \(\nu\) and \(\mu_{d+1}\) is half of
\(\EE|Y/\EE Y-1|\), which is the same order.
Complementation, as in the linear-band chain of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, treats
\(d\ge n/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Selection reduction for the container]
\label{thm:selection-reduces}
Let \(d\), \(m\), and \(G^*_0\sim G\bigl(n,(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\bigr)\) be as in the
matching-peel construction of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, and write
\(p=(1+\varepsilon_0)d/n\) and \(D:=d+m\).
Let \(N(G^*_0)\) be the number of \(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs of \(G^*_0\),
and let \(\nu_D\) be the law of a uniformly chosen such subgraph, conditioned
on \(N(G^*_0)\ge 1\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every \(D\)-regular graph \(G_0\) one has \(\PP(G_0\subset G^*_0)=p^{Dn/2}\).
\item \(\nu_D(G_0)=\EE\bigl[1_{G_0\subset G^*_0}/N(G^*_0)\bigr]/\PP(N\ge 1)\).
\item If there is a sequence \(c_n\) such that every \(D\)-regular graph \(G_0\) satisfies
\[
\EE\bigl[1/N(G^*_0)\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
=
(1+o(1))\,c_n,
\]
then \(\dTV(\nu_D,\mu_D)\to 0\).
\item If in addition \(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\nu_D)\to 0\), then
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every \(D\)-regular graph has \(Dn/2\) edges, which is (i).
Item (ii) is uniform selection among the subgraphs of a random host.
The rate-\(1/3\) sandwich gives \(\PP(N\ge 1)\to 1\).
Under (iii), \(\nu_D(G_0)=(1+o(1))\,p^{Dn/2}c_n\) for every \(D\)-regular \(G_0\),
so \(\nu_D\) is a \((1+o(1))\) multiple of \(\mu_D\).
The peel law is supported on \(D\)-regular subgraphs of \(G^*_0\), and the
triangle inequality with (iii) is (iv).
\end{proof}

\section{What v1.0 already forbids}

A hybrid almost-regular supergraph at Chernoff scale is at total variation
bounded away from both the binomial graph and \(\mu_D\)
(Propositions~hybrid-gap and hybrid-singular of~\cite{PlusOneV1}).
Supports of a non-regular degree sequence and a regular law are disjoint, so
their total variation is \(1\).

The origin-plus-binomial average of the \(L\)-factor second moment equals
\(1+L^3/(4n)+O(L^2/n)\), but that quantity is not \(\EE[Z^2]/(\EE Z)^2\):
exact pairwise mass cancels the leftover cubic, and the remaining-degree main
term independently contributes an unsigned \(O(L^3/n)\)
(Propositions~pairwise-mass, pairwise-pgf, and cubic-not-identified
of~\cite{PlusOneV1}).
The signed \(3\)-matching residual is \(O(L^6/n^2)\) and does not put the cubic
back.

An independent Bernoulli residual has factor-count second moment
\(\EE[p^{-K}]\ge p^{-\EE K}\) with \(\EE K=\Theta(d\log n)\), which diverges
(Proposition~bernoulli-moment of~\cite{PlusOneV1}).
That comparison is not the BIM residual.

The first moment of the binomial-host subgraph count \(N\) is flat for
\(D=o(n^{1/3})\) (Proposition~contain-first of~\cite{PlusOneV1}).
Jensen's inequality for \(x\mapsto 1/x\) blocks using that moment for
item (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces}.

These blocks are not attacks to retry.

\section{Intersection size and the host-count second moment}

The missing estimate in item (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} is
an upper bound on \(\EE[1/N\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]\) that is independent of
\(G_0\).
The first moment of \(N\) cannot supply it.
This section records the second-moment identity and shows that Chebyshev on
\(N\) itself is the same obstruction in another form.

\begin{proposition}[Intersection of a fixed factor with \(G(n,L)\)]
\label{prop:intersection-var}
Let \(n>3\) and let \(L\ge 2\) satisfy \(Ln\) even and \(L\le n-1\).
Let \(F_1\) be a fixed \(L\)-regular graph and let \(G\sim G(n,L)\).
Write \(K=\lvert E(F_1)\cap E(G)\rvert\), \(s=Ln/2\), and \(p=L/(n-1)\).
Then \(\EE K=nL^2/(2(n-1))\), and
\[
\Var K
=
sp(1-p)+2S
=
\frac{L^2}{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{L^3}{n}\Bigr),
\]
where \(S\) is the pairwise mass of~\cite[Proposition~pairwise-mass]{PlusOneV1}.
The relative variance is \(2/L^2+O(1/n)\).
In particular \(\Var K/(\EE K)^2\to 0\) whenever \(L\to\infty\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linearity and symmetry give \(\EE K=sp\), and the identity
\(sp=nL^2/(2(n-1))\) has difference \(0\).
The pairwise expansion of the variance is \(sp(1-p)+2S\).
The leading term of \(S\) is \(-(L-2)L^2/(4n)\), so
\(2S=-(L-2)L^2/(2n)\).
The series of
\[
\frac{L^2 n(n-1-L)}{2(n-1)^2}-\frac{(L-2)L^2}{2n}
\]
over \((\EE K)^2\) at \(n=\infty\) is \(2/L^2+O(n^{-1})\), which was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Off-host intersection]
\label{prop:off-host}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection-var}, let \(H,H'\) be
independent copies of \(G(n,L)\) and let \(G_0\) be a third \(L\)-regular graph.
Write \(K_{12}=\lvert E(H)\cap E(H')\rvert\) and
\(K_{012}=\lvert E(H)\cap E(H')\cap E(G_0)\rvert\).
Then
\[
\EE[K_{12}-K_{012}]
=
\frac{nL^2(n-1-L)}{2(n-1)^2}.
\]
The identity
\(\EE K_{12}-\EE K_{012}=nL^2/(2(n-1))-nL^3/(2(n-1)^2)\)
has difference \(0\).
For \(L=o(n)\) the mean is \(\Theta(L^2)\).
Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection-var}, one has
\(K_{12}-K_{012}=\Theta(L^2)\) with high probability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first moment of \(K_{012}\) is \(s\cdot p^2=nL^3/(2(n-1)^2)\) because each
edge of \(G_0\) lies in both \(H\) and \(H'\) independently with probability
\(p^2\).
Subtract from \(\EE K_{12}\).
Chebyshev on \(K_{12}\) uses relative variance \(2/L^2+O(1/n)\).
The mean of \(K_{012}\) is \(o(L^2)\) throughout \(L=o(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Second moment of the binomial-host subgraph count]
\label{prop:host-count-second}
Assume the setting of the contain-first proposition of~\cite{PlusOneV1}:
\(D=o(n^{1/3})\), \(G^*_0\sim G(n,p)\) with \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) and
\(0<\varepsilon=o(1)\), and \(N\) the number of \(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs
of \(G^*_0\).
Fix a \(D\)-regular graph \(G_0\), and let \(H,H'\) be independent samples from
\(\mu_D\).
Write \(K_{01}=\lvert E(H)\cap E(G_0)\rvert\), and likewise \(K_{02}\),
\(K_{12}\), \(K_{012}\).
Then
\[
\bigl\lvert\bigl(E(H)\cup E(H')\bigr)\setminus E(G_0)\bigr\rvert
=
Dn-K_{01}-K_{02}-K_{12}+K_{012},
\]
the counting identity having difference \(0\), and
\[
\frac{\EE\bigl[N^2\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]}
{\bigl(\EE\bigl[N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]\bigr)^2}
=
\frac{\EE\bigl[p^{-K_{01}-K_{02}-K_{12}+K_{012}}\bigr]}
{\bigl(\EE\bigl[p^{-K_{01}}\bigr]\bigr)^2}.
\]
The increment \(K_{12}-K_{012}\) is nonnegative, so the ratio is at least \(1\),
with equality if and only if \(E(H)\cap E(H')\subset E(G_0)\) almost surely.
That event has probability \(o(1)\) by Proposition~\ref{prop:off-host}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\), the remaining pairs are independent Bernoulli(\(p\)).
A pair \(H,H'\) is contained in \(G^*_0\) precisely when
\((E(H)\cup E(H'))\setminus E(G_0)\) is present, which has probability
\(p\) to the displayed power.
Summing over \(H,H'\in\Omega_D\) produces the second-moment identity.
The first-moment identity of~\cite{PlusOneV1} is the same argument with one
graph, and \(K_{01},K_{02}\) are i.i.d., so the denominator is a square.
Since \(p\in(0,1)\) and \(K_{12}-K_{012}\ge 0\), one has
\(p^{-(K_{12}-K_{012})}\ge 1\), hence the ratio is at least \(1\).
Equality throughout the expectation holds if and only if
\(K_{12}=K_{012}\) almost surely.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence approximation of the relative second moment]
\label{prop:indep-rv-closed}
Assume the edge-independence approximation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:near-disjoint-miss}, with \(\pi=D/(n-1)\) and
\(s=Dn/2\).
Under that model the relative second-moment ratio of
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second} factors over edges and equals
\[
\bigl(1-\pi^2+\pi^2 p^{-1}\bigr)^{\binom{n}{2}-s}.
\]
The contribution of every edge of \(G_0\) cancels exactly: the identity
\[
\frac{(1-\pi)^2+2\pi(1-\pi)p^{-1}+\pi^2 p^{-2}}
{\bigl((1-\pi)+\pi p^{-1}\bigr)^2}
=
1
\]
was checked.
The off-host factor satisfies
\[
\log\bigl(1-\pi^2+\pi^2 p^{-1}\bigr)^{\binom{n}{2}-s}
=
\frac{Dn}{2(1+\varepsilon)}+O(D^2)
=
\Theta(Dn)
\]
at \(n=\infty\) with \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\), which was checked.
In particular the independence approximation already makes the relative
variance of \(N\) diverge as \(\exp(\Theta(Dn))\), so Chebyshev about
\(\EE[N]\) fails by an exponential margin in that model.
(The actual ratio is the same quantity with the \(\mu_D\)-law in place of
independent edges; Proposition~\ref{prop:untilted-tilt} is the untilted
piece of that comparison.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under edge independence the weight
\(p^{-K_{01}-K_{02}-K_{12}+K_{012}}\) is a product over pairs.
On an edge of \(G_0\) the four occupancy patterns for \((H,H')\) give the
displayed numerator, while \(\EE[p^{-K_{01}}]\) contributes the denominator
factor \((1-\pi)+\pi p^{-1}\) once for each of \(H\) and \(H'\).
On an edge outside \(G_0\) one has \(K_{01}=K_{02}=K_{012}=0\) and
\(K_{12}\in\{0,1\}\), which produces \(1-\pi^2+\pi^2 p^{-1}\).
Raising to the number of off-host edges is the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Chebyshev on \(N\) is not item (iii)]
\label{rem:host-chebyshev}
Item (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} is an upper bound on
\(\EE[1/N\mid\text{containment}]\).
Chebyshev supplies that bound only if \(\Var N/(\EE N)^2=o(1)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second} rewrites that relative variance as
the \(p^{-K_{01}-K_{02}}\)-tilt of \(p^{-(K_{12}-K_{012})}\).
Under the untilted law the exponent is \(\Theta(D^2)\) in probability, and
\(p^{-\Theta(D^2)}\) is \(\exp(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D)))\).
Passing that lower bound through the tilt is a correlation estimate that this
paper does not claim.
The identity already shows that the second moment of \(N\) is not the first
moment: the ratio equals \(1\) only on an event of probability \(o(1)\).
Together with Jensen's inequality for \(1/N\), neither moment of \(N\) is a
proof of item (iii).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Untilted pairwise factor is exponentially large]
\label{prop:untilted-tilt}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second}, write
\(\Delta=K_{12}-K_{012}\).
Under the law of two independent samples from \(\mu_D\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:off-host} gives \(\EE\Delta=\Theta(D^2)\), and Jensen's
inequality for \(x\mapsto p^{-x}\) yields
\[
\EE\bigl[p^{-\Delta}\bigr]
\ge
p^{-\EE\Delta}
=
\exp\bigl(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D))\bigr).
\]
The identity \(\EE\Delta\cdot\log(1/p)=\Theta(D^2\log(n/D))\) was checked at
leading order.
The relative second-moment ratio of Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second}
is the \(p^{-K_{01}-K_{02}}\)-tilt of \(p^{-\Delta}\).
The untilted lower bound already diverges, but converting it into a lower bound
on the tilted expectation is a correlation estimate that this paper does not
claim, as in Remark~\ref{rem:host-chebyshev}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of \(x\mapsto p^{-x}\) on \(\mathbb R\) for \(p\in(0,1)\) is Jensen's
inequality, and the mean of \(\Delta\) is Proposition~\ref{prop:off-host}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The remaining Chebyshev gap is a tilt comparison]
\label{prop:tilt-gap}
In the notation of Propositions~\ref{prop:host-count-second}
and~\ref{prop:untilted-tilt}, write \(\mu\) for the law of two independent
samples from \(\mu_D\) and \(\widetilde\mu\) for the reweighted law with
Radon--Nikodym derivative proportional to \(p^{-K_{01}-K_{02}}\).
The relative second-moment ratio equals \(\EE_{\widetilde\mu}[p^{-\Delta}]\).
Under the edge-independence approximation,
Proposition~\ref{prop:indep-rv-closed} evaluates that ratio as
\(\exp(\Theta(Dn))\).
Under the genuine \(\mu_D\)-law the untilted factor
\(\EE_{\mu}[p^{-\Delta}]\) is already
\(\exp\bigl(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D))\bigr)\) by Proposition~\ref{prop:untilted-tilt},
but the tilt may raise or lower the expectation: on six vertices with
\(D=2\) and \(p=1/2\), exact enumeration gives
\[
\frac{\EE_{\widetilde\mu}[p^{-\Delta}]}{\EE_{\mu}[p^{-\Delta}]}
\approx
0.551
\]
for a fixed Hamiltonian \(G_0\), so the tilt decreases the pairwise factor on
that instance.
A lower bound on \(\EE_{\widetilde\mu}[p^{-\Delta}]\) under \(\mu_D\) is
therefore a separate correlation estimate, not a consequence of stochastic
domination of \(\Delta\), and is not claimed here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reweighting identity is the definition of the ratio in
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second}, since \(K_{01}\) and \(K_{02}\) are
i.i.d.\ under \(\mu\) and the denominator normalises the tilt.
The numerical ratio is exact summation over \(\Omega_2\times\Omega_2\).
\end{proof}

This does not disprove item (iii), and it does not close
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}.
The peel still has to match \(\nu_D\), and the interesting range for that
conjecture has \(D=\Theta(d)\) past \(n^{1/3}\).

\section{Signed sequential product}
\label{sec:signed}

Workstream A asks for the principal term of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} after a
typical host, with remaining stubs and phase together.
This section writes that term for a matching host and a disjoint next edge.
The phase cancels the empty-host error of order \(1/n\).
It does not cancel the stub-total shift of order \(t/(Ln)\).

\begin{proposition}[Unsigned stub-total shift]
\label{prop:unsigned-shift}
Let \(n>2\), \(L\ge 2\), and \(0\le t<Ln/2\). The unsigned remaining-stub
ratio for a pair of remaining degree \(L\) after a host of \(t\) edges is
\(L^2/(Ln-2t)\). Divided by \(p=L/(n-1)\) this equals
\[
\frac{L(n-1)}{Ln-2t}
=
1+\frac{2t}{Ln}-\frac1n+O\Bigl(\frac{t+L}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
The identity
\(L(n-1)/(Ln-2t)-1-2t/(Ln)\)
has series \(-1/n+O(n^{-2})\) at \(n=\infty\), which was checked.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Clear the denominator:
\(L(n-1)-(Ln-2t)=2t-L\), so the ratio minus one is
\((2t-L)/(Ln-2t)\).
The displayed series follows, and the checked expansion isolates the
\(-1/n\) empty-host term from the stub-total shift \(2t/(Ln)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Signed Gao term after a matching host]
\label{prop:signed-matching-host}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(n>8\), let \(L\ge 2\) satisfy
\(Ln\) even and \(L\le n/10\), and let \(H\) be a matching of \(t\) edges
with \(0\le t\le L^2\) and \(t<Ln/4\).
Let \(uv\) be disjoint from \(H\).
The remaining-stub sums are \(\widetilde M=Ln-2t\) and
\(\widetilde M_2=(L-1)(Ln-4t)\), and the phase on \(uv\) is
\[
\bar\phi=L^2-4L+2-2(L-1)\frac{\widetilde M_2}{\widetilde M}.
\]
The set \(\mathcal W\) contributes \(2t(L-1)^2/\widetilde M^2\).
The principal term
\[
q
=
\frac{L^2}{\widetilde M}
\Bigl(1-\frac{\widetilde M_2^2}{\widetilde M^3}-\frac{\widetilde M_2}{\widetilde M^2}-\frac{2t(L-1)^2}{\widetilde M^2}\Bigr)
\Bigl(1-\frac{\bar\phi}{\widetilde M}\Bigr)
\]
satisfies
\[
\frac{q}{p}-1
=
\frac{2t}{Ln}+O\Bigl(\frac{L^2+t}{n^2}\Bigr),
\qquad
p=\frac{L}{n-1}.
\]
At \(t=0\) this is the empty-host series \((-L^2+L-1)/n^2\) of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The phase removes the unsigned \(-1/n\).
It leaves the stub-total shift \(2t/(Ln)\).
Wolfram returned no series for the full \(q/p\); exact rational arithmetic
at \(L\in\{4,10\}\) and the listed \((n,t)\) in the ledger confirms the
residual after subtracting \(2t/(Ln)\) is \(O((L^2+t)/n^2)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identities \(\widetilde M=Ln-2t\) and
\(\widetilde M_2=(L-1)(Ln-4t)\) are the remaining-stub sums of an
\(L\)-regular graph minus a matching of \(t\) edges (each matching vertex
drops from \(L\) to \(L-1\)).
The phase is~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} with \(H_1=H\), \(H_2=\emptyset\),
and empty host neighbourhoods at \(u\) and \(v\).
Each matching edge contributes two ordered pairs to \(\mathcal W\).
At \(t=0\) the display reduces to the empty-host principal term, whose
ratio to \(p\) is \(1+O(L^2/n^2)\).
For \(t>0\) the unsigned ratio already contributes \(2t/(Ln)-1/n\).
The signed correction cancels the \(-1/n\) and does not cancel
\(2t/(Ln)\), by the recorded rational values.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Typical host still has relative error \(\Theta(L/n)\)]
\label{rem:signed-typical}
At the typical prefix \(t=\Theta(L^2)\) one has \(2t/(Ln)=\Theta(L/n)\).
A product of \(Ln/2\) sequential factors of relative size \(1+\Theta(L/n)\)
is again \(\exp(O(L^3/n))\).
The signed main term therefore does not improve the range
\(L=o(n^{1/3})\) of the sequential product.
Proposition~\ref{prop:triple-mass-order} sums the same principal term over
three-edge matchings. The constant \(4/n^2\) cancels through order \(1/n^4\).
The following term produces a mass of order \(L^8/n^2\), already larger than
the cubic \(L^3/n\) once \(L=\omega(n^{1/5})\), and the error permitted by
Theorem~6 of~\cite{Gao20} is the same order. A further remainder \(o(L^3/n)\)
needs a comparison that is not this sequential product.
The near-complete side \(D-L=o(n^{1/3})\) is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary},
by complementation rather than a signed product.
Keeping the stub shift \(2t/(Ln)\) and subtracting the adjacent wedges cancels
the cubic on every linear host; that is Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt},
for \(L^2=o(n)\). Degree \(n/2\) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt}.
The factor moment past \(\sqrt{n}\) stays open.
The long deletion is Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Principal three-matching mass]
\label{prop:triple-mass-order}
Let \(n>8\) and let \(L\ge 2\) satisfy \(Ln\) even and \(L\le n/10\).
Write \(p=L/(n-1)\) and
\(q_{\mathrm{disj}}=(L(n-4)+2)/((n-2)(n-3))\).
After a host of two disjoint edges, with
\(\widetilde M=Ln-4\) and \(\widetilde M_2=(L-1)(Ln-8)\), the phase and the
\(\mathcal W\)-contribution of Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-matching-host}
give the principal conditional probability \(q_3\).
The limit \(n^2(q_3-p)\to 4\) was checked.
The piece of the third cumulant that uses only this constant,
\[
p\,q_{\mathrm{disj}}\cdot\frac4{n^2}-2p^2(q_{\mathrm{disj}}-p),
\]
satisfies
\[
n^5\Bigl(p\,q_{\mathrm{disj}}\cdot\frac4{n^2}-2p^2(q_{\mathrm{disj}}-p)\Bigr)
-4L(L^2-5L+2)\to 0,
\]
which was checked. Multiplied by \(\binom{Ln/2}{3}\) it contributes a mass
\(O(L^6/n^2)\). The identity \((L^6/n^2)/(L^3/n)=L^3/n\) was checked, so this
piece is \(o(L^3/n)\) throughout \(L=o(n^{1/3})\).

The same expansion of \(q_3-p\) continues
\[
q_3-p=\frac4{n^2}-\frac{L^3}{n^3}+O\Bigl(\frac{L^2+1}{n^3}\Bigr).
\]
Wolfram returned no result for the coefficient of \(L^3\). Exact rational
arithmetic gives
\[
n\bigl(n^2(q_3-p)-4\bigr)-(-L^3+L^2-5L+20+8/L)
\]
equal to \(-6445528431584584717/30799677832475266995\) at \((L,n)=(5,400)\) and
to \(-26692586805897374537317/510146877521279604067995\) at \((5,1600)\), both
\(O(1/n)\). The summand \(-L^3/n^3\), multiplied by
\(p q_{\mathrm{disj}}=L^2/n^2+O(L^2/n^3)\) and by \((Ln/2)^3/6\), produces the
mass \(-L^8/(48n^2)\). The identities
\(n^2 p q_{\mathrm{disj}}-L^2\to 0\),
\((Ln/2)^3 L^5/(6n^5)=L^8/(48n^2)\), and
\((L^8/(48n^2))/(L^3/n)=L^5/(48n)\) were checked.
Thus the principal-term mass is
\[
-\frac{L^8}{48n^2}+O\Bigl(\frac{L^7}{n^2}\Bigr),
\]
and its ratio to the cubic \(L^3/n\) tends to \(0\) if and only if
\(L=o(n^{1/5})\).

The relative error allowed by~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} on one conditional
edge is \(O(L^2/n^2)\). Propagated through \(p q_{\mathrm{disj}} q_3\) it
changes the cumulant by \(O(L^5/n^5)\), the same order as the principal term
\(-L^5/n^5\). The coefficient \(-1/48\) is a property of the principal term.
It does not identify the third cumulant of the uniform model, and the
resulting truncation stays inside the range \(L=o(n^{1/3})\) already given by
the factor second-moment theorem of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cumulant of three pairwise disjoint edges, built from the principal
conditional probability, is
\[
\kappa
=
p\,q_{\mathrm{disj}}(q_3-p)-2p^2(q_{\mathrm{disj}}-p).
\]
Substituting the constant \(4/n^2\) for \(q_3-p\) leaves the displayed
combination, whose difference from \(4L(L^2-5L+2)/n^5\) tends to \(0\) after
multiplication by \(n^5\). There are \(\binom{Ln/2}{3}=O(L^3 n^3)\) such
triples, so the mass is \(O(L^3 n^3\cdot L^3/n^5)=O(L^6/n^2)\).

For the following term, the rational values identify
\(q_3-p=4/n^2-L^3/n^3+O((L^2+1)/n^3)\).
The product of \(-L^3/n^3\) with \(L^2/n^2\) is \(-L^5/n^5\).
Multiplication by \((Ln/2)^3/6\) is the checked monomial
\(-L^8/(48n^2)\). Every other summand in the expansions of
\(p q_{\mathrm{disj}}\) and of \(q_3-p\) contributes at most \(O(L^7/n^2)\)
to the mass. The ratio of the leading mass to \(L^3/n\) is the checked
monomial \(-L^5/(48n)\).

A relative factor \(1+O(L^2/n^2)\) on \(q_3\) changes \(\kappa\) by
\(p q_{\mathrm{disj}}\cdot O(L^2/n^2)\cdot O(L/n)=O(L^5/n^5)\), since
\(q_3=O(L/n)\) follows from \(n^2(q_3-p)\to 4\) and \(p=O(L/n)\).
That is the same order as the principal summand \(-L^5/n^5\).
\end{proof}

\section{Half-degree factors below the square root}
\label{sec:half-below-sqrt}

The signed product of Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-matching-host} is not
\(1+o(1)\) past \(n^{1/3}\). Its stub shift \(2t/(Ln)\) is a main term.
At degree \(n/2\) that main term, together with the exact falling factorial
on adjacent edges, cancels the cubic in the origin. The screen blocks
the claim that the signed product itself tends to \(1\); the argument below
does not make that claim.

\begin{lemma}[Janson lower tail for wedges]
\label{lem:wedge-tail}
Let each edge of a finite graph be retained independently with probability
\(\alpha\in(0,1)\). Let \(Q\) be the number of retained wedges, write
\(\mu=\EE Q\), and let \(\Delta\) be the sum of \(\EE[I_w I_{w'}]\) over
unordered pairs of distinct wedges that share an edge. Then
\(\PP(Q=0)\le\exp(-\mu+\Delta)\). For \(0<t<\mu\) and \(t\le\mu+\Delta\),
\[
\PP(Q\le\mu-t)\le\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t^2}{4(\mu+\Delta)}\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Order the wedges \(w_1,\ldots,w_N\) and write \(A_i\) for the event that both
edges of \(w_i\) are retained. Set
\(r_i=\PP(A_i\mid A_1^c\cap\cdots\cap A_{i-1}^c)\), so
\(\PP(Q=0)=\prod_i(1-r_i)\le\exp(-\sum_i r_i)\).
Fix \(i\). Let \(D\) be the intersection of \(A_j^c\) over those \(j<i\) whose
wedge shares an edge with \(w_i\), and let \(R\) be the intersection of
\(A_j^c\) over the remaining \(j<i\). Then
\[
r_i=\PP(A_i\mid D\cap R)
=\frac{\PP(A_i\cap D\cap R)}{\PP(D\cap R)}
\ge\frac{\PP(A_i\cap D\cap R)}{\PP(R)}
=\PP(A_i\cap D\mid R),
\]
because \(\PP(D\cap R)\le\PP(R)\). The event \(R\) is supported on edges
disjoint from \(w_i\), so \(\PP(A_i\mid R)=\PP(A_i)\) and
\[
\PP(A_i\cap D\mid R)=\PP(A_i)-\PP(A_i\cap D^c\mid R).
\]
Given \(A_i\), every remaining edge is still an independent Bernoulli trial of
the same probability. An extra absence either is disjoint from the edges of
\(A_j\setminus A_i\), in which case it does not change \(\PP(A_j\mid A_i)\),
or it forbids an edge of \(A_j\), in which case that probability drops to \(0\).
Hence \(\PP(A_j\mid A_i\cap R)\le\PP(A_j\mid A_i)\) and
\[
\PP(A_i\cap D^c\mid R)
\le\sum_{j<i,\,j\sim i}\PP(A_i\cap A_j).
\]
Therefore \(r_i\ge\PP(A_i)-\sum_{j<i,\,j\sim i}\PP(A_i\cap A_j)\).
Summing over \(i\) counts each dependent pair once, and
\(\PP(Q=0)\le\exp(-\mu+\Delta)\).

For the tail, fix \(\gamma\in(0,1/2]\) and let \(Q_\gamma\) be obtained by
retaining each present wedge independently with probability \(\gamma\).
Then \(\EE[(1-\gamma)^Q]=\PP(Q_\gamma=0)\). The thinned family has mean
\(\gamma\mu\) and dependency sum \(\gamma^2\Delta\), so the bound already
proved yields \(\EE[(1-\gamma)^Q]\le\exp(-\gamma\mu+\gamma^2\Delta)\).
Markov's inequality in the form
\(\PP(Q\le\mu-t)\le(1-\gamma)^{-(\mu-t)}\EE[(1-\gamma)^Q]\) and the inequality
\(-\log(1-\gamma)\le\gamma+\gamma^2\), valid for \(\gamma\le 1/2\), give the
exponent
\(-t\gamma+\gamma^2(\mu+\Delta)\).
The choice \(\gamma=t/(2(\mu+\Delta))\) is at most \(1/2\) and produces
\(-t^2/(4(\mu+\Delta))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Containment of a short subgraph]
\label{lem:contain-short}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(L\le n/10\) and let
\(d_0=\lceil\max(\log n,\, L^3(\log n)^2/n)\rceil\). Let \(A\) be a graph with
\(k\le 2L^2\) edges and maximum degree at most \(d_0\), and assume \(t\le Ln/4\)
throughout the exposure of its edges. Write \(p=L/(n-1)\), let \(d_v\) be the
degree of vertex \(v\) in \(A\), and let \(Q(A)=\sum_v\binom{d_v}{2}\). Then
\[
\log\frac{\PP(A\subset G(n,L))}{p^k}
=
\frac{k(k-1)}{Ln}+\sum_v\log\frac{(L)_{d_v}}{L^{d_v}}+\theta_A,
\]
where the error satisfies \(|\theta_A|\le\varepsilon_n\) and \(\varepsilon_n\to 0\)
as \(n\to\infty\), uniformly in all such \(A\), as soon as
\(L^{5/2}\log n=o(n)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expose the edges of \(A\) in any order. At a step with \(t\) preceding edges,
Theorem~6 of~\cite{Gao20} supplies the conditional probability \(q\) of the
next edge with relative error \(O(L^2/n^2)\), because
\(\Delta^5 n/M^3=O(L^2/n^2)\) and the remaining stub total is
\(\widetilde M=Ln-2t\ge Ln/2\).
A product of \(k\) such factors is \(\exp(O(k L^2/n^2))=\exp(O(L^4/n^2))\).

Write \(r_u,r_v\) for the number of preceding edges of \(A\) at the two
endpoints, and \(N_u,N_v\) for the sums of the remaining degrees of those
neighbours. The principal term has logarithm
\[
\log\frac{(L-r_u)(L-r_v)}{L^2}+\frac{2t}{Ln}
+\frac{N_u+N_v-r_ur_v-r_u-r_v}{Ln}+O\Bigl(\frac{L^2+t+Q_t+t^2/L^2}{n^2}\Bigr),
\]
where \(Q_t\) is the number of wedges in the preceding graph. The coefficient
of \(1/n\) is the expansion of that principal term at \(n=\infty\); the numeric
check recorded in the ledger shows that \(n\) times the difference from this
coefficient stays bounded. Summing the displayed \(1/n\) errors over the
exposure produces \(O(Q(A)/n)\), because
\(\sum(r_u+r_v)=Q(A)\) and \(N_u\le L r_u\).
On the graphs of this lemma, \(Q(A)\le kd_0\le 2L^2 d_0\). The hypothesis
\(L^{5/2}\log n=o(n)\) forces \(L^2 d_0/n\to 0\), and the accumulated \(1/n^2\) errors are
\(O(k(L^2+k)/n^2)=O(L^4/n^2)\to 0\).

The sum of \(\log((L-r_u)/L)\) over the exposure is exactly
\(\sum_v\log((L)_{d_v}/L^{d_v})\), and the sum of \(2t/(Ln)\) is
\(k(k-1)/(Ln)\).
The bound \(k\le 2L^2\) is used only as \(k=O(L^2)\). The same argument
applies with \(2L^2\) replaced by \(\kappa L^2\) for any fixed \(\kappa\),
the threshold \(\varepsilon_n\) then depending on \(\kappa\).
The degree cutoff is used only to force the error to \(0\). Before that
cutoff, the sum of the per-step \(Q_t/n^2\) errors is at most \(kQ(A)/n^2\).
For \(k\le\kappa L^2\) and \(L^2=o(n)\) this is absorbed in \(O(Q(A)/n)\), and
the remaining \(1/n^2\) errors are \(O_\kappa(L^4/n^2)\). Thus
\[
|\theta_A|
\le
C_\kappa\Bigl(\frac{Q(A)+1}{n}+\frac{L^4}{n^2}\Bigr)
\]
for every graph of maximum degree at most \(L\) with at most \(\kappa L^2\)
edges, once \(L\le n/10\) and \(L^2=o(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stub and wedges up to the square root]
\label{lem:stub-wedge}
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}. Let \(L\to\infty\) satisfy \(L\le n/10\) and
\(L^2=o(n)\). Let each edge of a fixed \(L\)-regular graph be
retained independently with probability \(\alpha\), where
\(c_1 L/n\le\alpha\le c_2 L/n\) for fixed positive constants \(c_1,c_2\).
Write \(K\) for the number of retained edges, \(Q\) for the number of retained
wedges, \(s=Ln/2\), \(m=s\alpha\), \(N_{\mathrm{adj}}=n\binom{L}{2}\), and
\(\mu=N_{\mathrm{adj}}\alpha^2\). Let \(\Xi\) be the logarithm of
\(\PP(A\subset G(n,L))/p^{|A|}\) on the retained graph \(A\), with
\(p=L/(n-1)\). Then, for every fixed \(\kappa\) large enough that
\(m\le\kappa L^2/2\),
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{K\le\kappa L^2}\bigr]
=
\exp\Bigl(\frac{m(m-1)}{Ln}-\frac{\mu}{L}+o(1)\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:contain-short} supplies, on \(K\le\kappa L^2\),
\[
\Xi
=
\frac{K(K-1)}{Ln}+\sum_v\log\frac{(L)_{X_v}}{L^{X_v}}+\theta,
\qquad
|\theta|
\le
C\Bigl(\frac{Q+1}{n}+\frac{L^4}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
The sum of logarithms is at most \(-Q/L\), because
\(\log(1-x)\le -x\). Also \(Q/n=(L/n)(Q/L)\) and \(L^4/n^2\to 0\), so
\[
|\theta|
\le
\varepsilon_n\frac{Q}{L}+\delta_n,
\qquad
\varepsilon_n\to 0,\quad\delta_n\to 0.
\]
Hence \(\Xi\le K(K-1)/(Ln)-(1-\varepsilon_n)Q/L+\delta_n\).

Write \(a(K)=K(K-1)/(Ln)\) and
\(\omega_n=\min\bigl(\log L,\,(n/L^2)^{1/4}\bigr)\).
Then \(\omega_n\to\infty\) and \(\omega_n L^2/n\to 0\).
Let \(W\) be the event \(|K-m|\le L\omega_n\).
On \(W\),
\[
|a(K)-a(m)|
\le
\frac{L\omega_n\,(2m+L\omega_n)}{Ln}
=
O\Bigl(\frac{\omega_n L^2}{n}\Bigr)
\to 0,
\]
since \(m=O(L^2)\).

Each wedge meets at most \(4L\) other wedges, so the dependency sum of
Lemma~\ref{lem:wedge-tail} satisfies \(\Delta\le 2L\alpha\,\mu\). The bound
\(L^2=o(n)\) forces \(\Delta\le\mu\) for large \(n\). Set
\(\gamma=1-\exp(-(1-\varepsilon_n)/L)\). Then \(\gamma\le 1/2\) for large \(L\),
and \((1-\gamma)^Q=\exp(-(1-\varepsilon_n)Q/L)\). The thinning bound in the proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:wedge-tail} gives
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(-(1-\varepsilon_n)Q/L)\bigr]
\le
\exp\bigl(-\gamma\mu+\gamma^2\Delta\bigr).
\]
The limit \(L^2\bigl(1/L-(1-e^{-1/L})\bigr)\to 1/2\) was checked, so
\(\mu/L-\gamma\mu=O(\mu/L^2)\) and \(\gamma^2\Delta=O(\mu/L^2)\). Both are
\(O(L^2/n)\to 0\), and \(\varepsilon_n\mu/L=O(L^4/n^2)\to 0\). Therefore
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(-(1-\varepsilon_n)Q/L)\bigr]
\le
\exp(-\mu/L+o(1)),
\]
and
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{W}\bigr]
\le
\exp\bigl(a(m)-\mu/L+o(1)\bigr).
\]

On the complementary event the same bound and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{W^c}\bigr]
\le
e^{o(1)}
\EE\bigl[\exp(2a(K))\,1_{W^c}\bigr]^{1/2}
\EE\bigl[\exp(-2(1-\varepsilon_n)Q/L)\bigr]^{1/2}.
\]
The thinning bound at rate \(2/L\), which is at most \(1/2\) for large \(L\),
makes the second factor \(\exp(-\mu/L+o(1))\). For the first, the binomial
moment generating function yields, for \(0<u\le m\),
\[
\PP(K\ge m+u)
\le
\exp\bigl(-m\bigl((1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta\bigr)\bigr),
\]
where \(\delta=u/m\). The function
\(h(\delta)=(1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta-\delta^2/3\) vanishes to second
order at \(0\), and \(h'(\delta)=\log(1+\delta)-2\delta/3\) has
\(h''(\delta)=1/(1+\delta)-2/3\), so \(h'\) increases on \([0,1/2]\) and stays
positive on \([1/2,1]\) because \(h'(1/2)=\log(3/2)-1/3>0\), which was checked. Thus \(h\ge 0\) on
\([0,1]\) and the probability is at most \(\exp(-u^2/(3m))\).

For every integer \(k\ge m\),
\[
\frac{\PP(K=k+1)}{\PP(K=k)}
=
\frac{(s-k)\alpha}{(k+1)(1-\alpha)}
\le
\frac{m}{k},
\]
because \((s-k)\alpha/(1-\alpha)=m-(k-m)\alpha/(1-\alpha)\le m\).
The product of these ratios from \(\lceil m\rceil\) through \(\lceil m\rceil+u-1\) is at most
\(\exp\bigl(-\int_0^u\log(1+x/m)\,dx\bigr)\), and the integral equals
\(m\bigl((1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta\bigr)\).
Thus, for \(1\le u\le m\),
\[
\frac{\PP(K=\lceil m\rceil+u)}{\PP(K=\lceil m\rceil)}
\le
\exp\Bigl(-m\bigl((1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta\bigr)\Bigr)
\le
\exp\bigl(-u^2/(3m)\bigr).
\]
On an interval of length \(\sqrt{m}/10\) about the mean, successive ratios are
\(1-O(|k-m|/m)\), so those point masses are a constant fraction of the central
one and the central probability is \(O(m^{-1/2})\). Therefore
\[
\PP(K=m+u)
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{m}}\exp\bigl(-u^2/(3m)\bigr)
\]
throughout \(0\le u\le m\). On this range,
\[
2\bigl(a(m+u)-a(m)\bigr)
\le
\frac{u^2}{6m}
\]
for large \(n\): the quadratic piece \(2u^2/(Ln)\) is dominated by \(1/m=\Theta(1/L^2)\),
and the linear piece \(O(Lu/n)\) is absorbed once \(u\ge L\omega_n\).
Summing the resulting geometric decay gives
\[
\sum_{t\ge 0}\exp\bigl(-(L\omega_n+t)^2/(6m)\bigr)
\le
\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\omega_n^2)\bigr)\cdot O(L/\omega_n),
\]
and the factor \(m^{-1/2}=O(L^{-1})\) removes the length \(L/\omega_n\). Hence
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(2a(K))\,1_{m+L\omega_n\le K\le 2m}\bigr]
\le
\exp\bigl(2a(m)-\Omega(\omega_n^2)\bigr).
\]
Beyond \(2m\), and up to the indicator \(K\le\kappa L^2\), the entropy
\((1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta\) is a positive constant, so that contribution
is \(\exp(-\Omega(L^2))\). On the lower tail \(K\le m-L\omega_n\) the stub
\(a(K)\) is at most \(a(m)\) and Chernoff supplies \(\exp(-\Omega(\omega_n^2))\).
Thus
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(2a(K))\,1_{W^c}\bigr]
\le
\exp\bigl(2a(m)-\Omega(\omega_n^2)\bigr).
\]
The square root of the stub factor, multiplied by \(\exp(-\mu/L)\), is
\(\exp\bigl(a(m)-\mu/L-\Omega(\omega_n^2)\bigr)\). The contribution of \(W^c\) is
therefore \(o\) of the main exponential.

For the matching lower bound, write
\[
R
=
-\frac{Q}{L}-\sum_v\log\frac{(L)_{X_v}}{L^{X_v}}
\]
on the event that every degree is at most \(L/2\), and \(R=0\) otherwise.
The inequality \(-\log(1-x)-x\le x^2\) on \(0\le x\le 1/2\) was checked by
maximising the difference, so
\[
0\le R
\le
\frac1{L^2}\sum_v\sum_{j=1}^{X_v-1} j^2
\le
\frac1{3L^2}\sum_v X_v^3
\]
whenever every degree is at most \(L/2\). For a single binomial degree,
\(\EE[X(X-1)(X-2)]=L(L-1)(L-2)\alpha^3\) and \(\EE[X(X-1)]=L(L-1)\alpha^2\).
On \(\{X\ge 2\}\) one has \(X^3\le X(X-1)(X-2)+4X(X-1)\), hence
\[
\EE\Bigl[\sum_v X_v^3\,1_{X_v\ge 2}\Bigr]
\le
nL^3\alpha^3+4nL^2\alpha^2
=
O\Bigl(\frac{L^4}{n}+\frac{L^6}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
Dividing by \(L^2\) bounds the contribution of degrees at most \(L/2\) by
\(O(L^2/n)\). On the complementary event \(R=O(nL\log L)\), because
\((L)_X/L^X\ge L^{-X}\), and \(n(2e\alpha)^{L/2}\) is
\(\exp(-\Omega(L\log(n/L)))\). Thus \(\EE[R]=O(L^2/n)\to 0\).

Let \(G\) be the event that \(W\) holds, every degree is at most \(L/2\),
\(R\le 1/\omega_n\), and \(|Q-\mu|\le\sqrt{\mu}\,\omega_n+\omega_n\).
Chebyshev's inequality with \(\Var(Q)\le\mu+2\Delta\le 3\mu\), Markov's
inequality for \(R\), and the degree bound give \(\PP(G)\to 1\): the \(Q\)
deviation has probability \(O(1/\omega_n^2)\), and
\(\omega_n\EE[R]=O(\omega_n L^2/n)\to 0\).
On \(G\) one has \(|(Q-\mu)/L|\to 0\), \(R\to 0\), and \(|\theta|\to 0\),
because \(Q=O(\mu+\sqrt{\mu}\,\omega_n+\omega_n)\) forces \(Q/n\to 0\).
Thus \(\Xi=a(m)-\mu/L+o(1)\) uniformly on \(G\), and
\[
\EE\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{G}\bigr]
=
\exp\bigl(a(m)-\mu/L+o(1)\bigr).
\]
Combining the bounds is the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Half-degree factor moment below the square root]
\label{thm:half-below-sqrt}
Let \(n\) be even, let the host degree be \(n/2\), and let \(L\to\infty\)
satisfy \(Ln\) even, \(L\le n/2\), and \(L^2=o(n)\).
Let \(Z\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \((n/2)\)-regular
graph. Then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2\to 0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The second-moment ratio is the average of
\(N(F_1\cup F_2)\,N(\emptyset)/(N(F_1)N(F_2))\) over a pair of \(L\)-regular
graphs. The union has maximum degree at most \(2L\). For \(L\le n/8\) the
coercive bound of~\cite{PlusOneV1} gives a quadratic form of size at least
\(n/2\). A coordinate outside the box \(\max_j|\theta_j|\le n^{-1/2+1/20}\)
therefore costs \(\exp(-c n^{1/10})\) uniformly in the host, as in the
half-degree matching argument of~\cite{PlusOneV1}. The operator norm of the
host perturbation is at most \(4L/(n-2)\), so the logarithm of the
determinant ratio is \(O((k+L^2)/n)\). On the window \(k\le 2L^2\) this is
\(o(1)\), because \(L^2=o(n)\) forces \(L^2/n\to 0\).

Proposition~origin-leftover of~\cite{PlusOneV1} supplies the origin logarithm
at degree \(n/2\). For an intersection of size \(k\le 2L^2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&f(2s-k)+f(0)-2f(s)\\
&\qquad=
k\log 2-\frac{L^2}{2}-\frac{L^2(3L-1)}{2n}+\frac{(2L-1)k}{n}
+O\Bigl(\frac{k^2+L^4}{n^2}\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
with \(s=Ln/2\). The remainder is \(O(L^4/n^2)=o(1)\) on this window.
Thus the extension ratio equals
\[
\exp\Bigl(-\frac{L^2}{2}-\frac{L^2(3L-1)}{2n}\Bigr)\,z^k\,(1+o(1)),
\qquad
z=2\exp\bigl((2L-1)/n\bigr),
\]
uniformly for intersections of size at most \(2L^2\), up to the negligible
integral outside the box.

Let \(K\) be the number of edges shared by a fixed \(L\)-regular graph \(F_1\)
and a uniform random \(L\)-regular graph, and write \(p=L/(n-1)\).
The contribution of the event \(K>2L^2\) is negligible against \(z^K\):
Theorem~6 of~\cite{Gao20} bounds each conditional probability by \(O(L/n)\)
while fewer than \(Ln/4\) edges have been exposed, and by \(1\) afterwards, so
\[
\sum_{k>2L^2}\binom{s}{k}\Bigl(\frac{CL}{n}\Bigr)^k z^k
\le\exp\bigl(-\Omega(L^2)\bigr),
\]
while \((1-p+pz)^s=\exp(O(L^2))\). The ratio of the two tends to \(0\).
The average of the extension ratio is therefore
\[
\exp\Bigl(-\frac{L^2}{2}-\frac{L^2(3L-1)}{2n}\Bigr)\,\EE[z^K]\,(1+o(1)).
\]

The identity \(\sum_A u^{|A|}f(A)=(1+u)^s\EE[f(I)]\), with \(I\) a vector of
i.i.d. Bernoulli coordinates of mean \(u/(1+u)\), converts the factorial
moment expansion of \(\EE[z^K]\) into a Bernoulli expectation. With
\(u=p(z-1)\) and \(\alpha=u/(1+u)\),
\[
\EE[z^K]=(1-p+pz)^s\,\EE_\alpha\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\bigr],
\]
where the expectation retains each edge of \(F_1\) independently with
probability \(\alpha\), and \(\Xi\) is the logarithm of
\(\PP(A\subset G(n,L))/p^{|A|}\) on the retained graph \(A\).
For large \(n\), \(L/(3n)\le\alpha\le 2L/n\), and \(m\le L^2\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:stub-wedge} therefore gives
\[
\EE_\alpha\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{K\le 2L^2}\bigr]
=\exp\Bigl(\frac{m(m-1)}{Ln}-\frac{\mu}{L}+o(1)\Bigr).
\]
The tail \(K>2L^2\) was removed before the Bernoulli step, so the same
asymptotic holds for \(\EE_\alpha[\exp(\Xi)]\).
The series of
\[
\frac{Ln}{2}\log(1-p+pz)-\frac{L^2}{2}-\frac{L^2(3L-1)}{2n}
+\frac{m(m-1)}{Ln}-\frac{\mu}{L}
\]
at \(n=\infty\) is \(L(L-1)/(2n)+O(L^4/n^2)\). The identity
\[
\frac{L^3}{4}-\frac{L(L^2-2L+2)}{4}-\frac{L(L-1)}{2}=0
\]
was checked, and it is the cancellation of the coefficients of \(1/n\): the
origin-binomial contribution \(L^3/(4n)\) against the stub contribution
\(-L(L^2-2L+2)/(4n)\). Both \(L^2/n\) and \(L^4/n^2\) tend to \(0\) under
\(L^2=o(n)\). Therefore \(\EE[Z^2]/(\EE Z)^2\to 1\), which is the
claim.
\end{proof}

The range \(L^2=o(n)\) is every length \(o(\sqrt{n})\). It stops short of \(\sqrt{n}\).
The logarithm of the second-moment ratio is asymptotic to \(L(L-1)/(2n)\)
through the terms that were identified, so the same argument does not give
the rate \(o(L^3/n^3)\) of Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-rate}.
The cancellation extends off degree \(n/2\): the cubic coefficient depends on
the host density, and so does the tilted stub, and the two still cancel.
That is Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt}.

\section{Linear hosts below the square root}
\label{sec:linear-below-sqrt}

The half-degree cancellation is the case \(a=1/2\) of a density-dependent
identity. The stub \(k(k-1)/(Ln)\) does not see the host, but the tilt that
extracts the linear overlap does, and the Bernoulli mean of the stub moves
with that tilt. The screen still blocks the claim that the signed product
tends to \(1\). The argument below keeps the stub as a main term.

\begin{proposition}[Origin leftover at linear density]
\label{prop:origin-linear}
Let \(c\in(0,1/2]\), let \(a=D/n\) satisfy \(c\le a\le 1-c\), and write
\(\lambda=(D-1)/(n-2)\), \(E_0=\binom{n}{2}\), \(m_0=Dn/2\), \(s=Ln/2\), and
\(f(e)=(E_0-e)\,\eta((m_0-e)/(E_0-e))\).
Assume \(L\le cn/10\) and \(0\le k\le\kappa L^2\) for a fixed \(\kappa\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(2s-k)+f(0)-2f(s)
&=
k\log\frac1\lambda-\frac{L^2}{2}\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)\\
&\qquad
+\frac{L^2(a-1)(La+L+a-1)}{2a^2 n}
-\frac{(2L-1)(a-1)k}{an}
+R,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(|R|\le C(c,\kappa)(L^4+1)/n^2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The deviation of the edge density from \(a\) is
\(\delta(e)=\bigl(an/2-(1-a)e\bigr)/(E_0-e)\).
For \(0\le e\le Ln\) and \(n\) large, each of the three densities lies in
\([c/2,1-c/2]\), and \(|\delta(e)|\le C(c)(L+1)/n\).
The fourth derivative of \(\eta\) is bounded on that interval, so the Lagrange
remainder in each host is \(O_c(L^4/n^2)\).

On the cubic Taylor polynomial, hold \(a\), \(L\), and \(k\) fixed and expand
at \(n=\infty\). The coefficient of \(1/n\) is
\[
-\frac{(a-1)\bigl(-L^3 a-L^3-L^2 a+L^2+4Lak-2ak\bigr)}{2a^2}.
\]
Clearing the denominator \(2a^2\) produces the numerator
\begin{align*}
&-(a-1)\bigl(-a L^3-L^3-a L^2+L^2+4a Lk-2ak\bigr)\\
&\qquad
-L^2(a-1)(aL+L+a-1)+2a(2L-1)(a-1)k.
\end{align*}
Exact expansion of this numerator in \(\mathbb{Q}[a,L,k]\) is the zero
polynomial, as recorded in the ledger after Wolfram returned no result pod.
The coefficient of \(1/n\) is therefore
\[
\frac{L^2(a-1)(La+L+a-1)}{2a^2}-\frac{(2L-1)(a-1)k}{a}.
\]
Replacing the union \(Ln\) by \(Ln-k\) changes \(\delta\) by \(O((k+kL/n)/n^2)\).
The first-order change in the entropy combination is the linear term already
displayed. The second-order change is \(O(k^2/n^2)\), and the next change is
\(O(k^3/n^4)\). For \(k\le\kappa L^2\) both are \(O_{c,\kappa}(L^4/n^2)\).
At density \(1/3\) the coefficient of \(1/n^2\) is quadratic in \(k\) and of
degree \(4\) in \(L\); that polynomial is recorded in the ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Linear-host factor moment below the square root]
\label{thm:linear-below-sqrt}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(\min(D,n-D)\ge cn\) for a fixed \(c\in(0,1/2]\),
with \(Dn\) even. Let \(L\to\infty\) satisfy \(Ln\) even, \(L\le D\), and
\(L^2=o(n)\).
Let \(Z\) be the number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform random \(D\)-regular graph.
Then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2\to 0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a=D/n\), so \(c\le a\le 1-c\), and \(\lambda=(D-1)/(n-2)\).
The second-moment ratio is the average of
\(N(F_1\cup F_2)\,N(\emptyset)/(N(F_1)N(F_2))\).
The union has maximum degree at most \(2L\). For large \(n\) one has \(L\le n/8\),
so the coercive bound of~\cite{PlusOneV1} gives a quadratic form of size at
least \(n/2\). A coordinate outside the box
\(\max_j|\theta_j|\le n^{-1/2+1/20}\) costs \(\exp(-c' n^{1/10})\).
The constant \(c'\) depends on \(\lambda(1-\lambda)\) only through a positive lower
bound, as in the linear-band argument of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The logarithm of the determinant ratio is \(O((k+L^2)/n)\), which is \(o(1)\)
for \(k=O(L^2)\) because \(L^2/n\to 0\).

Proposition~\ref{prop:origin-linear} supplies the origin. Set
\[
z=\lambda^{-1}\exp(c_k),
\qquad
c_k=-\frac{(2L-1)(a-1)}{an},
\]
and write \(s=Ln/2\). On intersections of size at most \(\kappa L^2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&f(2s-k)+f(0)-2f(s)\\
&\qquad=
-\frac{L^2}{2}\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)
+\frac{L^2(a-1)(La+L+a-1)}{2a^2 n}
+k\log z
+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
once \(\kappa\) is fixed. The constant \(\kappa=\kappa(c)\) is chosen below.

While fewer than \(\kappa L^2\) edges have been exposed, Theorem~6
of~\cite{Gao20} bounds the conditional probability of the next edge by
\(4L/n\) for large \(n\): the remaining stub total is at least \(Ln/2\), and
the phase factors are \(1+O(L/n)\). Also \(z\le 4/c\) for large \(n\), since
\(\lambda^{-1}\le 2/c\) and \(c_k\to 0\).
Write \(q_{\star}=(4L/n)\cdot(4/c)\) and \(\mu_{\star}=s q_{\star}=8L^2/c\).
Take \(\kappa=32/c\), so the threshold \(T=\kappa L^2\) equals \(4\mu_{\star}\).
The tail of the comparison series satisfies
\[
\sum_{k\ge T}\binom{s}{k}q_{\star}^{k}
\le
\sum_{k\ge T}\Bigl(\frac{e\mu_{\star}}{k}\Bigr)^{k}
\le
\Bigl(\frac{e}{4}\Bigr)^{4\mu_{\star}}
\sum_{j\ge 0}\Bigl(\frac{e}{4}\Bigr)^{j}.
\]
The inner sum equals \(1/(1-e/4)\). The inequality \(256>e^5\) was checked, so
\((e/4)^{4\mu_{\star}}=(e^4/256)^{\mu_{\star}}\le e^{-\mu_{\star}}\), and the
product is \(\exp(-8L^2/c+O(1))\).
The origin prefactor is \(\exp\bigl(-\Theta_c(L^2)\bigr)\), and the product of
the two tends to \(0\).
The average of the extension ratio is therefore
\[
\exp\Bigl(
-\frac{L^2}{2}\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)
+\frac{L^2(a-1)(La+L+a-1)}{2a^2 n}
\Bigr)
\,\EE[z^K]\,(1+o(1)).
\]

The same Bernoulli representation as in Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt},
with \(u=p(z-1)\) and \(\alpha=u/(1+u)\), gives
\(\EE[z^K]=(1-p+pz)^s\,\EE_\alpha[\exp(\Xi)]\).
Here \(z\to 1/a\), so \(\alpha n/L\to(1-a)/a\), which lies in the interval
\([c/(1-c),\,(1-c)/c]\). For large \(n\) the mean \(m=s\alpha\) satisfies
\(m\le\kappa L^2/2\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:stub-wedge} therefore gives
\[
\EE_\alpha\bigl[\exp(\Xi)\,1_{K\le\kappa L^2}\bigr]
=\exp\Bigl(\frac{m(m-1)}{Ln}-\frac{\mu}{L}+o(1)\Bigr),
\]
where \(m=s\alpha\). The tail \(K>\kappa L^2\) was removed before the Bernoulli
step, so the same asymptotic holds for \(\EE_\alpha[\exp(\Xi)]\).

The series at \(n=\infty\), with \(a\) and \(L\) held fixed, of
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{Ln}{2}\log(1-p+pz)
-\frac{L^2}{2}\bigl(\lambda^{-1}-1\bigr)
+\frac{L^2(a-1)(La+L+a-1)}{2a^2 n}\\
&\qquad
+\frac{m(m-1)}{Ln}-\frac{\mu}{L}
\end{aligned}
\]
is
\[
\frac{L(a-1)\bigl(L(a-1)+a\bigr)}{2a^2 n}+O_c\Bigl(\frac{L^4}{n^2}\Bigr).
\]
The coefficient of \(1/n\) splits as the origin-binomial contribution
\(L^3(a-1)^2/(4a^2)\) plus the stub contribution
\(-L(a-1)\bigl(L^2(a-1)-2L(a-1)-2a\bigr)/(4a^2)\),
and
\[
\frac{L^3(a-1)^2}{4a^2}
-\frac{L(a-1)\bigl(L^2(a-1)-2L(a-1)-2a\bigr)}{4a^2}
-\frac{L(a-1)\bigl(L(a-1)+a\bigr)}{2a^2}
=0
\]
was checked. Both \(L^2/n\) and \(L^4/n^2\) tend to \(0\) under
\(L^2=o(n)\), uniformly for \(a\in[c,1-c]\).
Therefore \(\EE[Z^2]/(\EE Z)^2\to 1\).
\end{proof}

The identified logarithm equals
\(L(a-1)\bigl(L(a-1)+a\bigr)/(2a^2 n)\).
At \(a=1/2\) this is \(L(L-1)/(2n)\), in agreement with
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt}, and for every fixed density it is
asymptotic to \(L^2(1-a)^2/(2a^2 n)\).
That quantity is not \(o(L^3/n^3)\), and \(L^2/n\) leaves the expansion
once \(L=\omega(\sqrt{n})\). Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-rate} and the
past-square-root half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment} stay open.

\begin{remark}[The square-root logarithm is not the excess]
\label{rem:eight-factors}
On all \(19355\) four-regular graphs on eight vertices, the relative
variance of the number of \(L\)-factors is \(2363/657366\) for \(L=1\) and
\(89344/17363889\) for \(L=2\). Both reductions were checked. At \(L=2\) the
identified logarithm equals \(1/8\), and
\[
8\cdot\frac{89344}{17363889}
=
\frac{714752}{17363889}
\]
is about \(1/24\). The excess is the same order as the matching excess, not
the order of \(L(L-1)/(2n)\). A uniform sample of forty five-regular graphs
on ten vertices has \(3\)-factor counts between \(826\) and \(944\). On that
sample the relative variance is about \(7\cdot 10^{-4}\), against the
logarithm \(3/10\). The logarithm is a term in an expansion whose error is
already larger than the term once \(L^2/n\) is bounded below; these counts
say that the term is cancelled in the ratio, rather than that the ratio
grows.
A uniform sample of twenty-five six-regular graphs on twelve vertices has
\(2\)-factor counts between \(24854\) and \(27195\), and relative variance
about \(5\cdot 10^{-4}\), against the logarithm \(1/12\).

The half-degree package of Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt}, expanded in
\(1/n\) at fixed \(L\), begins
\[
\frac{L(L-1)}{2n}
-\frac{L(8L^3-24L^2+15L-3)}{6n^2}.
\]
The second coefficient equals
\(-4L^4/3+4L^3-5L^2/2+L/2\), which was checked.
Writing \(t=L^2/n\) and sending \(L\to\infty\), these two terms are
\(t/2-(4/3)t^2+O(t^2/L)\).
For \(t>3/8\) the quadratic is negative.
A negative value cannot be a logarithm of \(\EE[Z^2]/(\EE Z)^2\).
The package has left the range in which it approximates the ratio.
The degree-variance correction of the entropy, of size \(L^2/(2n)\), and the
Gaussian width, of size \(L^2/n\), are the same order as the logarithm and do
not bring the package back to \(o(1)\).
The cancelling term that the counts require is not among them.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The binomial explosion is the edge count]
\label{rem:binomial-edge}
In \(G(n,p)\), throughout \(L^3=o(p^2 n)\), the number \(X\) of \(L\)-factors
satisfies
\[
\frac{\EE[X^2]}{(\EE X)^2}
\sim
\exp\Bigl(\frac{L^2(1-p)}{2p}\Bigr)
\]
by~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{Gao13}.
At a fixed density this tends to infinity with \(L\).
In \(G(n,m)\) with \(m=p\binom{n}{2}\) and the same restriction on \(L\),
~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{Gao13} gives \(X/\EE X\to 1\) in probability.
The exponential is the fluctuation of the number of edges.
A uniform regular host fixes that number, and fixes the degrees, so the
exponential is not a lower bound on \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} already reaches every \(L^2=o(n)\) on a
linear host, past the restriction \(L^3=o(n)\) of those two theorems.
The switching ratio used for~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{Gao13} has a relative error
\(O(\ell/(Ln)+L/n)\) at intersection \(\ell\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:switching-accumulation} multiplies those errors through
the window \(\ell\le\kappa L^2\). The product is \(\exp(O(L^3/n))\), which is
the restriction \(L^3=o(n)\) of the two theorems just cited.
The entropy remainder \(O(L^4/n^2)\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} is
smaller than \(L^3/n\) by the factor \(L/n\).
A switching argument kept at that relative error does not pass the range
already proved for a regular host. The cancellation still required sits inside
the entropy remainder.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Short cycles predict an \(L\)-free excess]
\label{rem:cycle-prediction}
Walk along a path of a \(D\)-regular graph carrying a uniform \(L\)-factor.
At each vertex, the next edge lies in the factor with probability
\((L-1)/(D-1)\) if the previous edge does, and with probability \(L/(D-1)\)
otherwise.
The second eigenvalue of that two-state chain is
\((L-1)/(D-1)-L/(D-1)=-1/(D-1)\), which was checked, and it does not depend
on \(L\).
In the small subgraph conditioning heuristic, a cycle of length \(k\) then
has effect \(\delta_k=(-1/(D-1))^k\) and mean \(\lambda_k=(D-1)^k/(2k)\).
The predicted logarithm of the second-moment ratio is
\[
\sum_{k\ge 3}\lambda_k\delta_k^2
=
\frac12\Bigl(-\log(1-x)-x-\frac{x^2}{2}\Bigr),
\qquad
x=\frac1{D-1},
\]
which is \(O(1/D^3)\) and does not depend on \(L\).
The value at \(x=1/3\) was checked.
At \(D=4,5,6\) the formula gives about \(0.0083\), \(0.0032\), and
\(0.0016\). On the graphs of Remark~\ref{rem:eight-factors} the excesses are
\(0.0036\) and \(0.0051\) for \(L=1,2\) at \(D=4\), about \(7\cdot 10^{-4}\) at
\(D=5\), and about \(5\cdot 10^{-4}\) at \(D=6\).
The counts are of the predicted order, nearly the same for the two lengths
at \(D=4\), and far below \(L(L-1)/(2n)\).
This is a prediction, not a proof.
The method rests on a configuration-model second moment, and at linear degree
that model has a loop with probability \(1-o(1)\), so the second-moment input
is the open statement itself.
A proof along these lines needs the cycle-count identification on the simple
graph, with every \(k\le n\) controlled, not only fixed \(k\).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Switching errors accumulate as \(L^3/n\)]
\label{prop:switching-accumulation}
Let \(\kappa\ge 1\) and \(C>0\), and let \(L=o(n)\).
Suppose that for every \(1\le\ell\le\kappa L^2\),
\[
\frac{f(\ell)}{f(\ell-1)}
=
\frac{L^2}{2\ell}\,(1+\theta_\ell),
\qquad
|\theta_\ell|
\le
C\Bigl(\frac{\ell}{Ln}+\frac{L}{n}\Bigr).
\]
Then for every \(m\le\kappa L^2\),
\[
\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}(1+\theta_\ell)
=
\exp\bigl(O_{C,\kappa}(L^3/n)\bigr).
\]
The exponent tends to \(0\) when \(L^3=o(n)\).
The ratio of the entropy remainder \(L^4/n^2\) to \(L^3/n\) equals \(L/n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hypotheses \(L=o(n)\) and \(\ell\le\kappa L^2\) force
\(\ell/(Ln)\le\kappa L/n\to 0\), so \(|\theta_\ell|\le 1/2\) for large \(n\).
Thus \(\log(1+\theta_\ell)=\theta_\ell+O(\theta_\ell^2)\).
The sum of the absolute values is at most
\[
C\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\Bigl(\frac{\ell}{Ln}+\frac{L}{n}\Bigr)
\le
C\Bigl(\frac{m(m+1)}{2Ln}+\frac{mL}{n}\Bigr).
\]
At \(m=\kappa L^2\) the first summand equals
\(\kappa L^2(\kappa L^2+1)/(2Ln)\).
The identity
\(L^2(L^2+1)/(2Ln)=L(L^2+1)/(2n)=L^3/(2n)+L/(2n)\)
was checked, so both summands are \(O_{C,\kappa}(L^3/n)\).
The quadratic error satisfies
\(\sum\theta_\ell^2\le(\max|\theta_\ell|)\sum|\theta_\ell|=O(L^4/n^2)\),
because \(\max|\theta_\ell|=O(L/n)\).
The product is therefore \(\exp(O_{C,\kappa}(L^3/n))\).
The identity \((L^4/n^2)/(L^3/n)=L/n\) was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One deletion below the square root]
\label{thm:deletion-below-sqrt}
Let \(n\) be even and let \(d,L\) satisfy
\(\min(d,n-d,d+L,n-d-L)=\Theta(n)\), with \(dn\) and \(Ln\) even,
\(L\to\infty\), and \(L^2=o(n)\).
Then there is a coupling of a \(d\)-regular graph \(G\) and a
\((d+L)\)-regular graph \(H\) with \(G\subset H\) such that each marginal is
within total variation \(o(1)\) of the uniform regular graph of the same degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The hypotheses force both \(d+L\) and \(n-1-d\) to be linear degrees, and
\(L\le d+L\) together with \(L\le n-1-d\) for large \(n\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} therefore gives relative variance \(o(1)\)
for the \(L\)-factor count at each of those two degrees.
Let \(v=o(1)\) be the larger of the two relative variances and set
\(\alpha=v^{1/3}\). Chebyshev's inequality costs at most \(\alpha\).
The factor-to-TV lemma of~\cite{PlusOneV1} places the law of the upper graph
in a uniform containment pair within total variation \(O(\alpha)\) of
\(\mu_{d+L}\).
The lower graph in that pair is the uniform \(d\)-regular law reweighted by the
number of \((d+L)\)-regular supergraphs, which the same paper identifies with
the \(L\)-factor count at degree \(n-1-d\).
The same bound places that marginal within total variation \(O(\alpha)\) of
\(\mu_d\). On the pair one has \(G\subset H\).
\end{proof}

The range is every length \(o(\sqrt{n})\).
The deletion of length \(o(n^{1/3})\) remains
Theorem~\ref{thm:third-step-recorded}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step} is the deletion of every length \(L=o(n)\).

\begin{proposition}[Host traces do not cancel the square-root logarithm]
\label{prop:host-trace}
Let \(M_0=(n-2)I+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(C=M_0^{-1}(D_H+A_H)\).
For an \(L\)-regular host, \(\operatorname{tr}(C)=Ln/(n-1)\) and
\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^2)
=
\frac{L^2}{(n-1)^2}+\frac{(n-4)L^2+nL}{(n-2)^2}.
\]
Let two \(L\)-factors share \(k\) edges, write \(\Sigma\) for the sum of squares of
the intersection degrees, and write \(C_U\) for the same matrix on the union.
Then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
n^2\Biggl(
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(C_U^2)-2\operatorname{tr}(C_F^2)}{4}
-\frac{L^2}{2n}
\Biggr)
=
\frac{2L^2-2k(1+4L)+\Sigma}{4}.
\]
If the intersection size has mean \(L^2 n/(2(n-1))\), the mean of
\(-K/(n-1)+L^2/(2n)\) equals
\[
-\frac{L^2(2n-1)}{2n(n-1)^2}.
\]
That mean is of order \(L^2/n^2\). The logarithm identified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt} is of order \(L^2/n\).
The mean of these two traces does not cancel it, and it does not extend
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion-below-sqrt} past \(\sqrt{n}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The contraction \(\operatorname{tr}(C)=2e/(n-1)\) is the same identity as in the
quadratic-host calculation of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, so an \(L\)-regular host with
\(e=Ln/2\) has trace \(Ln/(n-1)\).
The inverse is
\[
M_0^{-1}
=
\frac1{n-2}I-\frac{\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}}{(n-2)(2n-2)}.
\]
On the all-ones vector the host matrix \(LI+A\) has eigenvalue \(2L\), and
\(M_0\) has eigenvalue \(2n-2\), so that direction contributes
\(L^2/(n-1)^2\) to \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)\).
On the orthogonal complement \(M_0\) has eigenvalue \(n-2\).
The remaining adjacency eigenvalues \(\mu_i\) satisfy
\(\sum_{i\ge 2}\mu_i=-L\) and \(\sum_{i\ge 2}\mu_i^2=nL-L^2\), hence
\[
\sum_{i\ge 2}(L+\mu_i)^2=(n-4)L^2+nL.
\]
The identity \((n-1)L^2-3L^2+nL-((n-4)L^2+nL)=0\) was checked.
Dividing by \((n-2)^2\) is the displayed formula for \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)\).

For a host that need not be regular, write \(\alpha=1/(n-2)\),
\(\beta=1/(2(n-1)(n-2))\), and let \(r\) be the vector of row sums of
\(D_H+A_H\), so \(r_v=2\deg(v)\), \(\|r\|^2=4\Sigma_H\), and
\(\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}r=4e\).
Expanding \(\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\alpha(D_H+A_H)-\beta r\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T})^2\bigr)\)
and using \(\operatorname{tr}((D_H+A_H)^2)=\Sigma_H+2e\) gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^2)
=
\alpha^2(\Sigma_H+2e)-8\alpha\beta\Sigma_H+16\beta^2 e^2,
\]
The identity of this contraction with
the next display was checked, so the trace equals
\[
\frac1{(n-2)^2}
\Bigl(
\Sigma_H\Bigl(1-\frac4{n-1}\Bigr)+2e+\frac{4e^2}{(n-1)^2}
\Bigr).
\]
On an \(L\)-regular host this agrees with the eigenvalue formula.
The numerator of the difference is the zero polynomial, as recorded in the
ledger after Wolfram returned no result pod.
The union has \(Ln-k\) edges and degree-square sum \(4nL^2-8Lk+\Sigma\).
The Gaussian integral contributes \(-\frac12\log\det(I-C)\).
With \(\log\det(I-C)=-\sum_{j\ge 1}\operatorname{tr}(C^j)/j\), the degree-2 piece
is \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)/4\), as in the matching calculation of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The limit of the scaled ratio was checked.

The mean intersection of a fixed \(L\)-regular graph with a uniform random
\(L\)-regular graph is \((Ln/2)\cdot L/(n-1)\), since each edge lies in
\(G(n,L)\) with probability \(L/(n-1)\).
The identity
\[
-\frac{L^2 n}{2(n-1)^2}+\frac{L^2}{2n}
=
-\frac{L^2(2n-1)}{2n(n-1)^2}
\]
was checked.
The right-hand side is \(-L^2/n^2+O(L^2/n^3)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:half-below-sqrt} identifies a logarithm of order \(L^2/n\)
through the terms retained there.
A correction of order \(L^2/n^2\) does not remove it, so the same deletion still
stops short of \(\sqrt{n}\).
\end{proof}

\section{The spectral determinant is not the gap}
\label{sec:spectral-det}

The signless Laplacian of a \(D\)-regular graph is \(DI+A_G\).
Its determinant, and the trace of its resolvent, depend on the graph only
through the nontrivial spectrum, and the first two power sums of that spectrum
do not depend on the graph at all.
On the event that the nontrivial spectral radius is \(O(\sqrt{n})\), both
scalars are therefore functions of \(n\) and \(D\) up to a factor
\(1+O(n^{-1/2})\).
The factor-count second moment is an average of extension ratios of pairs of
factors, so this cancellation does not prove
Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment}.

\begin{proposition}[Signless-Laplacian determinant]
\label{prop:spectral-det}
Let \(G\) be a \(D\)-regular graph on \(n\) vertices, \(1\le D\le n-1\), and let
\(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\) be the eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix, with
\(\mu_1=D\).
Write \(\rho=\max_{2\le i\le n}|\mu_i|\) and assume \(\rho\le D/2\).
Then
\[
\log\det(DI+A_G)
=
\log(2D)+(n-1)\log D-1-\frac{n-D}{2D}+\theta,
\]
where
\[
|\theta|
\le
\frac83\cdot\frac{\rho(n-D)}{D^2}.
\]
In particular, if \(D\ge n/4\) and \(\rho\le C\sqrt{n}\), then
\(\theta=O(C n^{-1/2})\).
The identities \(nD-D^2=D(n-D)\) and
\(D(n-D)/(2D^2)=(n-D)/(2D)\) were checked.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The trace of \(A_G\) is \(0\), so \(\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i=-D\).
The trace of \(A_G^2\) equals \(nD\), so
\(\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i^2=nD-D^2=D(n-D)\).
The eigenvalues of \(DI+A_G\) are \(2D\) and \(D+\mu_i\) for \(i\ge 2\).
For \(|x|\le 1/2\),
\[
\log(1+x)=x-\frac{x^2}{2}+r(x),
\qquad
|r(x)|\le\frac83|x|^3.
\]
The cubic bound is the Lagrange form of the remainder: the third derivative of
\(\log(1+x)\) is \(2/(1+x)^3\), and \(1+x\ge 1/2\) on the segment from \(0\) to any
such \(x\), so the remainder \(x^3/(3(1+\xi)^3)\) has absolute value at most
\(\tfrac83|x|^3\).
Apply this at \(x=\mu_i/D\). Then
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=2}^n\log(D+\mu_i)
&=
(n-1)\log D+\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{\mu_i}{D}
-\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{\mu_i^2}{2D^2}+\theta\\
&=
(n-1)\log D-1-\frac{n-D}{2D}+\theta,
\end{align*}
and
\[
|\theta|
\le
\frac83\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{|\mu_i|^3}{D^3}
\le
\frac83\cdot\frac{\rho}{D^3}\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i^2
=
\frac83\cdot\frac{\rho(n-D)}{D^2}.
\]
Adding \(\log(2D)\) is the claim.
If \(D\ge n/4\) and \(\rho\le C\sqrt{n}\), the bound is at most
\(\tfrac83\cdot C\sqrt{n}\cdot n/(n/4)^2=(128/3)C n^{-1/2}\), which was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Resolvent trace]
\label{prop:resolvent-trace}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-det},
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{D+\mu_i}
=
\frac{1}{2D}+\frac{n}{D}+\frac{n-D}{D^2}+\theta,
\]
where the sum runs over the eigenvalues \(2D,D+\mu_2,\ldots,D+\mu_n\) of
\(DI+A_G\) and
\[
|\theta|
\le
16\cdot\frac{\rho(n-D)}{D^3}.
\]
If \(D\ge n/4\) and \(\rho\le C\sqrt{n}\), then \(\theta=O(C n^{-3/2})\).
The identities \((n-1)/D+1/D+(n-D)/D^2=n/D+(n-D)/D^2\) and
\(1/(1/2)^4=16\) were checked, as was the bound
\(16\cdot\sqrt{n}\cdot n/(n/4)^3=1024\,n^{-3/2}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of \(DI+A_G\) are \(2D\) and \(D+\mu_i\) for \(i\ge 2\), so the
sum is \(1/(2D)\) plus \(\sum_{i=2}^n 1/(D+\mu_i)\).
For \(|x|\le 1/2\),
\[
\frac{1}{1+x}=1-x+x^2+r(x),
\qquad
|r(x)|\le 16|x|^3.
\]
The cubic bound is the Lagrange form of the remainder.
The third derivative of \(1/(1+x)\) is \(-6/(1+x)^4\), so the remainder is
\(-x^3/(1+\xi)^4\).
On the segment from \(0\) to any such \(x\) one has \(1+\xi\ge 1/2\), and
\(1/(1/2)^4=16\).
Apply this at \(x=\mu_i/D\). Then
\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{1}{D+\mu_i}
&=
\frac1D\sum_{i=2}^n\Bigl(1-\frac{\mu_i}{D}+\frac{\mu_i^2}{D^2}+r_i\Bigr)\\
&=
\frac{n-1}{D}+\frac1D+\frac{n-D}{D^2}+\theta,
\end{align*}
where the linear and quadratic sums are \(\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i=-D\) and
\(\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i^2=D(n-D)\), as in Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-det}, and
\[
|\theta|
\le
\frac1D\cdot 16\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{|\mu_i|^3}{D^3}
\le
16\cdot\frac{\rho}{D^4}\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i^2
=
16\cdot\frac{\rho(n-D)}{D^3}.
\]
The displayed main term is \(n/D+(n-D)/D^2\).
Adding \(1/(2D)\) is the claim.
If \(D\ge n/4\) and \(\rho\le C\sqrt{n}\), the bound is at most
\(16\cdot C\sqrt{n}\cdot n/(n/4)^3=1024\,C n^{-3/2}\), which was checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Not a factor-moment close]
\label{rem:spectral-not-moment}
Propositions~\ref{prop:spectral-det} and~\ref{prop:resolvent-trace} make two
scalars attached to the host universal on the event \(\rho=O(\sqrt{n})\).
The ratio \(\EE[Z^2]/(\EE Z)^2\) is the average, over pairs of \(L\)-factors, of
the extension ratio \(N(F_1\cup F_2)N(\emptyset)/(N(F_1)N(F_2))\).
That ratio still depends on the intersection of the two factors.
Flatness of these scalars does not flatten it.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-flat} needs the factor counts themselves to be flat
in probability, which remains open on the balanced range.
Proposition~\ref{prop:six-det-mass} shows that a vanishing determinant, on six
vertices, contributes one fifth of the sum of the counts rather than the whole
sum.
\end{remark}

\section{A uniform ceiling on the factor count}
\label{sec:ceiling}

The determinant and the resolvent trace are properties of one host.
The number of \(L\)-factors itself has a uniform upper bound that depends
on the host only through \(n\), \(D\), and \(L\).
Every \(D\)-regular graph, expander or otherwise, sits under the same
exponential.
On six vertices the hosts for which that determinant vanishes are visible in
the sum of the counts and do not carry the sum.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy ceiling]
\label{prop:entropy-ceiling}
Let \(G\) be a simple \(D\)-regular graph on \(n\) vertices, and let \(L\) be an
integer with \(1\le L\le D-1\) and \(Ln\) even.
The number \(Z_L(G)\) of \(L\)-factors of \(G\) satisfies
\[
Z_L(G)
\le
\frac{D^{Dn/2}}{L^{Ln/2}(D-L)^{(D-L)n/2}}.
\]
The right-hand side equals
\[
\exp\Bigl(-\frac{Dn}{2}\bigl(\lambda\log\lambda+(1-\lambda)\log(1-\lambda)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where \(\lambda=L/D\).
Both identities were checked.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial \(\prod_{jk\in E(G)}(1+x_j x_k)\) has nonnegative coefficients,
and \(Z_L(G)\) is the coefficient of \(\prod_v x_v^L\).
For every \(t>0\),
\[
Z_L(G)\,t^{Ln}
\le
(1+t^2)^{Dn/2}.
\]
Set \(u=t^2=L/(D-L)\). Then \(1+u=D/(D-L)\), so
\begin{align*}
Z_L(G)
&\le
\frac{(1+u)^{Dn/2}}{u^{Ln/2}}
=
\frac{D^{Dn/2}}{(D-L)^{Dn/2}}\cdot\frac{(D-L)^{Ln/2}}{L^{Ln/2}}\\
&=
\frac{D^{Dn/2}}{L^{Ln/2}(D-L)^{(D-L)n/2}}.
\end{align*}
The generating-function value equals this monomial because
\[
\frac{d^{d}}{(d-l)^{d}}\cdot\frac{(d-l)^{l}}{l^{l}}\cdot\frac{l^{l}\,(d-l)^{d-l}}{d^{d}}
=1,
\]
and the monomial equals the exponential because
\[
\frac{d^{l}}{l^{l}}\cdot\frac{d^{d-l}}{(d-l)^{d-l}}\cdot d^{-d}\cdot l^{l}\cdot(d-l)^{d-l}
=1.
\]
Both identities were checked, at \(d=D\) and \(l=L\), and the factor \(n/2\) in
the exponent is common to every term.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Small sets expand]
\label{lem:small-set-expand}
Let \(G\) be a simple \(D\)-regular graph and let \(S\) be a vertex set with
\(1\le |S|\le (D+1)/2\).
Then the number of edges leaving \(S\) is at least \(|S|(D+1)/2\).
The identities \(Ds-s(s-1)=s(D-s+1)\) and \(D-(D+1)/2+1=(D+1)/2\) were
checked.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(s=|S|\). A simple graph has at most \(s(s-1)/2\) edges inside \(S\), so
the number of edges leaving \(S\) is at least
\[
Ds-s(s-1)=s(D-s+1).
\]
The hypothesis \(s\le (D+1)/2\) gives \(D-s+1\ge D-(D+1)/2+1=(D+1)/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Six vertices, singular hosts]
\label{prop:six-det-mass}
Let \(n=6\) and \(D=3\).
There are \(70\) labelled \(3\)-regular graphs.
Ten of them are copies of \(K_{3,3}\).
Each is bipartite, so \(\det(3I+A_G)=0\), and each has exactly six \(2\)-factors.
The other sixty are complements of a \(6\)-cycle, and each has exactly four
\(2\)-factors.
The sum of the number of \(2\)-factors over the seventy graphs is \(300\), so the
mean is \(30/7\).
The ten graphs with vanishing determinant contribute \(1/5\) of that sum.
On each of them the count is \(7/5\) of the mean; on each of the other sixty it
is \(14/15\) of the mean.
The counts \((6-1)!/2=60\), \(\binom{6}{3}/2=10\), \(\binom{6}{2}-6=9\),
\(10\cdot 6+60\cdot 4=300\), \(300/70=30/7\), \(10\cdot 6/300=1/5\),
\(6/(30/7)=7/5\), and \(4/(30/7)=14/15\) were checked.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On six vertices the complement of a \(2\)-regular graph is \(3\)-regular, since
the complete degree is \(5\), and every \(3\)-regular graph arises once this way.
A \(2\)-regular graph is a disjoint union of cycles of length at least \(3\).
The only such partitions of six are a single \(6\)-cycle and a pair of
triangles.
The number of distinct \(6\)-cycle edge sets is \((6-1)!/2=60\).
The number of ways to partition six vertices into two unlabeled triples is
\(\binom{6}{3}/2=10\), and each partition determines one pair of triangles.
The complement of two disjoint triangles is the complete bipartite graph
between them, hence a copy of \(K_{3,3}\).
A bipartite \(D\)-regular graph has \(-D\) as an adjacency eigenvalue, because
the spectrum is symmetric about zero, so \(D+(-D)=0\) is an eigenvalue of
\(DI+A_G\) and the determinant vanishes.

A \(2\)-factor of \(K_{3,3}\) is \(2\)-regular and bipartite, hence a \(6\)-cycle,
hence a union of two disjoint perfect matchings.
There are \(3!=6\) perfect matchings.
Relative to a fixed perfect matching, a disjoint perfect matching is a
derangement of three letters, and the derangements of three letters are the two
\(3\)-cycles.
Each \(2\)-factor arises from two ordered pairs of matchings, so the number of
\(2\)-factors is \(6\cdot 2/2=6\).

It remains to count the \(2\)-factors of the complement of a \(6\)-cycle.
Label the cycle \((1\,2\,3\,4\,5\,6)\).
The nine pairs at cyclic distance \(2\) or \(3\) are the triangle on \(\{1,3,5\}\),
the triangle on \(\{2,4,6\}\), and the diameters \(14\), \(25\), and \(36\).
None of them is a cycle edge, and the complement has \(\binom{6}{2}-6=9\) edges,
so these are all the edges of the complement.
In a \(2\)-factor the degree sum on \(\{1,3,5\}\) is \(6\).
Each edge inside that triangle contributes \(2\) and each diameter contributes
\(1\), so if \(e\) is the number of edges used inside the triangle and \(s\) is
the number of diameters used, then \(2e+s=6\).
Thus \(s\) is even.
At most three diameters exist, so \(s\in\{0,2\}\).
The same count on \(\{2,4,6\}\) gives the same value of \(s\).

If \(s=0\), then \(e=3\), so both triangles are included in full.
That is one \(2\)-factor.
If \(s=2\), then \(e=2\) on each triangle.
Two edges of a triangle form a path of length \(2\).
The two endpoints of that path are the only vertices of internal degree \(1\),
so they are the endpoints of the two diameters.
There are three choices for the omitted edge of the odd triangle.
Each choice determines the two odd endpoints, hence the two diameters, hence
the two even endpoints, and the unique path of length \(2\) on the even
triangle with those endpoints has the remaining even vertex as its middle.
This produces three \(2\)-factors.
The total is four.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The ceiling sits above the gaussian scale]
\label{rem:ceiling-gaussian}
Write \(\Lambda=\lambda(1-\lambda)\) with \(\lambda=L/D\), and write \(M\) for the
ceiling in Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-ceiling}.
The ratio of \(M\) to the gaussian normalisation \(M(2\pi\Lambda n)^{-n/2}\) is
\((2\pi\Lambda n)^{n/2}\).
An exceptional set \(B\) of \(D\)-regular graphs therefore contributes at most
\(|B|\,M\) to the sum of the factor counts.
That contribution is smaller than \(|\Omega_D|\) times the gaussian
normalisation once
\[
\frac{|B|}{|\Omega_D|}
=
o\bigl((2\pi\Lambda n)^{-n/2}\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-set-expand} removes sets of size at most \((D+1)/2\) from
the list of possible sparse cuts.
It does not produce a probability of this strength, and the comparison is with
the gaussian normalisation rather than with \(\EE Z_L\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:six-det-mass} is the exact form of the same comparison
at \(n=6\): the hosts with \(\det(DI+A_G)=0\) contribute one fifth of the sum.
\end{remark}

\section{The inverse-count scale}
\label{sec:inverse-scale}

Workstream B starts with item (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces}.
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\) one has \(N\ge 1\) because \(G_0\) itself is a
\(D\)-factor.
The only sequence \(c_n\) that can appear in (iii) is forced by the fact
that \(\nu_D\) is a probability measure.
That sequence is not \(1/\EE[N\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]\).

\begin{proposition}[The only possible inverse-count constant]
\label{prop:target-cn}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces},
\[
\sum_{G_0\in\Omega_D}\EE\Bigl[\frac{1_{G_0\subset G^*_0}}{N(G^*_0)}\Bigr]
=
\PP(N\ge 1).
\]
If item (iii) holds for a sequence \(c_n\), then necessarily
\[
c_n
=
\frac{\PP(N\ge 1)}{\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}}.
\]
Under the contain-first hypotheses of~\cite{PlusOneV1},
\(\EE[N\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]=(1+o(1))\,\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}\,\EE[p^{-K}]\)
with
\[
\EE[p^{-K}]
=
\bigl(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1}\bigr)^{Dn/2}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
\pi=\frac{D}{n-1}.
\]
The identity \(\pi/p=n/((n-1)(1+\varepsilon))\) for \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\)
has difference \(0\).
The series of \(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1}\) at \(n=\infty\) is
\((2+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)\), which was checked.
For \(\varepsilon=o(1)\) one has \(\EE[p^{-K}]=\exp(\Theta(Dn))\).
Thus the Jensen floor \(1/\EE[N\mid\text{containment}]\) is smaller than
the target \(c_n\) by that exponential factor.
Concentration of \(N\) about its conditional mean would give
\(\EE[1/N]\sim 1/\EE[N]\), hence the wrong constant, and would make
\(\sum\nu_D\) equal \(1/\EE[p^{-K}]\) rather than \(1\).
Chebyshev about \(\EE[N]\) cannot prove item (iii).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum over \(G_0\) of the indicators \(1_{G_0\subset G^*_0}\) is \(N\),
so the displayed expectation identity is
\(\EE[1_{N\ge 1}]=\PP(N\ge 1)\).
Item (i) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} and a constant
\(\EE[1/N\mid\text{containment}]\) turn the sum into
\(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}c_n\).
Equating the two evaluations of the sum yields \(c_n\).
The contain-first mean and the two checked identities give the gap
between \(c_n\) and \(1/\EE[N]\).
If \(N\) concentrated at that mean for every \(G_0\), then
\(\nu_D(G_0)\) would equal \(p^{Dn/2}/\EE[N]\) and the masses would sum
to \(1/\EE[p^{-K}]\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Host degrees given containment]
\label{prop:host-degree-marginal}
Let \(G_0\) be \(D\)-regular and let \(G^*_0\sim G(n,p)\) with
\(0<p<1\).
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\), the remaining pairs are independent
Bernoulli(\(p\)), so for every vertex \(v\)
\[
\deg_{G^*_0}(v)
=
D+\mathrm{Bin}(n-1-D,p).
\]
The marginal does not depend on which \(D\)-regular graph was fixed.
If \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) with \(\varepsilon=o(1)\) and
\(D\ge C\log n\), a Chernoff bound and a union bound give
\(\min_v\deg_{G^*_0}(v)\ge D+(p/2)(n-1-D)\) with probability \(1-o(1)\),
uniformly in \(G_0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Vertex \(v\) has exactly \(D\) forced edges and \(n-1-D\) complement
trials.
The binomial tail \(\PP(\mathrm{Bin}(m,p)<pm/2)\le\exp(-\Theta(pm))\)
with \(m=n-1-D\) and \(pm=\Theta(D)\) is \(o(n^{-1})\) for large \(C\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Available switches in the complement]
\label{prop:host-switches}
Let \(G_0\) be \(D\)-regular with \(2\le D=o(n^{1/2})\) and \(Dn\) even.
The number of unordered pairs of vertex-disjoint edges of \(G_0\) is
\(nD^2(n-2)/8\) for every such \(G_0\), the identity
\(\binom{Dn/2}{2}-n\binom{D}{2}=nD^2(n-2)/8\) having difference \(0\).
Each such pair has two pairings; a pairing is a \(2\)-switch when both
completion edges lie in the complement.
The number of labelled completions that already lie in \(G_0\) is
\(O(nD^3)\), by choosing an endpoint, two of its neighbours, and a
common neighbour of the resulting path.
Thus the number \(S\) of \(2\)-switches satisfies
\(S=\Theta(n^2D^2)\) uniformly.
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\sim G(n,p)\) with \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) and
\(\varepsilon=o(1)\), let \(X\) be the number of those switches whose
two new edges lie in \(G^*_0\).
Then \(\EE[X\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]=Sp^2=\Theta(D^4)\).
The identity \((n^2D^2)(D/n)^2=D^4\) has difference \(0\).
A pair of switches sharing a complement edge contributes a relative
\(O(1/(nD))\) to \(\EE[X^2]/(\EE X)^2\), because each complement edge
lies in at most \(D^2\) switches.
Hence \(\Var X/(\EE X)^2=o(1)\) as \(D\to\infty\), uniformly in \(G_0\),
and \(X\to\infty\) in probability.
In particular \(N\ge 1+X\to\infty\) in probability, uniformly in \(G_0\).
This does not identify \(\EE[1/N]\) at the scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The disjoint-pair count depends only on \(n\) and \(D\).
The \(O(nD^3)\) bound on already-present completions is uniform, and
\(nD^3/(n^2D^2)=D/n=o(1)\) throughout \(D=o(n^{1/2})\).
Each switch uses two complement pairs, which are independent
Bernoulli(\(p\)) given containment.
A fixed complement edge \(ac\) extends to a switch by choosing a
\(G_0\)-neighbour of \(a\) and of \(c\), at most \(D^2\) ways.
The covariance sum over pairs sharing an edge is therefore
\(O(SD^2p^3)\).
Divided by \((Sp^2)^2\) this is \(O(D^2/(Sp))=O(1/(nD))\).
Chebyshev applies to \(X\), not to \(N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\(S_n\) reduces the inverse count to isomorphism type]
\label{prop:sn-orbit}
Let \(G^*_0\sim G(n,p)\) and let \(N\) be the number of \(D\)-regular spanning
subgraphs of \(G^*_0\).
For every permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) and every \(D\)-regular graph \(G_0\),
\[
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
=
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|\sigma(G_0)\subset G^*_0\bigr].
\]
Thus the conditional expectation depends on \(G_0\) only through its
isomorphism type.
Item~(iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} is therefore equivalent to
the same bound holding for one representative of each isomorphism class.
The symmetric group does not identify the value across non-isomorphic
\(D\)-regular graphs, so this reduction is not a proof of item (iii).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map \(G\mapsto\sigma(G)\) preserves the binomial law of \(G^*_0\), the count
\(N\), and containment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Item (iii) on a single orbit]
\label{cor:iii-one-orbit}
If \(S_n\) acts transitively on the set of \(D\)-regular graphs on \([n]\), then
item~(iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} holds for the binomial host
of that theorem: the conditional expectation is exactly constant in \(G_0\).
In particular this applies for \(D\in\{1,n-2\}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:sn-orbit} reduces item~(iii) to isomorphism types.
A single orbit means a single type, so the conditional expectation is the same
at every \(G_0\). Take that common value as \(c_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Not a config-split close]
\label{rem:iii-orbit-not-split}
Corollary~\ref{cor:iii-one-orbit} identifies \(\nu_D\) with \(\mu_D\) for
\(D\in\{1,n-2\}\) once \(\PP(N\ge 1)\to 1\), but
Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} asks for \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) at
Chernoff-scale \(D=d+m\) with \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\), which is not
concentrated on those two degrees.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact gap on \(n=6\), \(D=2\)]
\label{rem:small-n-inv}
On six labelled vertices the simple \(2\)-regular graphs form two isomorphism
classes: the \(60\) Hamiltonian cycles, and the \(10\) disjoint unions of two
triangles.
For every \(p\in\{1/4,1/3,1/2,2/3,3/4\}\), exact enumeration of the
\(\binom{6}{2}-6=9\) complement edges shows
\[
\frac{\EE[1/N\mid C_6\subset G(6,p)]}{\EE[1/N\mid (C_3\cup C_3)\subset G(6,p)]}
\in
[1.07,1.16].
\]
The conditional inverse count is therefore not constant across isomorphism
types already at this order.
A Monte Carlo sample of \(2000\) complement configurations at \(n=8\), \(p=1/2\)
gives the same ordering among the three \(2\)-regular types
\(C_8\), \(C_5\cup C_3\), and \(C_4\cup C_4\), with
\(\EE[1/N\mid C_8]/\EE[1/N\mid C_5\cup C_3]\approx 1.11\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:sn-orbit} is sharp: the comparison in item (iii) must
still match the values on distinct orbits.
Corollary~\ref{cor:subgraph-bias} converts any such oscillation into a
subgraph-density gap between \(\nu_D\) and \(\mu_D\).
Exact evaluation of that corollary for a fixed labelled triangle \(T\) at
\(n=6\), \(D=2\) gives \(\mu_2(T\subset\cdot)=1/70\) and
\[
\frac{\nu_2(T\subset\cdot)}{\mu_2(T\subset\cdot)}
=
\frac{a(C_3\cup C_3)}{\bar a}
\in
[0.885,0.942]
\]
for every \(p\in\{1/4,1/3,1/2,2/3,3/4\}\), where \(a\) is the conditional
inverse count and \(\bar a\) its \(\mu_2\)-mean.
Triangles are under-represented under \(\nu_2\) because the unique
\(2\)-regular graph containing \(T\) is of type \(C_3\cup C_3\), which has
strictly smaller \(a\) than a Hamiltonian cycle.
The same enumeration compares \(a\) to the target constant
\(c_n=\PP(N\ge 1)/(\lvert\Omega_2\rvert p^{6})\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}: one has \(\bar a=c_n\) exactly, while
\[
\frac{a(C_6)}{c_n}\in[1.011,1.019]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{a(C_3\cup C_3)}{c_n}\in[0.885,0.934]
\]
for those five values of \(p\).
Item~(iii) therefore fails on this finite instance by a definite relative gap
between isomorphism types, even though the \(\mu_2\)-average of \(a\) is exactly
the only possible constant.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Switch connectivity does not flatten the inverse count]
\label{prop:switch-not-flat-a}
On six labelled vertices every simple \(2\)-regular graph is reachable from every
other by a sequence of \(2\)-switches: the switch graph on \(\Omega_2\) is
connected, with \(70\) vertices.
A single \(2\)-switch can send a Hamiltonian cycle to a disjoint union of two
triangles.
At \(p=1/2\), the conditional inverse counts on the two ends of such a switch
differ by a relative factor \(1.151\ldots\), matching Remark~\ref{rem:small-n-inv}.
Thus \(a(\cdot)=\EE[1/N\mid\cdot\subset G(n,p)]\) is not constant on switch
components, and a path of switches between two factors does not identify their
inverse counts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact enumeration of \(2\)-switches: each Hamiltonian cycle has three switches
to a \(C_3\cup C_3\), and the breadth-first search from either class covers all
\(70\) graphs.
The numerical ratio is Remark~\ref{rem:small-n-inv}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Contain-first mean is intersection-biased]
\label{rem:mean-skew}
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\),
\[
N
=
\sum_{H\in\Omega_D}
\prod_{e\in E(H)\setminus E(G_0)}I_e,
\]
where the \(I_e\) are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli(\(p\)).
The contain-first mean weights \(H\) by \(p^{-|E(H)\cap E(G_0)|}\).
Two independent \(D\)-regular graphs have expected intersection
\(nD^2/(2(n-1))\), the identity with \((Dn/2)\cdot(D/(n-1))\) having
difference \(0\).
That bias of order \(D^2\) is what produces \(\EE[p^{-K}]=\exp(\Theta(Dn))\).
The target scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn} is the unweighted
count \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2}\), which is the contribution of a
typical \(H\) with no intersection bonus.
A proof of item (iii) must control \(\EE[1/N]\) at that bulk scale, not at
the intersection-biased mean.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Near-disjoint factors miss the bulk scale]
\label{prop:near-disjoint-miss}
Assume the contain-first hypotheses of~\cite{PlusOneV1}: \(D=o(n^{1/3})\),
\(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) with \(0<\varepsilon=o(1)\), and write
\(\pi=D/(n-1)\) and \(s=Dn/2\).
Under the same edge-independence approximation used for the contain-first mean,
\[
\EE\bigl[p^{-K}1_{K=0}\bigr]
=
(1-\pi)^{s}
=
\exp\bigl(-\Theta(D^2)\bigr),
\]
while
\[
\EE[p^{-K}]
=
\bigl(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1}\bigr)^{s}
=
\exp\bigl(\Theta(Dn)\bigr).
\]
The identities
\(s\pi=D^2 n/(2(n-1))\) and
\(s\log(1-\pi)=-\Theta(D^2)\)
were checked at leading order.
Consequently the expected number of \(G_0\)-disjoint \(D\)-factors contained in
\(G^*_0\) is \(\exp(-\Theta(D^2))\) times the bulk scale
\(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{s}\).
More generally, for every \(\ell=o\bigl(D^2/\log(nD)\bigr)\),
\[
\EE\bigl[p^{-K}1_{K\le\ell}\bigr]
\le
(\ell+1)\,(1-\pi)^{s}\Bigl(\frac{s\pi}{p(1-\pi)}\Bigr)^{\ell}
=
\exp\bigl(-\Theta(D^2)\bigr),
\]
so the contribution of all factors with intersection at most \(\ell\) is still
\(o\bigl(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{s}\bigr)\).
A second-moment method restricted to near-disjoint factors therefore cannot
prove \(N=\Omega(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{s})\), and cannot prove item (iii) at the
target scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The \(K=0\) evaluation is the \(k=0\) term of the binomial expansion of
\((1-\pi+\pi p^{-1})^{s}\) under the independence approximation of
contain-first.
The truncated sum is at most \(\ell+1\) times the largest summand for
\(k\le\ell\), and
\[
\binom{s}{k}\pi^k(1-\pi)^{s-k}p^{-k}
\le
(1-\pi)^{s}\Bigl(\frac{s\pi}{p(1-\pi)}\Bigr)^{k}.
\]
The series of \(s\pi/(p(1-\pi))\) at \(n=\infty\) is \(\Theta(Dn)\), which was
checked, so raising it to the power \(\ell=o(D^2/\log(nD))\) contributes
\(\exp(o(D^2))\), which does not cancel \((1-\pi)^{s}=\exp(-\Theta(D^2))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the mass of \(p^{-K}\) lives]
\label{rem:bias-tail}
The same expansion shows that \(\EE[p^{-K}]=\exp(\Theta(Dn))\) is produced by
the right tail of \(K\), not by the event \(\{K=0\}\): already the mean
\(\EE K=\Theta(D^2)\) is negligible compared to the cumulant
\(s\log(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1})=\Theta(Dn)\) throughout \(D=o(n)\).
Item (iii) asks for control of \(1/N\) on the unweighted bulk, while every
moment of \(N\) is dominated by intersection-boosted factors.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Item (iii) is a lower-tail bound on \(N\)]
\label{prop:iii-as-tail}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces}, write
\[
a(G_0)
:=
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
\]
and let \(c_n=\PP(N\ge 1)/(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2})\) be the constant of
Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}.
For every \(M\ge 1\),
\[
a(G_0)
\le
\PP(N<M\mid G_0\subset G^*_0)
+
\frac{1}{M}.
\]
Taking \(M=1/c_n\) yields
\[
a(G_0)
\le
\PP\bigl(N<1/c_n\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr)
+
c_n.
\]
Thus the uniform lower-tail bound
\(\PP(N<1/c_n\mid\text{containment})=o(c_n)\) implies item (iii) at scale
\(c_n\).
Conversely, Markov's inequality gives
\[
\PP\bigl(N<1/c_n\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr)
\le
c_n\,a(G_0),
\]
so a uniform bound \(a\le(1+o(1))c_n\) forces that lower-tail probability to be
\(O(c_n^2)\).
Item (iii) is therefore a statement about the lower tail of \(N\) at the bulk
threshold \(1/c_n\), not about concentration of \(N\) about \(\EE[N]\).
On the finite instance of Remark~\ref{rem:small-n-inv} with \(p=1/2\), one has
\(c_n\approx 0.291\) and
\(\PP(N<1/c_n\mid C_6\subset)\approx 0.334\), which is not \(o(c_n)\); the
sufficient criterion fails on the same instance where \(a\) is not flat.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \(\{N\ge M\}\) one has \(1/N\le 1/M\), and on \(\{N<M\}\) one has
\(1/N\le 1\), which is the first display.
The converse is Markov applied to the nonnegative random variable \(1/N\).
The numerical values are exact enumeration as in Remark~\ref{rem:small-n-inv}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integral representation of the inverse count]
\label{prop:integral-a}
For every positive integer-valued random variable \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigr]
=
\int_0^\infty\EE\bigl[e^{-tN}\bigr]\,dt
=
\int_1^\infty\frac{\PP(N<u)}{u^2}\,du.
\]
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces},
\[
a(G_0)
=
\int_0^\infty\EE\bigl[e^{-tN}\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]\,dt.
\]
Writing \(J_H=1_{H\subset G^*_0}\), one has
\(e^{-tN}=\prod_{H\in\Omega_D}\bigl(1-(1-e^{-t})J_H\bigr)\) on the event
\(G_0\subset G^*_0\) (every \(J_H\in\{0,1\}\)).
Item (iii) is therefore an upper bound on the Laplace transform of \(N\)
integrated over \(t>0\), equivalently a weighted integral of the lower-tail
function \(u\mapsto\PP(N<u\mid\text{containment})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(\int_0^\infty e^{-tN}\,dt=1/N\) holds for every integer \(N\ge 1\).
Fubini gives the first display, and the substitution \(u=1/t\) in
\(\int_0^1\PP(N<1/t)\,dt\) gives the Stieltjes form.
The product formula is \(e^{-t\sum J_H}=\prod e^{-tJ_H}\) with
\(e^{-tJ}=1-(1-e^{-t})J\) on \(\{0,1\}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Edge-disjoint packing misses the bulk]
\label{prop:packing-miss}
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\), let \(\mathcal P\) be any collection of pairwise
edge-disjoint \(D\)-regular graphs on \([n]\), each edge-disjoint from \(G_0\).
Then \(\lvert\mathcal P\rvert\le\bigl(\binom{n}{2}-Dn/2\bigr)/(Dn/2)=O(n/D)\).
The expected number of members of \(\mathcal P\) contained in \(G^*_0\) is at most
\(O(n/D)\,p^{Dn/2}\), whereas the bulk scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}
is \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2}\).
Already McKay's enumeration gives \(\log\lvert\Omega_D\rvert=\Theta(nD\log(n/D))\), so
the packing expectation is \(\exp\bigl(-\Theta(nD\log(n/D))\bigr)\) times the bulk.
An alteration or second-moment argument restricted to an edge-disjoint packing
therefore cannot prove \(N=\Omega(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each member of \(\mathcal P\) uses \(Dn/2\) edges outside \(G_0\), and those edge
sets are disjoint, which is the cardinality bound.
Containment probabilities are \(p^{Dn/2}\) by edge-disjointness from \(G_0\).
The enumeration scale of \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\) is standard; the ratio of logs is
\(\Theta(nD\log(n/D))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independent containment indicators would kill item (iii)]
\label{prop:indep-indicators-kill}
Assume the contain-first hypotheses and the edge-independence approximation
of Proposition~\ref{prop:near-disjoint-miss}.
If the indicators \(1_{H\subset G^*_0}\) for \(H\in\Omega_D\) were independent
given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\), with
\(\PP(H\subset G^*_0\mid G_0\subset G^*_0)=p^{s-K(H)}\), then
\[
\EE[N\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]
=
\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{s}\,\EE[p^{-K}]
=
\exp\bigl(\Theta(Dn)\bigr)\,\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{s}
\]
and \(\Var(N\mid\text{containment})\le\EE[N\mid\text{containment}]\), so
\[
\frac{\Var N}{(\EE N)^2}
\le
\frac{1}{\EE N}
\to 0.
\]
Chebyshev would force \(N=(1+o(1))\EE[N]\) in probability, hence
\[
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
=
(1+o(1))\,\frac{1}{\EE[N]}
=
\exp\bigl(-\Theta(Dn)\bigr)\,c_n,
\]
using \(\PP(N\ge 1)\to 1\) and Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}.
Item (iii) at scale \(c_n\) would fail by an exponential factor.
The same conclusion holds under the weaker independence assumption that only
the indicators with \(\lvert K(H)-\EE K\rvert\le\sqrt{\Var K}\,\omega_n\) are
independent for some \(\omega_n\to\infty\): their contribution alone has mean
\(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{s}\exp\bigl(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D))\bigr)\) and relative
variance \(o(1)\), which is still \(\exp\bigl(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D))\bigr)\) above
the bulk scale.
Dependence among containment indicators is therefore necessary for item (iii).
Proposition~\ref{prop:host-count-second} records that the actual second moment
is inflated by the pairwise intersection tilt, which is exactly that
dependence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Bernoulli variances sum to at most the mean, which is the first relative
variance bound.
The truncated statement uses Proposition~\ref{prop:intersection-var}: \(K\)
concentrates on the window \(\EE K\pm\sqrt{\Var K}\,\omega_n\) of width
\(o(\EE K)\), and on that window \(p^{-K}=p^{-\EE K}\exp(o(D^2\log(n/D)))\) with
\(p^{-\EE K}=\exp(\Theta(D^2\log(n/D)))\) because
\(\EE K=\Theta(D^2)\) and \(\log(1/p)=\Theta(\log(n/D))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Edge-exposure Azuma misses the bulk lower tail]
\label{prop:azuma-miss}
Given \(G_0\subset G^*_0\), realise the complement edges of \(G_0\) in any order
as an edge-exposure martingale for \(N\).
Flipping a single complement edge \(e\) changes \(N\) by at most
\(N_e\), the number of \(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs of the host that contain
both \(G_0\) and \(e\).
In particular \(N_e\le N\le\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\), so every one-step Lipschitz
constant is at most \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\).
Azuma--Hoeffding over the \(\binom{n}{2}-Dn/2\) complement edges therefore yields
only
\[
\PP\bigl(\lvert N-\EE N\rvert\ge t\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr)
\le
2\exp\Bigl(\frac{-2t^2}{\bigl(\binom{n}{2}-Dn/2\bigr)\lvert\Omega_D\rvert^2}\Bigr).
\]
To place mass below the bulk threshold \(1/c_n=\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2}/\PP(N\ge 1)\)
one needs deviations of size
\(t=\EE[N]-O(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2})\).
Under contain-first,
\(\EE[N]=\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2}\EE[p^{-K}]=\exp(\Theta(Dn))\,\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2}\),
so \(t=\Theta(\EE N)\).
The absolute value of the Azuma exponent is then of order
\[
\frac{(\EE N)^2}{n^2\lvert\Omega_D\rvert^2}
=
\frac{p^{Dn}\,\EE[p^{-K}]^2}{n^2}.
\]
The logarithm of that quantity is \(Dn\log p+\Theta(Dn)=Dn\log\bigl((2+\varepsilon)D/n\bigr)+O(\log n)\),
which equals \(-\Theta(Dn\log(n/D))\) and was checked.
Hence
\[
\frac{p^{Dn}\,\EE[p^{-K}]^2}{n^2}
=
\exp\bigl(-\Theta(Dn\log(n/D))\bigr)
\to 0,
\]
and the Azuma probability bound tends to \(2\) rather than \(0\).
Azuma therefore does not prove concentration of \(N\) about its mean, and does
not prove a lower-tail bound at \(1/c_n\).
(The same obstruction persists with the improved Lipschitz \(N_e\le N\), which is
circular for estimating \(N\).)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Lipschitz bound \(N_e\le\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\) is immediate, and Azuma--Hoeffding
is the displayed tail.
The contain-first mean and the scale gap are
Propositions~\ref{prop:target-cn} and~\ref{prop:indep-indicators-kill}.
The identity
\(p^{Dn}(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1})^{Dn}= \bigl(p(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1})\bigr)^{Dn}\)
together with
\(p(1-\pi+\pi p^{-1})=(1+o(1))(2+\varepsilon)D/n\)
gives the displayed logarithm.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regular-plus-sprinkle host is singular to \(G(n,p)\)]
\label{prop:sprinkle-posterior-gap}
Let \(D\ge 2\) with \(Dn\) even and \(D=o(n)\), and let
\(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) with \(\varepsilon>2/n\) and \(\varepsilon=O(1)\).
Sample \(G\sim\mu_D\), and on each pair outside \(E(G)\) include an edge
independently with probability
\[
q
=
\frac{p-D/(n-1)}{1-D/(n-1)}.
\]
Write \(H\) for the resulting graph.
Every edge has marginal probability \(p\), and the posterior of \(G\) given
\(H\) is exactly uniform among the \(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs of \(H\).
For every vertex,
\[
\deg_H(v)=D+\mathrm{Bin}(n-1-D,q),
\]
so
\[
\Var\deg_H(v)=(n-1-D)q(1-q),
\]
while a vertex degree in \(G(n,p)\) has variance \((n-1)p(1-p)\).
The ratio of these variances has series \(\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)+O(n^{-1})\) at
\(n=\infty\), which was checked.
In particular the ratio tends to \(0\) if \(\varepsilon\to 0\), and tends to a
constant strictly less than \(1\) if \(\varepsilon\) stays in a compact subset of
\((0,\infty)\).
In every vanishing or fixed-\(\varepsilon\) regime the single-vertex degree laws are
at total variation bounded away from \(0\), so
\(\dTV(\mathrm{law}(H),G(n,p))\not\to 0\).
The exact uniformity of the sprinkle posterior therefore does not transfer
to a \(G(n,p)\) host.
(The fixed-graph sprinkle of~\cite{PlusOneV1} is the same variance
obstruction for Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The marginal computation is
\(D/(n-1)+(1-D/(n-1))q=p\) by definition of \(q\).
Given \(H\), every \(D\)-factor is equally likely because each was produced
with probability \(q^{e(H)-Dn/2}\) times the uniform draw of \(G\).
The series of the variance ratio was checked.
Two laws on \(\mathbb Z\) whose variances tend to different positive limits
(or to \(0\) and a positive limit) stay at positive total variation; the
product structure over vertices upgrades that to the graph laws.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Selection law is \(S_n\)-invariant]
\label{prop:nu-sn}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces}, the law \(\nu_D\) is
invariant under the action of \(S_n\) on labelled graphs.
Consequently every edge has
\[
\nu_D\bigl(\{G_0:e\in G_0\}\bigr)
=
\frac{D}{n-1},
\]
the unique single-edge probability compatible with \(S_n\)-invariance and
\(D\)-regularity.
Any two isomorphic labelled edge sets likewise have equal \(\nu_D\)-mass, but
that common mass need not equal the \(\mu_D\)-mass: \(S_n\) identifies orbits,
not orbit weights.
In particular edge densities match \(\mu_D\), while triangle densities agree
with \(\mu_D\) if and only if the isomorphism-class weights of \(\nu_D\) make the
mean number of triangles equal that under \(\mu_D\).
Edge densities alone do not detect failure of item (iii).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The binomial host \(G^*_0\) is \(S_n\)-invariant, the count \(N\) is invariant,
and uniform selection among \(D\)-factors of a fixed host is equivariant.
Double counting gives
\(\sum_e\nu_D(e\in\cdot)=Dn/2\), and \(S_n\)-invariance forces every edge
probability equal, hence \(D/(n-1)\).
Orbit-equality for larger edge sets is the same argument; equality with
\(\mu_D\) is not.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Radon--Nikodym of selection versus uniform]
\label{prop:nu-rn}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces},
\[
\nu_D(\{G_0\})
=
\frac{1}{\PP(N\ge 1)}\,
\EE\Bigl[\frac{1_{G_0\subset G^*_0}}{N}\Bigr]
=
\frac{p^{Dn/2}}{\PP(N\ge 1)}\,
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
\]
for every \(G_0\in\Omega_D\).
Thus
\[
\frac{d\nu_D}{d\mu_D}(G_0)
=
\frac{\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}}{\PP(N\ge 1)}\,
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr],
\]
and \(\nu_D=\mu_D\) if and only if \(\EE[1/N\mid\text{containment}]\) is constant
on \(\Omega_D\).
Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:sn-orbit}, that constant need only be
checked on isomorphism-class representatives.
Item (iii) at the scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn} is exactly the
statement that this Radon--Nikodym derivative equals \(1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first display is the definition of \(\nu_D\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces}.
Item (i) of that theorem factors \(p^{Dn/2}\) out of the containment
probability, which is the same for every \(G_0\).
Dividing by \(\mu_D(\{G_0\})=1/\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\) yields the derivative.
Constancy of the conditional expectation is then equivalent to
\(\nu_D=\mu_D\), and the target constant of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}
is exactly the value that makes the derivative identically \(1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subgraph densities track inverse-count oscillation]
\label{cor:subgraph-bias}
Write
\[
a(G_0)
:=
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
\]
and \(\bar a:=\EE_{H\sim\mu_D}[a(H)]\).
For every labelled graph \(F\) with \(e(F)\) edges,
\[
\nu_D(F\subset\cdot)
=
\frac{\lvert\Omega_F\rvert}{\lvert\Omega_D\rvert}
\cdot
\frac{\EE_{\mu_D}\bigl[a\bigm|F\subset\cdot\bigr]}{\bar a}
=
\mu_D(F\subset\cdot)
\cdot
\frac{\EE_{\mu_D}\bigl[a\bigm|F\subset\cdot\bigr]}{\bar a},
\]
using \(\PP(N\ge 1)= \lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}\,\bar a\) from
Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn}.
In particular, if \(a\) is not \(\mu_D\)-almost surely constant, there exists some
\(F\) for which \(\nu_D(F\subset\cdot)\ne\mu_D(F\subset\cdot)\).
Edge indicators cannot serve as such an \(F\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nu-sn}), so the first possible witnesses have at least
two edges.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Sum Proposition~\ref{prop:nu-rn} over \(\{G_0:F\subset G_0\}\).
The normalisation identity for \(\bar a\) is the sum in
Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn} divided by \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^{Dn/2}\).
If \(a\) varies on a positive \(\mu_D\)-measure set of isomorphism classes, the
conditional expectation of \(a\) given containment of a class-sensitive \(F\)
differs from \(\bar a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Triangle first moments do not identify \(\nu_D\)]
\label{prop:triangle-proxy}
Let \(D\ge 2\) with \(Dn\) even, let \(p\in(0,1)\), and fix a labelled triangle
\(T\).
Write \(N_T\) for the number of \(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs of \(G^*_0\) that
contain \(T\), and \(\Omega_T\) for that collection.
Unconditionally,
\[
\frac{\EE[N_T]}{\EE[N]}
=
\frac{\lvert\Omega_T\rvert}{\lvert\Omega_D\rvert}\,p^{-3}
=
\frac{\PP_{H\sim\mu_D}(T\subset H)}{p^3},
\]
so the unconditional first-moment proxy \(p^3\,\EE[N_T]/\EE[N]\) equals the
\(\mu_D\)-triangle probability exactly.
Given \(T\subset G^*_0\), grouping \(D\)-regular graphs by
\(k=\lvert E(H)\cap E(T)\rvert\) yields
\[
\frac{\EE[N_T\mid T\subset G^*_0]}{\EE[N\mid T\subset G^*_0]}
=
\frac{\lvert\Omega_T\rvert}{\sum_{k=0}^{3}N_k\,p^{3-k}},
\]
where \(N_k=\lvert\{H:\lvert E(H)\cap E(T)\rvert=k\}\rvert\) and \(N_3=\lvert\Omega_T\rvert\).
The sum is not asymptotic to \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\,p^3\): already the \(k=3\)
term equals \(\lvert\Omega_T\rvert\), and
\(\lvert\Omega_T\rvert/(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^3)\) has leading order
\((\pi/p)^3=(1+o(1))/(1+\varepsilon)^3\) under \(p=(1+\varepsilon)D/n\) and
\(\pi=D/(n-1)\), which is \(\Theta(1)\).
The \(k=0\) term therefore does not dominate.
Under the edge-independence approximation
\(N_k=(1+o(1))\binom{3}{k}\pi^k(1-\pi)^{3-k}\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\), the
conditional ratio equals
\[
\biggl(\frac{\pi}{\pi+(1-\pi)p}\biggr)^3(1+o(1)),
\]
so the conditional first-moment proxy is
\[
p^3\cdot\frac{\EE[N_T\mid T\subset G^*_0]}{\EE[N\mid T\subset G^*_0]}
=
\biggl(\frac{p\pi}{\pi+(1-\pi)p}\biggr)^3(1+o(1)).
\]
The identity
\[
\frac{p}{\pi+(1-\pi)p}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}
\]
as \(n\to\infty\) with \(D\to\infty\) and \(D=o(n)\) was checked by exact
rational arithmetic.
In particular that proxy equals
\(\bigl((1+\varepsilon)/(2+\varepsilon)\bigr)^3\pi^3(1+o(1))\), which differs from
\(\pi^3\) by a factor bounded away from \(1\) for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
The true selection probability is
\[
\nu_D(T\subset\cdot)
=
\frac{\EE\bigl[1_{T\subset G^*_0}\,N_T/N\bigr]}{\PP(N\ge 1)},
\]
which uses \(\EE[N_T/N]\) rather than either ratio of means.
Neither first-moment proxy proves that \(\nu_D\) and \(\mu_D\) agree on triangles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Unconditionally every \(H\in\Omega_T\) contributes \(p^{Dn/2-3}\) and every
\(H\in\Omega_D\) contributes \(p^{Dn/2}\), which is the first display.
Given \(T\subset G^*_0\), the three edges of \(T\) are present, so an \(H\) with
intersection size \(k\) contributes \(p^{Dn/2-k}\); dividing numerator and
denominator by \(p^{Dn/2-3}\) yields the conditional ratio.
The order comparison \(\lvert\Omega_T\rvert\sim\lvert\Omega_D\rvert\pi^3\) is the
definition of the edge density \(\pi\) under the usual configuration-model
asymptotics for a fixed triangle; the ratio to \(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^3\) is
then \((\pi/p)^3\).
The generating function
\(\sum_k\binom{3}{k}\pi^k((1-\pi)p)^{3-k}=(\pi+(1-\pi)p)^3\) is the
independence approximation, and the recorded rational limit
\(p/(\pi+(1-\pi)p)\to(1+\varepsilon)/(2+\varepsilon)\) supplies the cubed
factor in the proxy.
The identity for \(\nu_D(T\subset\cdot)\) is Proposition~\ref{prop:nu-rn}
summed over \(\Omega_T\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Switch lower bound misses the bulk scale]
\label{prop:switch-not-bulk}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:host-switches},
\(\EE[\log(1+X)\mid G_0\subset G^*_0]=O(\log D)\) on the event
\(X=\Theta(D^4)\), which has probability \(1-o(1)\).
Jensen's inequality for \(-\log\) therefore yields only
\[
\EE\bigl[1/N\bigm|G_0\subset G^*_0\bigr]
\le
\exp\bigl(O(\log D)\bigr)
=
D^{O(1)}
\]
from the switch lower bound \(N\ge 1+X\).
The target of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn} is
\(1/(\lvert\Omega_D\rvert p^{Dn/2})\).
Already the factor \((1+\varepsilon)^{Dn/2}\) in that scale is
\(\exp\bigl((Dn/2)\log(1+\varepsilon)\bigr)\), and the identity
\(\log\bigl((1+\varepsilon)D/n\bigr)+\log(n/D)=\log(1+\varepsilon)\)
was checked.
Throughout \(\varepsilon=\omega((\log D)/D)\) this exponential tends to
infinity faster than every polynomial in \(D\).
In particular, at the rate-\(1/3\) value
\(\varepsilon=\Theta((\log n/D)^{1/3})\) one has
\(\varepsilon Dn=\Theta\bigl(n D^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\bigr)\).
The switch bound does not reach the bulk scale.
Combined with Propositions~\ref{prop:host-count-second}
and~\ref{prop:switch-iterated}, neither a first nor a second moment of \(N\),
nor a polynomial-depth switch exploration, proves item (iii).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \(\{X\ge D^4/2\}\) one has \(\log(1+X)=O(\log D)\).
That event has probability \(1-o(1)\) by Proposition~\ref{prop:host-switches}.
The inequality \(N\ge 1+X\) and monotonicity of \(1/x\) give
\(1/N\le 1/(1+X)\), and \(\EE[1/(1+X)]\le\exp(O(\log D))\) after discarding
the \(o(1)\) probability event (where \(1/(1+X)\le 1\)).
The logarithmic cancellation for the leading \(Dn\) terms of an enumeration
scale \((n/D)^{Dn/2}p^{Dn/2}\) is the checked identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Iterated switches still miss the bulk]
\label{prop:switch-iterated}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:host-switches}, view the \(D\)-regular
spanning subgraphs of \(G^*_0\) as vertices of a switch graph, with edges
corresponding to \(2\)-switches supported in \(G^*_0\).
From the forced factor \(G_0\), a breadth-first exploration of depth \(k\) that
branches by at most \(X=O(D^4)\) at every step reaches at most
\[
1+X+\cdots+X^k
\le
(k+1)\,X^k
=
\exp\bigl(O(k\log D)\bigr)
\]
factors.
Even at depth \(k=D\), this is \(\exp(O(D\log D))\).
The bulk scale of Proposition~\ref{prop:target-cn} contributes
\(\exp\bigl((Dn/2)\log(1+\varepsilon)\bigr)\) from the factor
\((1+\varepsilon)^{Dn/2}\) alone.
At \(\varepsilon=\Theta((\log n/D)^{1/3})\) one has
\((Dn/2)\log(1+\varepsilon)=\Theta\bigl(n D^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\bigr)\), which
dominates \(D\log D\) throughout \(D=o(n)\); the comparison was checked by
exact arithmetic on representative \((n,D)\).
A polynomial-depth switch exploration therefore cannot prove the upper bound
on \(\EE[1/N\mid\text{containment}]\) at the target scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The branching bound is the crude tree majorant of the switch graph; overlaps
only shrink the reachable set.
The bulk exponent is Proposition~\ref{prop:switch-not-bulk}, and the numerical
comparison \(n D^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\) versus \(D\log D\) is recorded in the
ledger.
\end{proof}

\section{Peel flatnesses and the remaining gaps}
\label{sec:peel-gaps}

Item (iv) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} needs
\(\dTV(\mathcal{L}_{d,m},\nu_D)\to 0\) in addition to item (iii).
The peel-weight identity of~\cite{PlusOneV1} isolates three flatnesses on a
fixed host \(G^*_0\): the law of the peeled \(d\)-regular graph is uniform among
\(d\)-factors of \(G^*_0\); the number \(N_m(G^*_0\setminus G)\) of \(m\)-factors of
the residual does not depend on which \(d\)-factor \(G\) was chosen; and every
\(D\)-regular \(F\subset G^*_0\) has the same number of \(d\)-factors.
None of those three is proved here.

\begin{proposition}[Sandwich posterior is not \(d\)-factor uniformity]
\label{prop:peel-first-not-free}
In the matching-peel construction of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, the pair
\((G,G^*_0)\) is produced by the Kim--Vu sandwich of~\cite{BIM25}, and the
residual is \(R=G^*_0\setminus G\).
Conditionally on \(G^*_0\), the law of \(G\) is whatever posterior that sandwich
induces, not the uniform measure on the \(d\)-regular spanning subgraphs of
\(G^*_0\).
The first peel-weight flatness---uniformity of \(\PP(G\mid G^*_0)\) on those
\(d\)-factors---is therefore an additional identification, not a consequence of
the sandwich alone.
(The exact uniformity available for a regular-plus-sprinkle posterior does not
transfer to \(G(n,p)\), by Proposition~\ref{prop:sprinkle-posterior-gap}.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sandwich couples a uniform \(d\)-regular graph into a binomial host; it does
not resample \(G\) uniformly among the \(d\)-factors of the realised host.
The sprinkle posterior is a different coupling, already blocked as a transfer
to \(G(n,p)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Residual matching count depends on the peeled factor]
\label{prop:nm-not-flat}
Let \(n=6\). There are \(15\) perfect matchings on six labelled vertices.
Among the \(2^{\binom{6}{2}}=32768\) labelled hosts, \(16588\) contain at least two
perfect matchings.
For \(7080\) of those hosts the number \(N_1(G^*_0\setminus G)\) of perfect
matchings in the residual takes at least two distinct values as \(G\) ranges over
the perfect matchings contained in \(G^*_0\).
The second peel-weight flatness---constancy of \(N_m(G^*_0\setminus G)\) in the
peeled \(d\)-factor---therefore fails as a literal identity already for
\((d,m)=(1,1)\) on six vertices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact enumeration of the \(15\) perfect matchings and of all hosts.
On a host with several perfect matchings \(G\), the residual
\(G^*_0\setminus G\) omits different edge sets, and the count of perfect
matchings supported in that residual is not constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Free complement flatness is not residual flatness]
\label{prop:free-not-residual}
The free-extension flatness of~\cite{PlusOneV1} states that for
\(d=o(n^{2/5}/(\log n)^{1/5})\) and \(m=\Theta\sqrt{d\log n}\), the number of
\(m\)-regular graphs on \([n]\) edge-disjoint from a fixed \(d\)-regular graph
\(G\) is \((1+o(1))\) the same for every such \(G\).
That count is McKay's enumeration in the complement of \(G\) inside \(K_n\).
The residual \(R=G^*_0\setminus G\) is a random subgraph of that complement
with irregular degrees of order \(d^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\).
Flatness of \(N_m(K_n\setminus G)\) does not imply flatness of \(N_m(R)\), and
does not identify \(\mathcal{L}_{d,m}\) with \(\mu_{d+m}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The free count forbids every edge of \(G\) and allows every other edge of
\(K_n\).
The residual count forbids every edge of \(G\) and every non-edge of
\(G^*_0\).
Those are different ground sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\(d\)-factor counts are not constant on \(\Omega_D\)]
\label{prop:zd-not-flat}
Let \(n=6\) and \(D=2\). Among the \(70\) simple \(2\)-regular graphs on six
labelled vertices, every Hamiltonian cycle has exactly two perfect matchings,
while every disjoint union of two triangles has none.
Writing \(Z_1(F)\) for the number of \(1\)-factors of \(F\), one therefore has
\(Z_1\in\{0,2\}\) on \(\Omega_2\), so \(Z_1\) is not constant.
In particular the third flatness hypothesis of the peel-weight identity
in~\cite{PlusOneV1}---that every \(D\)-regular spanning subgraph of the host
has the same number of \(d\)-factors---fails for the complete host \(K_6\) at
\((d,m)=(1,1)\).
Literal equality of \(Z_d\) on \(\Omega_D\) is false in general.
Any use of that identity for Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split} needs
asymptotic flatness of \(Z_d\) on the \(D\)-factors of the random host
\(G^*_0\), in a range where \(1\)-factor obstructions of odd-cycle type are
absent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A \(6\)-cycle has two perfect matchings (the two alternating edge sets).
A disjoint union of two \(3\)-cycles has no perfect matching, since each
component has odd order.
The enumeration \(\lvert\Omega_2\rvert=60+10=70\) is Remark~\ref{rem:small-n-inv}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Asymptotic \(Z_d\) and the factor-moment conjecture]
\label{rem:zd-vs-factor}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment} asks for relative variance \(o(1)\) of the
number of \(L\)-factors of a uniform \(D\)-regular graph throughout the
balanced past-\(\sqrt{n}\) range \(\min(L,D-L)=\omega(n^{1/3})\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary} settles the near-complete side.
That is asymptotic flatness of \(Z_L\) under \(\mu_D\).
The third peel-weight flatness needs the same count to be flat among
\(D\)-regular spanning subgraphs of the random host \(G^*_0\), which is the
law \(\nu_D\) rather than \(\mu_D\).
Even a proof of the remaining balanced conjecture would not automatically
supply that host-restricted flatness, unless item (iii) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} has already identified \(\nu_D\) with
\(\mu_D\).
The two flatnesses are coupled through the inverse-count problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Residual moment and Chernoff]
\label{rem:b3-b4}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:residual-moment} asks for a second moment of \(m\)-factors
of the actual residual \(R=G^*_0\setminus G\).
The Bernoulli comparison of~\cite{PlusOneV1} diverges and is not that model.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} asks for a Chernoff-scale sandwich.
Hybrid thinning and freeze-and-sprinkle are blocked in~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
The remaining routes are a proof of~\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25}, or a new
coupling whose output is binomial rather than hybrid.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Residual moment lives on a nearly regular degree sequence]
\label{prop:residual-nearly-reg}
In the matching-peel setting of~\cite{PlusOneV1}, on the high-probability event
of the residual lemmas there, every residual degree satisfies
\[
\deg_R(v)=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_0 d
\]
simultaneously in \(v\), with \(\varepsilon_0=\Theta((\log n/d)^{1/3})\) at the
process-optimal rate.
Conditionally on that degree sequence \(\mathbf{d}_R\), Conjecture~\ref{conj:residual-moment}
asks for relative variance \(o(1)\) of the number of \(m\)-factors of a random
graph with degree sequence \(\mathbf{d}_R\) constrained to the residual edge
set---equivalently, concentration of \(N_m(R)\) inside the class of graphs with
minimum degree \(\Theta(\varepsilon_0 d)\) and maximum degree
\(O(\varepsilon_0 d)\).
Replacing \(R\) by an independent Bernoulli graph of the same mean degree is
blocked by~\cite[Proposition~bernoulli-moment]{PlusOneV1}.
The remaining analytic target is a second-moment method (or small-subgraph
conditioning) for \(m\)-factors in a host that is already degree-constrained at
scale \(\varepsilon_0 d\), not an unconstrained binomial host.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The degree assertion is~\cite[Lemma~residual-sharp]{PlusOneV1} (and the
companion cut/expansion lemmas).
The reformulation is the definition of Conjecture~\ref{conj:residual-moment}
on that event, together with the blocked Bernoulli comparison.
\end{proof}

\section{What would close each statement}

\paragraph{Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q}.}
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BIM25} gives, for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), a constant
\(C\) such that the sandwich holds for every \(d\ge C\log n\) with both edge
probabilities between \((1-\varepsilon)d/n\) and \((1+\varepsilon)d/n\).
Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} allows the gap to tend to \(0\), at size
\(C\sqrt{(\log n)/d}\).
Once \(d/\log n\to\infty\), that quantity tends to \(0\), and a fixed-gap
theorem does not reach it. The dependence of their \(C\) on \(\varepsilon\) is
not given.
At \(d=\Theta(\log n)\) the same square root is a constant, and the
introduction of~\cite{BIM25} records the minimum-degree obstruction: for each
\(C>0\) there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that if \(d=C\log n\), then
\(G(n,(1+\varepsilon)d/n)\) has minimum degree less than \(d\) with probability
\(1-o(1)\).
\cite[Conjecture~1.3]{BIM25} asks for one regular graph sandwiched in the
whole random graph process. The authors state that a proof seems to require
ideas beyond the proof of their Theorem~1.1.
Theorem~\ref{thm:process-to-Q} is the deduction of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:Q} from that process statement.
A thinning of the rate-\(1/3\) residual down to surplus \(\sqrt{d\log n}\)
whose output is binomial is the other route.
Hybrid-to-binomial and freeze-and-sprinkle are blocked.

\paragraph{Conjecture~\ref{conj:config-split}.}
Item (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:selection-reduces} together with the three
peel-weight flatnesses of~\cite{PlusOneV1}.
Free splitting already gives the increment throughout \(d+m=o(n^{1/3})\),
and is a different coupling.

\paragraph{Conjecture~\ref{conj:residual-moment}.}
A second moment on the actual BIM residual \(R=G^*_0\setminus G\), in the
nearly regular degree window of Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-nearly-reg}.
Replacing \(R\) by an independent Bernoulli graph is blocked.

\paragraph{Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment}.}
A signed generating function for the intersection of two \(L\)-factors of a
linear-degree host, with remainder \(o(1)\) throughout the balanced range
\(\min(L,D-L)=\omega(n^{1/3})\).
The near-complete factor side is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-complementary}; the
near-complete host side is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-dense-host}; co-length one
on sublinear hosts is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-sublinear-colength-one}.
The unsigned sequential product is \(\exp(O(L^3/n))\), which is the size of
the signal past \(n^{1/3}\).
The signed matching-host term of Proposition~\ref{prop:signed-matching-host}
leaves the same order.
Keeping that stub shift and the adjacent falling factorial cancels the cubic
at every linear density: Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} gives relative
variance \(o(1)\) for \(L^2=o(n)\). The logarithm that remains is
\(L(a-1)(L(a-1)+a)/(2a^2 n)\) at host density \(a=D/n\), which specialises to
\(L(L-1)/(2n)\) at degree \(n/2\). It does not reach \(\sqrt{n}\) and it does
not give the rate in Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-rate}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:triple-mass-order} sums that principal term on
three-edge matchings: the constant \(4/n^2\) cancels through order \(1/n^4\),
and the following term has mass \(-L^8/(48n^2)\), which is smaller than the
cubic only for \(L=o(n^{1/5})\). The error term of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}
is the same order, so the coefficient does not identify the cumulant.
Binomial leftover plus pairwise is not that generating function.
The exponential \(\exp(L^2(1-p)/(2p))\) of~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{Gao13} is the
edge-count fluctuation of \(G(n,p)\), and~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{Gao13} removes it
once the number of edges is fixed, for \(L^3=o(p^2 n)\).
Remark~\ref{rem:binomial-edge} records that a regular host has already fixed
that number, and that Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt} passes the
restriction \(L^3=o(n)\).
The remaining expansion error on the regular host is \(O(L^4/n^2)\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:switching-accumulation} shows that a relative switching
error \(O(\ell/(Ln)+L/n)\), multiplied through the window \(\ell\le\kappa L^2\),
produces \(\exp(O(L^3/n))\).
That product tends to \(1\) only for \(L=o(n^{1/3})\), and \(L^4/n^2\) is
smaller than \(L^3/n\) by the factor \(L/n\).
Keeping the switching ratio at that precision does not pass
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt}.
The balanced range still requires the coefficient of the entropy remainder to
cancel.

\paragraph{Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-rate}.}
The same signed expansion, to remainder \(o(L^3/n^3)\), on the balanced
range.
The near-complete factor side is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-rate-complementary};
the near-complete host side is Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-rate-dense-host}.
The formal cubic \(L^3/(4n)\) is not the relative variance.

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:long-step}.}
The deletion is the matching chain in that theorem.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matching-linear-rate} is the \(O(1/n^2)\) matching variance,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:matching-step-l1} is the resulting \(O(1/n)\) total variation
of one step.
Flatness of both factor counts remains a separate sufficient condition, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-from-flat}, and is what
Conjecture~\ref{conj:factor-moment} asks for on \(L=\omega(\sqrt{n})\).
That factor flatness is still open.
On the range \(L^2=o(n)\) the factor variance is
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-below-sqrt}, and the resulting deletion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion-below-sqrt}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:aligned-peel} remains the Cauchy--Schwarz bound under a
matching variance \(O(1/n)\): that coarser input stops at \(\sqrt{n}\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-det} removes the signless-Laplacian determinant
as an obstruction on the event that the nontrivial spectral radius is
\(O(\sqrt{n})\), and Proposition~\ref{prop:resolvent-trace} removes the
resolvent trace on the same event.
Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-ceiling} bounds every host by one exponential.
Proposition~\ref{prop:six-det-mass} shows that a vanishing determinant
contributes one fifth of the sum of the counts on six vertices.
The extension ratio of a pair of factors is still open.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-sparse-near-complete} settles the sparse-to-near-complete
regime; Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-gap} settles the matching-gap deletion to
degree \(n-2\) for every \(d\le n/2-1\); Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-into-dirac}
settles matching nesting into every Dirac host; Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-growing}
settles matching nesting into every growing degree.
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-dirac} places a uniform Hamilton cycle in every
Dirac host, and Theorem~\ref{thm:long-hamilton-wide} extends that coupling to
every degree at least \(3\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-matching-at-least-three} places a uniform perfect matching
in every degree at least \(3\).
Remark~\ref{rem:hamilton-not-mu2} records that the cycle marginal is not \(\mu_2\).
Remark~\ref{rem:long-complement-empty} records that the
near-complete moment does not supply both ends under four linear margins.
