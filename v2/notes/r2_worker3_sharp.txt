--- T1. The hypothesis, stated once -----------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{hypothesis}[Uniform form of {\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}}]
\label{hyp:gao6}
For every \(c\in(0,\tfrac12]\) there are \(C_G=C_G(c)\) and \(\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_0(c)>0\)
such that the following holds whenever \(D\le\varepsilon_0 n\).  Let \(H_1\subseteq K_n\) have all
degrees at most \(D\) and \(|H_1|\le(1-c)Dn/2\), and let \(uv\notin H_1\).  Put
\(\tilde d=D\mathbf 1-d^{H_1}\), \(\widetilde M=\sum_i\tilde d_i\),
\(\widetilde M_2=\sum_i\tilde d_i(\tilde d_i-1)\), let \(\mathcal W\) be the set of ordered
pairs \((x,y)\) with \(xy\in H_1\), \(xu,yv\notin H_1\), \(\{x,y\}\cap\{u,v\}=\emptyset\), and
\[
\bar\phi=-2(\tilde d_u+\tilde d_v)+2-\sum_{x\in N_{H_1}(u)}\tilde d_x-\sum_{y\in N_{H_1}(v)}\tilde d_y
-(\tilde d_u+\tilde d_v-2)\frac{\widetilde M_2}{\widetilde M}+\tilde d_u\tilde d_v .
\]
Then
\[
\PP\bigl(uv\in G(n,D)\mid H_1\subseteq G(n,D)\bigr)
=\frac{\tilde d_u\tilde d_v}{\widetilde M}\,(1-A)(1-B)\,(1+\eta),
\qquad |\eta|\le C_G\frac{D^2}{n^2},
\]
with \(A=\widetilde M_2^2/\widetilde M^3+\widetilde M_2/\widetilde M^2
+\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal W}\tilde d_x\tilde d_y/\widetilde M^2\) and \(B=\bar\phi/\widetilde M\).
\end{hypothesis}
This is~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} with \(d=D\mathbf 1\), \(H_2=\emptyset\), \(M=Dn\),
\(\Delta^5n/M^3=D^2/n^2\), and \(\widetilde M=Dn-2|H_1|\ge cM\); the only addition is that the
constant in \(O(\cdot)\) depends on \(c\) alone, which is how the theorem is stated.

--- T2. Proposition A and its proof -----------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Subgraph probabilities to relative error \(D^2|H|/n^2\)]
\label{prop:seq-gao}
Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:gao6}.  Let \(c\in(0,\tfrac12]\), \(D\ge2\), \(Dn\) even, and let \(H\)
be a graph on \([n]\) with \(e\ge1\) edges, degrees \(h_v\le(1-c)D\) and maximum degree \(\Delta\).
Put \(g=D\mathbf 1-h\), \(\bar h=2e/n\), and let \(\lambda=\lambda(g)\), \(\mu=\mu(g,H)\) be as
in the McKay estimate.  If \(D\le\varepsilon_0n\), \(C_GD^2\le n^2/2\) and \(18D\le c^3n\), then
\[
\log\PP\bigl(H\subseteq G(n,D)\bigr)
=\sum_v\log(D)_{h_v}-\sum_{k=0}^{e-1}\log(Dn-1-2k)-\lambda-\lambda^2-\mu+\frac{D^2-1}{4}+\rho,
\]
\[
|\rho|\le\Bigl(2C_G+\frac{90}{c^6}\Bigr)\frac{eD^2}{n^2}
+\Bigl(\frac{90}{c^5}+\frac{24}{c^3}+\frac{6\Delta^2}{c^2}\Bigr)\frac{e}{n^2}+\frac{1}{c^2Dn}
\;\le\;\Bigl(2C_G+\frac{210}{c^6}\Bigr)\frac{eD^2}{n^2}+\frac1{c^2Dn}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Covariances of edge-indicator functions]
\label{lem:seq-smooth}
Let \(E'\) be a finite set with \(|E'|\le e\), let \(S\subseteq E'\) contain each element
independently with probability \(t\), and let \(Y=Y(S)\) satisfy \(|Y|\le a\).  Put
\(b_\epsilon=\sup_S|Y(S\cup\{\epsilon\})-Y(S\setminus\{\epsilon\})|\).  Then
\(|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf 1_{\epsilon\in S},Y)|\le b_\epsilon/4\), so
\(|\mathrm{Cov}(Y,\sum_{\epsilon\in F}\mathbf 1_{\epsilon\in S})|\le\frac14\sum_{\epsilon\in F}b_\epsilon\)
for every \(F\subseteq E'\), and \(\Var Y\le\frac14\sum_\epsilon b_\epsilon^2\).  If moreover
\(X=Dn-2|S|\) and \(nT\) are in \([cDn,Dn]\) with \(|\EE X-nT|\le2\), then for \(p\in\{1,2,3\}\)
\[
\Bigl|\EE\bigl[YX^{-p}\bigr]-\frac{\EE Y}{(nT)^p}\Bigr|
\le\frac{p\,|\mathrm{Cov}(Y,X)|}{(cDn)^{p+1}}+\frac{p(p+1)\,a\,e}{2(cDn)^{p+2}}+\frac{2p\,a}{(cDn)^{p+1}} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning on the other coordinates,
\(\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf 1_{\epsilon\in S},Y)=t(1-t)\,\EE[Y(S\cup\{\epsilon\})-Y(S\setminus\{\epsilon\})]\),
of modulus at most \(b_\epsilon/4\); summing over \(\epsilon\in F\) gives the second claim, and the
variance bound is the Efron--Stein inequality with \(\Var_\epsilon Y\le t(1-t)b_\epsilon^2\).  For the
last display write \(m=\EE X\); since \(X,m\in[cDn,Dn]\),
\(X^{-p}=m^{-p}-p(X-m)m^{-p-1}+r\) with \(|r|\le\frac{p(p+1)}2(X-m)^2(cDn)^{-p-2}\).  Take
expectations against \(Y\), use \(\Var X=4|E'|t(1-t)\le e\), and replace \(m^{-p}\) by
\((nT)^{-p}\) at cost \(|\EE Y|\,p|m-nT|(cDn)^{-p-1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:seq-gao}]
\emph{Step 1 (exact chain).}  For any ordering \(f_1,\dots,f_e\) of \(E(H)\),
\(\PP(H\subseteq G)=\prod_k\PP(f_k\in G\mid f_1,\dots,f_{k-1}\in G)\).  Each factor is
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:gao6} with \(H_1=\{f_1,\dots,f_{k-1}\}\): its degrees are at most \(h_v\le D\),
and \(|H_1|<e\le(1-c)Dn/2\).  Since \(|\eta_k|\le\frac12\), \(|\log(1+\eta_k)|\le2C_GD^2/n^2\).

\emph{Step 2 (random order).}  Give each edge an independent uniform arrival time in \([0,1]\) and
order by arrival.  This order is uniform.  Given that \(f\) arrives at time \(t\), the set
\(S\) of earlier edges contains each edge of \(H\setminus\{f\}\) independently with probability
\(t\).  Averaging the identity of Step~1,
\[
\log\PP(H\subseteq G)=\sum_k\log\frac{\tilde d_u\tilde d_v}{\widetilde M}
+\sum_{f\in H}\int_0^1\EE\bigl[\log(1-A)+\log(1-B)\bigr]\,dt+\varepsilon_1,
\qquad|\varepsilon_1|\le\frac{2C_GeD^2}{n^2},
\]
where \(A,B\) are evaluated at the state \((S,f)\).  The first sum does not depend on the order: the
\(j\)-th edge at \(v\) meets \(\tilde d_v=D-j+1\), so \(\prod_k\tilde d_u\tilde d_v=\prod_v(D)_{h_v}\),
and \(\widetilde M_k=Dn-2(k-1)\).

\emph{Step 3 (linearisation).}  Write \(s_w=\deg_S(w)\) and \(X=\widetilde M=Dn-2|S|\in[cDn,Dn]\).
From \(\widetilde M_2\le D^2n\), \(|\mathcal W|\le2e\le Dn\) and \(\Delta\le D\):
\(0\le A\le3D/(c^3n)\) and \(|\bar\phi|\le7D^2+2D^2/c\le9D^2/c\), so \(|B|\le9D/(c^2n)\).  Both are
at most \(\tfrac12\), and \(|\log(1-x)+x|\le x^2\) there, so replacing \(\log(1-A)+\log(1-B)\) by
\(-(A+B)\) costs \(\varepsilon_2\), \(|\varepsilon_2|\le90eD^2/(c^6n^2)\).

\emph{Step 4 (main term).}  Put \(nT=Dn-2et=n(D-\bar ht)\).  Define the main term
\(\mathcal A(f,t)+\mathcal B(f,t)\) by substituting into \(A+B\)
\(X\mapsto nT\);
\(\widetilde M_2\mapsto m_2:=\sum_w\EE_{\mathrm{Bin}(h_w,t)}[(D-s)(D-s-1)]\);
\(\widetilde M_2^2\mapsto m_2^2\);
\(\sum_{\mathcal W}\tilde d_x\tilde d_y\mapsto2t\sum_{xy\in H}(D-1-(h_x-1)t)(D-1-(h_y-1)t)\);
\(\tilde d_u\mapsto D-(h_u-1)t\) (likewise \(v\));
\(\sum_{x\in N_S(u)}\tilde d_x\mapsto t\sum_{x\in N_H(u)\setminus v}(D-1-(h_x-1)t)\);
\((\tilde d_u+\tilde d_v-2)\widetilde M_2\mapsto(\EE\tilde d_u+\EE\tilde d_v-2)\,m_2\);
\(\tilde d_u\tilde d_v\mapsto\EE\tilde d_u\,\EE\tilde d_v\).
Under the law of Step~2 the last four replacements are exact in expectation: given \(ux\in S\),
\(s_x-1\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h_x-1,t)\); and \(s_u,s_v\) depend on disjoint edge sets.  The others cost
the following, per state and before summing over \(f\):
(i) \(\Var\widetilde M_2\le4eD^2\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:seq-smooth} with \(b\le4D\);
(ii) \(|\mathrm{Cov}(s_u+s_v,\widetilde M_2)|\le2\Delta D\);
(iii) \(|\EE\widetilde M_2-m_2|\le4D\): only \(s_u,s_v\) are \(\mathrm{Bin}(h-1,t)\), and
\((D-s)(D-s-1)\) has increments at most \(2D\);
(iv) the \(\mathcal W\) sum differs from its replacement by at most \((4\Delta+2\Delta^2)D^2\)
pointwise in expectation.  The pairs touching \(u\) or \(v\) number at most \(4\Delta\), the pairs
excluded by \(xu\in S\) or \(yv\in S\) number at most \(2\Delta^2\), and for the remaining pairs
\(\EE[\mathbf 1_{xy\in S}\tilde d_x\tilde d_y]\) is exactly the replaced product.
Dividing by the powers of \(cDn\) and summing over the \(e\) edges, (i)--(iv) contribute at most
\((24c^{-3}+6\Delta^2c^{-2})e/n^2\).  Finally apply Lemma~\ref{lem:seq-smooth} to the five terms
\(\widetilde M_2^2X^{-3}\), \(\widetilde M_2X^{-2}\), \(\sum_{\mathcal W}X^{-2}\),
\(\phi_1X^{-1}\) and \((\tilde d_u+\tilde d_v-2)\widetilde M_2X^{-2}\), where
\(\phi_1=\bar\phi+(\tilde d_u+\tilde d_v-2)\widetilde M_2/\widetilde M\).  Their
\((a,\sum_\epsilon b_\epsilon)\) are at most \((D^4n^2,\,8eD^3n)\), \((D^2n,\,4eD)\),
\((D^3n,\,6eD^2+2\Delta^2D^2)\), \((7D^2,\,16\Delta D)\) and
\((2D^3n,\,8eD^2+4\Delta D^2n)\).  For \(\phi_1\), only the at most \(2\Delta+2\Delta^2\) edges
meeting \(\{u,v\}\cup N_H(u)\cup N_H(v)\) move it.  With \(e\le Dn/2\) the five contributions sum
to at most \(90c^{-5}e/n^2\).  Altogether
\[
\sum_{f}\int_0^1\EE[A+B]\,dt=\int_0^1\sum_f(\mathcal A+\mathcal B)(f,t)\,dt+\varepsilon_3,
\qquad|\varepsilon_3|\le\Bigl(\frac{90}{c^5}+\frac{24}{c^3}+\frac{6\Delta^2}{c^2}\Bigr)\frac{e}{n^2}.
\]

\emph{Step 5 (evaluation).}  \(\sum_f(\mathcal A+\mathcal B)(f,t)\) depends on \(H\) only through
\(\bar h\), \(v_2=n^{-1}\sum_vh_v^2\) and \(s_{11}=n^{-1}\sum_{xy\in H}h_xh_y\).  Each sum over
\(f\) and over neighbours is a sum over vertices, edges or 2-paths of \(H\), and has no further
dependence on \(n\).  Substituting \(t=(D-\sigma)/\bar h\) turns the integrand into a Laurent
polynomial in \(\sigma\), and integrating gives exactly
\[
-\int_0^1\sum_f(\mathcal A+\mathcal B)\,dt=-\lambda-\lambda^2-\mu+\frac{D^2-1}4+\frac12\log\frac{D}{D-\bar h}.
\]
The coefficient of the logarithm is \(\tfrac12\), and the rational parts agree identically
(\texttt{v2/scripts/residual/r2\_worker3\_sharp.py}, section~1).

\emph{Step 6 (pairing normalisation).}  With \(a_k=Dn-2k\ge cDn+2\),
\(\log\frac{a_k}{a_k-1}=\frac12\log\frac{a_k}{a_k-2}+\frac12\log\bigl(1-(a_k-1)^{-2}\bigr)\).  The first
terms telescope to \(\frac12\log\frac{Dn}{Dn-2e}=\frac12\log\frac D{D-\bar h}\), and the second sum
has modulus at most \(1/(c^2Dn)\).  Hence
\(-\sum_k\log(Dn-2k)=-\sum_k\log(Dn-1-2k)-\frac12\log\frac D{D-\bar h}+\varepsilon_4\), and the two
logarithms cancel.  Collect \(\rho=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3+\varepsilon_4\).
\end{proof}

Notes for the paper.  (a) Every replacement in Step~4 is checked against exact enumeration: Gao's
\(A,B\) literally, over all states, for 11 graphs (script section 2).  The discrepancy is
\(0.6\)--\(3.9\,e/n^2\), inside the bound.  (b) For the star \(K_{1,r}\), where
\(\PP=(D)_r/(n-1)_r\) exactly, \(\rho=(r^3/6+5r^2/4+r/12+O(r^3/D))/n^2+O(D^4/n^3)\).  So the
proposition is confirmed against an exact probability.

--- T3. Consequence for Theorem sharp-rate-short (replacement text) ---------------------------------

In \((\ast)\), Lemma~\ref{lem:four-pairings} gives, for the simple graph \(X=B\cup F_1^\circ\cup F_2^\circ\)
with degrees \(h=2L-a\) and \(g=D\mathbf 1-h\),
\[
\PP(R\text{ simple, avoids }X\mid X)
=\PP(\mathcal S)\,\PP\bigl(X\subseteq G(n,D)\bigr)\,\frac{\prod_{k<|X|}(Dn-1-2k)}{\prod_v(D)_{h_v}},
\]
because both sides equal \(N(g,X)\prod_vg_v!/(Dn-2|X|-1)!!\).  The same holds for \(R'\) and \(F\).  The
factor \(\PP(\mathcal S)\) appears once in the numerator and once in the denominator of \((\ast)\)

\(2c\), since \(h_v\le2L\le(1-2c)D\), and with \(|X|\le Ln\), \(|F|=Ln/2\).  So the McKay errors
\(O(D^3/n)\) of the \(R\) and \(R'\) steps become \(O(D^2L/n+1/(Dn))\), uniformly in \(X\).  The
steps \(B,F_1^\circ,F_2^\circ,F\) keep~\cite[Theorem~14]{Gao21} with errors \(O(L^2D/n)\),
\(O(L^3/n)\), \(O(L^3/n)\), \(O(L^3/n)\), inside budgets (iv) and (ii).

\begin{theorem}[Sharp unconstrained \(m\)-factor rate, \(\delta^5m=o(n)\)]
Assume~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:gao6}) and~\cite[Theorem~14]{Gao21}.  Let
\(\delta\to\infty\), \(\delta n\) and \(mn\) even, \(1\le m\le(\frac12-c)\delta\), and suppose
\[
\frac{\delta^5m}{n},\quad\frac{m^4\delta^3}{n},\quad\frac{m^5\delta}{n},\quad\frac{m^2\delta^4}{n}\ \to0 .
\]
Then \(\Var Z/(\EE Z)^2=\frac1{6\delta^3}(1+o(1))\).  At \(m=O(1)\) this is \(\delta=o(n^{1/5})\);
at \(m=\delta^{1/2}\), \(\delta=o(n^{2/11})\); at \(m=\delta^{2/3}\), \(\delta=o(n^{3/17})\).
\end{theorem}
Proof: that of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-rate-short}, with error (i) now \(O(\delta^2m/n)\).  The
hypotheses \(D^3=o(n)\) and \(L^7=o(D^2n)\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:growing-degree-factor} follow:
\(m^7\le m^5\delta^2=o(\delta n)\).  The additive slack \(O(\delta^2m/n)\) is now \(o(\delta^{-3})\),
so it can be dropped from the statement.

--- T4. Remark: the barrier at delta^5 m = n, and how far a better Thm 6 would go ------------------

\begin{remark}[Where the \(n^{1/5}\) barrier sits]
The error of Proposition~\ref{prop:seq-gao} is \(e\cdot O(D^2/n^2)\), summed over edges.  The
per-edge term is attained: for \(H_1=\emptyset\) the principal term of~\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}
differs from \(D/(n-1)\) by the relative amount \(-(D^2-D+1)/n^2+O(D^3/n^3)\).  Along a star the
exact conditional probability \((D-j)/(n-1-j)\) shows that this \(D^2/n^2\) is the cross term \(AB\)
of the product \((1-A)(1-B)\), with \(A\approx D/n\) and \(B\approx-D/n\).  If the principal term is
written as \(\frac{\tilde d_u\tilde d_v}{\widetilde M}e^{-A-B}\) instead, the relative error along the
star is \(\bigl((j+1)^2/2+O(j^3/D^2)\bigr)/n^2\), with no growth in \(D\).
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Exponential form of~{\cite[Theorem~6]{Gao20}}]
Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:gao6},
\(\PP(uv\in G\mid H_1\subseteq G)=\frac{\tilde d_u\tilde d_v}{\widetilde M}e^{-A-B}
\bigl(1+O((1+\Delta_{H_1}^2)/n^2+D^3/n^3)\bigr)\).
\end{conjecture}
Under the conjecture, Steps 1--6 run with \(\varepsilon_2=0\) and give
\(\rho=O((1+\Delta^2)e/n^2+D^3e/n^3+1/(Dn))\).  The \(R\)-step error is then
\(O(m^3/n+\delta^3m/n^2)\), the first term inside budget (ii), and the theorem above holds with
\(\delta^5m/n\) replaced by \(\delta^6m/n^2\).  At \(m=O(1)\) the binding budget becomes (iv),
\(m^2\delta^4/n\), so \(\delta=o(n^{1/4})\); at \(m=\delta^{1/2}\) it is (ii)/(iv),
\(\delta=o(n^{1/5})\); for \(m\ge\delta^{2/3}\) nothing changes.

OBSTRUCTIONS PAST n^{1/4} (m = O(1)), NOT RESOLVED
 * Budget (iv) m^2 delta/n has two sources.  One is the McKay error of step B, which is
   O(L^2 D/n) and unstructured.  The other is the K-dependence factor e^{l^2 sigma_K^2/2} of
   Thm growing-degree-factor, which is an explicit term of E[Z^2]/(EZ)^2 of size O(m^2 delta/n),
   not just a lost constant.  If l = dPhi_0/dK is genuinely Theta(delta/n) at the typical overlap
   (not computed here), then Var Z/(EZ)^2 contains a true m^2 delta/n term.  In that case
   1/(6 delta^3) is NOT the variance for delta^4 m^2 >> n, and the slack form
   "1/(6 delta^3)(1+o(1)) + O(delta^2 m/n)" is the right kind of statement there.
   NEXT COMPUTATION: l at K = nq/2, exactly, from the four McKay exponents with abar free.
 * Section 5 gives some reassurance for the ratio.  ANY second-order Thm-6 error that is a smooth
   function of the degree statistics cancels in X - 2F down to O(D m^2/n + m^3/n).  So even without
   Conjecture B the true R-step contribution is probably budget-(iv) size.  This is a model, not a
   proof.

SIDE OBSERVATION (pre-existing, in Thm growing-degree-factor, "Dependence on K"; not checked in depth)
 The proof says the moderate-deviation LLT holds out to C_0 D standard deviations "because
 D^3 (nq)^{-1/2} -> 0".  With nq = nL^2/D this is D^{7/2}/(L sqrt n) -> 0, which D^3 = o(n) does
 not imply at L = O(1): it needs D = o(n^{1/7}).  Theorem sharp-rate-short uses this step at
 delta up to n^{1/6}, and the new theorem at n^{1/5}.  The step probably needs less.  On W,
 l |K - nq/2| <= l C_0 D sigma_K = O(D^{3/2} L/sqrt n), so e^{l(K-nq/2)} is nearly constant and
 only Q(W) -> 1 plus a cumulant expansion of E_Q e^{l xi} is needed.  That should be rewritten
 before the range is extended.
