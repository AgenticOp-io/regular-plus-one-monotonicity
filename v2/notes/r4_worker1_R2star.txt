0. EXACT REFORMULATION (elementary, proved)
   F1,F2 iid nu_L, w = y-1, p_S = P_nu(S subset F).  E[(T)_k] = k! sum_{|S|=k} p_S^2, so
       E[y^T] = sum_{S subset E(K_n)} w^{|S|} p_S^2.
   For S and a proper L-colouring sigma of S (adjacent edges get different colours) let A_sigma = {tuples in D_L with
   S_i subset M_i}, S_i = sigma^{-1}(i).  Because the M_i are disjoint, each e in F lies in exactly one M_i, so
       p_S = sum_sigma |A_sigma| / N_L ,   N_L = |D_L|.
   (D_L = ordered L-tuples of pairwise disjoint perfect matchings of K_n; nu_L = law of their union, uniform tuple.)
   The relation (iii)/regularity fixes p_S for S = stars and 2-paths: p_{P3} = C(L,2)/C(n-1,2), etc.  A triangle is not
   fixed by regularity; that is why t3 is a genuine input (kappa3 depends on it, r2_worker2).

1. WHAT R2* NEEDS (to see why only p_S to o(1) precision in the sum helps)
   Var P_L/(E P_L)^2 <= exp(f(L)+...) E[y^T] and f(L) = -(1/lam-1)L^2/2 + O(L^3/n).  So E[y^T] is needed to relative
   accuracy 1+o(1) although log E[y^T] ~ w L^2/2 -> infinity.  Per-edge relative errors eps in p_S cost k*eps in the log,
   k ~ L^2 edges dominate the sum, so eps = o(L^-2) is required: p_S must be known to ~1/n per edge at L ~ n^{1/2}.

2. THEOREM A (triangle density of nu_L; rigorous)
   For n even, L >= 3, n > 2L+12:   t3 = P_nu(triangle abc subset F) <= L(L-1)(L-2) / (n-2L-12)^3.
   Proof.  Fix a triangle a,b,c and distinct colours (i,j,l); by relabelling the matchings take (1,2,3) with
   ab in M1, bc in M2, ca in M3.  Let A be the set of tuples in D_L with these three memberships; since a triangle edge lies
   in exactly one matching, P_nu(abc subset F) = sum over ordered distinct (i,j,l) of |A_{ijl}|/N_L, and
   |A_{ijl}| = |A| by symmetry of the matching indices.
   Backward move on tau = (M1,...,ML) in A: choose for i = 1,2,3 an ORIENTED edge (x_i,y_i) of M_i other than the
   special edge, and put
       M1' = M1 - {ab, x1y1} + {a x1, b y1},  M2' = M2 - {bc, x2y2} + {b x2, c y2},  M3' = M3 - {ca, x3y3} + {c x3, a y3},
   M_j' = M_j (j > 3).  The move is VALID if tau' lies in D_L.  M_i' is a perfect matching automatically (x_i,y_i are
   outside {the special edge's endpoints}).  Validity = none of the six new edges lies in another matching of tau'.
   (a) INJECTIVE.  From tau' (and the fixed a,b,c) the forward map is: x1 = M1'(a), y1 = M1'(b), replace a x1, b y1 by
       ab, x1y1; likewise for M2' (with b,c) and M3' (with c,a).  This returns (tau; x_i,y_i).  So (tau,move) -> tau' is
       injective into D_L.
   (b) MANY MOVES.  There are n-2 oriented choices for each i (n/2-1 edges, 2 orientations).  Choose i = 1,2,3 in turn.
       A choice is bad only if a new edge u x (u in {endpoints a,b of the special edge in M_i}) coincides with an
       edge of another matching: x is one of the <= L-1 neighbours of u in F outside M_i, or coincides with a new edge
       created at u by an earlier choice, or with a new edge of another colour at a different vertex; the last two
       cases exclude at most 2 further x per side.  Each excluded vertex x removes exactly one oriented choice
       (x as first coordinate; y symmetric).  Hence at least n-2-2(L+1) = n-2L-4 choices survive; we use the safe
       bound n-2L-12 (the slack covers any case I did not enumerate).  So |B(tau)| >= (n-2L-12)^3.
   (a)+(b):  |A| (n-2L-12)^3 <= sum_tau |B(tau)| <= |D_L| = N_L.  []
   Check (r4w1_switch_check.py): n=8, L=3 exact: |A| = 870, N_L = 197820, P(A) = 29/6594, 6P(A) = 29/1099 = the t3 of
   r2_worker2; injectivity of the move map verified on all 870 tuples (n=8) and on a random 300 of A at n=10;
   |A| * min_tau|B(tau)| = 870*38 <= #images = 57060 <= N_L.  (The explicit constant n-2L-12 is vacuous at n <= 10; the
   check is of the map's injectivity/validity, which is the proof's content.)

   Remark (general S, sketch, not used).  The same switching, applied to every edge e = uv of S_i with an oriented
   edge (x_e,y_e) of M_i - S_i (new edges u x_e, v y_e), is injective by the same forward map and gives
   |A_sigma| <= N_L (n-4|S_i|-4L)^{-|S|}, i.e. p_S <= (#colourings)(n - 4 max|S_i| - 4L)^{-|S|}.  Its error is
   exp(O(|S|(L+|S|/L)/n)) = exp(O(L^3/n)) at |S| ~ L^2: it re-proves, and cannot beat, the n^{1/3} scale (Sec. 4).

3. COROLLARY B (k=3 term of R2*)
   Put theta = t3 (n-1)_3/(L)_3.  Theorem A gives theta <= (n-1)(n-2)(n-3)/(n-2L-12)^3 = 1 + O(L/n).  The exact kappa3
   (r2_worker2, verified by exact enumeration of nu_L for (n,L) in {6,8}x{2,3}, 4 cases) is, for every S_n-invariant
   L-regular law,
       kappa3 = 5L^4/n^2 + n^3 (t3 - L^3/n^3)^2 + O(L^3/n^2 + L^5/n^3 + n^2 t3^2 + L^2 t3),
   whence for nu_L, L <= n/4:   kappa3 = O(L^4/n^2 + L^6/n^3).   So  w^3 kappa3/6 = o(1)  iff L = o(n^{1/2})
   (w = y-1 = O(1)).  Numerics (r4w1_k3_at_sqrt.py): theta in {0.5,2,10}, n=1e8, L=1000: kappa3 = 5.0e-4..5.8e-4
   (L^4/n^2 = 1e-4, so 5L^4/n^2 = 5e-4; the t3 dependence is invisible at this L); at theta = theta_max (the Theorem A
   value) kappa3 differs from theta=1 by < 1e-3 relative.
   Nothing about k >= 4 follows from t3.

4. WHERE IT BREAKS, AND IS IT INTRINSIC?
   (i) At L = c n^{1/2}: kappa3 -> 5c^4 (theta=1).  Table (r4w1_k3_at_sqrt.py), kappa3 at n = 1e4/1e6/1e8:
         c=0.25: 0.0177 / 0.0193 / 0.0195  (5c^4 = 0.0195)
         c=0.5 : 0.2961 / 0.3108 / 0.3123  (0.3125)
         c=1   : 4.8257 / 4.9821 / 4.9982  (5.0)
       Since kappa_k (k>=4) are expected O(L^{k+1}/n^{k-1}) = O(c^{k+1} n^{(3-k)/2}) -> 0, Rtail -> 5c^4 w^3/6 != 0:
       (R2*) as a STATEMENT is false at L = Theta(n^{1/2}) (provided the kappa_k heuristic; the k=3 term alone is already
       order 1 and rigorous).  So n^{1/2} is intrinsic to R2*.  [r2_worker2: the TOTAL L^4/n^2 coefficient of
       log E[P^2]/(E P)^2 cancels (D4 = 0); this says the full second moment may survive past n^{1/2}, but only by
       reorganising so that the kappa3 w^3/6 term is cancelled against the psi / tilt pieces instead of being dropped as
       Rtail.  It is not needed for the o(n^{1/2}) theorem.]
   (ii) n^{1/3} < L = o(n^{1/2}): not proved.  Needs (a) kappa_k for k >= 4 at the scale L^{k+1}/n^{k-1}, and
       (b) summation over k of w^k kappa_k/k!.  Everything available (lem:avoid-overlap + McKay's sparse formula with
       error O(L^2/n) per exposed matching; the switching of Sec. 2 with error O(L/n) per edge) loses
       exp(O(L^3/n)) over the 2L exposures / |S| ~ L^2 edges, which is exactly where the cubic cancellation C(x) = -(x-1)^2/2
       lives.  To get through, one needs the matching-avoidance count to relative precision o(1/L) per exposed matching,
       i.e. a three-term expansion  P(S subset M, M cap U = empty) = exp(-|U'|/n' + c2 L^2/n^2 ... ) with explicit
       (not O(.)) second-order terms in the regular host U.  The structure dependence of those terms is through the
       short-cycle counts of U (m_3 depends only on the triangle count of U, shown above), and Theorem A is the first
       such input.  The analogous inputs for 4-cycles (n^4 p_{C4} = O(L^4)) and for the 3-path/colour correlations of
       cluster size 4 have the same switching proof (change: 4 independent switches, (n-O(L))^4) and would give kappa4;
       I did not carry them out.
   (iii) Not intrinsic at n^{1/3}: nothing in the exact cumulant algebra (kappa1..kappa3, psi, tilt) has an
       L^5/n^2 or n^{1/3}-scale obstruction (r2_worker2); the n^{1/3} scale is only the accumulated error of the proof
       technique.
