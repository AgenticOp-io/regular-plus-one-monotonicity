# r53 — L1' (arbitrary d, D<=n^c): no proof; one new reformulation, minimal missing lemma stated

Scope: this note + `v2/scripts/r53_ratio.py`. Nothing proved. Labels: proved / sketch / false / open.

## Route tried (not recorded in r34/r47): exact integer-d "tilted-measure" form of Isaev-McKay
- **proved (trivial)**: with N(d)=[x^d] prod_{i<j}(1+x_i x_j) = (2pi)^-n ∫ prod(1+r_i r_j e^{i(th_i+th_j)}) e^{-i d.th} / r^d dth,
  d -> d+e_u+e_v is *exactly* multiplication of the integrand by e^{-i(th_u+th_v)} (shift of one lattice point, no
  interpolation in d). So N(d+e_u+e_v)/N(d) = r_u^-1 r_v^-1 <e^{-i(th_u+th_v)}>_d, an expectation under the complex
  measure of the d-count integral, same saddle r for both. This answers r34 route 2's objection (continuation in d has
  no counting meaning): none is needed.
- **sketch**: ln<e^{-i th_u}> = -sum cumulants (-i)^k kappa_k(th_u)/k!. Gaussian part gives the G0-type term; kappa_4 and
  mixed (th_u,th_v) cumulants carry the D/n^2 target. Needed: kappa_k(th_u) for k<=4 to absolute error o(D/n^2).
- **why it stops (open)**: Isaev-McKay control the *total mass* ∫ with relative error O(D^?/n) by a tail bound plus
  cubic/quartic Taylor remainder under a Gaussian. For a ratio of two such integrals with a bounded smooth factor, the
  common mass error cancels only if the remainder is expanded to the order of the target: that is the quartic+sextic
  cumulant expansion of the log-integrand, with remainder bounds uniform in d (each degree d_i enters as its own
  Gaussian variance ~ d_i) and the non-Gaussian tail beyond |th|~D^-1/2 . No such bound is published. Equivalent in
  difficulty to route 4 (KP cluster) — it is the same expansion in Fourier dress; no gain in rigor.

## Minimal missing lemma (precise)
(M) Uniformly for degree sequences with D<=n^c and the standard Isaev-McKay saddle r(d): for u,v,
 |ln<e^{-i(th_u+th_v)}>_d - [-(sigma_u^2+sigma_v^2)/2 - sigma_uv + quartic-cumulant term]| <= C D/n^2,
i.e. the log-integrand's cumulant expansion to 4th order with remainder O(D/n^2) beyond |th|<=D^-1/2 log n. Equivalent
to L1' for B=2; B>2 follows by iteration (B steps, error B(D+B)/n^2 if (M) is uniform along the path).
